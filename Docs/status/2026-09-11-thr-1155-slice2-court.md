# THR-1155 slice 2, step 5 — a nation keeps a court

**2026-09-11 · Engine · membership**

Conquest was in place as of step 4 and waiting for an army to arrive. Step 2 had already
named why none would: `selectCommander` picks the highest-Iron **member** of a faction,
and a Realm had no members worth the name.

The cause was one argument. `buildDataDrivenFactionLocationMap(graph, factionDefIds)`
was built from the *static*-definition roster alone, so a Realm — whose definition is
dynamic, minted at worldgen through the THR-1322 door — could not appear in it at any
Location, and `assignFactionsToExistingNpcs` had nothing to route to it. **Nobody was a
subject of a nation.** Not because a check failed, but because the question was never
asked.

## Four changes

**The node carries the `factionType` its own definition already declared.** The readers
split, and that is the whole trap: `factionNetwork.ts:268` falls back to the definition,
but `pickFactionForNpc`'s type bracket reads the *node* property — as `factionSeeding`
does when it mints a definition-backed faction. `buildRealmDefinition` has always said
`factionType: 'political'`; the mint never stamped it, so a Realm was invisible to the
very routing that fills a court. Every arm below reports an empty court when the stamp is
removed.

**Realms join the recruiting map** beside the static roster. `ensureFactionControlAtHomeLocations`
deliberately keeps the static roster only — a Realm has no `homeLocationId`; its ground is
the territory the mint wrote.

**A Realm recruits its court, never its census.** A Realm holds every town of its domain,
so it is present at almost every Location the map covers, where a guild is present at one.
Left in the untyped fallback of `pickFactionForNpc` it would have absorbed every role
whose preferred type is absent locally — a mason in a guildless town, a brewer, a
wanderer — which is roughly a thousand `member_of` edges against the ~190 that exist, and
the wrong reading of the ladder besides. **Its lowest rung is *stranger***: one becomes a
*subject* by joining (`realm.join`, content owed by THR-1454), not by being born on held
ground. So a Realm is a candidate only for roles naming `political` or `military` — the
guards, captains, nobles, stewards and heralds a court is actually made of — and it wins
those through the bracket on merit, as any faction does.

**The captain of a capital's garrison answers to that capital's Realm.** The garrison pass
predates Realms and read the holder off the capital's lowest-id `controls` edge, which was
the best available proxy when the holder was a nameless generic faction. It is wrong twice
over now: the guild-hall reconciliation drops a Realm's edge where a definition faction
keeps its home, so at **two of seed 42's three capitals the Realm holds no edge at all**
and the only remaining holder is the guild. The captain of the nation's seat swore to the
Arcane Circle at `loc_9` and to the Temple of Spheres at `loc_1`, and each took the *hold
the seat* host with them — a library guild fielding an army to hold a kingdom's capital.

That one matters more than it looks, because ambient NPCs are minted with **no
`domainCapabilities` at all**: every seeded NPC scores raw 0 in every reach and lands at
Iron tier 1. The garrison captain carries `WORLDGEN_GARRISON_CAPTAIN_IRON` and is
therefore the only member of a Realm's court who can clear `ARMY_SPAWN_IRON_TIER_MIN`.
Losing them to a guild was losing the Realm's only possible commander.

## And one thing the garrison had to stop doing

Giving all three Realms a garrison exposed a simplification that was harmless while
exactly one faction per world had one. The pass wrote the ambition to the **faction** as
well as the host — and that ambition carries `targetNodeId: null`, so `phaseFactionAmbitions`
never abandons it, while that phase scores a want only for a faction with no active
`pursues` edge. Three garrisons would have pinned all three Realms to
`resource_acquisition` for the life of the run and shut the territorial want —
**the gate the entire conquest path opens through** — permanently.

Measured both ways: with the faction edge written, no Realm held a territorial want at
tick 150. Without it, `faction_1` does, and raises a host for it. A garrison is a standing
commitment, not a nation's want; the host keeps its own pursuit of that ambition node,
which is all `spawnArmy` needs.

`army_faction_1_150` is the point: a host raised through the **ambition path**, at tick

prevent. The static factions lose 29 members between them, none to zero, the smallest
(`rangers_brotherhood`) unchanged at 10; 19 of the Realms' 48 new members had no faction
at all before.

## A crash on the way, and the line drawn around it

Realms fielding hosts routed a siege into a settlement whose `prosperity` was not a
number, and `applyAftermath` was the codebase's **one** reader of that field without a
`typeof` guard — `(…prosperity as number) ?? 0` catches null but not a wrong *type*. It
went straight into `prosperityBefore.toFixed(2)` and threw inside `runTick`, whose
fail-soft catch returns the unchanged state; the tick stopped advancing and every later
tick re-threw. 300 `runTick` calls reached tick 167, which is what `tickEventIdUniqueness`
reported as 61 duplicate ids. The guard now matches `phaseUnrest`, `guildSeeding`,
`sublocation` and `complicationEffects`, which all already read it that way.

**The corrupt value itself predates this ticket and is not fixed here.**
`applyNodeChanges` understands a relative change written as the string `'-25'` but not the
object `{ delta: -25 }`, and `monster-encounter-content` authors the horde raid's
prosperity and lair defence hits in the object form — so the executor writes the object
verbatim into the property, and every guarded reader then treats that settlement as
destitute forever. Measured on `origin/main` at tick 300 of seed 42, **with no Realm
change in the tree: three Locations already carry `{ delta: n }` in `properties.defense`.**
Which shape is canonical is a design call that changes what a horde raid does to a town,
so it is THR-1456's, not this commit's. This commit owns only the rule that the tick loop
survives bad data whoever wrote it (NFP #4).

## Falsified, not watched

Every arm was perturbed and the failure read: removing the `factionType` stamp empties the
courts; removing the realm filter lands `trader` and `brewer` in them
(`expected [ 'trader', 'trader', 'brewer', …(2) ] to deeply equal []`); reverting the
garrison owner puts the captain back in a guild and drops two of the three hosts;
restoring the faction `pursues` edge fails exactly
`expected [ 'amb_faction_0_garrison' ] to not include 'amb_faction_0_garrison'`; restoring
the prosperity cast throws `prosperityBefore.toFixed is not a function`.

The first attempt at the fourth of those injected the edge *outside* its guard, where it
threw a duplicate-id error caught by the pass's own try/catch — so the arm went red for
the wrong reason and was redone inside the guard before being believed.
