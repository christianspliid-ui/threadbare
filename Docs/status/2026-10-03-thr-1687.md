### 2026-10-03 — THR-1687: the fair own-hex draw is built and measured, and switched off pending a decision

**What shipped.** The cap's own-hex pass (30 slots for templates on a mortal's hex) can now fill by a fair draw instead of catalogue order. Catalogue order is why the newest, harder encounters never reach the mortals they were written for. The switch is `CAP_FILL_LOCAL_ORDER`, and it **ships `'walk'` (the old pass, exactly)**. That is the plan's kill-criterion fallback: one of its guard rails failed with the draw on.

- **The draw:** `'template_hash'` takes one entry per own-hex template and fills the slots with the smallest bit-mixed `hashString(agent:tick:template)`.
- **One change from the plan:** a murmur3 mixer on top of the hash. The bare polynomial hash only shifts every same-length id by one constant, so `…_01, …_02, …` kept suffix order on every tick; a unit test caught it.
- `runFilterPipeline` takes an optional `capFill`, so `readers/cap-band.ts` really A/Bs the two orders. The `encounter_filter` trace names the order in `capLocalOrder`.

**What the draw does when it is on** (medium, seeds 42 · 99 · 7, `main` d20c72c3 vs branch, same session; raw output in `Docs/audits/2026-09-25-living-world-data/output/cap-band-2026-10-03-thr1687-shipped.txt`).
- Expert deciders, cap keep rate for expert-fit ÷ novice-fit content: 0.09 · 0.12 · 0.14 → 0.67 · 0.74 · 0.72. The 0.7 target is met on 99 and 7; seed 42 straddles it (0.64 every 5 ticks, 0.74 over 200 ticks).
- Experts' mean attempted difficulty: 0.13 · 0.16 · 0.12 → 0.23 · 0.24 · 0.22, above journeymen. The invariant's journeyman → expert clause passes.
- `reach.ts 42,99 200`: firings 1,918 → 2,175, drawable templates fired 217 → 235, top-10 share 0.258 → 0.182.
- In-window share: 0.474 · 0.462 · 0.438 → 0.464 · 0.441 · 0.478 (THR-1689's question).

**Why it is off.** The world shifted in ways nobody has read yet (seed 42, 200 ticks):
- Planner decisions fell 863 → 693, and `start_local` fell 753 → 620. That is −17.7%; the guard rail is −10%.
- Strategic decisions fell 44 → 28, and 104 → 38 on seed 99, even though the cap never sees them.
- Mortals were *not* simply busier: busy mortal-ticks went 4,142 → 3,998, and template length is flat.
- Whole-tick time rose +10%. A shadow build that computes the draw and discards it runs +0.0% · +1.9%, so that cost is the different world, not the pass.
- Masters began winning 0.74–0.76 (ceiling 0.70) on expert work below their window.

**The invariant.** The old rise-plus-window clause is split three ways, all skipped with owners:
- *Journeyman → expert* is `TODO(THR-1687)`: it un-skips with the flip.
- *Expert → master* waits on master content (THR-1688).
- *In-window share* waits on THR-1689.
- Master level success stays live under `'walk'`, with a note that the flip will break it until THR-1688 lands.
