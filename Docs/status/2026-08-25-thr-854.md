### THR-854 — three faction pairs rendered byte-identical heraldry (2026-08-25)

**The defect.** The procedural coat-of-arms generator collapsed a faction's eight-dimensional reach profile down to `(dominantReach, secondaryReach, factionType)`, and `secondaryReach` was set *only* when the second domain sat within 20% of the first. Two factions agreeing on the survivors rendered the same shield, byte for byte: 17 distinct shields for 20 registered definitions. Identical heraldry is not a missing sigil — it is a positive claim of shared allegiance, and the Underking's Court reading as the Thieves Guild is a kinship the world never asserts.

**Why it was deferred from THR-638 and what answered the gate.** The fix was a design question — what *should* distinguish two factions with the same reach profile and type? — and picking one inside an art batch would have been improvisation. `tb-orchestrator` promoted it on the rule-4 reading: that distinct heraldry is wanted is not in dispute, so which axis carries the distinction is a *how*, and the body already enumerated three candidates.

**Two of the three candidates were ruled out on mechanics, not taste.** A **defId hash** would difference two factions because their *strings* differ, asserting nothing true about them — heraldry here is derived from what a faction is. **`factionType` as a second axis** cannot work at all: all three colliding pairs *share* their `factionType`. What remained is the third — and the collisions turned out to be precisely the information the generator was already discarding.

**Shipped: the shield now names a faction's three strongest domains, on three heraldic slots.** Rank 1 keeps the field tinctures and the primary charge. Rank 2 is **always** carried, and `SECONDARY_REACH_THRESHOLD` stops deciding *whether* the second domain appears and starts deciding *how boldly* — a rivalling second domain renders exactly as before, a distant one smaller and dimmer. Rank 3 colours the **bordure**, which is where real heraldry has always put a difference mark.

**Both halves are forced by the data, not preference — and this is the part worth keeping.** Deleting the threshold outright was the obvious move and it is wrong: `adventuring_guild` (eye, iron-distant) would then have collided with `rangers_brotherhood` (eye, iron-close), which share even their third reach — trading three collisions for a new one. And the bordure alone is not enough either: `thieves_guild` and `underking_court` agree on rank 1 *and* rank 2 (shadow, then gold), so nothing short of rank 3 separates them, while `monster_energy` and `monster_time` differ *only* at rank 2. Each axis fixes a pair the other cannot.

**Result: the membership predicate selects the empty set — 20 distinct shields for 20 definitions.**

**The gate is real, proven from both sides.** The replaced assertion was deliberately weak (`distinct.size > 1`) and named this ticket as its owner. Its replacement pins the *predicate*, never the count — the body says so explicitly, and `17 of 20` rots the next time a definition is added — and it reports the colliding **ids** rather than a number, so whoever breaks it learns which two factions started claiming kinship. Run against the unfixed generator it goes red naming exactly `adventuring_guild == lorekeepers_covenant`, `thieves_guild == underking_court`, `monster_energy == monster_time`; run against the fix, green. Two further tests guard the individual axes, since the sweep would pass if some unrelated change happened to differentiate a pair.

**One trap the test had to dodge.** The clip-path id is seeded per definition, so the raw data URIs were *always* distinct while the heraldry was not — a distinctness test over unnormalised URIs would have passed on the broken tree and asserted nothing. Both the sweep and the render evidence normalise it away.

**Verification.** Code track. **1065 files / 17,639 tests green**, ratchet 3014 unchanged from baseline, build ✓ 14.52s. No engine smoke owed — the diff touches no engine, tick, or type surface.

**Browser-verify substitution: jsdom-render — unattended run, no startable dev server.** `preview_start` refused outright (*"Dev servers can't be started from unattended sessions"*, impediments #546/#574), which closes the Playwright route too since it presumes a running server; the Browser pane separately could not composite. Evidence is a mount of the real `CoatOfArms` component on the actual colliding definitions, proving the generator's string reaches the DOM through `useMemo` + `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` and that the shields differ *as mounted elements* — red on all five without the fix. **The one thing jsdom cannot judge is whether it looks right**, so the single aesthetic risk the change introduces — a dim bordure losing the shield's silhouette against THR-638's crushed dark field — is closed by assertion instead of by eye: every reach's bordure colour is pinned above the brightest crushed field's luminance, for all eight reaches rather than the three that happened to move.
