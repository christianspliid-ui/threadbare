# THR-1152 — the step re-read says its own name

**Shipped 2026-08-17.** Director bug report from live play of the deployed Unsafe Bridge
aftermath, filed during his THR-1130 batch-1 sample verdict.

## What was wrong

THR-1136 §2 shipped step replay on the aftermath and shipped it *correctly*: the navigator
receives `onSelectStep`, resolved dots are clickable, and a click swaps in the existing
`StepReplayView`. Every wire is connected. What it did not ship is an **affordance**.

The whole control rendered as a 7px coloured dot beside the italic word *"resolved"*. On a
single-step encounter — the Bridge — that is **one dot**, and it reads as a status bullet.
Nothing on the surface said, in words, that a finished step could be re-opened. The director,
who ordered the feature in THR-1136, played the surface and reported the capability absent:

> *"no ability to move between finished and active steps of 'the unsafe bridge' encounter to
> brush up on the context (if you forgot what encounter this aftermath is part of due to
> parallel threads going on in the game at the same time)."*

A control only its implementer can find has not shipped. That is the whole defect, and it is
worth naming precisely because every mechanical test THR-1136 wrote was green and stayed green.

## The fix

The dots keep doing the work; a **labelled text control announces it in words**, beside the
status readout, on both the aftermath and the live path. It opens the earliest resolved step —
from there the dots teach themselves, because the player has now seen what they do.

Wording is positional rather than clever: `re-read this step` / `re-read the steps` on the
ending, `re-read the finished step` / `re-read the finished steps` mid-encounter.

Three decisions that were not free:

**The status text stays status.** Overloading the word *"resolved"* with a second, silent job
as a button is the same class of failure as the dot — a thing that is secretly a control. The
invitation is its own element, gold and underlined against the ghost-toned readout, because
gold italic *beside* ghost italic was already the ambiguity the director could not read.

**THR-1003's rule survives intact.** A control that cannot act is not drawn: no host handler,
or no resolved step, means no invitation. The live path counts **resolved** steps only — the
current step's dot is clickable there, but as the way *back* to the present, so counting it
would invite a re-read of a step that has not happened.

**The invitation withdraws during a replay.** `StepReplayView` already owns the one
return-shaped control on screen; a second entrance beside it is the Law 21 failure the
aftermath's exit is suppressed to avoid.

## The body behind the door

Making the control findable raises the stakes on what is behind it, so the ticket asked for the
replay body to be verified substantive across the slice. The check had to be written where it
can fail: `StepReplayView` renders `replayNarrative || afterimage`, and the adapter's last
resort is the bare words `'Succeeded'` / `'Failed'` — so a non-emptiness assertion at the
component **passes on a template that authored nothing at all**. The vacuous shape exactly.

The sweep therefore runs at the source, over `SLICE_TEMPLATE_IDS` (a predicate, not the
ticket's "five" — the slice ships eight, and a ninth is covered the day it lands): every
concrete step, branch routes included, must author `narrativeTemplate` **and** an afterimage on
both sides. All eight pass. **Falsified before being believed:** the same predicate run across
the whole registry fails for **212 of 683 templates** (196 steps with no narrative, 218 missing
an afterimage), so the slice passing is real authoring rather than a toothless assertion.
