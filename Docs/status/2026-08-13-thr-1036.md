# THR-1036 — the encounter corpus stops handing the player raw `{adj}` and `{verb}s`

**Shipped 2026-08-13.** The ticket reported `encounter.trial_by_combat` rendering *"Syntar is accused of {adj} crime"* and *"{verb}s the court's {adj} attention"* into the Chapter Ledger — a Law 43 violation ("placeholders never leak"). It read like one broken template. It was 153 of 168.

## The noun before the verb

The ticket's own guess was that the enrichment pipeline "doesn't recognize or isn't invoked for this template". Half right, and the half that was wrong is the interesting half.

A resolver for exactly these tokens already existed and was **correct**: `resolveEncounterNarrative` in `encounter-content.ts` handled `{verb}s` as base-form-plus-`s`, and cycled `{adj}` through a pool so four `{adj}`s in one sentence got four *different* adjectives. It had **zero callers** — one reference in the whole repo, its own definition.

Meanwhile `chapterArchive.ts` renders encounter prose through `enrichProse()`, which had never learned the tokens. So the corpus was authored against a contract that one function honoured and nothing invoked, while the function that actually ran had never heard of it. Same class as THR-933's `{actor}`, and the comment THR-933 left in `proseEnrichment.ts` describes this defect exactly, one token family over.

**Not the sphere vocabulary.** `SPHERE_VOCABULARY` also defines `{adj}`/`{verb}` pools and `narrative.ts` resolves the same token names from it — and it is the wrong source here, because its verbs are past tense (`shattered`, `forged`) while the corpus writes `{verb}s` expecting a base form. Routing through it would have emitted "shattereds". The pools that ship are base-form by construction.

## What the pools revealed once they ran

Wiring the resolver made the corpus legible for the first time, and it was carrying three further defects that a leaked `{adj}` had been hiding:

| Defect | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Both verb forms conjugated to `+s` | 144 bare `{verb}` sites all have **plural** subjects — "the merchants {verb} in anger", "{They} {verb} a sentence" — against 394 singular `{verb}s`. The old resolver conjugated both, producing "They trembles a sentence". | `{verb}s` → third person, bare `{verb}` → base form |
| Naive `verb + 's'` | `flash` → "flashs", `echo` → "echos" | `thirdPersonSingular()` — sibilants and consonant+`o` take `-es` |
| Three authoring typos | Braces around ordinary English words: `{the}`, `{can}`, `{defeated}` | Braces removed |
| `{themselves}` unknown to the enricher | 5 sites; the dead resolver knew it, `enrichProse` did not | Added, with a they/them reflexive fallback |

## The regression lock Law 43 asks for

`src/engine/__tests__/encounterWordPlaceholders.test.ts` sweeps every prose field of every template — openings, step narratives, and all six afterimage bands, including inside branch variants and fallbacks — and asserts **zero raw `{…}` of any shape** survives enrichment. 1,913 fields.

Widening it from "this token family" to "any token" is what caught the last four rows of that table; the narrow version passed while `{the}` was still on screen.

It was falsified, not assumed. Reverting the one call in `proseEnrichment.ts` fails it with **1,545 leaking fields**. An earlier, narrower version of the sweep reported 810 — the gap is the afterimages, which is where the ticket's own evidence came from. A companion assertion pins that the corpus still *uses* the tokens (>50 templates), so the sweep can never pass by going vacuous.

## The named template got written, not mad-libbed

Substitution made `trial_by_combat` grammatical. It did not make it good — *"A duel is seasoned demanded"* is what mad-lib authoring produces once the tokens resolve, and no resolver can fix an adjective sitting in an adverb slot. The template the ticket named was therefore authored out of the token shape entirely, in Threadbare register:

> The charge is read twice — once in law, once in plain words — so nobody can claim they misheard it. {actor} is offered the old remedy: answer it in court, or answer it with a blade.

> The accuser is patient and {actor} is not. A shoulder gives, then a knee, and the sand takes the rest. {actor} is still alive when it stops, which the room reads as its own verdict.

## Deliberately not done

**The other 152 templates still read as mad-libs** — grammatical now, and mediocre. *"Syntar's resolute skill surges the noble seasoned down."* That is 1,900-odd prose fields of authoring work, a content campaign rather than a ticket, and it is filed as THR-1101. This ship closes the *defect* (Law 43) and raises the floor; it does not claim the corpus is well written.

**The tone tier is wired but unfed.** `encounterToneTierForThreat()` maps `threatRating` → `early`/`mid`/`late`, and `NarrativeContext.encounterToneTier` accepts it, but no caller populates it: `threatRating` does not survive `toUnifiedTemplate`'s field allowlist, so `chapterArchive` cannot see it. Adding it means touching `UnifiedActionTemplate` (278 importers) — out of scope here. Every encounter currently draws from the neutral `mid` tier, which is grammatical everywhere. Filed as THR-1102.
