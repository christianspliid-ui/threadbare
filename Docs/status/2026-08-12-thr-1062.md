# THR-1062 — slot 2 of Meeting Batch A converts

**Shipped 2026-08-12.** The 24 `reach_specific` slot-2 templates are formative
tests, and every reach clears `MEETING_POOL_MIN_CONVERTED`. Batch A is complete
at 64 conversions (40 slot-1 axiological from THR-875, 24 slot-2 here).

## What was blocking it

`meetingNudgeStage.test.ts` asserted `test.valuePair === template.targetValuePair`
unconditionally. `FormativeTest.valuePair` is a required `ValuePair`;
`DilemmaTemplate.targetValuePair` is optional and **all 40** `reach_specific`
templates leave it undefined — they key on `targetReach` alone. So the first
slot-2 conversion would have had to satisfy `valuePair === undefined`, which the
type forbids. The contract test failed on template one, by construction.

Christian's director verdict (2026-08-09, recorded on the ticket): **option 2,
"loosen the rule"**. The assertion now binds only when the template declares a
target pair.

## Why the relaxation is narrow rather than a hole

All 45 `axiological` templates declare `targetValuePair`, so the guard against a
mis-keyed axiological conversion covers every template it ever covered — 40
converted, 0 mismatches, verified by iterating the real library rather than read
off a doc. No `reach_specific` template declares one, which is exactly the case
the relaxation exists for.

That freedom is the point. `RC-IRON-02` is the weapons master offering blade or
shield — decisive ending against enduring protection — which is
`courage_prudence`, not the `mercy_ruthlessness` iron maps to. Stamping the
reach's pair on it would make the declared axis a lie about the fiction, and the
`poleLean` cards would argue an axis the scene never raises. **18 of the 24 sit
on a pair other than their own reach's**; 6 on their reach's own.

## What the relaxation costs

There is now no cross-check on a `reach_specific` test's declared axis — nothing
catches an `RC-*` whose `valuePair` is simply wrong.
`axiologicalPolarity.test.ts` does not cover these (it filters to
`category === 'axiological'`), so the pole binding rests on authoring discipline:
pole `a` is the first-named pole, `bandProse.cleanA` narrates the choice pole `a`
argues for, and each conversion carries a comment recording which choice is which
pole. The trade was accepted at director level and the cost is written into the
library header so the next author inherits it rather than rediscovering it.

Extending the polarity contract to converted `reach_specific` templates — which
would restore the machine check — is filed as
[THR-1091](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1091/converted-reach-specific-templates-have-no-polarity-guard).

## Coverage

New test `every reach has ≥N converted slot-2 candidates` asserts the predicate
per reach rather than a snapshot count, and is non-vacuous: dropping one iron
conversion takes it to 2 and fails.

```
iron    3/5  [courage_prudence ×3]
gold    3/5  [honesty_cunning, revelation_discretion, courage_prudence]
shadow  3/5  [honesty_cunning ×2, courage_prudence]
veil    3/5  [tradition_novelty ×2, honesty_cunning]
heart   3/5  [sacrifice_survival, tradition_novelty, preservation_transformation]
eye     3/5  [courage_prudence, preservation_transformation, revelation_discretion]
stone   3/5  [preservation_transformation, sacrifice_survival, tradition_novelty]
star    3/5  [revelation_discretion, tradition_novelty, courage_prudence]
```

Iron's three all landed on `courage_prudence`. That is honest rather than lazy —
all three iron scenarios (blade/shield, charge/withdraw, duel pace) are about how
danger is met, not about mercy — but it does mean an iron slot-2 draw always
writes the same axis. Noted rather than fixed: forcing variety by assigning a
scenario an axis its fiction does not raise is precisely the failure this ticket
existed to prevent.
