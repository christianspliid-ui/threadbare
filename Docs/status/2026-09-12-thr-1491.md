# THR-1491 — content cards, the codex as the content sheet, and an anchor status nothing can lie about

*Slice 2 of three for [THR-1482](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1482). Blocked by slice 1 ([THR-1490](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1490), landed) and by [THR-1485](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1485) (the content-object registry, landed).*

Slice 1 made every **world object** open the same way. This slice does the same for every
piece of **authored content** — a template, a definition, a rule someone wrote — and then
turns the authoring catalog's routing claim from a hand-written sentence into a derivation
the build checks.

## Two vocabularies, one dispatch

`src/types/contentRef.ts` is the sibling of `worldRef.ts`: import-free for the same reason
(the anchor generator reads it as *text*), with `ContentObjectKindId` re-declared as a
literal union and pinned to the registry by test. `AnyRef = WorldRef | ContentRef`, and
`isContentRef` discriminates on kind membership alone — which is only sound because the two
unions are disjoint, so that is asserted rather than assumed.

The router takes `AnyRef` now. `SURFACE_BY_CONTENT_KIND` joins `SURFACE_BY_WORLD_REF` in
the surface registry, total over its own vocabulary, and the arms stay strictly separate:
**a world reference can never reach the codex and a content reference can never reach a
world sheet.** That is [THR-1315](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1315)'s ruling
kept rather than worked around — `codex` did not come back to `WorldRefKind`, and
`openCodexEntry` is a *separate* config arm precisely so that giving `NavigationTarget` a
codex arm cannot happen by the side door. A test pins both directions.

## The split was measured, and the plan's guess was short by two

The plan named four content kinds as lacking a codex category. Probing every catalog id in
`CONTENT_OBJECT_KINDS` against `getAllCodexEntries()` found **six**:

| kind | ids | in codex | category |
|---|---|---|---|
| `action_template` | 239 | 239 | divine · hex · location · artifact · company · threads · actions |
| `item_template` | 134 | 119 | possessions |
| `undertaking_template` | 116 | 60 | undertakings |
| `condition_template` | 46 | 40 | conditions |
| `power_template` | 25 | 12 | conditions |
| `agreement_template` | 7 | 7 | agreements |
| `encounter_template` | 557 | **0** | — |
| `omen_template` | 44 | **0** | — |
| `nudge_card` | 37 | **0** | — |
| `ambition_template` | 20 | **0** | — |
| `companion_template` | 9 | **0** | — |
| `legendary_template` | 3 | **0** | — |

The two the plan missed are the interesting ones. **`legendary_template`** is the surprise:
a `possessions` category exists and simply never reads `ARTIFACT_TEMPLATES`, so three
legendary artifacts — each with its own trait graph and activated effects — are catalogued
nowhere a player can read them. **`nudge_card`** is the deck the player plays from, with no
reference surface at all. All six are [THR-1495](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1495),
filed with its coordination block as its first comment and with a note telling its executor
to **re-measure rather than trust that table** (THR-688 rule A — the predicate is "a kind
whose catalog ids resolve to zero codex entries", the counts rot).

**The CTA asks two questions, not one.** Four of the six covered kinds are covered only
*partially* — an undertaking template has a codex card only where its grid cell is live —
so the registry row says whether the **kind** reaches the codex and `codexHasEntry` says
whether **this entry** does. Trusting the row alone would draw "open in codex ↗" over
nothing for 56 undertaking templates, which is the Law 25 failure the registry exists to
remove. That two-condition gate is falsified in the render evidence: collapsing it to the
row alone fails by name.

## The resolver builds on the catalogs rather than beside them

The first draft of `contentEntryResolver.ts` statically imported all 32 catalogs and did its
own prefix narrowing — and `src/data/contentCatalogs.ts` (THR-1485/1486) already did exactly
that, plus `effectiveTags`, which unions authored tags with the ones *projected* from typed
fields. Rewritten on top of it, the file shrank to the part that genuinely does not exist
anywhere: the **prose** fields.

Those need twelve adapters because, measured, the catalogs share no spelling for them. An
item is a graph-node literal whose text lives in `properties`; an undertaking carries
`displayName` and `activityProse`; a companion has **no name field at all** (`profession` is
the word for the role) and arrives with a `joinSentence`; a nudge card has a `title`; an
omen a `tagline`. `id` is the only field all twelve share, and `contentCatalogs.ts` says so
in as many words.

Building on `effectiveTags` rather than reading `tags` is load-bearing, not tidiness: an
action's reach is a *typed field* and becomes `#iron` only by projection, so reading the raw
field would have silently halved every action card's marks. Falsified — swapping it out
fails the sweep.

**Every sweep runs over the shipped corpus, never a fixture.** The whole risk here is an
adapter reading a field the catalog has not got, and a fixture invents both sides of that:
it would render beautifully while every real card showed its id. So the test asserts *no
entry of any kind falls back to its id for a name* — falsified by pointing the companion
adapter at `name`, which named nine companions immediately.

## A content page is not a graph page, and the type now says so

`generateDetailPage` starts by asking the graph for a node and gives up when there is none —
right for a thing in the world, wrong for a template that may never have been granted to
anyone. Rather than thread a "sometimes there is no node" branch through it,
`DetailPageKind` gained `'content'` and a new `GraphPageKind = Exclude<DetailPageKind,
'content'>` keys `DETAIL_PAGE_REGISTRY`, `KIND_LABELS`, `NodeRef` and the generator itself.
The alternative — a `content: []` schema row — compiles and means "this page kind has no
sections", which is false and reads like a bug. *The graph generator cannot be asked for
this* is the honest shape.

The page shape is shared: same `DetailPage`, same `Section` union, same `DetailModal`. Three
sections — what it is, its voice, its marks — and the voice section is **omitted** rather
than filled when the entry has none, because a heading over a fallback apology is worse than
no heading. The marks section renders even when empty, because *a template nothing can
query* is information rather than absence. Fallback selection hashes the id, so no PRNG is
drawn and two opens never disagree (NFP #3).

Tag chips carry `tag.*` tooltips ([THR-1486](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1486))
— a tag is a *concept*, so it explains itself and opens nothing, which is the line Law 20
draws between a tooltip and a card. An **unseated** spelling still renders (hiding it would
hide the drift) but carries no tooltip, since the resolver has nothing to say about a word
outside the vocabulary and a hover onto an empty box is worse than no hover.

The footer CTA reads **"open in codex ↗"**, not "open her sheet ↗" — different rooms, and
one label for both sends the player looking for a page that is not there.

## The anchor catalog's routing claim is now derived

`linked` versus `named` is a claim about **routing**, and it was hand-written, which means
nothing could check it. It went stale exactly as you would expect: the `location` row said
*"no `visualKind` member exists"* for months after THR-1172 gave it one, and a package pass
shipped two location anchors at different tiers in one batch because of it.

Since slice 1 the code does say. `deriveAnchorStatus` reads `SURFACE_BY_WORLD_REF` through a
curated `WORLD_REF_BY_ANCHOR` map (curated rather than regexed off the row's prose — a wrong
entry there is worse than a missing one, because it promises a click the router cannot
make), and **a kind with a surface row is `linked`, full stop**. Three curated rows
contradicted the registry and were corrected: `companion` ×2 and `holding`. A new **Opens**
column renders what the registry says each anchor opens (`` `place` card → `location` sheet``,
`` `actor` card only``).

The override and the guard are deliberately **two** things. `resolveRowSet` keeps the
*output* honest; `assertCuratedStatusAgrees` keeps the *input* honest by failing the
generator by name — because a silent override would quietly fix the printed table and leave
the source wrong for its next reader. Falsified: curating `NodeType.companion` back to
`named` fails with `'NodeType.companion' is curated 'named' but the surface registry routes
kind 'companion' — derived 'linked'`.

The `action_template` and `encounter_template` rows said *"a definition, not an object the
player can be pointed at"*, and half of that stopped being true today. Both now state the
distinction that actually matters: a **chip** reports what happened to someone and takes a
`WorldRef`; a codex cross-link or a batch-report link names the rule itself and takes a
`ContentRef`. Pointing an aftermath chip at a template would say "this happened to a
definition", which is still not a sentence — so the chip rule is unchanged, and the
definition is reachable.

## One scope item delivered differently, and why

Scope item 7 asked for two interface-map rows. `content-ref-opens-codex-overlay` is there.
**`anchor-status-derived-from-surface-registry` is deliberately not**, and the reason is
recorded at the site in `interface-contracts.ts`: its consumer is
`scripts/generate-anchor-catalog.ts`, and the map's `collectSources` walks `src/` only. A
row whose read site the scanner structurally cannot see classifies LEAKED on every run,
which would mean carrying a permanent deferral ticket for a defect that does not exist — the
"green tests on a dead contract" pathology this map exists to kill, inverted. The contract is
guarded where it lives instead, by a required CI gate, and falsified above. That is a
stronger gate than a map row, not a weaker one.

The plan also asked for the `systems-prompt.md` edit to *replace* `linked`/`named` guidance
there; that file had none — the guidance lives in `package-prompt.md`, which is where the
trap paragraph was rewritten. `systems-prompt.md` gained the rule as a new numbered audit
section instead, since it is the stage holding the declared ids.
