# THR-1155 slice 3, part 3 — the words (and the lever nobody had pulled)

**Date:** 2026-09-11 · **Pillars:** Engine · Content · **Closes THR-1155.**

Slice 3's last part owed the bullets that turn a shipped system into one the next session can find: the registry row, the canon pages, the wiki section, the glossary entry, the interface-map narrative — and the browser Done-when.

## The words

**Realm is seated.** Christian ruled the headword in attended chat this morning (THR-1453), so the glossary entry is written rather than parked: a Faction whose class is *realm* — the landed polity of a culture, seated at a capital, holding towns through `controls`, with the court ladder *stranger · subject · yeoman · sworn · thane · counsel*. Four words retire from player-facing surfaces with it — *kingdom*, *barony*, *domain*, *province* — the first two surviving only as flavour inside a Realm's **name**, never as a second object. The entry says **never *holdings*** in as many words: that noun is the Freehold's and belongs to `owns`, where a Realm's territory is `controls`.

**The two-partition rule is now written down in five places rather than implied in one.** The Area is geography and does not change hands; the Realm is politics and does; they may cross freely and neither derives from the other. It reads on the world-objects catalogue, the rulebook's *What the World Is*, the quick-reference card, the agents canon page and the wiki — because it is the sentence that stops the next design from trying to make one the parent of the other.

**A registry row it deliberately did not get.** A Realm is a **class of Faction**, not a kind, so it takes no `K({...})` of its own: the Faction row's note carries it. `classes` on that row would have been the obvious move and is wrong — the contract test pins every class member to a *claimed discriminator value*, and the discriminator is `actorType`, where `factionClass` is a different property. The note says so, so the next person does not rediscover it against a red test.

## The browser Done-when, and the defect it found

`preview_start` is refused outright in an unattended lane, which shuts the Playwright route with it (impediments #546 ×10, #574). Recorded as `Browser-verify substitution: jsdom-render`. But the bullet's claim — *drive a conquest through `window.__DEBUG.conquerLocation` and the border visibly moves* — is a claim, not a picture, and it is asserted here on the same lever over a generated seed-42 medium world, through the real `createBorderMesh`. Vertex data is what the capture would have photographed.

That turned out to be worth more than a substitution. **`conquerLocation` had no test at all.** `battleAftermath.conquest.test.ts` proves the engine path it wraps and `BorderMesh.test.ts` proves the geometry moves on a *fixture* pair of projections, but nothing tied the two ends together, and nothing exercised the bridge's own ref resolution, army proxy or cleanup. It is the lever a reviewer reaches for and the one the Done-when names — the worst possible thing to discover broken at review time.

**It was broken, quietly.** The stand-in army the lever mints to stand in for a victorious host carried `actorType: 'army'` — a value **no world-object kind claims** — and a `member_of` edge missing all three properties its schema requires. The conquest still worked, because `applyConquestOrVacuum` reads only the `member_of` edge; what it did instead was shout four schema warnings into the console **every time the lever was used**, which is to say into the console of the browser session the lever exists to produce evidence for, where console cleanliness is itself part of that evidence. The proxy is now shaped the way `armySpawning` shapes a real one (`actorType: 'group'` + `groupKind: 'army'`), and an arm asserts the silence so it cannot drift back.

**Falsified, not watched.** Two perturbations, complementary signatures, neither reddening the whole file:

- Revert the proxy shape → **1 of 5** red, the schema-silence arm alone. The other four stay green, which *is the finding*: the conquest works with a malformed proxy, which is exactly why nobody caught it.
- Neuter the conquest → **1 of 5** red, the border arm alone. The non-vacuity guard stays green in both, which is what makes the border arm mean something rather than passing over an empty world.

The suite also carries a claim that is falsifiable here and nowhere else as cheaply: a conquest moves politics and leaves **every Area stamp untouched**. A conquest that dragged geography with it would be the single worst way to get the two-partition rule wrong, because the map would still look plausible.

The chronicle and faction-sheet halves of the bullet were already discharged in slice 2 by the same sanctioned route — `HeldByLine.test.tsx` (9 arms) and `FactionSheet.realm.test.tsx` (6), rendering the real components on a generated world, plus the 33-of-33 agreement arm that holds over every settlement at once instead of the one a screenshot shows.
