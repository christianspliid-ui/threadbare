# THR-1451 — the rest of the percentage sweep: four classes, three of them conversions

**2026-09-10 · UI · Game Design · project Encounter Experience**

THR-1424 settled *progress proportions* and, while running its membership sweep, found the sweep was
wider than one family and filed the residue here. The framing that ticket left is the one that
mattered: **what is left is not one more batch of the same thing.** Sweeping "drop it" uniformly
across these four classes would have deleted readings the design system explicitly says to *convert*.

## What each class turned out to need

**Class A — deltas are a conversion.** `+7%` on a sphere row is a *realised state change*, whose
sanctioned language Law 15's 2026-08-12 rescope already names: the delta cluster. THR-1424 left a
pointer in `MandateDetail` saying exactly this and deliberately did not act on it; this discharges
that pointer. `sphereDeltaReading` bands a delta through the existing `GROWTH_MAGNITUDE_BANDS` ladder
and lives **beside** that ladder rather than in either component (UI Law 3, following THR-1426's
`sustainFlowSpheres`). The design call inside the class: a **required** delta draws the same way as an
**observed** one, because the checkpoint's whole question is "did I clear it?", and two runs of
triangles side by side is a comparison the eye makes without reading. Recorded in `laws.md` rather
than left implicit.

**Class B — probabilities are a conversion too, but the bar for entry is THR-977's, not "is it
0–1 shaped".** Two of the four filed candidates failed that bar on inspection and were
**reclassified rather than converted**, which is the part the ticket could not have known from a grep:

- `EncounterLog`'s **leverage** already renders a `ProgressBar` on the same value directly below the
  numeral. That is THR-1424's duplicate-rendering case, not the "no sanctioned reading" case pips
  answer — and a pip row beside a bar would put two magnitude languages on one quantity, Law 10 in
  reverse. Dropped.
- `EncounterVeil`'s two percent helpers are reachable **only** from `ResolutionReadoutBlock`, which
  hard-returns `null` without the designer view (THR-1124). Law 13 puts numbers on "the designer view
  only" — so this was never in membership at all, and the ticket's line numbers pointed at a block
  that had already been gated.

The two genuine odds-space sites converted: `InterventionConfirm`'s detection risk and `EntityCard`'s
trigger firing chance.

**Class C — the most common finding was not "this number is undrawable" but "a sibling component
already drew it and kept the ladder private".** `HexDetailView` had banded elevation/temperature/
moisture to words months ago and left the table as a file-local const, so `InfoPanel` and `HexTooltip`
went on printing `73%` of the same quantity. `hostilityLabel` was a closure inside `RivalPanel`'s
component body while `RivalsButton`, unable to reach it, spoke `highest hostility: 73%` instead. Both
ladders now live in `src/data/`. That is the shape UI Law 3 exists to prevent, and it is recorded in
`laws.md` as the sweep's headline lesson: **before ruling a numeral undrawable, check whether a
sibling already drew it.** The genuine drops — where nothing else rendered the quantity and no ladder
existed — were `FactionSheet`'s ambition priority (a sort key wearing a readout) and `AgendaPicker`'s
boost size. `OmenDetail`'s encounter bias kept its **direction** as a word and lost its magnitude:
direction is not magnitude, and which way an omen leans is the part the player acts on.

**Class D — ruled out of membership in writing**, so it stops resurfacing: `SettingsModal`'s volume
slider is an OS-style control reading back its own setting, chrome the player brought with them
rather than one of the game's magnitudes. Left in code with a comment pointing at the ruling.

## The accessibility ruling this forced

`RivalsButton` made a distinction worth recording. An **`aria-label` is player-facing prose** — a
percentage in one is a percentage on a surface, and this one was worse than an ordinary leak because
*no sighted player is shown that number*: it ran the visibility-parity clause backwards, handing one
player a magnitude deliberately withheld from everyone else. An **`aria-valuenow` on `role="meter"` is
not** — it is the bar's own machine value, the accessible form of the rendering the sighted player
gets, and stripping it would leave the meter with no value at all. The test is whether a person hears
a number. Both halves are in `laws.md`.

green — the THR-1426 discipline. All five went red on cue and back green on restore. The
`createPortal` trap is now on its third recurrence, so the modal arms assert on `baseElement` and each
