# THR-733 subject 4 — the betrayal, told from inside it

**Shipped 2026-08-18.** The fourth and last subject of the company-drama sweep:
**The Quiet Offer** (`encounter.company.quiet_offer`). A company spread out over its
own work is a company nobody is within earshot of, and somebody has been waiting for
that. What is asked for first is small — where the company is going, whose coin it is
on — and the smallness is the whole difficulty, because there is no version of this
where the first ask is bad enough to make refusing easy. One **Shadow** step
("Settle the offer"), a five-card hand spanning mind/light/time/darkness plus one
common option, five authored outcome bands. Verdict **clean**, systems
`cast, rewards, seeds, conditions, reputation` — five against a quota of three. The
corpus moved `checked 194 · clean 9` → **`checked 195 · clean 10 · failing 0`**.

**The judgement call both prior checkpoints deferred is now decided, on evidence.**
They flagged the betrayal as the likeliest of the four to need a threshold-fired pool
rather than a drawn template. It does not — but the reason it does not is the reverse
of the one expected, and it is what shaped the scene.

**The subject is a *member*, and the aftermath vocabulary cannot name one.** The scene
sentinels are exactly `$actor`, `$target` and `$cast:<key>`; there is nothing for "a
different member of the actor's company". The three shipped subjects never needed it —
the sacrifice's volunteer and the dispute's decider are both `$actor`, and the romance
explicitly settled its pair offscreen. Betrayal cannot dodge it: the betrayer *is* the
subject, and anchoring them to a materialized cast NPC would make them an outsider and
turn `actorAffinities: ['group']` back into decoration — the exact trap subject 3
recorded. So the scene is told **from inside the betrayal**: `{actor}` is the one being
asked, and every consequence anchors to the actor by default. The `critical_failure`
mark takes no `targetAgentId` at all, which makes it the only mark in the file that
lands on the member rather than on a witness. A test pins that `undefined` as
load-bearing, because a later author "helpfully" redirecting it at the buyer would
invert the scene — selling the company would then leave no trace on whoever sold it.

**The ending this subject was described as leading to does not exist.** The ticket
frames the slow burn as "the version that *leads* to" the dissolution-by-betrayal
ending. `DissolutionReason` declares `'betrayal'` and `selectPartingVariant` consumes
it — and `groupDissolution.ts` assigns exactly `undersize`, `leader_death`,
`cohesion_floor` and `goal_complete`. Nothing produces it; `dissolveGroup` has one
caller. A green test at `groupParting.test.ts:72` sits on the dead branch by passing
the literal in itself, which is what stopped anyone questioning it. So **no band here
promises it**: the burn runs on marks, bonds and seeds, all real, and a company that
comes apart over this comes apart by cohesion like any other. Filed as **THR-1174**
(Engine, Medium) with its coordination block rather than papered over in prose or
absorbed into a content-only sweep.

**The history is minted, not presumed.** The file's register rule forbids asserting
agent history, and an offer already half-accepted has no question in it anyway. So the
first payment *happens* in the worst band rather than being backstory — which also
means the scene had to survive losing its intended condition.

**One defect the gate caught, and the enumeration error under it.** The taken-offer
band originally attached `trait.condition.debt`. No such node exists: the real id is
`trait.condition.debt-laden`, and the `grep -o "trait\.condition\.[a-z_]*"` used to
enumerate the vocabulary stopped at the hyphen and produced a *well-formed, entirely
fictional* id that looked exactly like its neighbours (impediment **#672** — a
truncating character class is generative, not merely lossy). The real id is no help
either: `debt-laden` is written by `phaseEconomicTraits` every tick it applies and is
absent from `ATTACHMENT_TEMPLATE_SOURCES`, so it can anchor no chip and carry no
tooltip (impediment **#673**, logged not filed — one occurrence, below the materiality
bar). The band now anchors to `$actor` — the person its `hidden_mark` actually lands
on — and the obligation lives in the overview, the resolution this file already uses
for grief and for a job quietly reassigned.

**Both new assertions were falsified, not merely watched.** Redirecting the mark to
`$cast:the_buyer` turns exactly one test red by name (`expected '$cast:the_buyer' to
be undefined`); giving the offer a `ruin` setting **with an opening supplied** turns
the widened disjointness case red with `quiet_offer must not be drawn at the ruin`.
The opening was supplied deliberately — subject 2 recorded that omitting it trips the
opening-per-class guard instead, proving a different rule. Both reverted to green.
`arcane + battlefield` were the last two unused classes, so the four subjects now
partition their settings exactly.
