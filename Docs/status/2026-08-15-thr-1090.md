### THR-1090 — three lanes scanned the queue daily and every check was about form (2026-08-15)

Christian's 2026-08-11 queue review found 9 of 36 Ready-for-Dev tickets below the materiality bar and 7 more that belonged in one batch — a third of the queue — sitting in a board that `tb-orchestrator`, `keep-work-flowing-cc` and `daily-backlog-grooming` all scan every day. His question, verbatim: *"why is our orchestrator routine or our keep work flowing routine not sanity checking the backlog already?"* The answer is that none of them was ever asked to. The orchestrator promotes on the `Blocked by` line, keep-work-flowing briefs, and grooming checks hygiene — orphans, missing coordination blocks, stale claims. Every one of those is a question about **form**. Nothing owned **worth**.

**Placement follows the diagnosis.** The sweep lands as a new **§ 2.5**, immediately after the hygiene step and before the roadmap cross-reference — the seam the ticket identifies, where form-judgment ends and nothing began. The 2026-08-08 materiality bar and the 2026-08-10 filing throttle were both forward-looking; they stopped new sub-bar filings and left the already-promoted shelf untouched, because the weekly retro batches the *impediment log*, not the queue.

**Form checks are Goodhart-satisfiable, and the evidence is in the cancellations themselves.** THR-1057 and THR-1060 both carried textbook `costs ~X; not fixing costs ~Y` lines and were canceled anyway. The same class of agent writes the tickets and grooms them, so the presence of a cost/benefit line proves nothing about the size of the number in it. The step is therefore written to judge **magnitude**, with that sentence stated in the prompt rather than left implicit — a groomer that reads "check the cost/benefit line" will check that it exists.

**The one real judgment call: two rules, one population, two different terminal states.** The prompt already carried a *Rule-0 minting bar* section instructing grooming to "demote any open process ticket lacking that line to **Idea**". The new sweep cancels. Those target overlapping tickets and prescribe different states, and a lane handed both with no precedence picks one arbitrarily — which is how a rule becomes noise. So the two are now explicitly framed as halves of one rule with a stated tie-break: the minting bar asks whether the line is *present* (form, at filing), § 2.5 asks whether its number is *real and large enough* (magnitude, retroactively), and where they collide § 2.5's cancel wins for an in-scope ticket failing on magnitude, with demotion-to-Idea reserved for the narrower case of a line simply missing.

**The report template had to change or the Done-when could not be checked.** The ticket asks that "the next grooming run's report shows the sweep executed (including *0 canceled* as a valid result)" — a step with nowhere to report is unfalsifiable, and this lane's report is capped at 35 lines, so a section that is not in the template does not get written. Frontmatter gains `swept:` and `canceled:`; the body gains a `## Materiality sweep` section whose own text states that `swept N, 0 canceled` is a complete entry. The finding being recorded is that the judgment *ran*.

**The scope guard is the part worth being careful about.** This is a deletion tool pointed at the work queue, and the standing failure mode of this project is process work crowding out product — so a sweep able to cancel product tickets would be the same disease with a new vector. Product work (Content/UI/Engine tickets describing player-visible behavior) is explicitly out of scope for cancellation, and the prompt says why in one line: an empty product shelf is a supply problem to surface, never a backlog to prune harder. `When in doubt the ticket stays and the doubt goes in the report` is carried through verbatim from the ticket, for the same reason — a queue nobody trusts is the failure this step exists to prevent.
