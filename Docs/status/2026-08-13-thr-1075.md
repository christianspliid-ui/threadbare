# THR-1075 — three artifact verbs stop telling the player they are "NOT YET WIRED"

**Shipped 2026-08-13.** `ACTION_TECHNICAL_EFFECTS` carried `INTENDED: … NOT YET WIRED — empty step ops, no engine bridge; deducts essence and narrates only (THR-605)` for `artifact.attune`, `artifact.nullify` and `artifact.curse`. All three had been wired by THR-605 months earlier. The entries were the exact inverse of the `artifact.enchant` defect THR-996 had just fixed, where the string promised a bridge that did not exist — a description surface wrong in both directions.

## The predicate found twice what the ticket named

The ticket named three entries and asked for a sweep on the predicate rather than the list, which was the right instruction: **six** entries carried an unwired disclaimer while `effectSourceFor()` classified their template as wired.

| Entry | Classified | Wired by |
|---|---|---|
| `artifact.attune` | `template-ops` | `attune_artifact` (graphOpExecutor) |
| `artifact.nullify` | `template-ops` | `nullify_artifact` |
| `artifact.curse` | `template-ops` | `curse_artifact` |
| `loc.fortify` | `template-ops` | `fortify_location` |
| `sub.trap` | `template-ops` | `plant_trap` (via `unifiedActionResolution`, the second sanctioned path) |
| `sub.vision` | `template-ops` | `scry_sublocation` |

The three the ticket did not name — `loc.fortify`, `sub.trap`, `sub.vision` — are the return on writing the guard before the fix. Equally, `sub.sanctify` and `sub.sanctify_tavern` match the same grep and were **left alone**: they classify `none`, so their disclaimers are true and the authoring contract wants them exactly as they are. A list-driven pass would have rewritten those two into lies.

## The mechanism, which is the part worth keeping

The six were not merely stale — they were **unreachable**. `UNIFIED_ACTION_TEMPLATES` assembles with `t.technicalEffect != null || !authored ? t : { ...t, technicalEffect: authored }`, so a template that authors its own string never reads the map. All six templates carry their own correct, code-sourced `technicalEffect`, written when THR-605 wired them.

A probe over the whole map made the correlation exact: **171 entries, 6 shadowed, 165 live — and the 6 shadowed were precisely the 6 carrying the false claim.** Perfect overlap. Shadowing is not correlated with the rot; it is the mechanism of it. The author who wires a verb writes the true string next to the ops they just added, the duplicate in the map goes invisible, and nothing corrects it because nothing renders it.

So the fix is **deletion, not rewriting**. Rewriting the six would have restored two copies of one code-sourced fact, in the arrangement that had already failed once, guaranteed to drift again. Deletion is provably render-neutral — the string reached no consumer — and restores the map's actual invariant: an entry in the map *is* that template's `technicalEffect`.

## Guard

`src/data/__tests__/action-technical-effects-wiring.test.ts`, two invariants, both predicate-based:

1. **No entry claims `NOT YET WIRED` for a template `effectSourceFor` calls wired.** This is the ticket's Done-when. Falsified by restoring any one of the six: 1/1 red, naming the entry and its classification.
2. **No entry is shadowed by a template that authors its own `technicalEffect`.** This is the mechanism guard, and it is the stronger of the two — it catches the next stale duplicate whatever its wording, including one that never uses the disclaimer phrasing at all. Test 1 alone would have missed the defect if THR-605's author had left behind a merely-inaccurate string instead of an explicitly-wrong one.

The authoring contract at the head of `action-technical-effects.ts` now carries the rule in prose, with the THR-605 history as its motivating example, so the next author is told rather than left to rediscover.
