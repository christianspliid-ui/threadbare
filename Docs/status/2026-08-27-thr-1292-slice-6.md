# THR-1292 slice 6/6 — the cutover that did not happen, and why that is the answer

**Status:** closed. `Fixes THR-1292`.

Slice 6 was to flip `UNIFIED_DECISION_BOARD_MODE` to `'live'` behind the §4 gate, then delete
control upkeep behind the §6 gate. **Neither landed**, and the plan's own clause is what that
satisfies: *"if a gate fails, the slice waits and says so — shipping the gate-fail evidence is a
valid completion of those items."*

What slice 6 actually produced is the **diagnosis**, which overturns slice 5's read of the same
number, and three tickets that carry the remaining work.

## The gate, re-measured

Both seeds reproduced slice 5 byte for byte (1913 / 1809 decisions, 11.9% / 4.1%), so this is the
same world, not a drifted one.

| | seed 42 | seed 99 | criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| undertaking share | 11.9% ✅ | **4.1%** ❌ | `[0.10, 0.35]` |
| encounter share | 70.7% ✅ | 82.5% ✅ | ≥ 0.15 |
| idle share | 17.5% ✅ | 13.4% ✅ | ≤ 0.40 |

## Slice 5 nominated the wrong lever, and the sweep says so

Slice 5's conclusion was that an undertaking is *genuinely* worth 3.3–3.8× less per tick, so the
lever is `UNDERTAKING_PAYOFF_SCALE`. It deliberately did not turn it. That restraint was right;
the diagnosis was not.

Re-ranking every decision with strategic scores scaled by k — score is linear in the constant, so
this is exact within the traced top-5:

| k | seed 42 | seed 99 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12.1% | 4.0% |
| 2 | 12.6% | 5.4% |
| 5 | 13.6% | 8.3% |
| 8 | 14.3% | **10.5%** |
| 12 | 15.7% | 12.7% |

Seed 99 needs **k ≥ 8**, and the curve saturates: a *twelvefold* scale moves seed 42 only 3.6
points. A constant documented as a verb-impact→payoff units bridge cannot honestly absorb an 8×
correction. That is not a bridge; it is a missing term wearing one.

## The actual cause: undertakings are ranked with no personality signal at all

`boardScore = EVT × desireMultiplier × temperamentWeight`. Measured over 150 ticks, the
undertaking `desireMultiplier` is **`0.354` at p25, p50 and p75 — on both seeds**. It is a hard
constant, and both of its inputs are pinned independently:

- `computeDesireScore(template.motivations, profile)` is `0`, because **`motivations` is unauthored
  on all 43 strategic templates** — the seam slice 5 declared for doc 2 and pinned as an identity.
- `getAmbitionBoostForEntry` returns its flat boost whenever the agent pursues the template's
  primary reach — and a strategic candidate only *exists* because an active ambition generated it
  from its own `templateIds`. True by construction. This is the **same vacuity slice 5 caught in
  the temperament bracket (#829), one term over**, and it survived that slice because the fix was
  applied to the bracket rather than to the class.

So an undertaking's score is `EVT × 0.495`: a pure rescale of EVT. Encounters meanwhile carry a
desire multiplier spanning **0.011–1.863**. The cross-family comparison is therefore decided by the
*encounter* desire distribution — which is exactly what differs between seeds. The tell was in the
legacy numbers the whole time: legacy gives a **stable** project share of 13.1% / 12.3% across the
two seeds, while the board gives 11.9% / 4.1%. **The board's instability was the symptom, not its
level**, and no scale constant can fix a variance.

## Why the payoff scale was not turned anyway

Setting `k = 8` would have made both seeds pass. It would also have shipped a board that cannot
tell one undertaking from another by personality, with an 8× fudge frozen into a constant whose
doc comment claims it is a units conversion — and the gate, having been made to agree, would have
reported the system healthy for as long as anyone trusted it. Slice 5 named this failure mode
precisely; the only correction here is about *which* constant it would have applied to.

## What shipped

Nothing that changes behaviour. Two things that stop the gap from hiding:

- **`decisionBoardLiveness.test.ts` — the frozen-desire guard now covers the frozen half.** The
  existing "the desire multiplier is not frozen" assertion pooled both families into one array, so
  the encounter side's 170× spread satisfied `distinct.size > 1` while the undertaking side sat at
  a constant. A guard over a mixed population only ever measures its loudest member. Split by
  family; the undertaking side is now pinned to `size === 1` as a **documented identity with a
  population floor**, so it fails the moment doc 2 authors the first `motivations` row. Falsified:
  authoring one row turns exactly that test red.
- **`decisionBoardModeGuard.ts` — `'live'` no longer silently means shadow.** The mode union
  declares `'live'` and nothing implements it, so setting the constant would have scored the board
  and let legacy decide anyway, with the trace stamping `mode: 'live'` on every row and the suite
  staying green: a cutover that reads as shipped from every surface while changing nothing. It now
  warns once per process. It is scaffolding for a gap and **THR-1301's Done-when requires deleting
  it** along with the gap.

## What remains, and who has it

- **THR-1301** — the cutover. Blocked by THR-1297 (doc 2) on the data precondition, not a file
  collision.
- **THR-1302** — the ambition boost's construction-vacuity. Authoring `motivations` unpins one of
  the two desire inputs; this is the other.
- **THR-1303** — the §6 control deletion. Its gate is *defined* over a post-cutover run, so this
  slice could not evaluate it at all: **unevaluable, not failed.** Blocked by THR-1301.
- **THR-1287** stays open — it is superseded when the deletion lands, not when it is scheduled.
