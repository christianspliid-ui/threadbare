# 2026-09-08 — THR-1303: the control family deletes, but not the machine underneath it

The §6 gate of the undertaking-substrate plan finally became evaluable, passed, and the
deletion it guarded shipped — smaller than the plan wrote it, because the world moved
underneath the plan between authoring and pickup.

## The gate

`Docs/plans/2026-08-26-thr-1292-undertaking-substrate.md` §6 defined the deletion over a
*post-cutover* decision mix, and the cutover had not landed when the plan closed. It has
since: THR-1349 slice 3 flipped `UNIFIED_DECISION_BOARD_MODE` to `'live'`, deleted
`decisionBoardModeGuard.ts`, and took the bridge and clamp with it. The named blocker
(THR-1301) still sits in `Todo` with no closer of its own, but its *reason* is
verifiably inapplicable — which is a technical verdict, not a judgment call.

THR-1277 method, seeds 42 + 99, medium map, at 150 ticks (the gate's floor) and 300:

| run | undertaking share (excl. control) | idle share |
| -- | -- | -- |
| 150t seed 42 | 32.4% | 0.0% |
| 150t seed 99 | 20.3% | 0.0% |
| 300t seed 42 | 31.4% | 0.0% |
| 300t seed 99 | 23.8% | 0.4% |

Floor 0.12, ceiling 0.40, both seeds. Undertaking share is read *excluding* the control
family on purpose: a deletion must not be credited with the supply the deleted thing
provides. Including it only raises the numbers, so the criterion passes either way.

Near-zero idle would pass the ceiling trivially, so the obvious failure mode was
falsified rather than banked — the emitter is reachable by construction (live mode with
no board winner nulls `decision.selected`, and the idle branch is that `else`), and it
demonstrably fires: seed 99 at 300 ticks records idle at 0.4%.

## What the plan did not know

Plan §6 listed the whole control apparatus for deletion, on the premise that control was
a vestigial family with no successor. Between 2026-08-26 and pickup, the undertaking grid
made `control:claim` one of its six verbs — it sits in the division rule tables
(dominion, discovery, legacy), the codex, and the verb difficulty/payoff/duration tables,
and `src/data/undertaking-objects.ts` routes it to `mode: 'claim_control'` with the
comment *"the sustained `claim_control` mode — upkeep, degradation, collapse."*

That is an explicit, recent dependency on the state machine §6 wanted deleted. Executing
the DELETE list verbatim would have broken a shipped system.

So the deletion split along the line the world actually draws:

**Deleted — provably dead.** The six control templates; `computeControlPressure` and the
`controlPressure` score component and its weight; `evaluateControlClaimGate` and
`STRATEGIC_CONTROL_RECLAIM_COOLDOWN_TICKS`; the `control_obligation` generation reason.
Every one of these gates on `template.verb === 'control'`, and after the six templates
went, no template in `src/data` satisfies that predicate.

**Kept — load-bearing.** `StrategicControlState`, the neglect/degradation loop,
`retireControl`, the `claim_control` execution mode, and the `'strategic_control'`
balance decisionType. The grid mints stances through exactly this loop.

## The measurement that decided it

Re-running the census after deleting the six templates returned results **identical to
the digit** — 1347 and 1263 decisions, `strategic_control` at 58 and 18, unchanged. The
bundle was verified fresh (built after the edit, deleted ids absent, a retained id
present), so this is a real reading: the six templates were dead content, and every
control decision in the world already came from the grid.

That is the finding worth keeping. The plan's premise — that deleting these templates
would remove the control family from play — was already false when it was written.

## Follow-up filed

THR-1286's churn protection (the reclaim cooldown and already-held gate) only ever
covered `verb === 'control'` templates, so it never protected the grid's `control:claim`
producer, and it goes out with them. Whether the live producer needs equivalent
protection is a real question this deletion surfaces but does not answer — filed as
THR-1442 rather than folded in. Today's live control volume (4.3% / 1.4%) is an order of
magnitude below the 39.5% churn THR-1286 was built against, so it is prevention, not
relief, and sorts by priority.
