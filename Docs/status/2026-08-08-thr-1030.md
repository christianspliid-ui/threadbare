**Any authored ending of an encounter is now reachable from a URL, and the lever says when it did not get you there.** The director's ask was *"I would like to test all the different aftermaths"*; there was no lever for it. `?spawn=` stages an encounter and `?testavatar` balances the actor, but the band is rolled by fate at resolution — and on a balanced avatar the tails are the *rare* bands by design, so the endings that most need review were the hardest to reach. `?outcome=<band>` pins the resolved outcome of the `?spawn=`ed template; the two shapes the ticket offered were weighed and **(1) force the band** taken over **(2) enumerate the variants**, for the reason the ticket gave: the failure this must catch is *"this authored band is unreachable"*, and only a real resolution can prove reachability — a preview renders the authored model and reports a band as fine whether or not anything can produce it. So the pin sits at the **tail** of `resolveUncontestedStep`, after the roll, the two probability floors, resist and the nudge riders have all run and traced; the roll and probability stay honest and only the band is substituted, so `step.onSuccess`/`onFailure`, step effects, prose band, aftermath band and capability growth all fire off it exactly as a rolled band would. Zero rng draws, so a pinned run and an unpinned run consume the same stream. Scoped to one `templateId` on purpose — a global pin would force every agent's every step and make the reviewed ending unrepresentative of one a player could reach — and it also reaches the two auto-success early returns, or a reviewer asking for a failure on a divine action would be handed a silent success. **The anti-vacuity half is the deliverable, not a nicety.** A pinned band nobody authored would render the *base* ending while the URL claimed a band, laundering the exact defect THR-989 and THR-973 exist to find; so every pinned resolution records a verdict at aftermath assembly — `band_rendered` only when the band is authored *and* the action landed on it, `unauthored_band` when it did not exist, `outcome_diverged` when the action aggregated the pinned steps elsewhere (a pinned `near_miss` always does, having no `UnifiedActionOutcome` counterpart; a pinned `failure` does unless `failBehavior` is `fail_action`), each warning on the console and readable via `__DEBUG.getOutcomePinVerdict()`. **Falsified before trusted, in the browser as well as in tests:** the same `encounter.slice.unsafe_bridge` rendered two genuinely different endings on demand (`critical_failure` → *"The beam went at the worst step of the worst stride"* + a WOUND chip; `critical_success` → *"They read the boards the way the keeper's sons have never dared to"* + a STANDING chip), and asking for `success` — a band that template does **not** author — correctly returned `unauthored_band` with the base ending on screen rather than passing it off. The corpus test earned its place too, catching a shipped template carrying an `aftermathConfig` with no `variants` map, which threw the reviewer's read-only query before the fail-soft guard went in.
