# THR-1298 — the reactive loop reaches the player (slice 7 of 7)

**2026-09-02 · closing slice.** Slices 1–6 built a harm into a drive, gave it heat, a lifecycle and three ways to end. None of it was visible: a player opening the sheet of a mortal nursing a vendetta saw an ordinary ambition called "Seek Revenge" with no culprit, no provenance and no indication the world had put it there. Slice 7 is the surfacing, and the closing PR carries `Fixes THR-1298`.

## What a player sees now

A minted drive says where it came from, in the sentence rather than beside it:

```
Seek Revenge   burning · against Oswen, after the razing of Wilderness (13, 6) — Oswen's work
Rebuild from Ashes   Because of the razing of Thornhall
Conquer Territory
```

Heat is a word — `burning · hot · cooling`, banded in `grievance-prose.ts` against the tuning constants — and the numeral never leaves the engine. The culprit is a link, so a vendetta is one click from the person it names. An ordinary self-chosen want renders no provenance line at all, which is what makes the line mean something.

The Bonds tab gained a **Blood** section for standing agent-to-agent grudges: *"There is blood between them and Oswen — an old wrong that never quite closed."* Deliberately separate from Relationships — a soured bond can sweeten, and a grudge is a fact about what happened. It renders only when blood exists, so the section's presence is itself the signal.

Also shipped: `window.__DEBUG.getGrievances(agentRef?)` (world sweep or one agent; a selector matching nobody returns empty rather than widening back to the world), a grievance/grudge block on the CLI's `agent <name>`, the rulebook's new §10.7 and its quick-reference block, and two interface-map contracts.

## The finding the observation run turned up

The plan's Done-when asked for a 300-tick run on seeds 42 and 99 as a tuning baseline. It reported **zero grievances minted on both seeds** — and its kill criterion says to surface that rather than absorb it into constant-twiddling.

The mechanism is not at fault. 73 and 83 `undertaking_outcome` nodes were written, well-formed, 13 of them carrying a real culprit. Of those 13, taken at the moment of the harm: **6 victims were factions** (which hold no `pursues` edges, so the ambition phase never reaches them) and **7 were individuals already carrying two active ambitions**, blocked by the `currentActiveCount < 2` mint gate — after which the harm ages out of the 25-tick lookback and is never offered again. World-wide, free slots are common (`{0: 452, 1: 1, 2: 158}`); they simply never coincide with being harmed.

The structural shape: the one-slot/replacement rule governs grievance-vs-grievance. **Nothing lets a grievance in past two ordinary wants** — a vendetta cannot displace a want, only another vendetta, which is the opposite of the urgency the design gives it once it is on the board. Filed as [THR-1383](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1383) with the measurement and three candidate directions; recorded honestly as a `[DESIGN]` gap in the rulebook rather than left implied.

Sweeping all 14 free-slot autonomous agents, `property_destroyed` minted one vengeance and one rebuild; `named_death` minted two vendettas. That is the temperament funnel working as designed — a harm offers a *set*, and character picks — not a defect.

## Two defects found while wiring

**A faction is an `actor`.** The first draft of `getAgentGrudges` filtered on `node.type === 'actor'` to keep guilds out of a list of *people*. Factions, cultures and companies are all `type: 'actor'` distinguished only by `actorType`, so that filter admitted every one of them. Caught by the typechecker rejecting `type: 'faction'` in the test fixture that was supposed to prove the exclusion — the fixture and the reader were wrong in the same direction, which is exactly the shape that passes review. Now an explicit exclusion of the collective kinds, stated as an exclusion rather than an `=== 'individual'` allowlist so an actor with a missing or new `actorType` still renders their name.

**A vacuous negative arm.** The "an ordinary want has no grievance block" test passed on its first run — against a fixture whose template lookup returned `undefined`, so no intent was produced at all and every assertion on it held vacuously. The revenge templates live in `GRIEVANCE_AMBITION_TEMPLATES`, not `AMBITION_TEMPLATES`; the fixture now resolves through `findAmbitionTemplateById` and non-null-asserts, so a retired template fails at import rather than downstream as "the reader produced nothing".
