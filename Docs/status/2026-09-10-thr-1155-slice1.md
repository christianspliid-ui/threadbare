# THR-1155 slice 1 — one geography

**2026-09-10 · Engine · Content · UI · [THR-1155](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1155/nations-and-named-areas-are-rendered-not-simulated-promote-both-to) slice 1 of 3**

Plan doc: [`Docs/plans/2026-09-10-thr-1155-realms-and-areas.md`](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/main/Docs/plans/2026-09-10-thr-1155-realms-and-areas.md).
`Fixes THR-1155` deliberately does **not** ride this PR — it rides slice 3.

## What was wrong

The world kept one geography and the map drew another.

`worldSeed` minted its `region` nodes from a flood-fill detector. `hexGrid` ran a *second* detector — a border-cost watershed — for the renderer. `gameInit` then reconciled the two by copying graph names onto renderer clusters through `region_${geo.id}`: an index join between two different partitions of the same land. The name a player read over a mountain range belonged to whichever unrelated cluster happened to share its position in a list.

Both partitions had holes, in different places. Measured on `main` across seeds 42/99/7 at all four map sizes: the flood-fill left **22–198** land hexes in no region, the watershed **0–24**.

## What it is now

One detector, one partition, one set of nodes; every surface reads a projection of them.

- **The mint moved to the watershed.** `seedWorld` takes the clusters `generateWorld` already detected, so the graph's Areas and the map's geography are the same partition by construction rather than by a join. The flood-fill `detectRegions` is **deleted**, not deprecated — a spare detector nobody calls is how the two grew apart.
- **The stamp is total.** `detectRegionsBorderCost` gained a nearest-cluster fill: land the watershed could not reach (an island with no province capital on it) joins the cluster holding the nearest hex, ties broken on the lower cluster id, orphans visited in coordinate order. Deterministic (NFP #3). The `area_coverage` trace reports what is left, and it must be zero.
- **`areaProjection` is the map's read**, owned by `SimulationRuntime` in the `ensureEncounterCache` pattern — not a React state, not a module singleton. `GeoBorderMesh` and the geographic label tier read it; `getHexRegionData` stays as the Area's *point* reader for the chronicle. Two reads, one source, no third model.
- **`effectScope('region')` is real.** It was a radius-4 disc around the caster: it crossed every border, stopped inside large Areas, and gave the same answer on a hex the player is told belongs to the Iron Crags as on one two ranges away. It resolves Area membership now — and honours the `regionId` the scope has always declared and the disc never read. Given no projection it resolves to **nothing**, loudly, rather than falling back to a plausible wrong answer.
- **`area` is a reference kind.** It joins `WorldRefKind`, `visualKind`, `NavigationTarget` and the three mirrored chip unions, and routes to the Area's centre hex — the chronicle is the Area's surface. It carries no entity-visual tile (there is no portrait of a mountain range), the `attachment` treatment, recorded with its reason in the anchor catalog. The catalog's *Named area* row moved **`named` → `linked`** and its tripwire now asserts the flipped row.

## Two defects found on the way, fixed rather than filed

**The watershed listed the same hex in two clusters.** The split pass never pruned `hexSet`, so a later chunk's BFS walked back over hexes an earlier chunk had claimed. `remaining` was filtered; `hexSet` — the set the BFS actually consults — was not. Measured on `main`: **147** hexes in two clusters on a seed-42 medium world, **1735** in two or three on an epic one. It inflated every split region's `hexes.length`, and with it the label-size gate and the Area's `hexCount`. Surfaced because the new partition-identity assertion is *exactly one*, not *at least one* — a coverage-only check cannot see it.

**`edgeBorderCost` read `geoParams.elevation` unguarded.** Harmless while the watershed only ever saw generated tiles; reachable the moment it became the one detector, because hand-built fixture tiles across the corpus omit the field. Three test files crashed. A missing elevation now reads as no steep barrier (NFP #4), and `seedWorld`'s fallback detector run is wrapped the way `hexGrid`'s already was.

A third, in the layer: `GeoBorderMesh` set its position attribute only when it had segments, then called `computeLineDistances()` unconditionally — which reads `position.count` and throws. A world with a single Area draws no geographic border and must still render.

## What slice 1 leaves for slice 2

The plan's slice-1 Done-when says *"the setter-less `regionData` state is gone"*. It is **half** gone: the geographic half of `RegionData` no longer reaches the map, but `regionData` still carries the political tiers, because `BorderMesh`, `CapitalMarkers` and the domain/province labels have no other source until `realmProjection` replaces it in slice 2. Removing the state now would ship a `main` with no political borders for as long as slice 2 takes. Each slice merges on its own PR, so each must leave `main` shippable — that bullet completes across slices 1+2 rather than inside slice 1, and this is the record of the deviation.
