## The finding this batch adds

**The family had two failure shapes, not one, and the second is the one that produced the
ungrammatical readings.** Seven templates were full mad-libs — every prose field assembled
from tokens, the shape batches 1 and 2 met. The other nine were *already authored prose*
with tokens wedged into finished sentences, which is where the corpus's worst readings
came from: `patch_the_walls` promised the wall would "stand another generation at
`{adj}` least", and `dig_a_well` offered "Good `{adj}` mud." Those nine wanted surgical
removal, not rewriting — the sentences were fine and the token was a parasite on them.

Worth carrying forward: a family's token count does not tell you how much authoring it
owes. Grep the *lines*, then read a sample before sizing the slice.

## Two defects caught by rendering, not by reading

Both were invisible in source and obvious once run through `enrichProse`:

1. **`{They}` resolves to a singular pronoun**, so a bare verb after it reads *"She proceed
   with a compromise"* (`fortification_engineering`). Mine, introduced this batch, rewritten
   to drop the pronoun.
2. **Two pre-existing agreement bugs** in `mend_equipment`'s critical-band afterimages —
   *"everything she mend after"*, *"longer than she carry the broken gear"*. `{s}` is the
   engine's agreement token and fixes the first; the second needed a rephrase, since `{s}`
   cannot do `carry → carries`.
