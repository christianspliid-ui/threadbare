# THR-1091 — the 24 reach trials nothing was checking

**Shipped 2026-08-18.** `src/engine/__tests__/axiologicalPolarity.test.ts` now covers the converted `reach_specific` templates. It previously filtered to `category === 'axiological'`, so the 24 `RC-*` slot-2 conversions sat outside the guard **by construction** — THR-1062's own header said so in as many words: *"the pole binding is carried by authoring discipline alone."*

## Why the axiological contract could not just be widened

Two of the five assertions in the existing block have nothing to bite on for a reach trial, and neither absence is a gap to be papered over:

- **The pair binding.** A reach trial names its own axis (THR-1062 director verdict). `RC-IRON-02` is blade against shield — `courage_prudence`, emphatically not iron's `mercy_ruthlessness`. 18 of the 24 sit on a pair other than their reach's, so there is no `targetValuePair` to bind `test.valuePair` to.
- **The legacy path.** These carry `axiologicalShifts: {}` and move `reachChanges` instead. The authored-shift half of the THR-1071 defect pair *cannot occur* here — and equally cannot be checked.

What is left is the formative path alone, which is precisely the half that shipped inverted on all five stone templates. So the new block asserts both absences explicitly: a template that grows a target pair or authored shifts fails this suite rather than slipping past a guard written when it had neither.

## The open question the ticket asked to answer first

`narratedChoiceIndex` separates clean bands by content-word overlap against `choices[].text`. The reach set's choice texts are the **legacy** prose, which the THR-1062 band prose deliberately does not re-narrate word for word — so the axiological measurement (min 35.2% relative margin) did not transfer, and the ticket rightly refused to assume it.

Measured over the 24 before writing a line of guard:

| | axiological (baseline) | reach_specific |
|---|---|---|
| bands | 80 | 48 |
| min relative margin | 35.2% | **14.3%** |
| p10 | 53.4% | 53.1% |
| median | 81.7% | 77.3% |
| under 35% | 0 | 2 |
| ties | 0 | 0 |
| `cleanA`/`cleanB` pick the same choice | none | **none** |

It separates, less comfortably. The floor is `RC-STONE-03` cleanB at 14.3% and `RC-STAR-02` cleanA at 16.7%. But the property the guard actually rests on holds outright: on all 24, `cleanA` and `cleanB` pick **opposite** choices and no band ties, so the reading is decisive everywhere it is used. Recorded in the heuristic's doc comment rather than smoothed over — a rewrite of those two templates' clean bands is the realistic way this guard goes false.

## The table is a hand reading, not the heuristic's output

`RC_FIRST_POLE_CHOICE_INDEX` was read off each template's recorded ``Pole `a` = …`` comment and the choice text it names — the same way the axiological table was built, and for the same reason: deriving it from `narratedChoiceIndex` would make the guard assert against its own output. The two were compared **after** recording and agree 24/24. The disagreement that comparison could have surfaced is the whole reason to record by hand first.

## Falsified, both halves

A green guard that survives inverted data is worth nothing, so both new assertions were run against a deliberate defect and reverted:

- Swapping `RC-IRON-02`'s `cleanA`/`cleanB` — the exact inverted-binding shape — produces one named failure: `RC-IRON-02: cleanA: expected 1 to be +0`.
- Blanking `cleanA`'s vocabulary so it matches neither choice **slips past the pole assertion** (margin 0 → the `>=` tiebreak returns 0 → matches `firstIdx`) and is caught only by the margin assertion: `cleanA does not distinguish the two choices (margin 0.0%)`.

That second result is the one worth keeping: a tie reads as a confident answer, which is why the strict-separation assertion exists alongside the pole check rather than instead of it.

## Not covered, and still not

There is still no cross-check on whether an `RC-*`'s declared `valuePair` is the *right* axis for its scene — nothing catches a reach trial whose axis is simply wrong. That is a reading of fiction with no second surface to check it against, and this pass does not claim it. What is now closed is the binding: given the axis, which pole each choice argues for, and whether the pole-`a` band narrates it.
