# THR-1297 slice 2/6 — a destroy verb needs a reason

*2026-08-27 · Proactive Agent Actions, plan doc 2 of 6 · [plan](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/main/Docs/plans/2026-08-27-thr-1297-action-library-works-holdings-naming.md)*

## What shipped

**The motive gate.** The corpus held exactly one `verb: 'destroy'` template in 43 —
`strategic_raid_supply_lines` — and it was offerable against any town, city, camp or fort in
reach with no quarrel behind it at all. A warlord could starve a settlement they had never heard
of, while the completion prose said *"the enemy will feel the lack"* about people who were not
their enemy. A destroy template may now declare a **motive gate**, and candidate generation
refuses the verb unless the actor holds one of the named motives toward whoever holds the target.

The four motives read relations the world was already writing, so nothing new is recorded to make
this work — the world's existing quarrels simply became load-bearing:

| Motive | Read from |
|---|---|
| `rivalry` | a standing `hostile_to` edge, whatever wrote it |
| `grudge` | a `hostile_to` stamped with a specific past injury — a band engagement, a broken mentorship |
| `contested_ambition` | actor and holder both `pursues` the same ambition node, both `active` |
| `faction_war` | the two factions declared rivals (`relates_to.isRival`), via the existing `areFactionsHostile` |

**The kind registry** (`src/data/undertaking-kinds.ts`) and its no-destroy-no-kind gate. A kind row
is the CRUD closure for one kind of work, and it must name at least one destroy template, every
named id must resolve, and every destroy must carry a motive gate — counter-play closure by
construction rather than by audit.

## The registry ships empty, and that is the gate working

No kind's counter-play exists yet: the one destroy verb in the corpus is a raid, not any kind's
undoing. Registering rows anyway would mean pointing `destroyTemplateIds` at create verbs or at
ids nobody implements — the exact vacuous satisfaction the plan names as a kill criterion. So the
rows land with their destroy verbs in slice 5, and until a kind can be undone it is not a kind.

Which leaves the validator needing to be proven some other way. It takes its template resolver as
a **parameter** rather than importing the pack registry, so the schema test drives it with
adversarial fixtures — one per problem code, each differing from a known-good row in exactly one
respect. A validator that could only run against an empty registry could never be shown to reject
anything, which is how a gate becomes a comment.

## Refusals stopped being invisible

The candidate board reported `candidatesRejected` as a bare **count**. Every generation gate —
this one, `no_valid_targets`, `no_eligible_apprentice` — was therefore invisible from a trace
dump, which is a poor place for a rule whose own fail-soft row reads *"never a silent skip"*. The
board trace gained a capped additive `refusals` list; the count stays exact. No new trace
category, per the plan.

Two refusal reasons, kept distinct because they want different fixes: `no_motive` (the target is
held and the actor has no quarrel with its holder) and `no_motive_unowned` (nobody holds it, so
the destruction is aimed at no one).

## What a player should expect to see change

Raiding becomes rarer and *earned* rather than ambient. Measured on seed 42 / medium at tick 60:
all 21 raidable settlements carry a controlling faction — so the `unowned` arm is not the common
case — against 30 `hostile_to` edges and 12 declared faction rivalries across 49 factions. The
verb stays reachable and grows more so as grudges accumulate, but a warlord must now have somebody
to raid.
