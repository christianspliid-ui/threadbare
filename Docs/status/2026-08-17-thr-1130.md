# THR-1130 — the batch-1 re-pass: chips that name what changed

**2026-08-17** · [THR-1130](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1130) · Encounter Experience

The director played The Grateful Kin on 2026-08-17 and filed three findings against
batch 1. This run is the re-pass they asked for. All three turned out to be the same
defect wearing three costumes: **a chip that renders, passes every gate, and tells the
player nothing they can act on.**

## What he saw, and what was actually wrong

> *"the bond… the roof they are owed: again this simply doesn't communicate what game
> state has changed."*

The write was real and well-formed the whole time — `favor_creation` mints an
`owes_favor` edge with **debtor = target, creditor = actor**. Every *word* of the chip
was fiction: `BOND · the roof they are owed`, titled "A Roof, Remembered", detailing
beds and hearings. The word *favour* and the debtor appeared nowhere. A player reading
it could not tell an edge now existed, or who was on the other end of it.

Two fixes, and the second one is why this needed an engine change:

1. **State first.** The `stateNoun` names the mechanic (`a favour owed`); the `detail`
   names the endpoints (`{target} owes them a favour now — …`); the fiction moved to the
   tail, decorating a claim the player has already read.
2. **Anchor the debtor.** The chip is a sentence *about the target*, and the chip-side
   anchor vocabulary had no `$target` — only `$actor`, `$cast:`, `$faction:`. So the
   shipped content anchored `$actor`, which routes the click to **the traveler reading
   the chip** rather than the person who owes them. The effect side has had the sentinel
   since THR-695; the asymmetry was the whole bug. Added `ANCHOR_SENTINEL_TARGET` to
   both halves of the rule (classify + resolve) and passed `activeAction.targetId`
   through the adapter.

Not a design gap after all, which is where the ticket expected it to go: the runtime
already had the id: it just never reached the resolver.

## The one he saw, and the fourteen he could not

Finding (b) named `slice.kin.the_room_carries_it` as an authored `reputation_tally`
chip — a Law 13 visibility-parity violation, because per-Reach tallies render **only**
in `TalliesDebugTab`, the designer view. Confirmed at the source; `TalliesDebugTab`'s own
header says it: *"Nothing here is a route back to a player-facing tally chip: that is the
thing §5 removed."*

Grepping the kind found **fifteen** of them across the vertical slice. He caught one,
because a person reads the encounter in front of them. The rule shipped as prose in
`laws.md` on 2026-08-16 and **nothing enforced it**, so fifteen chips reading green
through every gate sat in the corpus — including on both encounters handed to him to
sample. So the fix is the predicate, not the instance: all fifteen retired, plus
`chipVisibilityParityViolations` wired into `checkCompositionContract` so the sixteenth
cannot ship.

The tally **effects** are untouched, deliberately. They keep steering scoring and gating
and keep minting the Whispered/Known/Legendary traits — and a minted trait *is*
sheet-visible and reports normally. Banning the effect would be a different and much
larger rule than the one that was given.

**One caveat that cost a judgment call per chip:** fold the words only if they were true.
Several tally chips also asserted a debt — *"the family owes them the road south"*,
*"a caravan master owes them a debt"* — that no effect on that band wrote. Folding those
into the overview would relocate a false state claim (Law 56 clause 1) instead of
retiring it. Those were deleted; the rest were folded, the file's own THR-1153 precedent.

## Density — the rule that was not vagueness

> *"the writing is in situ with specific people mentioning other specific people. this is
> a problem because there is not enough room to write so many details into the story and
> keep it gamey and understandable."*

The Grateful Kin's opening carried an innkeeper, her brother, his family, three children,
a nine-day walk, a letter and a pair of boots — in sixty words, to set up *someone is
thanking you in public*. Every sentence was concrete, which is exactly what the three
THR-974 plainness moves ask for. The failure is **too many concrete things at once**, and
none of the existing moves names it. Added as plainness move 4 in
`nudge-authoring-spec.md`, with the rule of thumb: one named person on stage, props only
where the player can act on them, judged at game speed.

## Scope note

The tally sweep covers the whole slice file (all eight templates), not only the batch-1
six. Four of the fifteen sit in `snow_on_the_pass` and `riders_behind_caravan`, which are
still on the retrofit ratchet — but they are the same defect in the same file, and
leaving known Law violations in a file being rewritten is not defensible. The gate's
*blocking* scope is unchanged: the ratchet still holds the 185 un-retrofitted templates.
