# THR-866 — the apex encounter keeps its fork, the player stops making it, and the mortal decides on the axis the Star reach already binds

**Shipped 2026-08-11.** `encounter.apotheosis.ascension` is the last template in the game still using `authoredChoices` — the "pick between two authored futures" screen the Nudge Model rejected outright (THR-772). It is also the capstone of the Influence ladder (THR-479), which is why the five mechanical WS5 batch cells refused to absorb it and five consecutive orchestrator runs held it rather than filing it as a same-shape sub-batch. This ticket was the design gate, not the conversion; its output is a committed decision plus the implementation ticket that now carries it, **THR-1086**.

## The decision

**The fork survives. The player stops making it. The mortal makes it, and the god leans.**

`ActionStepBranch.decidedBy` (THR-894) is the shipped general-purpose replacement for choice-keyed branching — resolver at `src/engine/encounters/branchDecision.ts`, three live users in `vertical-slice.ts` — and its contract is the exact sentence this encounter needed: *"The player never picks. They lean; the mortal chooses; the choice is theirs to keep."* The engine reads the mortal's live standing on a declared axis plus the net `poleLean` of the cards the god committed, and records the winner through the existing choice-history path.

**The axis was not a judgment call — it was already in the data.** Star's bound value pair is `sacrifice_survival` (`REACH_VALUE_PAIR.star`): Martyr vs Survivor. The template is `reach: 'star'` and already declares `motivations: ['sacrifice_survival', …]`. *Do I let myself be poured into and unmade as an ordinary person, or do I remain wholly my own?* is that axis stated in full, so the two existing branch variants map onto `positive`/`negative` without either one changing meaning.

## The conversion pays for itself with a branch the design always wanted

Both steps currently sit at `difficulty: 0`, and the file says why — *"the choice is the point, not a roll."* Once the choice is not the player's, that justification is spent and the roll is free to carry what the button was carrying. **It should carry the branch the original director verdict named and the first implementation could not afford.** From the template's own header, lines 18–22:

> the director verdict named accept / refuse / **"unmade"** branches. This first implementation ships accept (grant) + refuse (no grant); the "unmade" fail-forward (a mortal frame that cannot hold the divinity) is rendered as narrative weight in the accept prose and tracked as a content follow-up rather than a mechanical branch, so the grant path stays deterministic.

THR-969's outcome-keyed aftermath (`AftermathVariant.byOutcome`) is what makes it affordable now: each pole's ending splits by the band actually rolled — the aspect made, or **unmade** with a lasting mark; the doorway closed gently, or the withdrawal *felt*. So the conversion is not a dilution accepted to buy program compliance. It restores a designed branch and pays for it with the mechanism the program brought.

## What the decision deliberately refuses

**Failure does not flip the pole.** `MEETING_POLE_SHIFT_BY_BAND` writes the *opposite* pole on a failed formative test, and that is right there — a soul value is soft and re-writable. `aspect_of` is explicitly permanent and irreversible, so here the band controls **magnitude and cost, not direction**: a Survivor decision never becomes an aspect because the roll went badly. Direction stays with the decision; only the quality of the ending is fate's.

**The grant stops being a button.** `apotheosis_consummate` currently *is* the grant — a reaction no player would ever decline. Post-conversion it belongs to the Martyr pole's upper-band aftermath, and whatever reaction survives is a blessing, not the switch.

**Nothing shared is edited.** Every field the conversion needs — `StepNudge.poleLean`, `ActionStepBranch.decidedBy`, `AftermathVariant.byOutcome` — is already shipped, so `src/types/unifiedAction.ts` (278 importers) stays untouched. That also resolves THR-868's conditional mutex against this work as **not met**.

**No apex-specific UI.** The ticket asked this to be flagged if the chosen approach needed one; it does not, and that is a property of the decision rather than an omission — an apex encounter with its own component would fork `NudgePhaseShell`, the failure mode THR-868 named.
