# Current focus — the ownership of people-things (THR-1438)

**2026-09-08 · shipped.** The third band of the undertakings wayfinder map ([THR-1396](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1396)), in the order set on THR-1399: readers → dormant kinds → **people-things** → yield. Seven cells that change **who commands a group of people** — the first thing a mortal in this world could never do anything about.

## What shipped

**One command writer.** `setCommander` (`src/engine/groups/groupCommand.ts`) is now where a standing command changes hands: it removes the old `commanded_by`, writes one carrying `via` (`formation | promotion | claim | mutiny | coup`), and makes the `member_of` roles agree. The dissolution sweep's `promoteNewLeader` calls it with `'promotion'`; the three spawn-time writers keep their ids, because they say who a group was *raised* under rather than who took it. That sweep was the defect the band existed to fix — it repointed the edge silently when a commander died, so no company ever read as unowned and `claim × Company` could not have fired at all.

**Two hooks on the object type**, the shape of THR-1436's `gateExemption`. `ownershipOverride` replaces a variant's default ownership rule at cell synthesis (one cell needs it — the candidacy). `eligibility` states a precondition about the *world* rather than about who holds what; it is consulted after the ownership rule and before the motive gate, refused on the board as `ineligible:<reason>:<target>`, and fails closed on a throw.

**The seven cells.** Claim × Company (a member, or whoever stands where it stands) and Claim × Army (the army's own faction, nobody already commanding a host) share one op. **Seize × Company is a mutiny** — motive-gated *and* refused `ineligible:cohesion_holds` unless the cohesion system already reads the band as *frayed* or *breaking*. **Seize × Army is a coup**, decided by the faction and the band. **Claim × Faction is a candidacy, not a coronation** — it writes the same `will_succeed` edge an anointment writes and the succession phase stays the one arbiter. **Seize × Faction is a usurpation**, running the phase's own seating early. **Observe × Army is scouting.** Nobody dies in any of them; `markMortalDead` is still the only writer of a death.

**`seatLeader` extracted** from `phaseFactionSuccession` and called by it, so there is one seating rather than two kept in step. It must move `leaderSnapshotId` — otherwise the phase reads a usurpation as an exit and re-seats over the top of it, which is the kill criterion the extraction exists to make impossible.

**The succession queue is ranked now.** The god's anointment (10) outranks a notable's heir (5) outranks a mortal's candidacy (1–2) outranks the derived ladder. Both anointment writers stamp a priority, because `pickSuccessor` reads unset as `-Infinity` and any positive candidacy would otherwise have outranked every divine anointment ever written.

## Three things the plan had wrong, found by measurement

1. **`promoted: true` had no readers.** The plan said its one reader would be updated; measured, it had one writer and zero readers, so the rename to `via` repointed nobody.
2. **The doubled standing loss was a silent no-op.** `USURPATION_STANDING_LOSS` was planned at 0.15 and `REPUTATION_WITH_MAX_DELTA_PER_OUTCOME` is exactly 0.15 — so doubling it on a `critical_failure` was capped straight back and the two failure arms were identical. The constant is **0.075**: a plain failure costs half of what one outcome may ever cost, a critical failure the whole of it. The test that caught it now asserts both absolute scores, not just their difference.
3. **A candidacy would have run the usurpation.** `resolveVerbVariant` re-derived the control split from ownership at completion. With the candidacy's ownership overridden to `any`, both faction control cells sit on the same ownership and the split is ambiguous — a candidacy would have come back as `control:seize` and silently run the usurpation the mortal never attempted. The resolver now honours the **proposed** variant when the type declares it and the ownership rule admits it, falling back to the ownership ladder otherwise.

## Two grey zones, decided at execution

- **The coup's `critical_failure` arm** doubles the standing loss rather than writing a `member_of` role of `'outcast'`. There is no role union: `role` is a free string, and an `'outcast'` value would meet ~49 `member_of` readers that know nothing about it — the property-bag failure the group layer already paid for once.
- **The mutiny's cohesion cost** goes through `applyCohesionDelta`, not `applyCohesionEvent`. The nearest event kind is `dissent`, which a Bless window suppresses and a Sunder window amplifies; a mutiny is neither, and it has its own tunable.
