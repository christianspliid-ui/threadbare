# THR-1140 — the designer view that §5 promised, built rather than pruned

**2026-08-17** · `tb-opus-pickup` hourly run · project *Encounter Experience*

## What the ticket held

THR-1136 §5 ruled per-Reach reputation tallies **system-visible**: they keep steering
scoring and gating and keep minting the Whispered/Known/Legendary traits, but they never
speak to the player. The `reputation_tally` chip and `reputationTallySentence` went with
that ruling. What survived was a promise — §5 preserved tally inspectability *"in traces
and the designer view"* — and two exported helpers with **no production caller**:
`describeTallyKey` and `TALLY_MAGNITUDE_BANDS`, the only vocabulary that can turn
`star.positive` into words.

The ticket offered two closes: build the designer surface, or prune both with their tests.

## The call, and why

**Built.** Pruning would have deleted the only key→words vocabulary a designer surface
could ever use, and THR-1161's grilling of the *bookkeeping* class — whether a
system-visible quantity owes the player or the designer any surface at all — is open and
unresolved, with reach-reputation tallies as its motivating example. Outcome 1 is
compatible with every answer that grilling can produce; outcome 2 forecloses a question
Christian has not been asked. The orchestrator's promotion comment flagged exactly this
interaction and left the scope call to pickup; this is that call.

## What shipped

A **Tallies** tab in the debug panel (`src/components/Game/debug/TalliesDebugTab.tsx`),
the designer view §5 named. Per actor carrying a live tally it renders the key resolved
through `describeTallyKey`, the raw value, which trait rung the tally currently mints, the
distance to the next rung, and — when the trace window holds two or more increments — the
movement, banded through `TALLY_MAGNITUDE_BANDS`. Both orphaned exports now have a
production caller, and the module header in `aftermathWords.ts` was rewritten to say so
rather than continuing to describe them as uncalled debt.

**Raw numbers are correct here and that is not a Law 13 exception.** Law 13 bans raw
magnitudes on *mortal-facing* surfaces and names traces and the designer view as where
numbers do live. The debug panel is the designer view. Law 14's key ban is likewise a
player-surface rule, so the tab shows the key *and* its words — a designer needs to know
which key they are reading. Nothing here is a route back to a player-facing tally chip;
Law 13's visibility-parity clause still bars one until a tally gains a sheet surface, and
the module header now says that in the place an author would look.

## The bug the fixtures could not have caught

The first cut read the actor's name from `node.properties.name`. Every jsdom test passed,
because the fixture put it there too — the fixture invented both sides of the contract.
A live CLI probe (seed 42, tick 60) showed `Object.keys(actor.properties)` carrying
`templateName` and `cultureIdentity` and **no** `name`: the name is a top-level
`GraphNode` field. Shipped as written, every row would have read "(unresolved)" against a
real world — a Law 4 broken-fallback, on the only surface the ticket exists to build.
The same probe earned the tab its population check: live worlds do accumulate tallies
(five actors carrying them by tick 60), so the surface is not a view onto an empty set.
Both facts are now regression-locked, the second by a fixture that carries the real shape.
