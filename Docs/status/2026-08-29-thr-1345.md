# THR-1345 — the reach bag that was carrying two different retired mistakes

**Shipped 2026-08-29.** Five `reachWeights` keys across two faction data files were not
`ReachDomain`s. They are gone, and the hole they came through is closed at two levels.

## The finding was two findings

The ticket framed `flesh` and `force` together as "a retired cosmology's names". Only half
of that is true, and the halves need opposite fixes:

* **`flesh` is a genuinely retired Reach.** It was the 9th, removed in TB-075 Phase 1 and
  migrated to Quintessence. `Docs/canon/cosmology.md` says so in as many words — *"Do not
  re-introduce Flesh as a Reach"* — and gives a content-migration table (healing → Gold,
  athletics → Iron, body modification → Stone, survival → Star). That table, not lexical
  similarity, decided every `flesh` remap here.
* **`force` is not retired at all.** It is a live *Creation Sphere*. Reaches and Spheres are
  orthogonal axes (load-bearing decision), so a Sphere name in a Reach-typed bag is an axis
  confusion, not a stale term. Force's Reach equivalent is Iron.

## Per-faction decisions

| Faction | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adventuring Guild | `flesh 0.4`, `star 0.1` | `star 0.4` | Canon: Flesh survival → Star. Star was implausibly low for a guild of explorers *because* Flesh was carrying that weight. |
| `monster_force` (Beast Pack) | `iron 0.9, force 0.7` | `iron 0.9, stone 0.7` | `force` restated the Iron lead on the wrong axis. The description's second clause is holding territory → Stone (endurance, preservation). |
| `monster_energy` (Storm Flock) | `force 0.8, star 0.6` | `iron 0.8, star 0.6` | Force → Iron; a storm flock strikes directly. Star already carried "riding the currents". |
| `monster_life` (Behemoth Herd) | `flesh 0.9, eye 0.4` | `iron 0.9, eye 0.4` | Canon: Flesh athletics → Iron. "Overwhelm through sheer biological mass" is direct confrontation. |
| `monster_entropy` (Plague Shamble) | `shadow 0.8, flesh 0.5` | `shadow 0.8, stone 0.5` | Canon: Flesh body → Stone. The shamble's competence is grinding persistence. |

`monster_force`'s second reach was **Star until a test refused it.** The sigil registry's
distinct-heraldry invariant caught that `iron/star` collided with `monster_energy`, making
two factions visually identical. That is precisely the failure the ticket predicted when it
warned `{ iron: 0.9, force: 0.7 }` "loses most of its meaning if `force` is simply dropped" —
an existing invariant, not a new one, is what stopped it.

## Why the typechecker did not stop this — both answers were true

The ticket offered two hypotheses (red baseline vs. widened annotation) and asked which.
It is **both, in different places**, which is why one guard was not enough:

* Excess-property checking *does* fire on these literals. `npx tsc -b` emits exactly five
  `TS2353`s — one per stale key. They sat unread in `typecheck-baseline.json` (THR-489):
  `faction-definitions.ts: 1`, `monster-faction-definitions.ts: 4`. The ratchet compares
  counts, so five recorded errors are five permitted errors, indefinitely.
* Elsewhere the same drift is invisible to `tsc` outright. `revelationEmitter.ts`'s
  `REACH_TO_VALUE_MAP` is `Record<string, string>` and carries a `flesh` key that no
  annotation will ever reject.

So the guard is two-layered: the baseline drops by five (the ratchet now fails any new
stale key on an annotated literal), **and** `assertValidReachWeights` sweeps the real
definition corpus at test time, which is the only layer that survives a widened annotation.

The sweep is guarded against the vacuous shapes this codebase keeps producing: it asserts
the corpus is populated before asserting anything about it, and it carries a negative
control for both retired names. Proven by falsification — reintroducing `force: 0.7`
turns the real-corpus sweep red with a readable message, not merely the fixture control.

## Verified on a generated world

Seed 42 / medium / tick 3: 19 non-empty settlement reach profiles, `keysSeen` exactly the
eight live Reaches, zero keys outside `REACH_DOMAINS`. Thornhaven now reads `star: 0.4`
where the ticket measured `flesh: 0.40`. Holds on seed 99 (24 profiles, same key set).

## Left undone, deliberately

`flesh` survives as a key in ~10 other production sites — `tierPromotion.ts`,
`revelationEmitter.ts`, `seedAttachments.ts`, `scry.ts`, `codexRegistry.ts`,
`aftermathWords.ts`, `proseEnrichment.ts`, and three content files. Some are deliberate
old→new mappings; some are unreachable residue. That is a wider class than this ticket's
`reachWeights` scope and is filed as THR-1359 rather than swept in here — including the
`Record&lt;string, …&gt;` widening, which is the half no guard added here can reach.
