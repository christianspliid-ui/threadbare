# 2026-08-29 — THR-1360: the foreshadowing pools were never measured, and it showed twice

The ticket named four clauses in `src/data/foreshadowing-content.ts` opening on `Something`, the evasive lexicon's outcome-hider. The count was low and the reason it was low is the ticket's own second question: **nothing measures these pools.** `foreshadowing-content.ts` is imported by exactly two composers and their two tests, and no `countVagueness` caller touches it — so the ticket's four were the sentence-initial capitalised ones a human eye catches, while `countTerms` matches `giu` (case-insensitive, word-boundary). The real set was **seven**: three more sat mid-sentence in the `star`, `intel` and `chain` pools.

All seven rewritten as judgment calls, not substitutions — canon's THR-899 lesson is that dodging a banned word mechanically is what "damaged the prose it was protecting". Two were pure cuts (`Something about {matter} sits wrong` → `{Matter} sits wrong`), three named the thing the hedge was hiding (`Something {subject} {v:take} for a sign` → `A sign, or what {subject} {v:take} for one`), two rephrased (`{Subject} {v:know} something about {matter} that few others {v:hold}` → `{Subject} {v:know} what few others {v:hold} about {matter}`). No slot grammar changed. The one `the matter` hit in the file is inside a doc comment, not a shipped clause.

**The second defect was the more serious one, and the same blind spot produced it.** Six clauses open on `{Matter}` — and neither composer bound a `Matter` key. `NOUN_SLOT` looks keys up case-sensitively and a miss fails soft to `''`, so those six rendered *headless*: `"hums at a pitch they answer to, and they follow it."` shipped to players with no subject. Four were pre-existing; two I had just written before checking the slot roster, which is how I found it. Proven by rendering, not by reading.

`composeReceipt.test.ts` could not have caught this. Its guard is `expect(text).not.toMatch(/[{}]/)` — a check for *leftover* braces — and the fail-soft path deletes the braces along with the slot. The assertion passes precisely because the slot vanished. Fixed additively (NFP #6): both composers now bind `Matter` as the capitalised matter phrase, the way they already bind `Subject` and `Object`; every matter phrase is lowercase by construction (`what stirs at Ashmarket`), so all six clauses now render as authored. The realizer's documented slot roster gained `{Matter}` and an explanation of why capitalised variants are separate keys rather than a transform.

**Coverage — the ticket's more valuable half.** New `foreshadowing/__tests__/foreshadowingSlotCoverage.test.ts` sweeps every exported pool via a generic flattener (so a new pool is covered without being added to a list): evasive vagueness at zero in `scene` class (natural indefinites stay legal — the `bond` pool's "Someone {subject} {v:trust}" is correct English), every named slot must be one the composers bind, and a live-render arm that drives `composeReceiptForeshadowing` across 14 motive kinds × 40 ticks asserting every sentence begins with a capital. Non-vacuous by construction: the corpus-size assertion fails if an export is renamed or emptied, and both detectors carry controlled arms. **Falsified before trusting it** — removing the `Matter` binding turns the live arm red with "headless sentences" while the five data-layer tests stay green, which is the correct separation: only the live arm can see a composer-side regression.
