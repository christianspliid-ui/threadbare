# THR-1349 — the decision-board cutover: what the throughput loss was made of

**2026-09-02 · engine.** The one prioritization board (THR-1292 §4) decides. `UNIFIED_DECISION_BOARD_MODE` is `'live'`; contest B, `STRATEGIC_ENCOUNTER_SCORE_BRIDGE` and the strategic clamp are deleted. Fourth pass of the cutover gate, first as a design session and then as three executor slices in one single-executor day (PRs #1780 and the closing one).

## What the three earlier passes could not see

The census in `'shadow'` reports the board's *preference*. Nobody had read the `legacyWinner` column — what contest B actually did. It chose an undertaking on **42–46% of spotlight decisions** (the plan's own envelope tops out at 35%) and let one mortal carry **eight to eleven concurrent undertakings**, because nothing but "this template is already running" gated a start. The 892-start "throughput" the third pass sized a floor against was that. Against what the world did, the live board sits inside the envelope on both seeds, starts *more* encounters (472 → 692, 389 → 627), idles the same or less (571 → 525, 847 → 417), and holds fewer undertakings per mortal.

## What shipped

- **Slice 1 — gates re-derived from the design.** `CENSUS_UNDERTAKING_START_FLOOR` (700) deleted; `CENSUS_STARTS_PER_MORTAL_PER_100_TICKS_FLOOR = 4` (a spotlight mortal begins a new undertaking about every two days); variety at a **fixed start sample** (`CENSUS_VARIETY_SAMPLE_STARTS`, `CENSUS_DISTINCT_AT_SAMPLE_FLOOR`) because run-total distinct tracks sample size; per-mortal concurrency reported, never gated (THR-1387 owns the cap). Green on the shipped arm first.
- **Slice 2 — two organic guards became constructed assertions.** `edgeIntegrity` drives the real `trades_with` writer once after the seeded run; `lairClearing` clears the most steeped active lair with the real writer when the organic run supplies none. Each falsified once. Both files run on THR-1384's heavy lane.
- **Slice 3 — the flip.** Mode `'live'`, contest B and its bridge deleted, the liveness pin flipped, the CLI block relabelled *Decision board*, the comparison trace's `legacyWinner` re-described as the encounter scorer's own pick, `BOARD_VARIETY_PENALTY_WEIGHT` retuned.

## Two numbers corrected at execution

The plan wrote the variety sample at 300 starts with a floor of 30, and the weight at 0.5. The first census under `'live'` reported *"sample of 300 never filled"* on seed 42 while the throughput gate beside it passed — the sample was inconsistent with the plan's own throughput floor, which underwrites ~162 starts. Corrected to **150 / 25** (derived from the floor, not chosen), and at that sample the weight's plateau is **0.7** on both seeds (27 / 27 distinct) where 0.5 sits on the floor on seed 99. Contest B measured 36 / 41, the uncalibrated 0.18 board 23 / 24; the floor separates them with a two-template margin. The reasoning is in the constants' docblocks and in the plan's *Executor amendments* section. The kill criteria were not tripped: the red was a defect in the gate's derivation, not in the board.

## One consequence the census does not read, found by the inventory

Regenerating `Docs/canon/systems-inventory.md` for this commit flipped **Spheres & Quintessence** from 🟢 ACTIVE to 🟠 DORMANT on the standard 120-tick seed-42 run. Traced: the badge's only hit on that run was `saturation_tick`, which `phaseMagicalSaturation` emits when a location holds non-zero `magicalSaturation` — 38 hits in 120 ticks before the flip, **0** after, and 4 by tick 200. The decay phase is alive; what changed is that the undertakings which *write* saturation are started later and less often by the board than contest B started them (contest B's 46% pick rate built saturated sites early). A mix consequence rather than an engine break, and outside every gate the census reads — recorded here and on the ticket, not hidden in a regenerated file. Worth a look from whoever takes THR-1387, since a concurrency cap lowers starts further.
