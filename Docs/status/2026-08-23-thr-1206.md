# THR-1206 — Reputation is the one social score between any two parties

**Shipped 2026-08-23.** Plan: `Docs/plans/2026-08-23-thr-1206-reputation-unification.md`

Six mechanisms wore the word *reputation* and disagreed, the UL defined none of them, and a seventh did reputation's job under a bespoke noun the director had just vetoed on player surfaces. The director's ruling, verbatim: *"custom concepts are difficult for players to learn and understand. if we do have reputation as our concept for 'the social score that modifies interactions between a and b', then lets use that everywhere."*

This is a **read unification plus one new store** — deliberately not a store migration. `getReputationWith(graph, a, b)` (`src/engine/reputation.ts`) dispatches membership → edge → bond → default, and every leg's band word comes from the one `getReputationWord` vocabulary. That shared word is what makes six stores one concept on every surface; the stores stay where they are.

The new `reputation_with` edge family (actor → actor|location) fills the two pairs nothing covered: agent↔location, and agent↔faction **without** membership — `applyFactionReputationGain` no-ops with `not_a_member`, so a non-member could never earn standing with a community at all. Sparse by construction: minted on first write, decayed toward neutral in phase 6.6, and deleted once it arrives, so the sweep is O(edges that exist).

**Every consumer shipped with it**, so this is not a write nobody reads: a `requiredReputationWith` eligibility gate at both filter sites, a *signed* opening-leverage term in social scenes (standing that has soured is as real as standing earned — the other opening terms are bonus-only), the Location Profile's Standing row, and the agent Overview's Standings list. The FactionSheet's two raw-percentage leaks band to the same words on the way past.

**First migrated content** is the Grateful Kin gratitude beat — the case that triggered the ruling. Its three bands replaced `apply_condition → standing_welcome` with `reputation_with` deltas, and its four chips now say *reputation with {target}*, which unblocks THR-1205's deferred content half. `standing_welcome` is retired with zero writers, its definition kept registered for saved worlds.

**One thing found on the way.** `reputation_tally` accepts only `<reach>.positive|negative`; everything else is traced and discarded. A recursive walk of the corpus found **171 of 518 authored tally writes (33%), across 78 keys**, landing on keys the engine refuses — content promising consequences that had never once occurred. The three engine-authored offenders are fixed here; the 78 content keys are frozen by a shrink-only ratchet and drain via THR-1207.

The gate for that is deliberately **two** gates. `check:encounter --all` sweeps `encounter.*` ids only — 15 of the 687 templates holding an invalid key, and 8 of the 78 keys — so a rule living only there would inspect a tenth of the class and report green over the rest. The line is held by a corpus-wide test; `check:encounter` is the fast feedback beside it.

Follow-ups filed: THR-1207 (tally sweep, unblocked and re-briefed), THR-1210 (the UL entry), THR-1211 (four reputation-adjacent dead reads found in the survey). THR-1182's gate was re-spec'd off the retired condition and onto the new one.
