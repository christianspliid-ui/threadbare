# THR-1488 — content finds content by family, and 48 promised sequels stopped withering

*Slice 4 of five for [THR-1481](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1481). Slice 3 built
the content query and proved it against the reward pool; this slice puts it under the two
places that name *content by content*: an encounter's sequel and an undertaking's catalyst.*

## The measurement that decided the shape

The plan predicted rot and named the mechanism. The corpus was worse than the prediction,
and measuring it first is what turned a refactor into a repair.

Of the **51 distinct `encounterFamily` values** the shipped corpus authors, **41 match no
template at all** — `stone.legacy`, `investigation`, `civic_guard`, every `eye.*` and
`gold.*` family, 168 authoring sites across 39 families. `encounterFamily` named an *id
prefix*, so a family was only ever as real as the punctuation of the ids in it. Alongside
them, **7 seed sites named `templateId`s that do not exist**: `cg.patrol.wall_walk`,
`cg.senior.inquisition` (×2 authoring sites), `noble.commission` (×4).

Forty-eight kinds of promised follow-up had been withering on arrival since they were
written. The author saw no error. The player saw a narrative event saying something was
"stirring", and then nothing came. It is THR-844's reveal-family rot one system over,
undetected for as long for the same reason: **an unreported nothing is indistinguishable
from a check nobody wrote.**

The undertaking catalyst was worse still, and simpler. Every id the seven packs spelled was
`encounter_<name>` while the corpus spells encounters `encounter.<name>`, so **not one of
the thirty-three ever resolved** — the field sat in THR-1481's substrate inventory as
DORMANT for exactly that reason.

## What shipped

**The four-edit trap, done once.** `UnifiedActionTemplate.tags`, `EncounterEntry.tags`,
`FactionEntry.tags`, and the `tags` passthrough in **both** `toUnifiedTemplate` converters.
Both converters are field allowlists rather than spreads, so a field declared on the entry
type and forgotten in the converter compiles, reads correctly to a reviewer, and connects
nothing — which is how this corpus's own `favorGeneration` sat inert until THR-724 and the
nudge fields until THR-838. The Done-when is therefore not the type but
`encounterTagPassthrough.test.ts`, which asserts a tag authored on a *real* entry is present
on the *exported* template, and was **falsified by deleting each passthrough in turn** (5 of
8 arms red, each naming its converter).

**Seventeen family tags, and each one a game word.** The twelve faction quest lines get the
word the body that sets the errand would use — `#circle_errand` for the Arcane Circle,
`#watch_errand` for the Civic Guard, `#thieves_errand`, `#consortium_errand`, `#court_errand`
and so on — plus `#tavern_night`, `#delve`, `#threshold_errand`, `#broker_errand`,
`#craft_commission`. Never `#ac_quest`: an id spelling wearing a `#` is the same rot, and the
word reaches the player through the withered-seed narrative event, so it has to read as
English. Applied by prefix to 78 templates, which is what makes the migration checkable
rather than a matter of taste.

**`encounter_seed.query` and `ENCOUNTER_FAMILY_TAGS`.** A seed now resolves
`templateId` → `query` → legacy prefix. The query **travels on the seed and resolves at fire
time**, not plant time — a sequel owed twenty ticks out finds the family as it stands when it
comes due, and may draw a member that did not exist when the promise was made. The alias
table rewrites the seventeen live prefixes for one release; a prefix with *no* row falls
through to the pre-change scan, deliberately, so the 41 dead families behave exactly as they
do today while being counted rather than changing shape on the way to being reported.

**`StrategicActionTemplate.catalystQuery`**, read by `maybeSeedCatalyst`, which stores the
query on the planted seed so the *one* seeding site resolves it — the same path an aftermath
seed takes, which is why the two planters can no longer drift about what a dead reference
does. All 35 catalyst sites across the seven packs migrated; zero literals remain.

**The seven dead sequels were repaired, not ratcheted.** The Court's tip to the watch, its
two district inquiries, and the courtier's off-books commission now name `#watch_errand` and
`#court_errand`. They can arrive for the first time.

## Three judgment calls, each recorded at its site

**The eligibility filter is preserved, and it narrows.** `matchFamilyTemplate` filtered
candidates to individual-performable templates accepting the target's current location
subtype. `resolveSeedByQuery` keeps exactly that filter *after* the resolver, because
membership in a family is not eligibility and folding it into the query would mean teaching
the resolver about actors and locations. The consequence is real and is documented in three
places: **a `query` sequel can wither for being in the wrong place where a `templateId`
sequel would have spawned anyway.** That asymmetry is now written into the systems-prompt,
the authoring spec, and the canon page, because an author choosing between the operands needs
it.

**The Full Moon exemplar was deliberately not converted.** The plan names "the Full Moon pair
in `vertical-slice.ts`" as the seed exemplar. Converting it would have added that location
gate to the flagship review route's sequel — `SLICE_FULL_MOON_COLLECTION` declares
`settings: ['wayside']`, so a traveler who has drifted off the road twenty ticks later loses
the collection entirely. That is a content regression [THR-1476](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1476)
has not licensed, and the plan's own parenthetical asks for "the placeless form THR-1476
leaves". So the seed exemplars are the three repaired sites instead, which are strictly
better exemplars: dead → live, rather than working → working-differently.

**Candidate order changed from array order to id-ascending.** The resolver is totally ordered
by contract. For every family that resolves today the *set* is identical — the widest is
`#tavern_night` at ten — so this changes which member a given seed draws, not which were
available. `FAMILY_SEED_MAX_CANDIDATES` (12) retires into `CONTENT_QUERY_MAX_CANDIDATES` (64)
per the plan; no family in the corpus reaches either.

## The gates, and why there are two of them

`validateEncounterSeedRefs` is fatal on a dead `templateId` and an empty `query`, advisory on
a legacy prefix matching nothing. Wired into `check:encounter` **and** into a corpus-wide
vitest over all 744 templates — and the second is not belt-and-braces. `check:encounter --all`
sweeps the `encounter.` prefix only (206 templates), and **every one of the seven fatal
findings lived outside it**: three in the Underking's Court content, four in a Shadow Court
audience. The runner alone would have reported the corpus clean. Same division of labour
`tallyKeyCorpus.test.ts` settled for reputation keys.

The advisory tier carries a ratchet at **168 sites**, failing in both directions so a drained
backlog must lower the number in the same commit.

`check:undertaking` gains a `catalysts` block, fatal on both operands.

**Falsifications, all reverted.** Seed gate: a dead `templateId` and an empty `query` on
`encounter.slice.bargain_at_crossroads`, each failing by name in both the runner and the
vitest. Catalyst gate twice, and the second was necessary — restoring `encounter_ruin_trap`
on `strategic_chart_the_wilds` produces the violation but that template is ratcheted, so the
**exit code stays 0**; breaking the query on `strategic_mount_expedition`, which passes, turns
`check:undertaking -- --all` from exit 0 to exit 1. A falsification that only reddens a
ratcheted row is half a proof. Alias path: removing the `broker.quest` row reddens exactly the
arm asserting the resolver was reached — and the seed still spawns via the prefix scan, which
is the right coupling (the test proves the path, not the outcome).

## Live proof, and the limit it exposed

The seed query site is proven through the real `evaluateEncounterSeeds` and the real trace
buffer: `content.query_resolved` with `site: 'encounter_seed'`, the candidate count, and the
picked id matching the spawned action — for an authored query *and* for a shipped
`encounterFamily` seed reaching the resolver through the alias table. Constructed rather than
cited from an organic run, on purpose: whether a given seed fires on seed 42 at tick N depends
on where its target is standing, and a Done-when satisfied by luck is not satisfied.

The organic behaviour was measured anyway. On seed 42 / medium over 100 ticks, aliased-family
seeds (`mct.quest` → `#consortium_errand`) reach the query site and trace
`content.query_empty`, because their targets are not standing anywhere the family's templates
accept. That is the same nothing the prefix scan produced — **now visible**, which is exactly
what made the seven dead `templateId`s findable.

**The catalyst site has no live claim, deliberately, and that is a finding rather than a
gap in the work.** `UNDERTAKING_MODEL` is `'cells'`, and under the cells model a profile's
`templateIds` are not walked (THR-1403). All 35 carriers of `catalystQuery` are pack
templates on the legacy arm; **0 of 60 cells declare one**. So the field went from *never
resolving* to *resolving correctly and unreachable from the live board*. Filed as
[THR-1497](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1497) with its coordination block, recorded
on `Docs/canon/undertakings.md`, and not papered over with an invented claim.
