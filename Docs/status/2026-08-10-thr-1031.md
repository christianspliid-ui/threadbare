## Law 45 was the substantive finding, and it was invisible in the source

The ticket asked for one contrast measurement, on `PremonitionModal`'s header. Measuring properly turned up **eleven** sub-floor text tones across two components, all of them invisible to a reader of the source:

- `#b91c1c` (`doomed`, `critical_failure`) is a *literal* and reads as a deliberate colour choice. It measures **3.05:1** on `--veil-void` — and on any legal background, since Law 32 caps backgrounds at `--bg-surface`, where it is worse (2.68:1). Both its sites colour **words**: the forecast word and the replay outcome word. Retired for full loss red (**7.14:1**), which also makes the ladder symmetric — `perilous 0.85 → doomed 1.0` now mirrors `favorable 0.8 → fated 1.0`, so severity reads as intensity instead of as a different red.
- **The two hues are not interchangeable, and the code assumed they were.** Measured on `--veil-void`, gain green clears AA from alpha 0.55 (4.73:1); loss red does not clear until **0.8** (4.86:1) — 0.75 is 4.39:1, near enough to look fine and still under the floor. Four shipped text sites sat at 0.6–0.7 (3.17–3.95:1) *because they had been authored to match the green's alpha beside them*. `--veil-loss-text-alpha` now names that floor once.
- `PremonitionModal`'s defect was not its colours at all. Its hexes measured 8.3:1 and 7.9:1 at full opacity; the failure was **six Tailwind `opacity-40`/`-50`/`-70` utilities stacked on top of them**, dropping the agent name and the option footers to ~2.5:1. The colour reads as "the text token" in source and paints at a quarter of it — the same shape THR-1010 found in the veil's gold, which is why Law 45 says *composed surface* and means it.

Every one of those is now measured on the live surface: **11 of 11 text rows on `PremonitionModal` clear AA, worst 4.69:1**, `subAA: []`.

## Composing `Modal` fixes less than the ticket assumed

`EmergenceDilemmaModal` now composes `Modal` — measured after: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `zIndex: 60` (from the stacking table, replacing an invented `1200` — Law 35), portaled to `document.body`. That is Laws 23, 26 and 35.

It is **not** Law 50. The ticket reasoned that the fork was why the surface had "no focus move-in, no focus return on close", and that composing the primitive would fix it. `Modal` has no focus management either — grep it for `focus`, zero hits — so every consumer in the codebase is non-conformant in exactly the same way. Filed against the primitive as [THR-1079](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1079), which is also **the decided answer to THR-1024's *"same overlay-forking fix shape — decide it once"***: fix focus once in `Modal`, never per consumer, because N component-level focus traps would themselves be the Law 26/27 defect.

The one judgment call: this surface is a **forced choice with a countdown** and has no dismiss, so `Modal`'s close contract needed a meaning rather than a no-op. It resolves `let` — not a design decision but a *readback*, since `phaseDelveEmergence` already fires `emergenceChoice: 'let'` on timeout. Escape cannot reach an ending that waiting would not, which keeps it clear of Law 48; the three options that spend anything still require their own click. Verified live: Escape → `['let']`, backdrop click → `['let']`. A gate pins the constant to the engine's value so the two cannot drift.
