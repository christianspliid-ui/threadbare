# THR-943 — the encounter badge's reopen affordance is tier-dependent by construction

**Shipped 2026-08-10.** A verification verdict, not a repair: THR-943 asked whether THR-664's route into a live encounter beat is dead or merely unreachable at one tier. It is unreachable at one tier, alive at the other, and the difference falls out of `attentionMode` rather than out of a defect.

## What was asked

THR-934's Done-when had four clauses. Three were evidenced live; the fourth — *"clicking that badge reopens the stage"* — had **no reachable state to test** at `the_first`/`pause`, so it was filed rather than claimed. The ticket set the fork explicitly: verify the `auto_resolve` path, and *"if it works there, this ticket is a docs/Done-when correction… if it fails there too, THR-664's designed route into an ongoing encounter's current beat is dead in both tiers, which is a real defect."*

## What was measured

`?view=game&seeded&size=medium`, driven with `window.__DEBUG.tick()` and `suppressBeats(true)`, Playwright at 1920×1080. The seeded world hands both arms over for free: **Vara** (the `?spawn=` target) seeds at `the_first`/**`pause`**, and **Kael Thornweaver** seeds at `the_first`/**`auto_resolve`** — the load-bearing variable is the attention mode, not the court position, so no thread surgery was needed.

* **`pause` — the affordance is unreachable, exactly as filed.** Vara's step notification auto-opened; clicking **Resume** flipped it `viewed: true → resolved: true` in the same frame, and the badge retired with it. There is no state in which the encounter is live, the stage is closed, and the badge is present.
* **`auto_resolve` — the affordance is alive.** Kael's row badged its pending notifications (`autoResolveTick` 39 and 45, both `viewed: false, resolved: false`). Clicking the badge **opened the stage**, marked that record `viewed`, and the badge fell back to the remaining one — count 2 → 1, label re-derived from the new primary.

So THR-664's route is not dead. It is the *only* route at `auto_resolve`, and structurally redundant at `pause`, where every beat interrupts on its own and the veil's single close route (`onDisregard`) resolves the record that anchors the badge.

## What shipped

The finding is worth more than the click, so it is written where the next reader will look rather than only in a ticket: `isBadgeWorthy` now carries the tier split, names why the reopen transition cannot hold at `pause`, and states the rule that would have prevented THR-934's clause 4 — **do not write a Done-when asserting "clicking the badge reopens the stage" without naming the tier it holds on.** Three assertions pin the mechanism across the `encounterBadgeModel` / `encounterNotificationRuntime` seam, held together so a future edit cannot make the badge redundant at *both* tiers with every unit test still green.

## What is deliberately not claimed

Establishing the verdict surfaced a **separate defect at the same tier, filed as THR-1068 rather than absorbed**: `autoResolveTick` has no consumer. `buildEncounterNotification` stamps `tick + RETINUE_VIGNETTE_TIMEOUT` on every `auto_resolve` notification, and the five production readers all treat it as a flag or a label — nothing ever resolves a notification when the tick arrives. Measured at tick 113: three of Kael's notifications sat 32, 36 and 37 ticks past their deadline, all still pending, with the badge count climbing 2 → 3 across the run and never falling. Two player-visible consequences follow — the badge becomes a pile nothing drains, and the veil's countdown (`autoResolveTick - tick`) renders negative. It is routed rather than fixed here because the repair is a design call first: expiring silently discards a beat the player never saw, which may be right for `auto_resolve` or exactly wrong if unattended beats were meant to queue. `RETINUE_VIGNETTE_TIMEOUT` names a timeout that has never fired.

No behaviour changed in this ticket, so the badge renders exactly as before; the browser pass is evidence for the verdict, not for a visual delta.
