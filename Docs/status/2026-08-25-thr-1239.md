### THR-1239 — the effect executors were never broken; nothing was raising the events that reach them

Stage 1 of the six-stage effect-vocabulary activation program (plan doc: `Docs/plans/2026-08-25-effect-vocabulary-activation.md`, PR #1614). The ledger measured the gap: 53 members in the `AttachmentEffect` union, ~21 executing on the live path. This stage takes the honesty guards, the missing event producers, and consumables.

**The shape of the defect.** `getReactiveTrigger` has mapped `entered_hex` and the combat pair to reactive triggers since the primitive architecture landed, and `processEffectEvent` has always known how to dispatch them. What did not exist was any **producer** — outside a single `encounter_outcome` raise inside the orchestrator, no site in the engine ever constructed an `EffectEvent`. So the entire executor family (teleport, spawn, compel, cascade, …) was unreachable in normal play while every part of it read as wired. Adding a producer meant either pasting ~35 lines of dispatch per site or naming the sequence once; `raiseEffectEvent` (`src/engine/effects/effectEventDispatch.ts`) is that name, and **the pre-existing orchestrator site was migrated onto it rather than left as a second copy** — so the count of hand-rolled dispatch blocks went from one to zero while the number of producers went from one to four.

**Four producers now, each traced.** Movement arrival raises `entered_hex`; battle create/resolve raise `combat_started`/`combat_ended`; the orchestrator's encounter step and `phaseDoom` keep theirs and now trace identically. Every raise emits `effect.event_raised` carrying its site and reactive count — **including when nothing was listening**, which is the whole point: "the producer is live and no attachment cared" and "the producer was never wired" were previously indistinguishable, and that indistinguishability is how this sat unnoticed.

**Three semantics decided in-doc, and each rejects a plausible alternative.** `entered_hex` fires on **final arrival only** — not per hex step (≈4× the event volume for no design gain) and not on same-hex sublocation moves. Combat audience is **participants only**, which resolves to the two commanders, because an army is an `actor` node carrying a headcount rather than a roster of agent nodes; bystander coupling is a design decision deliberately not taken. And `combat_ended` fires **before** `applyAftermath` — a `killed` commander is removed from the graph there, and raising after it would mean the loser's `leave_combat` expiries silently never fire, since the raise fail-softs on a missing agent.

**Consumable charges now deplete.** `chargesRemaining` had a reader, an initialiser and a destroy-at-0 branch, and **no decrement anywhere** — so the destroy branch was unreachable and every "3 charges" item in the reward catalogs was, in play, unlimited. One charge is now spent per completed encounter step whose reach matches the charge's `onUse.reach`, at step granularity rather than action granularity (a five-step encounter uses a matching item five times). Spending is automatic because an agent's gear has no "use item" verb to opt into.

**The `never` guard, and what it found.** The `default` arm of `executeEffect` was returning `success: false` with an "Unknown effect type" warning for **13 union members** — the whole query/tick-layer family — that are working exactly as designed elsewhere. They joined the modifier fallthrough, and `default` became `const _exhaustive: never = effect`, so a future union member added without a case is a compile error rather than a silent runtime lie. This is the one deliberate non-fail-soft failure in the subsystem, and it fires at build time, never in the tick loop.

**`resource_manipulate` one_shot fired nowhere.** `effectTick` documented it as "handled by the event handler" and the event handler had no case for it; both halves were wrong. It now fires on the bearer's first `encounter_outcome` after attach, and — deliberately — **is spent even when it cannot land**, so an `other_agent` shot in a solo encounter does not re-attempt on every later encounter forever.

**Evidence.** 1072 files / 17,720 tests green; typecheck ratchet 3014 **unchanged from baseline**, which is the `never` guard's own proof; `vite build` ✓; 30-tick seeded medium smoke ✓ (384 agents, tick 30). Live raise proven in a real run rather than only in unit tests: `traces 5000` on seed 42 medium shows **10 `entered_hex` raises at `movement_arrival`** by tick 30. The charge-spend call site is proven by a **falsified** integration test against the real `executeStepResult` — 5 of its 6 tests go red when the call site is disabled, the sixth being the negative case that correctly should not. That test exists because the CLI could not settle it either way: only **1 of 13** charged artifacts in that world has a bearer at all, so a natural matching-reach step inside 60 ticks is a long shot, not a guarantee.

**One gate caught this change and was right to.** The THR-1065 emitTrace cast ratchet went 107 → 109 on two new `as unknown as` sites in the new module. The fix was the ratchet's stated intent rather than a raised ceiling: legacy-shaped payloads now funnel through a single cast site, and migrating the orchestrator onto the shared dispatcher removed two more — landing at **106**, below the ceiling it started at.

`Browser-verify exempt: engine-only change, no UI-pillar files touched.` Wiki pages updated where behaviour changed (armies-battles, tick-cycle); `turn-structure` and `clocks` exempted, the first for a behaviour-identical refactor and the second for a trace-only addition.
