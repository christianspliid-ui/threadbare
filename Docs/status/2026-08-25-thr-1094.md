# THR-1094 — conditions were already a tooltip class; the gate could not see it

**Shipped 2026-08-25.** `Docs/design-system/laws.md` (Law 17), `src/engine/tooltipResolver.ts`,
`src/engine/__tests__/conceptTooltipIds.test.ts`,
`src/components/Game/encounter-stage/adapters/buildAftermathConsequences.ts`.

## What the ticket asked, and why the answer was already on disk

THR-1094 was filed as a design fork: conditions are named in aftermath prose
(*"an exhaustion that will not walk off"*), Law 1 says every concept the player
meets by name explains itself, and `condition.*` resolved to nothing. So either
`condition.*` becomes a registry class and every shipped condition gets authored
copy, or conditions are ruled permanently plain prose.

Neither. **Conditions are already a tooltip class**, and have been since THR-1122
shipped the `attachment.*` prefix three days before this ticket was written. A
condition is an attachment; `attachment.trait.condition.exhausted` resolves
through `attachmentTemplateIndex.ts`, and a concept declaring
`entityId` + `visualKind: 'attachment'` never has to name the id at all —
`attachmentTooltipIdFor` derives it. Measured against the shipped corpus, all 13
`CONDITION_TRAIT_DEFINITIONS` resolve, the seven bearer conditions and the six
location ones alike.

The ticket was not carelessly written. It quoted Law 17 exactly, and **Law 17 was
four prefixes stale** — it named `ui.* sphere.* reach.* terrain.* archetype.*
doom.* agent.*` while the registry had grown `faction.*` (THR-1149),
`quintessence.*` (THR-1118), `attachment.*` (THR-1122) and `location.*`
(THR-1172). Read faithfully, the law said the route did not exist.

## The real defect underneath

The route worked and **nothing proved it kept working.** `conceptTooltipIds.test.ts`
is the Law 17 gate, and it scans authored `tooltipId: '...'` literals. A concept
on the attachment route deliberately has none — that is the point of deriving the
id — so the derived half of the corpus sat outside the one gate that exists to
catch a dangling id. A typo'd `entityId: 'trait.condition.exhusted'` would resolve
to nothing, the chip would quietly draw the word plain, and every gate would stay
green. That is the dead-underline class THR-1033 was filed for, one derivation
removed from where anyone was looking.

## What shipped

- **A second scanner in the Law 17 sweep** covering `entityId` + `visualKind:
  'attachment'` pairs, with its own non-empty guard (22 authored concepts across
  three content files — the sweep cannot go vacuous unnoticed).
- **The sweep calls the shipped derivation.** `attachmentTooltipIdFor` is now
  exported and invoked by the test rather than having `attachment.` + id rebuilt
  beside it, so a change to the derivation rule — the explicit-`tooltipId`
  short-circuit, the `visualKind` guard — moves the gate instead of passing it.
- **A falsification pin**: an unshipped template id must still *produce* an id
  and that id must *not* resolve, so the sweep cannot go green by being blind.
- **Law 17's prefix list corrected to all 13**, with a note recording why the
  drift was expensive, and the resolver's routing-table header cross-referencing
  it so the next prefix amends both.

The guard was falsified against the real corpus before shipping: injecting
`trait.condition.exhusted` into the swollen-ford exemplar turned the sweep red and
named the file and the id; reverting restored green.

## The ruling, recorded

Conditions are a tooltip class, via `attachment.*`. No `condition.*` prefix is
added — a second route to the same templates would be two registries for one
concept. No authoring work is owed: the copy already exists on the template nodes
and `plainRegisterBody` draws it, deliberately skipping `mechanicalSummary` where
the numerals live (Law 13).

This was taken as an agent verdict rather than escalated. The fork the ticket
described had already been decided by Law 1 plus a shipped route; what remained
was a missing gate, which is calibration. Christian's veto stands if he wants
conditions ruled plain prose after all — that would mean *removing* a working
hover, so the burden sits on changing it.

## Pillars

- **Engine** — the sweep and the exported derivation. No runtime behaviour changed.
- **Content** — none owed; the corpus was already complete and is now pinned.
- **UI** — none. The `export` keyword is the whole component-side diff.
