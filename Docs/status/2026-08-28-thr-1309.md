# THR-1309 — T3 undertaking tier: the warband, and the order

**Shipped 2026-08-28.** Tier 3 of the undertaking kind registry, on the same terms as
tiers 1 and 2: a kind arrives with the verb that undoes it, or it does not arrive.
Absorbs THR-1295.

## What landed

One kind row — `warband` — and the op the tier could not exist without. T1's objects
were edges, possessions and actor-side records; T2's were places. T3's object is the
first that can **act back on the world by itself**: a company of mortals who move,
fight, fray, and can be led badly.

`create_group` is that op, and it has two arms because they are the same authored act
at two scales. A commander raises a *company* through `createGroup` — the one code
path that mints one, so the node arrives already carrying `groupKind: 'company'` with
its `member_of` roster and `commanded_by` owner. A founder charters a *faction*
through a `FactionDefinition` synthesized from an authored `factionSeed` into
`seedFactionFromDefinition`.

The CRUD closure: **raise** (`strategic_recruit_warband`, warlord) → **reinforce**
(`strategic_reinforce_warband`, warlord) → **suborn** (`strategic_suborn_warband`,
court — cross-family and motive-gated, because the D column is what the *world* can do
to take a work back and a self-spend is a use, not a counter).

## The tier that had to remove something to arrive

`strategic_recruit_warband` has completed for the whole life of the corpus **while
minting nobody**. Its mutation wrote an intelligence record called
`warband_recruited`, so the verb entered the completion history and every dashboard
counted it, and no band ever existed. Registering a `warband` row against it in that
form would have been exactly the vacuity the registry gate exists to refuse —
presence of a create id, no created thing — and the gate could not have seen it,
because the gate checks the *destroy* column.

Second half of the same repair: `strategic_found_order` was folded out of the retired
initiative pipeline carrying only half its old payoff. It built a guild-hall
sublocation and stopped, with a `TODO(THR-1295)` at its hint. Meanwhile
`dynamicFactionDefinitions` — the `GameState` field whose entire purpose is holding
run-authored faction definitions — had **no live producer anywhere in the codebase**
since that retirement: a declared field, an IA-manifest row, and nothing that writes
it. The world could not gain a faction after worldgen by any path. It can now, and the
hall is not lost by the switch: `seedFactionFromDefinition` seeds 3–5 real halls
anchored at the founding settlement.

## Trap 1, answered in the target rule

The plan warns that selection and resolution can disagree silently and that it reads
as dead content — `press_the_mark` refused correctly without a held mark, but its
target rule selected on *role*, so it completed three times against three strangers
and minted zero debts.

A warband walks straight into that shape: a company is an `actor` node with **no
`located_at` edge of its own** (position derives from its leader), so no existing rule
could see one. `colocated_actor` reads `located_at` incoming and finds nothing,
`actor_with_trait` wants a trait companies never carry, and `self` returns the
commander rather than the band. The new `group_node` rule takes an `ownership`
discriminator — the update targets only bands the actor **commands**, the destroy only
bands they **do not** — so both verbs are checked at selection rather than refusing at
resolution. `resolveTargetHex` learned to resolve a group through `getGroupPosition`,
so the rule inherits THR-1310's proximity fix instead of silently falling back to
insertion order.

## The finding: a cast halts this verb completely

The recruits *should* be a cast slot — that is the trap-1 guarantee, making the people
a precondition the binding system supplies rather than a hope about who is standing
there. It was authored that way first, and it made the verb inert. Three controlled
arms, seed 99 / medium / 150 ticks, each confirmed applied before it ran:

| Arm | `strategic_recruit_warband` | Warbands standing |
|---|---|---|
| cast, `must-persist` | 0 ok / 6 fail | 0 |
| cast, `scene-only` | 0 ok / 6 fail | 0 |
| **no cast** | **13 ok / 1 fail** | **7** |

So it is the *presence* of a cast on this template, not the persistence mode — and not
casts in general, since `strategic_establish_spy_network` carries a structurally
identical `must-persist` cast and completes 3/4 in the same run. Both `mercenary` (4)
and `guard` (51) exist in a seeded world, so it is not unbindable roles either.

Shipping the cast anyway would have delivered a `warband` kind whose **create verb
never fires** — the same "counted by everything, mints nothing" shape this ticket
exists to repair the template *out of*, and worse for being freshly written. Filed as
**THR-1321** rather than fixed inline, for the reason THR-1308 filed THR-1320: the
bind pass is a shared subsystem owing its own golden comparison. The receiving code
stays live and unit-tested (`raiseWarband` takes bound ids and prefers them), so
restoring the cast is a content edit once the halt is understood.

## Two more findings from reading a real world

**A name doubled.** `reinforceWarband` returned a `createdId`, and
`christenCompletedWork` renames whatever carries one — so every reinforcement
re-christened an already-named company and produced **"The Murkford Company
Company"**. `createdId` means *a node was created*; reinforcing creates edges. Both
the update and destroy ops stopped returning it. Same class as T2's "The The Shattered
Sanctum–Greycity Road", and visible only by reading names out of a 150-tick run.

**A malformed edge nothing threw on.** The faction founding first wrote
`constructed_by` from the faction node — schema `location|sublocation → actor`, so
every founding logged a `[GraphSchema]` violation and put a malformed edge in the
world. It writes `commanded_by` (actor → actor) instead, which says the truer thing
anyway — the plan marks a faction "led, not held" — and earns its keep, because
`getCommandedEntities` reads that edge. This is trap 2 exactly: assert
well-formedness at the writer.

## A test that pinned a coincidence as a law

`undertakingKindRegistry.test.ts` asserted `ownable === (tier >= 2)`. True of every
row that existed when it was written, false as a rule: both tier-2 rows are ground (a
route, a settlement) and holdings is about ground, but `warband` is tier 3 and its
object is **people**, which the plan's own table marks "commanded" — exactly as it
marks the tier-1 `network`. The predicate is now an explicit ground-kinds set. Its own
comment had invited this ("a future row that breaks this will have to say why here
rather than drifting into it").

Two sibling assertions were re-pinned rather than relaxed, and one changed meaning:
"registers no tier above 2" became "registers no kind whose counter-play is
unshipped", because **tier was never the predicate** — a shipped, reachable,
motive-gated destroy is.

## What is deliberately still absent

`faction` has no kind row, and this is the sharpest case yet for the rule: its *create*
half now ships — `strategic_found_order` charters a real faction — and the row still
may not land, because nothing in the corpus **dissolves** one.
`phaseSchismResolution` exists and splits factions, but it is an ambient process, not
a motive-gated `destroy` verb an agent can choose, which is what a D column names.
Registering against it would be presence of a counter-play rather than a counter-play.
`sublocation` stays absent for the older reason: its six builds ship, its raze does not.

who does not, and the op then breaks the band. The world satisfies the precondition
too: at tick 150 on seed 99 there are 20 bands, 17 commanders, 91 `hostile_to` edges,
and **4 of them already aimed at a band commander**. It did not get selected inside
150 ticks. Reordering it ahead of the always-available `expose_cache` in the revenge
profile changed nothing, which is how we know it is not list-position starvation.
