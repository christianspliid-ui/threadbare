# THR-1053 — the aftermath anchor rule, settled against the renderer

**2026-09-11 · Content / Engine · `tb-opus-pickup`**

The Composition Contract demanded `concepts` on every aftermath change. The type it
governs documented the opposite. The ruling goes to **Done-when option (b)**: the rule
is narrowed to **an anchor per change — `stateNoun` or `concepts`** — and all three
surfaces now say the same thing.

## Why the rule's own justification did not survive reading the surface

The rule cited Law 2: every named game concept must be reachable. Three readings off
`buildAftermathConsequences.ts`, taken by construct rather than by the line numbers
(which have drifted twice since this ticket was filed):

| Fact | Reads |
|---|---|
| `link()` runs on `detail` unconditionally (`:660`, `:666`) | `applyConceptDecorations(link(id, enrich(body)), concepts)` — the link is applied *inside* the argument, never gated on `concepts` |
| decorations are a no-op without `concepts` (`:537`) | `if (!concepts \|\| concepts.length === 0) return paragraph;` |
| the icon tile is `stateNoun`'s job (`:668-670`) | *"`concepts` merely decorates the sentence, so it is the fallback"* |

So the narrative linker already delivers Law 2 reachability on `detail` for every entity
it can resolve, independently of `concepts`. What `concepts` genuinely adds — per its own
doc — is the **derived** vocabulary no scan can find: a reach name, a standing tier, a
reward. Valuable, but narrower than the row claimed. Demanding it on every change asked
authors either to re-declare what the linker already decorates, or to invent a concept
for a sentence naming none.

## Why it was narrowed and not deleted

`chipAnchorViolations` (Law 56 clause 2) excludes the chip declaring *neither* anchor, on
the stated ground that *"the `concepts` rule plus the composition ratchet already hold
those templates."* Deleting the rule would have turned that documented exclusion into a
silent hole — nothing would require a chip to say what it points at. The floor stays; only
its shape changed, and the new shape is the renderer's own precedence order.

**Ruling 3 is untouched.** It forbids an exemption *mechanism* — a per-template opt-out.
Every template is still measured by the same predicate, and none gets a waiver. This is
gate calibration on a measured fact, delegated to the agents (CLAUDE.md § User review
interface, rule 4); the veto window closed 2026-09-11.

## What it released — exactly the two encounters, and no others

```
before   checked 206   clean 28   failing 0   on ratchet 178
after    checked 206   clean 30   failing 0   on ratchet 176
```

The two stale ratchet entries were `encounter.slice.snow_on_the_pass` and
`encounter.slice.riders_behind_caravan` — the two excluded from retrofit batch 1 **and**
batch 2 on this exact rule, in the batch-2 brief's own words: *"if that ruling goes the
other way, the work is thrown away."* Batch 3's status fragment closed the pilot volume
"except for the two the `concepts` ruling still holds". It no longer holds them.

**Honest correction to the ticket's own estimate.** It predicted *"~191 templates get
materially closer to passing"*. Measured: 35 violation lines → 25, and **2** templates
pass outright. The other 174 on the ratchet fail on other rules too, so this rule was a
large share of the *seed* and a small share of the *remaining* work. Batch 4 is the real
route off the ratchet; this unblocks two encounters, not the corpus.

## The test that actually pins the ruling

The existing falsification arm stripped `concepts` and expected a violation — and stayed
green after the narrowing, because the golden exemplar carries **no `stateNoun` on any
change** (0 occurrences, verified). It would pass identically under the old rule and the
new one, so it pinned nothing. Added the missing half: a change anchored by `stateNoun`
alone must now pass. Falsified by reverting the predicate — that arm goes red, and the
ratchet test goes red with it, so the ruling has two independent pins.

The non-compliant fixture now strips `stateNoun` alongside `concepts`, so that giving the
exemplar a state noun — a plain content improvement, and the direction the authoring spec
now steers toward — cannot silently turn the whole block vacuous.
