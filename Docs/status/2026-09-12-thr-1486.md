# THR-1486 — the closed tag vocabulary, and the attachment line's first machine gate

**Shipped 2026-09-12.** Slice 2 of five for [THR-1481](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1481) (content model), on top of slice 1's registry ([THR-1485](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1485)). Where slice 1 said *what kinds of content exist*, this one says *what an author may call them* — and makes the corpus obey it.

## Why a closed list

A tag is how one piece of content asks for another without naming its id. That only works if the words are finite. Measured across the registry's own catalogs, the corpus held **153 distinct spellings**, 62 of them worn by one or two entries and read by nothing at all. A query written against that matches whatever the last author happened to type — which is the same failure mode as the literal ids the model exists to replace, one layer up.

## What changed

**The vocabulary — `src/data/content-tags.ts`.** 96 tags across five axes. `reach` (8) and `sphere` (12) are **derived** from `REACH_DOMAINS` and `SPHERE_NAMES` rather than restated, and each derived tag owes a description at compile time, so a ninth reach cannot ship unexplained. `polarity` is the two words the condition proxy-event classifier already reads. `form` (what the thing *is*) and `family` (what class of story-object, and what walk of life) are authored.

**`family` is the wide axis and the file says so.** It holds both "what kind of object" (`#relic`, `#trinket`) and "what walk of life" (`#combat`, `#knowledge`, `#trade`), because the corpus treats those as one idea — an entry carries `#weapon` *and* `#combat`, never one instead of the other. Splitting them is a sixth axis, which is a design decision and not this slice's to take; the strain is recorded rather than hidden. It costs little, because **the axis is presentation and completeness, never query semantics**: the resolver matches tags, not axes.

**The migration, under one stated rule.** A spelling survived with ≥1 runtime reader (a `tagFilters` query site or a hardcoded read) **or** ≥ `CONTENT_TAG_MIN_BEARERS` (3) bearers.

| Verdict | Count | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Derived — reach | 8 | moved onto the derived axis |
| Derived — sphere | 7 of 12 seated | the other five have no bearer yet |
| Polarity | 2 | `#positive` / `#negative` |
| Seated (authored) | 74 | 16 of them rewritten from a bare spelling to its `#` form |
| Retired | 63 | removed from their entries (83 occurrences) |

That is 41% retired, comfortably inside the plan's kill criterion (*"more than half"* reopens the axis set with Christian), and no retired tag had a runtime reader — which is what the rule guarantees by construction.

**The ratchet is empty**, and that is the migration's result rather than an oversight: nothing was left carrying an unseated tag. It is what let the catalog-entry types tighten to `readonly ContentTag[]` in the same slice (`ArtifactTemplate`, `CompanionTemplate`, `AgreementRewardTemplate`, `SpellTemplate`). Node-property bags stay `string[]` — saved worlds carry arbitrary strings, and the vocabulary binds catalog literals only.

**The generated catalog** — `.claude/skills/encounter-pipeline/reference/content-tag-catalog.generated.md` (`npm run generate-content-tag-catalog`, blocking under `check:generated-freshness`). One table per axis, each tag with its meaning and a bearer count per kind. **Seven tags ship DEAD**: six a `tagFilters` site asks for that no entry wears — a real hole in the corpus and this page is the only surface that shows it — and one sphere the corpus has not reached, which is not a defect and is never deleted. The page distinguishes the two, because badging them identically would make the deletable list wrong.

**The attachment line's first gate** — `npm run check:attachment -- <id> | --all`, over `src/data/content-eval/attachmentContract.ts`. Five structural blocks (`tag_vocabulary`, `required_axes`, `sphere_affinity`, `tier_range`, `census_tag`); green over **224 entries** across the six attachment kinds. The registry's `requiredAxes` column was filled from **measured** coverage, so a required axis never ships red: every attachment kind requires `family`, a Condition also `polarity`, an Agreement also `reach`. Twelve entries were missing a `family` tag and were authored one each rather than the column being weakened to fit them.

**Player surfaces.** The codex gains a tag-filter row on possessions, conditions, agreements and undertakings — chips grouped by axis, each carrying its `tag.*` tooltip, narrowing **ALL-of** (the reward pool's own rule, so the control teaches what the engine does). Only tags the entries in view actually wear are offered; a row of chips that select nothing is the dead-link-that-looks-live shape Law 21 names. The attachment sheet now renders its tags as chips with the tag's own word instead of printing `#iron` — a raw key on a player surface (Law 14) with nothing behind it (Law 17).

## Two plan premises that did not survive measurement

Slice 1 falsified two; this slice falsified two more, and says so rather than quietly complying.

1. **`censusTag` kept its `scale` half.** The plan calls the field *"metadata nothing reads"* and schedules it for deletion from all seven carriers. Measured: true of `reach`, false of `scale`. 132 of 193 authored literals carried `scale` and nothing else, `contentCensus/matrix.ts` reads it, and **no other field in the corpus carries an entry-level scale** — retiring it would have taken the census's scale coverage from 193 entries to zero, destroying [THR-474](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-474)'s backfill to tidy a field rather than replace it. The reach half is gone, and `dominantReachFromEffects` with it.

2. **The derivation was wrong often enough to matter.** That helper counted `effects[].reach` and took the mode — and it disagreed with the author's own reach tag on **9 of 106** attachments, **3 of 5** spells and **6 of 33** conditions that carried both. This is precisely why [THR-477](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-477)'s open derive-vs-persist question closes in favour of *authored on the tag axis*. The 18 entries that had a reach only by derivation were authored at the migration; three of them (the legendary artifacts) were authored **against** the derivation, which had called an anvil a thing of shadows and a void-gate a thing of the heart.

An omen's reach was already authored — on `censusTag.reach`, for all 44 tracks — so it moved onto the tag axis rather than being lost; `OmenTrackTemplate` gained an optional `tags` field to carry it. Its *sphere* stays unprojected for the reason slice 1 recorded: nested and conditional, on 6 of 44.

## What slice 3 inherits

A closed vocabulary with a generated reference, a registry whose `requiredAxes` and `projections` columns are populated and measured, and `effectiveTags(kind, entry)` in `contentCatalogs.ts` — the exact predicate `resolveContentQuery` needs. The DEAD badges are the first real reading of how much of the corpus a query can already reach.
