**Every slice ending said the same thing, whatever happened.** All 14 authored aftermath variants across `src/data/encounters/vertical-slice.ts` shipped `changes: []`, and the bridge's whole ending was one un-banded fallback — so "they crossed as if the bridge were new" and "a beam gave under their full weight" resolved to the identical paragraph and the identical single "Walk on". That is precisely the failure the April migration doc named in advance: *"No 'migrate now, wire later' — that's how you get templates in a new format with the same empty aftermath."* Christian played the bridge on 2026-08-08 and reported it back — one option, no explanation, no consequences — which is the finding confirmed live six days after it was filed.

**The endings are now keyed on how the encounter ended, and each one costs or pays something.** All eight templates carry `byOutcome` bands (30 banded combinations), authored on the variant itself so the choice-less templates — the bridge, the pass, the caravan, the full moon, the kin — have distinct endings at all. Measured across every (template × variant × outcome) pair: **98 endings, 0 with no chip**, 13 distinct chip sets, and 4–8 distinct closing paragraphs per template where each previously had one. The persistent half is real rather than prose-shaped: the ford costs `exhausted`, a fall from the middle span costs `wounded`, two days on open ground above the pass cost both, the alley behind the corn scales costs `wounded` and standing, and the kin's welcome mints its favor at a **thinner** magnitude when the thanks is fumbled than when it lands.

**Two structural rules govern every band, and both are documented at the top of the file rather than left to be rediscovered.** `applyAftermathOutcomeBand` replaces `reactions` *wholesale*, so a band on a variant whose base reaction plants a seed either restates that seed or authors no reactions at all — the crossroads accept path and the family help path are exactly that shape, and a band that quietly dropped their seed would un-plant a sequel with no error anywhere. Second, seeds stay single-sourced (THR-971): a planted `encounter_seed` already renders its own chip from the effect, so no band re-authors one as a `future_hook` change, which would be a chip promising a sequel that never arrives. **One band drops a seed deliberately** — the family's `critical_failure` keeps the swindler (his face is known either way) and drops the Grateful Kin, because a day of failed guiding that thinned the family's faith in strangers mints no word of a kindness.

**Three chips were mis-tagged and the browser pass is what caught it.** `classifyChangeKind` reads a `trait` at `mixed` or `loss` as a **WOUND**, so "The Word Given" — a promise, not an injury — rendered as a wound on a *critical success*. Corrected to `info` (MARK) on the three changes where nothing was actually taken; the bands that genuinely cost something keep `loss`. The full moon's at-cost and crit-fail endings were likewise a single `item`/`mixed` chip reading TOLL, which under-reported that the gift *was* handed over — now split into a PRIZE and a TOLL so each polarity is legible on its own (Law 31).

Four gates added to `__tests__/vertical-slice.test.ts`, and **the seed gate was falsified, found holed, and fixed**: its deliberate-drop waiver was keyed on the *band*, which exempted every drop that band could make — including the swindler seed it is supposed to keep — so a mutant deleting that seed passed. Re-keyed to name the specific seed; the same mutant now fails. The typed-kind gate is a `Record<EncounterAftermathChangeKind, true>` rather than a string set, so adding a kind to the union without adding it here is a compile error and the gate cannot silently go blind.
