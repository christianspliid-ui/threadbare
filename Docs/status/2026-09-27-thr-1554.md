# 2026-09-27 — THR-1554: every monster family has its own face

**Ticket:** [THR-1554](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1554) · **Project:** Physical Conflict · **Plan:** `Docs/plans/2026-09-23-fight-on-screen.md` (follow-on to F1, THR-1550); families from `Docs/plans/2026-09-23-monsters-as-opponents.md` §1–3 · **Pillars:** Content (eight pre-baked portraits), UI (resolver, lair card, opponent header, styleguide). Engine N/A (no tick, graph or trace change; reads the existing `monsterState.family`).

## What shipped

- **Eight portraits.** `public/portraits/monster-{beast,golem,stormkin,behemoth,mindthing,wraith,echo,blight}.jpg`, 896×1200 (3:4, the portrait crop), dark-fantasy oil in the STYLE.md actor recipe, each lit by its sphere's colour. Contact sheet: `Docs/evidence/thr-1554/contact-sheet.jpg`. Two were regenerated after the sheet review: the first wraith came back as a photo of a canvas on an easel, and the first echo was an ordinary deer.
- **Family lookup with a generic fallback.** `MONSTER_FAMILY_PORTRAITS` + `getMonsterPortraitUrl(family)` in `src/data/portrait-assets.ts`. An unknown, missing or art-less family resolves to the generic `/portraits/monster.png`, so missing art never blanks a card. `entityVisualResolver`'s `monster` kind reads `monsterState.family`.
- **The graph-free surfaces carry the portrait.** The lair card chip and the fight's opponent header render `EntityVisual` without a graph, so they could not see the family. Found only by the live capture: the resolver test passed while the real lair card still drew the generic portrait. Both models now carry `portraitSrc` (via `portraitSrcFor`), passed as `knownSrc`.
- **Styleguide.** `?view=styleguide` → EntityVisual shows all eight families in a row.
