# THR-1489 — the harness closing sweep: the content query becomes *measured*

**Slice 5 of five for [THR-1481](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1481).** Slices 1–4 made the content query exist, gated it, and put it on encounter seeds and undertaking catalysts. This slice answers the question those four could not: **is anyone reaching for it?** THR-1481's own kill criterion — *"if two encounter batches after slice 5 author zero queries, the retro names it a dead primitive"* — needs a number per batch to be reachable at all, and before this slice there was none.

The rule the work is built around is THR-1479's, applied to its own author: a capability is alive only when **the brief can roll it, a gate counts it, the live proof proves it, and the census reports it.** All four now exist.

## What shipped

**1. The brief rolls it.** Decision-shape die B gains an eighth face, `query_prize` — an ending that hands out its prize *by query, not by id* — with a **floor of one per batch of six**. It is the only floor on a die whose every other bound is a cap, because the two rot in opposite directions: a capped axis converges, an unreached primitive is simply never written. The floor is enforced the way the scale floor already was — by narrowing the table on the last slot that can still satisfy it, never by re-rolling — so the packet stays deterministic off the brief slug. `check-authoring-brief.ts` gains the brief-side half, failing a batch of six that records zero.

**The face is a consequence constraint riding a structure die, and that tension is recorded at the site rather than smoothed over.** It sits there for two reasons: die B is the only per-slot axis an author reads *before* writing the consequence, and a two-face die with a 1-in-6 floor is not a die, it is a floor wearing a die's clothes. The cost — that `query_prize` composes with a step shape rather than replacing one — is written into the face's own guidance and into the brief format.

**2. A gate counts it.** `content_query` joins the systems-quota connection keys. **The double count is the mechanism, not an oversight:** a seed carrying a query earns both `seeds` and `content_query`, so it is worth two of the quota's three where a seed naming a literal id is worth one. A quota that priced the query identically to the literal would give an author no reason to prefer it — the cheaper route *is* the incentive. Counted off an explicit `ContentQuery` literal only: a legacy `RewardPoolRecipe` projects onto a query at resolution time but is not *authored* as one, and counting it would hand the key to the whole legacy corpus on day one and make the census measure nothing.

**3. The live proof proves it.** `content_query_resolved` on `check:encounter-live`, gated through the same `scopeOf` every delivery claim uses. Read off the `content.query_resolved` trace rather than off the planted seed, because a seed carrying both a `templateId` and a `query` plants either way — the literal wins at fire time — so `pendingEncounterSeeds` cannot tell a resolved query from a bypassed one.

**4. The census reports it.** New `npm run check:content-model-census`, wired into the weekly hygiene routine as § 11, plus a content-query census block in both batch reports.

## The finding — and why `catalyst_seeded` does not pass

The census's first run (seed 42, 200 ticks, medium) is the slice's real output:

- **106 live tags, 7 DEAD** (`#blackmail_evidence`, `#community`, `#contraband`, `#light`, `#military`, `#stewardship`, `#supply`). A DEAD tag is a query nothing can answer: the author gets an empty pool and no error.
- **4 of 6 query sites silent.** Only `step_reward_pool` fired (8 resolutions).

`undertaking_catalyst` is silent because it is **structurally unreachable**, not unauthored: all 35 `catalystQuery` carriers are legacy-arm pack templates, and `UNDERTAKING_MODEL: 'cells'` does not walk a profile's `templateIds` ([THR-1497](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1497), filed by slice 4 while producing its own live proof).

So `catalyst_seeded` ships as a **real, falsifiable claim that is honestly `not_declared` on today's corpus** — and the gap is made loud by a corpus-reachability line (`catalystCorpusGap()`) printed on *every* run, rather than left to be inferred from a wall of quiet `not_declared` rows. The handoff comment offered three ways through and this is option 1 sharpened: asserting a pass against a pack template reached by a review lever would have been a green check on an uncovered condition — the exact pathology the census exists to catch.

**The lesson is bigger than the ticket.** That catalyst contract is wired, gated, contract-green and 🟢 LIVE on the interface map — and no player will ever meet it. Every connectivity hook was satisfied; none of them asked *"is this site on the path the live decision board walks?"* Reachability under the live model is a separate question from wiring, and only a census hit answers it. Recorded in the contract's verdict, in wiring-guide Capability 30, and in the cross-check comment on [THR-1479](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1479).

## The mutex that dissolved

The promotion comment made [THR-1496](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1496) a mutex on the stated ground that both tickets write `contentQueryRetrofitPending.ts` in opposite directions. **They do not.** Scope item 2 names `RETROFIT_PENDING` — which is `retrofitPending.ts`, the Composition Contract's ratchet — not `contentQueryRetrofitPending.ts`, the empty-query ratchet THR-1496 drains. And adding a *positive* connection key is purely additive: it can only make a template pass the quota more easily, never fail, so no ratchet edit is owed at all. **This diff does not touch that file**, and THR-1496 is unblocked. Recorded per THR-688 rule B: a mutex may be reversed when its stated reason is verifiably inapplicable.

## Two latent hazards fixed in passing

`generate-content-tag-catalog.ts` called `main()` unconditionally at import. The census reuses its `buildViews` — the deliberate choice, so the census and the generated catalog cannot disagree about what DEAD means — and without a guard, *reading* the census would have **written the catalog as a side effect**. Guarded on the entry file's basename, the THR-686 idiom, because `esbuild --bundle` rewrites `import.meta.url` to the bundle's own path and the usual guard evaluates true inside a bundle. `check-authoring-brief.ts` needed the same guard to become importable by its test.

`check-authoring-brief.ts`'s three "cannot judge drift" paths exited 0 unconditionally; they now return the floor gate's code. The two questions are independent — a missing `Docs/authoring-brief.md` says nothing about whether a batch rolled its query-prize face — and one shared early exit would have made the newer gate silently unreachable whenever the older one could not judge.

## The vacuity, reported rather than hidden

The brief-side floor gate currently judges **nothing**: eight briefs sit in `Docs/plans/encounters/` and exactly one records a rolled slot block at all — a single slot, below the batch threshold. A bare `OK` there would be the empty-population false pass, green because the sweep found no subject and indistinguishable from green because every subject complied. It prints `VACUOUS: 8 brief(s) scanned, none records 6+ rolled slots, so no batch was judged.` every run, so the first real six-slot brief is the moment the number moves rather than the moment someone wonders whether the check ever worked.

Falsified end-to-end anyway, with a temporary six-slot brief: zero `query_prize` faces → **exit 1** naming the file and the shortfall; one slot re-rolled to the printed label form (`Query Prize  [any + queried ending]`) → **exit 0**. Both arms then pinned as unit tests over synthetic briefs, because the live corpus cannot carry them.
