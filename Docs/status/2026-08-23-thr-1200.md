# THR-1200 — `{value}` renders the noun register, not the adjective

**Shipped 2026-08-23.** Third defect found on the same table in one day, after THR-1187 (pole inversion) and THR-1199 (`{fear}` as a phrase). Same blindness each time: the pins asserted a placeholder was *present* in the template and *absent* from the rendered text, and a grammatically wrong substitution satisfies both.

## What was wrong

`fearResolver` substituted `VALUE_LABELS` — the **adjective** register (`Courageous`, `Prudent`, `Honest`) — into `FEAR_PROSE`'s `{value}`. Every slot in that table is a **noun** slot, so the backstory stratum shipped lines like:

```
What Mira will never admit is that Courageous is maintained partly against the fear of exposure
Mira's Honest contains a quiet, specific fear
It is of the moment their Courageous is measured against someone whose version of it costs nothing
```

One body proved the intent by writing the noun literally beside the placeholder: *"Beneath the {value} in {name} is a fear they do not name: that **the courage they display** would […]"* — both registers, one sentence.

## The measurement that decided the fix

The ticket offered two options and estimated 97 of 108 bodies affected, with ~11 reading as defensible nominalisations. Sweeping the table through the real resolver gave a sharper number: **109 `{value}` slots across 100 bodies, of which 0 are sentence-initial and 0 are adjective slots.** Every one wants the noun. So option 1 (a `VALUE_NOUNS` table) fixes all of them and breaks none, against ~100 per-body rewrites for option 2.

The sweep also found one thing the ticket did not: `loyalty_ambition_negative` body 1 reads `"bound by a {value}"`, and four of the nine negative nouns are vowel-initial. That body was already shipping the broken `"a Ambitious"`; it is authored `"an {value}"` now.

## What shipped

- **`VALUE_NOUNS`** in `src/data/strand-content.ts` § 1b — nine axes, lowercase, same index convention as `VALUE_LABELS`. Two entries deliberately do not echo their `ValuePair` key, both evidenced by the prose rather than by taste: `tradition_novelty` negative is `innovation` (a body names it outright, and `novelty` renders the tautology *"novelty is not appetite for the new"*), and `revelation_discretion` positive is `candour` (every body treats the slot as a standing disposition about plain speech; `revelation` names an event).
- **`fearResolver`** reads the new table for `{value}`. The other four `VALUE_LABELS` consumers were checked and deliberately left alone — `strands.ts`, `agentDetail.ts` and the CMS registry want the adjective and are correct; `turningPointResolver` wants the noun and has the identical defect, filed as THR-1201 so its ~54 bodies get their own render review rather than riding along on an untested repoint.
- **The `{value}` contract** documented in the `FEAR_PROSE` header, mirroring the `{fear}` contract THR-1199 added, including the vowel-initial article trap.
- **`VALUE_NOUNS` on the CMS browsing surface**, beside the adjective register it is easily confused with.

## The pins, and their falsification

Three new layers, all reading the composed sentence rather than the placeholder:

1. Per-key, recover what `{value}` **actually substituted to** by matching the body's own literal text around the placeholder, and assert it equals the expected noun, is not the adjective, and is lowercase — plus a coverage guard that the pattern matched every placeholder-carrying body, without which the assertion is vacuous.
2. Per-key article agreement, forbidding only the article that *disagrees* with that key's noun.
3. A literal reviewed `VALUE_NOUNS` table in `strand-content.test.ts`, and an overlap enumeration against `VALUE_LABELS` — `cunning` is the one pole where the two registers legitimately share a spelling, so it is pinned by name; a second entry appearing there means the register split has collapsed.

**Falsified, three ways.** Reverting the resolver to `VALUE_LABELS` reddens **38 tests**, including all 18 noun-register assertions. Reverting the `an` → `a` body reddens the article test on exactly `loyalty_ambition_negative`. Mispointing the CMS registry row at `VALUE_LABELS` reddens 2 of its 3 arms.

Two of those guards were **wrong on first write and caught by their own falsification** — the article probe forbade both articles unconditionally (rejecting the correct `"an ambition"`), and the register-disjointness assertion forbade an overlap that `cunning` legitimately has.

## Also found, filed not fixed

- **THR-1201** — `turningPointResolver` / `TURNING_POINT_PROSE` has the identical adjective-in-noun-slot defect (*"when they chose Merciful"*, *"settled into Ascetic"*). One-line repoint once its own render review agrees.
- **THR-1202** — `preservation_transformation`'s first four bodies on *both* poles read as authored for a control/authority axis that no longer exists in `ValuePair`. Semantic, not grammatical: THR-1200 made them grammatical and could not make them say the right thing. Second pair to show THR-1187's four-and-two split.

## Note on the surviving blind spot

Every pin on this table now reads grammar and register. **None reads aboutness** — a body about dominance renders as flawless prose under the correct noun, which is exactly THR-1202. That is stated rather than papered over: the golden renders will trip when those bodies are rewritten, but nothing flags that they are off-axis today.
