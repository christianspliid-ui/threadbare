# 2026-09-10 — THR-1287: a hold is kept by working it

Control upkeep existed as a promise everywhere except in code. The wiki said *"a grip you stop renewing slowly opens"*, the strategic prose called monopoly *an activity*, and the neglect loop only ever counted **up**: the single `neglectTicks: 0` in `src/` was stance creation, `degradation` was monotone, and every hold a mortal claimed collapsed at grace (10) plus twenty degrading ticks no matter what its holder did. Control was a timer wearing a commitment's words.

Christian ruled the fork on 2026-09-10 (the ticket had been design-blocked since August): a claimed town is a **commitment**. A *Freehold* (`owns`) is property with no clock; a *hold* (`control:claim`, the `controls` stance) decays unless it is worked. The faction-position half of his answer is THR-1448 and was deliberately not built here.

**The shape is upkeep as a property of work the holder already does.** `renewControlStance` — pure over the controls array, one call site per completion arm — renews when the completed cell's variant is `use` or `change:raise` on a Location the actor holds through an active stance: `neglectTicks` to 0, `degradation` down by a quarter, floored. No new verb (THR-1392 ruled `hold` out), no new cell, and the loop, grace, rate, `retireControl`, the collapse and the re-claim cooldown are all untouched. The band bar is a rank test on the exported `STEP_OUTCOMES` ladder rather than `isStepSuccess`, which admits `near_miss` and would have left the constant decorative.

**Two things the plan could not have known, both found by writing the tests first.**

The plan's fail-soft table said a completion with no band renews nothing. Following it literally would have made the plan's *primary* path — the harvest — unable to renew anything, because `use × Location` carries duration `[0,0,0]`, synthesises as an **instant** cell, and reaches the resolver with no band at all. `executeInstantMutation`'s own parameter contract settles it (*"the instant path has no checkpoint, so its readers take the plain-success row"*), so an instant cell renews on its completion and a checkpointed one is graded. Shipping the literal reading would have been a lever that cannot fire — the exact defect class this ticket exists to remove.

The seize pin was written as *test first, repair only if it fails*, and it failed. `transferHolding` resolves owners through `findOwnersOf`, which reads `owns` edges **only** — so a Location held through a `controls` stance reads as unowned to it and a seize took the "seize of the unowned is a claim" branch: the seizer got a Freehold (correctly) while the incumbent kept both a live record *and* a live `controls` edge over somewhere already handed on, then sat out a full window before collapsing on it. `applySeizeRetirement` closes it, and THR-1286's invariant — live `controls` edges equal active stances — is exact across a change of hands again.

**Measured, honestly.** The generated-world acceptance is green: a small seed-42 world, two mortals claimed onto two unheld Locations they actually stand at, driven 45 ticks with the only difference being that one holder harvests every five — the worked hold is still `active` past the collapse window with its edge intact, the unworked twin is gone. Organically, renewal is **rare**: 0 renewals on seed 42 and 1 on seed 99 across 150 ticks each. That is a supply fact, not a correctness one, and the plan anticipated it — but the census also turned up **why**, which is now THR-1450: `use × Location` banks **zero wealth in every live run**, because `yieldBandScale(undefined)` is 0 and the instant arm never passes a band. THR-1439's harvest is all cost and no payoff, so an EVT-ranked board correctly declines it. That is a different ticket's scope and a different balance decision; renewal keys on completion, not on the lump, so it is unaffected either way.

**Evidence:** `npm test` 19768/19768; `test:heavy` 202/202; typecheck ratchet unchanged at the 2914 baseline (no refresh); `vite build` green; 30-tick CLI smoke at 493 agents. 22 new assertions across `controlRenewal.test.ts` and the heavy-lane `controlRenewalReach.test.ts`, including a sweep of all six bands against the constant and both arms of the bandless-instant rule.
