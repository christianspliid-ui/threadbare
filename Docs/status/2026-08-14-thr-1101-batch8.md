## The regression lock crossed, and it was lowered to its contract rather than to the next snapshot

`encounterWordPlaceholders.test.ts:201` asserted `withTokens.length > 50`. Draining `create` takes the population **61 → 48**, so this is the batch batch 7 flagged: the one that owes the deliberate lowering the ticket asks for.

It was lowered to **`> 0`**, and the reason is worth more than the number.

The guard's contract is the sentence in its own test name — *the corpus does carry these tokens, so the sweep below is not vacuous*. The minimum truthful statement of that is "at least one". `50` was never the contract; it was the population on the day the guard was written. A bound pinned to a population that is **deliberately shrinking** has to be re-argued at every batch that crosses it — four more times on the families still queued — and each re-argument is a fresh arbitrary number chosen under deadline. `> 0` is the same check, stated honestly, and it never needs revisiting.

It also fails at the right moment. This campaign is driving the population to **zero**; when it lands there, `> 0` goes red and the guard is retired alongside the corpus it guards. That is the correct end for it, and it happens deliberately rather than by someone noticing. The comment in the test says so explicitly, and warns the next reader off the tempting softening to `>= 0` — which is precisely the vacuous pass the guard exists to catch.

## The ratio swung back, and the two-sided spread is now the settled reading

`create` came in **6 full mad-libs to 7 wedged-token**, against `acquire` 8/5, `build` 7/9, `trade` 7/8, `assist` 6/9 and `lead` 5/9.

Batch 5 called the ratio predictive; batch 6 called its variance real; batch 7 put the spread on both sides of half and concluded the ratio *chooses between two candidate families and does not replace reading a sample*. This batch is the confirmation rather than a new finding: `create` sits almost exactly on half, back on the wedge side, one family after the widest mad-lib-side reading yet. Six families in, the honest summary is that the ratio has no trend — it is a per-family property, cheap to measure and not worth extrapolating.

The cost tracked the split as expected. The six full mad-libs (`brew_potion`, `inscribe_ward`, `compose_saga`, `craft_talisman`, `raise_monument`, and `trial_of_flame` in part) were whole-template rewrites; the seven wedged ones were surgical.

## Two pre-existing defects the rewrite surfaced

**A mangled `{s}` suffix that had been rendering as a non-word.** `hedge_remedy.gather`'s success line read `{actor} has what {they} need{they}.` — `{they}` written where `{s}` was meant, so a gendered render produced *"she has what she needshe"*. This is not the `{they}`-plus-bare-verb class batches 3–7 met, where the token is right and the grammar is unreachable; the token itself is wrong, and the output is not English in either arm. It has shipped since the template was written.

**One instance of the `{they}`-plus-bare-verb class.** `blessing_of_passage.prepare` carried *"from whatever faith `{they}` carry"* — a gendered render reads "she carry", and `{s}` cannot reach a verb inside a subordinate clause (`carry → carrys` is wrong anyway, the ceiling batches 3–5 established). Fixed by dropping the pronoun subject, per the settled rule.

Both were found by the render pass, not by reading source. Seven consecutive runs that pass has paid for itself.

## Vagueness: 9 → 0, and the batch's own contribution was the majority

Nine hits at the strictest applicable scope, **six written by this batch** and three pre-existing — the first batch where the new prose contributed most of them, which is the honest thing to record rather than the flattering one.

The six: two `something` in outcome prose, one `thing`, one `things`, one `way`, and one `something` in *scene* prose — that last one mattering because `something` is in the **evasive** set, enforced at zero in every field class, not the outcome-only natural indefinites. All six rephrased at no cost to the sentence; none required a contortion.

The three pre-existing: `local_tales`'s *"carries something worth more than coin"*, `master_local_craft`'s *"in the one way the tradition warns against"*, and `hedge_remedy`'s *"says nothing"*. All three fixed, all three cheap.

**Zero remaining, and zero `rather than`.** Batches 4–7 each left a tail of the comparative conjunction tripping the intensifier substring, correctly ruling that rewriting a correct comparative to dodge a warn-level match is the damage THR-899 exists to prevent. This batch simply did not reach for the construction, so there is no tail to rule on. Second-person and nominalised-placeholder sweeps were clean on the first pass.
