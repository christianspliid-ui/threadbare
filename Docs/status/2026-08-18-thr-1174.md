# THR-1174 — a company can be sold, and now it can end that way

**Shipped 2026-08-18.** `DissolutionReason` declared `'betrayal'`, `selectPartingVariant`
consumed it and mapped it to the bitter register, and nothing produced it: `dissolveGroup`
has one caller and the `reason` reaching it was assigned in exactly four places —
`undersize`, `leader_death`, `cohesion_floor`, `goal_complete`. So a company could end for
four reasons and "one of us sold us" was not among them. The union member, its consumer
branch and the design intent behind it were all live text describing an unreachable state.

**It survived because a green test sat on the dead branch.** `groupParting.test.ts` asserted
`selectPartingVariant('betrayal', 0.9) === 'bitter'` by **passing the literal in itself** —
verifying the mapping and proving nothing about whether the state was attainable. That is
the standing *fixture invents both sides* shape, and its effect is precise: it stops the
next reader questioning the branch.

**The ticket offered produce-it or retire-it; produce-it won on a shipped dependency.**
THR-733's fourth subject, The Quiet Offer, merged the day before (`ce92fc5e`) and its worst
band mints exactly the missing input — a `hidden_mark` of category `betrayal`, at
`COMPANY_SOLD_SEVERITY 0.65`, taking **no `targetAgentId`** so it lands on the member who
took the coin rather than on a witness. Retiring would have thrown away a mechanic whose
content half was already on `main`.

## The trigger, and why it is gated on cohesion

`findCompanyBetrayer` (exported from `groupDissolution.ts`) fires when a **current** member
carries a `betrayal` mark at or above the new `GROUP_BETRAYAL_SEVERITY_FLOOR` **and** the
company is already below `GROUP_FRAY_THRESHOLD`. The second condition is the load-bearing
one. The mark is concealed by construction — nobody in the fiction knows it exists — so
letting it dissolve a company that is still holding would have the engine act on knowledge
no character has. What it does instead is decide *how a company that was already coming
apart ends*: not "the bond wore out" but "one of us sold us".

It sits **ahead of `cohesion_floor`** in the trigger order, and that placement is what makes
the reason reachable in practice rather than only in code. A company rotten enough to end is
almost always also below the floor, so of the two true statements the more specific one has
to answer first or it never answers at all.

`GROUP_BETRAYAL_SEVERITY_FLOOR = 0.35` tunes two things at once. It sits under what the
encounter mints, so a fresh sale qualifies; and because marks decay at `MARK_DECAY_PER_TICK`
once the 20-tick grace closes, it also sets how long a sale stays lethal — from `0.65` that
is roughly **50 ticks**, a little over four days. After that the company has outlived what
was done to it, which is the more interesting outcome to have available.

## The parting is told in its own register

`betrayal` folding into `bitter` was defensible only while nothing produced it. The bitter
pool is about a bond wearing through — *"old grievances given their say at last"* — and
putting that over a scene where the grievance was **never said** is a prose lie about the
state that produced it. New `GROUP_PARTING_BETRAYED` pool and a third `PartingVariant`: five
lines whose through-line is the *not knowing*, since the company comes apart around a fact
it does not have. Nobody is named, nobody is accused, and the suspicion outlives the company.

UI needed no change and was verified rather than assumed: the debug Companies tab already
renders `dissolutionReason` as a raw string, and `BAND_ACCENT` is documented fail-soft
(unknown band → falls through to sphere/gold) — `bitter` and `bittersweet` are not in that
map either, so `betrayed` behaves exactly as its two siblings already do.

## The tests drive the trigger, never the mapping

Eight new rows in `groupLifecycle.test.ts`, every one of them going through `runGroupUpkeep`
and reading the reason off its result. None hands `'betrayal'` to anything — that is the
defect, not the test strategy. **Falsified before trusted:** disabling the branch reds the
three positive-reachability rows by name; the negative rows (severity floor, wrong category,
former member, still-holding company) stay green by design, because they assert absence.
Severity is pinned with **literals** — `0.65` qualifies, `0.2` does not — plus a separate
range guard on the constant, so the fixtures cannot become tautologies against a retune.

Two comments that had recorded the old reality as settled fact are corrected in place rather
than deleted: `company-drama.ts`'s header and the `companyDrama.test.ts` row that had "that
DissolutionReason has no producer" **in its title**. That row still holds, for a better
reason — the ending is the engine's to decide from cohesion, not the template's to assert.

One new interface-map contract, `a-concealed-sale-ends-the-company-that-was-sold`
(Encounters → Companies), classifying 🟢 LIVE. Its mechanism is declared as the
`hiddenMarks` state array rather than the `hidden_mark` effect kind — the authoring
vocabulary is not the thing that crosses the boundary (impediment #206's rule, the same trap
the sibling `reunion-reads-the-edges-not-the-roster` row records).

**Note for the next reader:** the ticket named `evaluateGroupDissolution` as the site. No
such function exists — the trigger block lives in `runGroupUpkeep` (impediment #675).
