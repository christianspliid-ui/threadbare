## What shipped

`src/engine/decisionBoard.ts` ranks every candidate an agent has, of every family, on one
currency — **expected value per tick** — and records what it would have chosen without changing
what happens. `UNIFIED_DECISION_BOARD_MODE` ships `'shadow'`: the legacy contests still decide.

Until now the cross-family comparison an agent's decision turns on was **never traced**. Nothing
anywhere recorded what the losing family's best score was, and the two scorers being compared were
incommensurate by construction — an encounter score unbounded above, a strategic score clamped into
`[0.08, 0.851]` by a single bridge constant. There was no baseline to flip against. There is one now.

Two channels: the `decision_board_comparison` trace (full ranking, per decision) and three fields on
the balance event that already fires on every decision path, rolled up into a `balance summary`
block that prints each gated share next to its criterion.

## The gate does not pass, and that is the finding

The plan makes the cutover conditional on a measured decision mix over two seeds. Measured:

| | seed 42 | seed 99 | criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| decisions scored | 1913 | 1809 | — |
| undertaking share | **11.9%** ✅ | **4.1%** ❌ | `[0.10, 0.35]` |
| encounter share | 70.7% ✅ | 82.5% ✅ | ≥ 0.15 |
| idle share | 17.5% ✅ | 13.4% ✅ | ≤ 0.40 |
| agreement with legacy | 50.6% | 41.9% | reported, not gated |

So the mode stays `'shadow'` and slice 6 does not flip it. Per the plan, shipping the gate-fail
evidence is a valid completion of that item.

**Why it fails, measured rather than guessed.** In the common currency an undertaking is genuinely
worth 3.3–3.8× less per tick than an encounter (median EVT 0.0096 vs 0.0318 on seed 42; 0.0090 vs
0.0342 on seed 99) — eighteen-plus ticks of work for one payoff unit against a few ticks for an
encounter reward. The legacy bridge was hiding that behind its clamp. The temperament mix cannot
close the gap: it is already sitting at 1.4 of a 1.5 maximum. The lever slice 6 needs is
`UNDERTAKING_PAYOFF_SCALE`, and it was deliberately **not** turned here — tuning a constant until
the gate agrees, in the same change that builds the gate, is how a measured gate becomes a
decoration.

## Three things worth carrying forward

**A plan bracket had a vacuous reading, and the obvious one was it.** "[an active ambition names
this kind]" is true of every strategic candidate *by construction* — candidate generation only ever
walks an active ambition's own `templateIds` — so that weight would have been a constant `+0.3`
tuning nothing, and every unit test built on a fixture would have agreed with it. Implemented as
`preferredVerbs` membership instead, which genuinely discriminates (several ambitions omit `change`
or `destroy`). The guard is a test that walks the *shipped* ambition data and asserts the predicate
is neither always-true nor always-false.

**`successProbability` is the obvious source for P(advance) and it is wrong by ~5 points, always in
the same direction.** `near_miss` is a *succeeding* roll that the checkpoint band map sends to a
halt. Since the EVT squares this number, that is not rounding. The forecast enumerates all 100 rolls
through the same four functions the dice use, and the contract test drives the real `resolveStepCore`
over every roll and requires an exact match.

**The forecast is flat over the range you would naturally test it on.** Capability 0.2–0.8 returns a
constant 0.14, because below ~0.65 the regional scale floor decides the band instead of the roll.
Real agents sit at 0.79–1.00 (measured over 384 checkpoints), where it is monotone and spread
0.21–0.48. A test written on the natural-looking sweep would have been right about the numbers and
wrong about the system.
