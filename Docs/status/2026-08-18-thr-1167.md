# THR-1167 — the residue THR-1049 named, and the two pieces that were not residue

**Shipped 2026-08-18.** THR-1049 deleted the five named prototype surfaces under `src/components/Game/Encounter/` and filed what its census found but its scope did not cover. This pass answered the per-module fork — **displaced** (delete with its tests) or **built ahead of its wiring** (file a ticket naming the producer) — and the two answers did not fall the same way.

## The census, re-run at pickup rather than trusted

The ticket said its counts would rot and to re-derive them. Re-running the predicate mattered twice.

**First, the string trap.** A `grep -rn AscendantHand src` returns eight non-test files and reads like a live component. Every one of them is the *engine* concept — `phaseAscendantHandFilter`, `handFilter`, `encounter-contract-adapter`. Grepping the **import path** instead returns nothing: the component has zero importers, and the live UI for the hand is `NudgePhaseShell`'s cards. The filing had this right; a lazier check at pickup would have reversed it.

**Second, two members the filing did not name.** `AFTERMATH_ANIMATION_FADE_IN_MS` is dead on the same predicate as its named sibling `_SETTLE_MS` — the pair is one retired animation spec, and retiring one while leaving the other would have left the next census a single orphan with no context. And `__snapshots__/` still held `CapabilityStrip.test.tsx.snap` and `EiraHeroPanel.test.tsx.snap`, whose test files THR-1049 deleted: pure residue, invisible to an importer census because a snapshot has no importers.

Each went with its test file and snapshot in the same commit, which is the pathology THR-1049 was cleaning up: `AscendantHand` was a green snapshot suite on a component nothing mounted.

**One test in the deleted tree was deliberately kept.** `DetailPage/__tests__/TypedDetailPages.test.tsx` lives beside `openDetailPage.ts` but imports none of it — it exercises `DetailModal`, `generateDetailPage` and the stack contexts, all live. Directory adjacency is not a dependency; deleting it would have removed real coverage of the live detail-page pipeline.

**The styleguide removal is the one player-visible half**, and leaving it would have been the worse state: `?view=styleguide` was demoing a *non-shipping* choice card while the live one in `EncounterVeil.tsx` had diverged from it — THR-1048 had just given the live card banded stance words the prototype never had. A living contract showing a dead component is worse than one section shorter.

## Built ahead of its wiring — kept, and filed as THR-1168

Two audio moments came back the other way, and the deciding evidence is the same shape in both: the *UI* consumer was a prototype, but the implementation underneath is authored, complete and independently tested.

- **`useThreadReveal.ts`** has **zero** production value-consumers. Its only non-test importer was `ThreadOverlay.tsx`, and that import was `import type` — a type-only edge, which an importer count reports identically to a real one. It is also the **sole** caller of `beginTensionReveal` / `endTensionReveal` / `playResolveNote`, so deleting it would have silently orphaned three more audio functions. It carries an authored THR-346 beat clock (60 + 380 + 520 + 420 + 240 ≈ 1.62s).
- **`playRegistrationCue`** lost its caller to THR-1049. Kept as a cue-*library* API: `encounterSoundDesign.test.ts` asserts its Web Audio synthesis directly across four kinds, so its coverage proves something true for any future caller — materially unlike a snapshot on an unmounted component.

Both are recorded in-code as `TODO(THR-1168)` naming their producer (`EncounterVeil` step-commit; the consequence-chip block), so the next census does not re-litigate them from zero. THR-1168 states plainly that whether the veil *should* carry a 1.6s tension reveal is a feel question and Christian's fork — the mechanical half (where it attaches, what it reads) is settled there.

## The gate this pass added, and why

Retiring a styleguide section means editing a nav registry and a `<section>` ~1000 lines apart. Delete one and not the other and the nav keeps a dead anchor that scrolls nowhere — a broken page rather than a removed demo, and nothing was checking it. `styleguideSync.test.ts` is scoped to `src/components/shared/` primitives (Law 29), and `EncounterChoiceCard` never lived there, so this edit passed through it untouched in both directions.

Added: nav ↔ section sync in both directions, plus a **vacuity guard first** — if either matcher stops parsing the file, both assertions go green against empty sets and prove nothing. **Falsified for real, not just by its negative-control case:** injecting a dead nav entry into `StyleGuide.tsx` turns the suite red on the named assertion, and it returns green on revert. 29 nav ids, 29 section ids, in sync.
