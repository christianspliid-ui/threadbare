# THR-1387 — a mortal's hands are finite: the per-mortal undertaking cap

**2026-09-02 · engine.** `UNDERTAKING_MAX_ACTIVE_PER_ACTOR = 3`. A mortal already carrying three active undertakings is offered no fourth: candidate generation refuses every template with `active_cap:N` in the rejection list, the board never sees an undertaking for them, and their encounters keep flowing because nothing marks them busy. The census gate that THR-1349 left as *reported, never gated* is now a gate — `active undertakings per mortal ≤ 3` on the end-of-run concurrency histogram — and it is green on both seeds.

## Why at generation, not at the busy-gate

The cap sits in `generateStrategicCandidates`, before the template walk, as one `activeCount` read of the runtime's project list. Two designs were rejected. Gating at `phaseAgentDecision` would have marked the mortal as unavailable and starved their *encounter* decisions too — the opposite of what a "hands full" rule should mean. Gating at `startUndertaking` would let the board rank and pick an undertaking and then silently drop it, which costs a decision every time and leaves no trace of why. Refusing at proposal means the rejection is inspectable in the same list as every other refusal (`strategic_candidate_board` trace, `encounters` CLI block), and the board's pick is always startable.

Both are the cap doing its job — the mortals it bites are exactly the ones whose fourth and fifth undertakings were repeats — and neither crosses a gate, but the seed-99 margin is the number to watch if a later change lowers starts again.

## What else shipped

- A unit test in `strategicActionCandidates.test.ts`: a mortal at the cap gets zero candidates and only `active_cap:3` rejections; one below the cap is offered work; completed and failed projects do not count as hands in use. Falsified once by raising the fixture's count.
- The essence-control wiki page carries the rule in one sentence, beside the THR-1349 paragraph it extends.
- The census label reads `concurrency (cap 3, THR-1387)` so the next reader sees the ceiling next to the histogram.

## The saturation observation, re-read under the cap

THR-1349 recorded that the live board emitted zero `saturation_tick` traces in 120 ticks where contest B emitted 38, because the undertakings that write saturation start later and less often, and the inventory badge for Spheres & Quintessence flipped to 🟠 DORMANT. The expectation going in was that a cap, lowering starts further, would leave it there. **It did the opposite:** regenerating `Docs/canon/systems-inventory.md` for the cap commit flipped the badge back to 🟢 ACTIVE on the same standard 120-tick seed-42 run (26 subsystems, 1 dormant, down from 2). The plausible reading is that the cap frees the busiest mortals' slots from their repeats, so a saturation-writing undertaking gets started early enough to be seen in the window — but that is a reading, not a measurement; nothing in this ticket counted the hits. What the two flips together do establish: the badge is a single-hit threshold on one seed and one window, and this subsystem sits close enough to it that a decision-mix change moves it either way. Treat it as a weather reading, not a health verdict, until a second saturation writer exists.
