# THR-1071 — showing mercy made an agent more ruthless

**Shipped 2026-08-11.** Project: Encounter Experience. Pillars: Content + Engine (test). No UI pillar — `FormativeTestBeat` is untouched, and the ticket's Done-when specifies content-pillar acceptance via a headless sweep.

## What was wrong

`AxiologicalProfile` is signed: `+1` is the **first-named pole** of a `ValuePair`, `-1` the second. Four places say so and agree — `src/types/agent.ts`, `ARCHETYPE_NAMES`, `poleLean.ts`, `meetingEncounter.ts`. Nothing negates between authoring and the profile write.

A converted dilemma writes that profile down **two independent paths**: the legacy choice scene, through the authored `choices[].axiologicalShifts`; and the formative test, through `resolveFormativeTest`, which writes `+magnitude` for pole `'a'` and renders the `*A` band prose. Nothing checked either path against the convention, or against the other.

`AX-IRON-01` choice `a` — *"kneels in the dirt beside the boy … holds the cup to his lips"*, `gateTags: ['heroic_origin', 'mercy_shown']`, `traitSeeds: ['unyielding_compassion']` — wrote `{ 'mercy_ruthlessness': -0.3 }`. That moves the profile toward **Conqueror**. Showing mercy made an agent more ruthless, and it did so across seven of the eight reaches.

## The finding that changed the fix: it was 40, not 37

The ticket measured "37 of 40 converted templates carry a negative shift on choice `a`" and called the other three clean. That predicate is a proxy for the defect, not the defect, and it was wrong in both directions:

- It **counted the 5 stone templates as broken**. They are not. `preservation_transformation` was renamed from `humility_pride` at import, which inverted the pole *names* — humility is the transforming side, pride the preserving one — so on stone the rename and the sign inversion cancel and the authored shifts land right. The library's own header said this; the audit confirmed it independently, per template, rather than taking it on faith.
- It **scored `AX-IRON-05`, `AX-SHADOW-02` and `AX-SHADOW-04` clean when they are as inverted as the rest.** They narrate the second pole first, so choice `a` legitimately carries a negative shift — the proxy could not see past choice order. `AX-IRON-05` choice `a` performs an execution and wrote `+0.2` on `mercy_ruthlessness`.

So the real partition is **35 + 5 = 40**, disjoint, and the two halves need **opposite** remedies. This is what the recorded decision anticipated in asking for a per-template audit rather than a uniform flip.

| set | defect | remedy |
|---|---|---|
| 35 non-stone | authored shift signs inverted | negate both choices' shift |
| 5 stone | `test` pole letters inverted | swap `poleLean` a↔b, `cleanA`↔`cleanB`, `brokenIntoA`↔`brokenIntoB` |

The stone half is why a uniform flip would have been worse than doing nothing: it would have broken the one reach that was correct, while leaving its cross-path disagreement in place.

## Why nothing else in the library shares the defect

Measured, not assumed. Only **45** templates carry `axiologicalShifts` at all, and **40 of them are exactly the converted set**. There are **zero** templates with shifts but no `test`, so no unconverted legacy-only population is still writing backwards, and no non-axiological template carries a `test`. `reach_specific`, `domain_specific` and `general` — 122 templates — carry no shifts whatsoever, which also retires THR-1064's parting concern about `RC-STONE-01…05`: they have neither a shift nor a target pair.

The remaining 5 axiological templates are `AX-META-01…05`, the `courage_prudence` meta pair. They carry neither a shift nor a test, sit outside Batch A's membership predicate by construction, and are unconverted content pending THR-875's later batches — expected state, not a defect, and deliberately not filed as one per the 2026-08-10 process-work throttle.

## The guard

`src/engine/__tests__/axiologicalPolarity.test.ts`, 202 assertions. Its absence is what let 40 templates ship.

It rests on one hand-recorded table — `FIRST_POLE_CHOICE_INDEX`, which choice expresses the first-named pole — because that is a reading of the prose and no derivation can establish it. Deriving it from the shift signs would make the file circular. Each row was read off the choice text, its `gateTags` and its `traitSeeds`, which agree in every case.

Everything else is derived. The cross-path assertion matches a band's prose to the choice it narrates by content-word overlap: a clean band re-narrates its choice's concrete nouns, and across all 40 templates the correct choice wins by **≥35% relative margin**. A second hand table there would only have been the file asserting against itself.

**One deliberate bound, and it is a property of the content rather than a gap in the guard.** The `brokenInto*` bands are *not* matchable this way, scoring 26/40 and 20/40 at times on a zero margin — because they narrate a *crossing*, opening on one pole's setup and ending at the other (*"the three apprentices assembled … on the mountain road within the hour"*), so they carry both choices' vocabulary by construction. Asserting the clean pair is sufficient: `selectFormativeProse` keys all four keys off the same `writtenPole`, so a binding that flips flips them together. A distinctness assertion covers the half-applied case.

## Falsified, not merely green

The guard was run against the pre-fix library, and fails on **40 distinct templates with zero overlap** — 35 on the legacy-path assertion, 5 on the formative-path assertion, and the 5 are exactly `AX-STONE-01…05`. That is the ticket's two defects separating cleanly along the predicted seam, and it is the evidence that the partition above is real rather than a story told over a passing test.
