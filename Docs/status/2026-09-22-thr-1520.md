## What shipped

**A mortal is never dealt what they already hold.** `assembleRewardPool` filtered per-bearer only for companions; a possession, condition or bestowed power could be dealt to the same mortal twice. It now populates `ContentQuery.exclude` — a field that existed since THR-1487 with no caller — from everything the recipient's `possesses` / `has_trait` edges point at, with the template id recovered from `instantiateReward`'s instance form (`reward_<bearer>_<tick>_<template>`; the round-trip is pinned, so a change to the form fails a test rather than silently ending dedup). A bearer-less assembly deduplicates nothing, because there is nobody to deduplicate against.

**The leak the falsifying arm found.** A cloned prize wears its template's `type` and tags, and the world view scans by type — so the pool offered every mortal's prize back as a candidate, and after dedup by template id would still have dealt a mortal a clone of their own clone by that route. The carve now skips nodes stamped `properties.source === 'encounter_reward'` (the constant moved to `types/attachments.ts` so the resolver can read it without importing the pool). Pre-fix arm: the frozen by-type scan returned the clone; post-fix: it is returned to no one; strip the stamp and it comes back.

**A bearer-trait term, judged where the bearer is.** `ContentQuery.requiresBearerTrait?: TraitPredicate` (and the same field on `RewardPoolRecipe`, projected through `toContentQuery`) says *only a bearer holding this trait may be dealt from the pool*. The resolver never reads it — it is pure and bearer-blind, and returns identical hits with and without the term — and `contentQueryAdmitsBearer` (`src/engine/contentQueryBearer.ts`) judges it at the call site through `resolveTraitPredicate`, the engine's one trait gate (THR-786), so tag refs, display names and `minLevel` behave as every other predicate does. No bearer to judge means unmet: nothing offered rather than offered wrongly, the companion rule. The bad-outcome flip drops the term along with the tag filter — a mortal who lacks the trait is not thereby spared the wound.

**The trait catalogs are content the model can see.** The six trait content files were not registered catalogs, so `check:attachment` and the tag contract never saw them. Now: a new content kind **Trait** (`trait_template`, game word *Trait*, `instantiatesAs: 'trait'`, gate `check:attachment`) claims the five mortal-trait files under `trait.core.` / `trait.personality.` / `trait.mastery.` / `trait.reputation.` / `trait.cultural.`; the condition-trait file and the economic file's two scars and one condition join the existing **Condition** kind under `trait.condition.` / `trait.scar.` — the same nodes the graph carve has always returned for that kind. The economic file is shared between the two kinds and partitioned by prefix, pinned the way the encounter/action split of the unified array is. The resolver serves the new kind (`{ kind: 'trait_template', tags: ['#mastery'] }` is a query, not an id list).

**Forty-five spellings seated, two retired.** Registering the files put every trait tag under the closed vocabulary. `#condition` / `#location` were the ticket's two; `#place` has no bearer in the corpus and was not seated (a seated tag with no bearer fails `contentTags.test.ts` by the test's own rule, and the ticket's Done-when is that test green). The rest — the five category words, the two pole words, the five Core continua, twelve walk-of-life words and the twenty reputation words — are seated on the `family` axis rather than stripped: this is a designed vocabulary (exactly one positive and one negative word per reach for reputations), every spelling has a bearer on the day it lands, and stripping the reputation words would leave *Iron, positive* and *Iron, negative* indistinguishable to a query. `#general` (one bearer, no meaning) and `#power` (one bearer, a kind's word) were retired from their entries instead. The two economic scars gained `#negative`, which the Condition kind's polarity requirement demands and which a scar is. **Flagged for veto** on the ticket: seating at this scale is a design-session call by the vocabulary's own header, taken here because the alternative was a ratchet the ticket forbids.

**Inspectable.** `content.query_resolved` / `content.query_empty` carry `excludedCount` (candidates dedup removed — an empty pool with a non-zero count is a bearer who holds everything, a different fix from a filter that names nothing) and the traced query carries the `exclude` list and the trait term. `window.__DEBUG.queryContent(query, bearer?)` and the CLI's `query <json> --bearer <agent|@hero>` take a bearer: what they hold becomes the exclude list, `excludedIds` names what it removed, `bearerAdmitted` is the term's verdict.

## Recorded, not built

- **Instances of other writers.** Only `instantiateReward`'s stamp is read. Freeholds are `type: 'artifact'` tagged `#holding` (`holdings.ts`) and are still by-type candidates of `item_template`; a possession draw could in principle deal a clone of a freehold. Not this ticket's leak; noted for the world-objects owner.
- **A codex shelf for traits.** `trait_template` routes to no sheet — the codex has no category reading `mastery` / `reputation` / `core` / `personality` / `cultural`. The surface row's note records it; chartering a shelf is a design decision.
- **Agreement dedup.** Agreements are edge-shaped and keep their catalog filter; a held agreement template is not excluded. Same shape as the companion rule, a later slice if wanted.
