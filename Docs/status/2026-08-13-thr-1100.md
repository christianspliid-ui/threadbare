# THR-1100 — the third row of the tier-advancement ramp finally reaches the player

**Shipped 2026-08-13.** Project: Content Architecture. Engine pillar; Content and UI both genuinely N/A.

**The gap.** `attachment-tier-content.ts` authors three parallel ladders indexed by the tier an artifact advances *from* — essence
cost, difficulty, and duration. THR-996 imported the constants instead of hard-coding literals; THR-1073 gave cost and difficulty
their consumers and deliberately scoped duration out. So `TIER_ADVANCEMENT_DURATION[2..3]` sat unconsumed and a Mythic→Legendary
rite took the same **2–3 ticks** a Mundane→Storied one did. The authored intent — later tiers take longer — never reached the game.

**Why it was not a one-liner, and what the seam turned out to be.** Cost and difficulty each had a site that already knew the
target: `preparePlayerCast` for the price, the resolver's `effectiveDifficulty` for the roll. Duration is drawn in
`computeStepDuration`, called from `createUnifiedAction`, which received the template and a target **id** and had nothing to
resolve it against. The ticket sketched two directions — pre-resolve a range at the call site, or thread a whole `WorldGraph`
through the constructor. **Neither was taken.** What the draw needs is not a graph but the *target's property bag*, which is
exactly what the cost and difficulty seams already read (`graph.getNode(targetId)?.properties`). Passing that keeps the lifecycle
free of graph traversal — it stays a module of pure functions over plain data — while every one of the five production call sites
already holds the node. Blast radius is one optional field, not a new dependency.

**No third marker was minted, and that is the substantive call.** The obvious shape was a `durationContext` to sit beside
`difficultyContext`. Reading the type first showed why not: `ActionStep.difficultyContext`'s doc comment has said
*"`'target_tier_scaled'` — difficulty **and duration** come from the authored per-tier ramp"* since THR-1073. The vocabulary was
never the thing that was missing; **only the duration reader was**. So this closes a doc/behaviour drift rather than widening the
surface a template author has to learn, and it needed **zero edits to `unified-action-templates.ts`** — both `artifact.enchant` and
`artifact.empower` already carried the marker and have been silently under-claiming it ever since. The two numbers also belong at
the same level: `difficulty` and `duration` are per-step, `essenceCost` is per-template, which is why that marker split existed at
all and why this one should not.

**Threaded at both draws, not just the first.** `advanceStep` takes the target properties too. No tier-scaled template is
multi-step today, so this is unreachable — deliberately. The scaling is a property of the *step*, not of being step 0, and the
failure mode of the narrower fix is silent: a later multi-step tier-scaled template would draw the tier-1 range from step 1 onward
and nothing would say so. Fail-soft in the other direction as well (NFP #4): a caller with no graph in reach omits the field and
`sourceTierOf` clamps to the ramp's first entry — precisely today's behaviour, never a throw or a `NaN` duration on the offer path.
No tuning number moved out of `attachment-tier-content.ts`, per the ticket's third Done-when.

**Evidence.** New `targetTierScalingDuration.test.ts`, 10 assertions across the helper, the wiring, and the opt-in boundary.
The wiring tests assert **band membership rather than a pinned draw** — the authored ranges (2–3 / 3–4 / 4–6) do not overlap, so a
bound holds under any seed and these do not decay into snapshots the next time the RNG stride moves. One test guards against a
vacuous pass by requiring the catalog to actually hold unmarked steps before looping over them. Falsified by neutering the helper
to return the authored range: **7 of 10 red**, including the Done-when's own `expected 2 to be greater than 2`.

1008 files / **16,033 tests green**, ratchet unchanged at **3447**, build clean. Engine smoke owed and run — `tick 30` reached tick 30
with 383 agents, 45 events and a populated outcome distribution. **No browser evidence owed and none claimed**: the diff is
`src/engine/` + `src/types/` + one test, which fires no UI-pillar path trigger, and duration already rendered from whatever the
action carried.
