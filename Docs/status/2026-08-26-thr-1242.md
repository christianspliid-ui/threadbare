## THR-1242 — Effect activation 4: one capability, one spelling

Stage 4 of the six-stage effect-vocabulary activation program
(`Docs/plans/2026-08-25-effect-vocabulary-activation.md`). Stages 1–3 gave the
effect system its executors, its persistence, and its readers. This is the stage
that takes vocabulary *away* — and it is the one where the deadness was pointing
at authors rather than hiding from them.

**Nine spellings left the `AttachmentEffect` union.** Three (`graph_mutation`,
`outcome_shift`, `auto_succeed`) had no content and no executor. The other six
are the interesting half: each was a second spelling of a mechanism that was
already live under a different name, and **in every case the spelling shipped
content used was the dead one** — 13 artifacts authored `haste`/`slow`/
`freeze_duration` while `modify_rules` sat beside them with a working executor,
5 authored `create_barrier` beside `alter_terrain`, 3 authored `reroll` beside
`test_shaper` (one item carrying both). The union was not merely oversized; it
was actively steering authors toward the arms that did nothing. All 22 content
refs migrated onto the live mechanism, and one new rule key
(`duration_decay_multiplier`) and one new terrain overlay (`warded`) were minted
because the freeze and the movement-barrier had no honest destination otherwise.

**Three primitives were wired rather than migrated, each a different shape of
dead.** `suppress` was the inverse of the usual case — a *consumer with no
producer*: four readers have honoured `EffectRuntimeState.suppressed` since the
primitive architecture landed and nothing ever set it, so four artifacts promised
to silence magic and silenced nothing. `reveal` had 17 content refs and no
consumer of any kind. `tag_immunity` had a complete query with zero callers
**and** a namespace mismatch that would have made wiring it alone insufficient:
condition trait nodes carry `#`-prefixed tags while most immunity content wrote
them bare, so `fear` could never have matched `#fear` — a ward that runs its
check and blocks nothing, which is worse than dead because the trace shows the
comparison happening. Content is migrated to the `#` spelling, comparison
normalizes both sides, and the namespace is now a ratified UL term.

**The `create_barrier` migration forced the overlay store's first production
reader.** Stage 2 built `GameState.activeTerrainOverlays` and stage 3 wired the
rule keys, but nothing in production ever *read* an overlay. Migrating five
artifacts onto `alter_terrain` without that would have moved them from a dead
spelling onto a dead mechanism — persisted, traced, and still changing nothing a
player could feel. `movementCost` now taxes a `warded` hex and
`encounterAwareness` narrows sight on a `shrouded` one.

**Two content guards became enforced rather than advisory.**
`MAX_EFFECTS_PER_ATTACHMENT` was declared as 6 with a CMS row and no enforcement,
while `effectWalker` privately obeyed its own `MAX_EFFECTS_PER_NODE = 12` — the
duplicate-spelling pathology one layer down, with the untunable number winning.
The walker now obeys the declared constant, raised 6 → 8 because four shipped
items carry 7 effects and enforcing the declared number would have silently
truncated authored content. `ACTION_TRIGGER_MAX_PER_ATTACHMENT` is enforced at
the same boundary.

**On testing the deletions.** A green typecheck after removing a union member
proves only that nothing referenced the member — never that the capability it
named survived. So the retirement sweeps run against the **real catalogs** rather
than a fixture (the claim is about what ships, so what ships is the input) and
match only a `type: 'x'` position, because a bare substring sweep for `slow` hits
an adjective table in `archetype-content` and would report a false positive
forever. Both new guards were falsified: reverting the walker to its private 12
fails exactly the three content-guard tests, and removing the self-cancel guard
from `applySuppressions` fails exactly the one that names it.

**Still not done, stated rather than implied:** `reveal`'s `agent` and
`attachments` targets are collected by the query and have no consumer — they name
UI inspection surfaces, and 3 of the 17 content refs use them. The wiring guide
and the query's own docstring say so.
