# THR-1186 — Repertoire enters the glossary

**Shipped 2026-08-19.** The companion half of THR-1181, drained in the same run that filed it.

## Why it existed

THR-1181's handoff comment recorded that *Repertoire* had **zero hits across all nine UL shards** while its proposed definition leaned on the word, and offered two routes: define attunement against `[[Nudge]]` and concrete nouns, or file a companion proposal. Both were taken — THR-1181's entry avoids the word entirely, and this is the other half, so the finding was neither silently canonised nor dropped.

The term is not incidental. It names a module (`src/engine/nudgeCardRepertoire.ts`), a type (`RepertoireEntry`), a context (`RepertoireContext`), the builder (`buildRepertoire`), a pricing seam (`repertoireCardCost`) and a liveness report. An undefined term carrying that much weight is the drift the UL exists to prevent.

## The distinction that does the work

**A repertoire is per-run and library-level; a hand is per-step and encounter-level.** `buildRepertoire` answers *which cards does this god hold*; `buildNudgeHand` answers *which of this authored step's cards may be played right now*. They meet at exactly one seam — `repertoireCardCost` quotes a price that `effectiveNudgeCost` then charges — and the module's own header says keeping them separate is what stops a repertoire question being answered with one encounter's data. That is the sentence the entry had to carry, because "the god's cards" reads as either one.

Three more, each read off the implementation rather than the ticket:

- **Access is not unlock, and both must pass.** Access asks whether the sphere identity may touch the card (`full` / `discounted` / `locked`); unlock asks whether the member has been earned (`starting` / `milestone` / `god_trait` / `sphere_attunement`). `memberAccess` runs first — which is the mechanism behind `[[Sphere Attunement]]`'s "depth, not a key", so the two entries now explain each other instead of each asserting it separately.
- **A held card's access is never `locked`**, and the type says so (`HeldCardAccess = Exclude<CardAccess, 'locked'>`) rather than the prose alone.
- **Echo cards bypass access entirely**, the locked check included — which is why they are appended after the loop rather than filtered through it.

## Placement
