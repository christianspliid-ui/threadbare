# 2026-08-29 — THR-1327: the status page stops being a function of how long the last author wrote

`Docs/project-status.md` spliced whole fragment bodies into a 60-line budget, so how many entries the page showed depended on the prose length of whoever shipped last. The ticket recorded `1 of 281 fragment(s) rendered` (2026-08-27) with the *shortest* of that day's eight entries the one that fit. Re-measured on pickup, the same generator reported **`4 of 341`, 49 lines, WARN silent** — nothing changed but the corpus. That swing is the actual defect: the distribution is bimodal (median 48 lines, max 162, a long tail of 1-liners), so the page oscillated between defeated and merely thin while its WARN blamed the last author for a rendering rule.

Fixed by **option 2 of the three the ticket weighed** — the page renders one summary line per fragment (label, link, gist) at a fixed cost of one line each. The calibration call was the executor's to make and is recorded in the module docstring: option 1 (raise the cap) keeps the coupling to authored length and only moves the threshold, and option 3 (shrink the authored norm) fights what the Definition of Done's "Current-Focus narrative" elicits — #858 showed the shortest-in-class entry still missing by 38%. Now **35 of 341 entries index in 58 lines**, the cap buys a stable entry count instead of a lottery, and page overrun is impossible by construction.

**The label is read from the filename, not the body, because the corpus has two shapes.** 241 of 341 fragments lead with a `#` heading; 100 lead with a bold sentence under the older norm. A body-derived label would have been absent for a third of the corpus, so `fragmentLabel` parses the filename — which carries date and ticket by construction and is already the page's sort key — and degrades to the basename for `2026-07-22-marathon.md` rather than throwing. `fragmentGist` handles both bodies: a heading's text (minus the `2026-08-29 — THR-1364:` or older `THR-1358 —` prefix the label already repeats), or a bold lead's first sentence with emphasis stripped, truncated on a word boundary. A trailing `(THR-1335)` / `(THR-1000, 2026-08-06)` is stripped after the sentence cut, since in the bold-lead shape the id sits at the end of the first sentence rather than the end of the line; a parenthetical carrying real content (`(THR-1343, closes THR-1337)`) is deliberately left alone.

**The WARN was re-aimed rather than deleted.** Page overrun can no longer happen, so the old page-level warning had nothing left to say. `MAX_FRAGMENT_LINES = 120` now checks the **newest** fragment only — the entry being authored right now, which is the one an author can still act on — and is set *inside* the measured range on purpose: 6 of 341 fragments exceeded it when this landed, so it flags real outliers rather than sitting above the corpus doing nothing. Verified both ways: silent on today's norm-length entry, and on a 201-line injected probe it fires **while the page holds at 35 entries / 59 lines**, where the old renderer would have collapsed to one.

**Guard falsified, not just passed.** Restoring body-splicing in `assemble` reddens three tests including both new ones; the first attempt to inject that perturbation via `sed` silently failed to apply and the resulting green was discarded rather than cited. The pre-existing cap test was also repaired: its 40 one-line fragments no longer exceed an index budget, so its `dropped.length > 0` premise had gone vacuous — it now builds `MAX_PAGE_LINES * 3` fragments so the held-back set is non-empty by construction. `check:generated-freshness` unaffected: `Docs/project-status.md` is untracked and registered as an EXTERNAL_GENERATED_ARTIFACTS member.
