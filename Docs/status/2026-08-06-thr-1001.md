**A guard that cannot see the failure it was built for, because the failure removes its witness (THR-1001, 2026-08-06).** `check:task-heartbeat` flags a lane as stalled only when a **sibling** of equal-or-tighter cadence kept firing — the clause that stops a powered-off machine reading as a fleet of broken lanes. It follows that a silence stopping *every* lane at once has no witness and reports `ok`, and the probe's own test asserts this. That is precisely the shape of the 62-hour 2026-08-03→08-05 gap: three hourly lanes, two weekdays, nothing written to `main` or `ops`, no lane self-diagnosing it, found a week later by `weekly-workflow-retro` and reasonably read as an outage. It was a controlled pause on token/usage limits, and Christian flagged it will recur. **Widening the sibling clause would have destroyed the guard to reach its blind spot**, so this is a second probe, `check:lane-silence`. **It reads commit history, not `lastRunAt`, against the ticket's own suggested first cut** — on resume the scheduler fires a catch-up burst that clusters every lane's `lastRunAt` at the wake edge (measured 2026-07-31: a Monday-only weekly task reporting a Friday `lastRunAt` within 36 ms of two hourly lanes), so at the single moment this check matters, every lane looks current and the gap is invisible; commits are stamped when the work happened. **The pause marker is the half that keeps it from crying wolf**: `~/.claude/threadbare-pause.json`, any content, presence is the declaration — outside the repo because setting it must not require an agent, a session or a commit, and a token limit is exactly when none exist. It suppresses both this probe and the per-lane stall verdict. Two guards stop the marker becoming the new silence: an expired `until` covers that window only, and a marker still set while the lanes are demonstrably writing reports `pause-stale` and asks to be deleted. **The ceiling is stated rather than oversold** — this probe runs inside a lane that goes silent too, so detection is retrospective: a repeat surfaces within 6h of the fleet returning rather than at the next weekly retro, and live paging would need a watchdog outside the fleet, deliberately not attempted. Threshold measured, not guessed: 6h against a largest-healthy-gap of 3h00m on real `ops` history. Three live dry-runs re-measured the incident at **61.9h** from a data source the retro never touched.
