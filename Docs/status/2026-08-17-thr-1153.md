# THR-1153 — the chip that named a river the world does not have

**Shipped 2026-08-17.** Director bug report from live play of the deployed Unsafe Bridge
fallback aftermath, the same sitting that produced THR-1152.

## What was wrong

THR-1141 had already "fixed" this chip once. Its sweep found `PATH · THE RIVER CROSSING`
sitting on a band whose one reaction carried `effects: []` — a chip claiming state nothing
wrote — and backed it with a real `intelligence` record. Law 56's letter was then satisfied:
the engine wrote something, so the chip reported something.

The director played it and reported the same chip still broken, for a reason the letter of
the law did not cover:

> we still have a bond that connects to nothing or at least if it connects to anything that
> can not be seen by the player at all, which is a no no.

His ruling moved the bar from **backing** to **anchoring**:

> all chips have to connect to an existing part of the gamestate and any prose and
> explanation of the chip has to refer to that particular game state.

Under that bar the chip fails for a reason no pointer could repair. Its referents — *"the
river crossing"*, *"the ford upstream"* — are landscape fiction. They are not graph objects,
so there is nothing for the prose to name and nothing for a link to reach. Adding a tooltip
would have made an unanchored claim *clickable*, which is worse, not better.

## Fold, and why not bind

The ruling allowed exactly two repairs — **fold** the chip into band prose, or **bind** the
encounter's spawn envelope to hexes that really carry the feature — with the steer to fold
"unless envelope-binding is cheap and the river/crossing feature is queryable from world
state". Those are two conditions, and they split:

- **Queryable: yes.** `hasRiver` is real world state on the hex (`src/types/index.ts`), set
  by a river generation pass.
- **Cheap: no.** The setting envelope is a *location-subtype* vocabulary end to end.
  `SETTING_CLASS_MAP` expands a class to `LocationSubtype[]` and `encounterCache` filters on
  exactly that; there is no terrain or hex-feature axis anywhere in the registration path.
  Binding would mean adding one — a design change, and not this ticket's.

So: fold. The words survive in the band overview, the chip is gone. The template registers at
`wayside`, which expands to `camp | oasis | wilderness`, so it will keep spawning where no
river need exist — the prose can carry a river as scene texture, because prose surfaces never
claim state. A chip cannot.

## What left with it, and what stayed

The `intelligence` record went with the chip. THR-1141 added it *to back this chip*, and it
filed knowledge about the ford — the object that is not in the game state. With the claim
folded, a knowledge record about a fiction has nothing left to support.

The keeper's `hidden_mark` **stayed**, and the distinction is the whole rule in miniature: it
targets `$cast:keeper`, a cast actor the template declares and the world instantiates. That
is an existing graph object. It is also correctly *not* chipped — it is a concealed action,
discoverable later through `investigation` reveal content, so surfacing it on the ending
would give away the thing it exists to hide.

## The ending now renders no chips on a plain success

Worth stating plainly, because it looks like a regression and is not. Bands substitute
`changes` **wholesale** (`override.changes ?? baseChanges`), and this variant authors no
`success` override — so the base face *is* the ending a plain success shows, and it now
carries zero chips. `EncounterVeil` guards the whole consequence block on `length > 0`, so
there is no orphan heading and no empty container: the section simply is not there.

That is the honest Law 56 rendering. The only state that ending writes is deliberately
hidden. An ending that changed nothing visible should show nothing, and the four bands that
*do* write — `critical_success`, `success_at_cost`, `failure`, `critical_failure` — each
author their own chips and are untouched.

## Scope actually delivered, and what remains

This ships the reported defect and the measurement the rest of the sweep needs. It does
**not** ship the corpus-wide anchor gate, and the reason is a number: an inventory across all
683 live templates found **65 chips that name a referent carrying no anchor** — not the 27
from THR-1141's table, and not the 493 the naive count suggests (437 chips declare no named
referent at all, an older shape the rule does not reach). Nineteen of the 65 are `hod.*` chips
all naming *"The Dawn"*.

Two reasons not to sweep them in this pass:

1. **The catalog is another ticket's deliverable.** THR-1154 is chartered to ratify the
   anchoring rule and ship the authoring anchor catalog — *"this ticket supplies the catalog
   its declaration forms must match"*. Sorting 65 chips into anchored/fold/bind before that
   vocabulary exists means inventing it, against CLAUDE.md's standing rule about not
   inventing node types.
2. **A gate is not shippable ahead of its sweep.** Law 56 has a no-exemptions posture, so an
   anchor-resolution gate turns 65 chips red the moment it lands. Gate and sweep have to
   arrive together.

Remaining, carried on the ticket: the 64-chip sort, the anchor-resolution gate, and the
adapter route. The Bridge is fixed and the worklist is now measured rather than remembered.
