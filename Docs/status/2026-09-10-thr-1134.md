## 2026-09-10 — The incident snapshot ships (THR-1134)

Christian plays long runs on the deployed build and periodically sees a world that
looks wrong. Until today the only thing he could send was a screenshot and a
sentence — enough to answer *why is this on screen*, never *why did the simulation
drift like this*. There was no serialization of any kind in the repo, and the whole
diagnostic bridge sits behind `if (import.meta.env.DEV)`, so on `threadbearer.co`
every existing accessor was unreachable.

Settings now carries a **Trouble** section: *Record what happens*, *Include the
whole world*, and *Save a snapshot*. The button writes a `.json` file naming the
build, the seed, the tick, the last thousand things that happened, a per-tick
census going back three hundred ticks, every crash the engine caught with its
stack, what the player had open, and the graph neighbourhood of whatever they were
looking at.

**Two accumulators had been running in production the whole time with no door.**
`tickHealthMonitor` has been called unconditionally every tick since it was
written, keeping a hundred health reports and a hundred crash entries *with
stacks*; `encounterTimeline` appends with no DEV gate at all. Both were producers
whose only consumer was stripped from the build — this map's headline failure
shape. The bundle is their first production reader. `exportDiagnostics` was also
being invoked with no state argument, so its counters block had been `null` on
every export since the bridge existed; it now receives the registered state.

**`JSON.stringify(state)` does not throw — it lies.** On seed 42 / medium at tick
100 it returns 1,661,839 bytes of plausible JSON with the entire graph rendered as
fifty-one characters of `{"nodes":{},…}` and every `Map` on the state flattened to
`{}`. A naive bundle would have looked complete and contained no world. Every
section is built by hand from public getters, and the serializer keeps a manifest
that compares collections walked against collections rewritten — a bundle whose
counts disagree is tagged `serialization.incomplete` rather than shipped silent.

**Measured on the real thing**, large map, tick 140, captured from the button:
the incident tier is 831,173 bytes and the world tier 18,364,921 bytes, with
`walked` and `rewritten` both 14 and `incomplete: false` — including a
`visibilityMap` of 1,728 entries that a naive capture would have lost entirely.
The census ring shows the graph growing 3,921 → 5,720 nodes across those 140
ticks, which is the point: drift reads as a line rather than a guess from one
sample. The flight recorder wrote 18,662 events to keep the newest thousand, with
zero swallowed appends.

The production proof is the one that matters. Against `vite preview` on the built
bundle, with `window.__DEBUG === undefined` asserted in the same pass, the Save
button fired a real browser download — the surface works exactly where the debug
bridge does not exist.

Recording stays off by default and says so: `traceBuffer` evicts with `shift()`
plus a full renumber, which a saturated tick pays per evicted entry, so the toggle
names its cost in words rather than hiding it. Nothing on the surface carries a
numeral — the byte counts live in the file and on the ticket, and the oversize
warning is the phrase *this one may be too big to attach*.
