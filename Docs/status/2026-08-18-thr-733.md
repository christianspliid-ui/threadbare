### THR-733 — company drama, subject 1 of 4: The Gate Held (the sacrifice)

**The scoping fact that shaped this pass, measured rather than assumed.** THR-733 asks for
four intra-company drama encounters. A new `encounter.*` template cannot join
`RETROFIT_PENDING` — the ratchet only ever shrinks — so every one of them must pass the full
Composition Contract on the day it lands. `npm run check:encounter -- --all` on entry read
**`checked 191 · clean 6 · on ratchet 185`**: composition-complete is a standard that exists
**six times in the whole corpus**, all six being the `encounter.slice.*` vertical slice, at
~440–750 lines apiece. Four of those is not one pass at the quality the coordination block
names as the deliverable ("prose quality is the deliverable, not the plumbing"). So this pass
ships **one subject, composition-complete**, and the remaining three are checkpointed on the
ticket rather than delivered thin. The ticket stays In Dev.

**Subject shipped — The Gate Held.** A company withdrawing through a place with one usable
exit; the exit stays open exactly as long as somebody stands in it. One Iron step
("Hold the gate"), a five-card hand spanning force/order/life/darkness plus one common
option, and five authored outcome bands. `{actor}` is the member the engine spotlighted and
the others are present in every sentence he appears in — a company encounter whose prose
reads as one person acting alone is a solo encounter wearing a company's affinity.
`check:encounter` verdict: **clean, systems `cast, rewards, seeds, conditions, reputation`** —
five connections against a quota of three. It is the corpus's seventh composition-complete
template.

**Group-EXCLUSIVE by authoring claim, not by sweep.** `actorAffinities: ['group']` with no
`'individual'`, plus `minGroupMembers: 2`, exactly as the THR-74 party delves do it — "only a
company may take this" is a design claim and is not derivable from a template's shape, so
`withGroupAffinity` must leave it alone. A company of one cannot have a rearguard, which is
the entire subject, and the suite pins that third case explicitly.

**Three failures the gates caught, each a real defect and none of them cosmetic.**

1. **A dead trait ref.** `trait.core.core_resolve.virtue` does not exist;
   `traitRefReconciliation.test.ts` pins the dead-ref set with `toEqual`, so it failed by
   name. Repointed to `trait.core.core_integrity.virtue` — and the factor line was rewritten
   to *mean* integrity ("they hold ground they already said they would hold") rather than
   keeping resolve's words over a different trait's id.
2. **Openings that were data nothing read.** The template authored an `openings` table and
   skipped `compileOpeningEnvelope`, which is what turns it into the `opening` context-fragment
   set the prose layer actually renders. `settingClasses.test.ts` (THR-932) caught it. The
   export now maps through the compiler, matching the slice.
3. **The THR-811 polarity trap, walked into directly.** Registering the template in
   `LOCATION_BRANCHING_ENCOUNTER_TEMPLATES` made it cache-registrable while
   `getAnyEncounterById` could not resolve it — which reads to
   `prerequisiteTemplateResolution.test.ts` as a template *acquiring* exclusivity. The delves
   avoid this by living in `ENCOUNTER_TEMPLATES`; this one is registered in the registry array
   only and reaches agents through the path `unifiedActionPhases` actually uses (the whole
   `UNIFIED_ACTION_TEMPLATES` passed to `generateUnifiedCandidates`), which is the path the
   group gate is enforced on. The reason is recorded at the removal site so the next author
   does not helpfully add it back.

**Law 56 forced an honest chip rather than a convenient one.** The success-at-cost and
critical-failure endings wanted a grief state; the shipped condition vocabulary has six
entries and none of them is grief. A chip may only name a state the engine actually writes,
so the chips claim `wounded` and `terrified` — both genuinely written on those bands — and
the grief stays in the `overview`, where scene facts belong and nothing is claimed of the
engine. The alternative was a chip pointing at `trait.condition.grieving`, which
`check:encounter` rejects by name as a dead pointer rendering as a live link.
