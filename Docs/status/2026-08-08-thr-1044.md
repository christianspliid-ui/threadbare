## THR-1044 — the `encounter.*` family can bind cast (Encounter Factory item 1)

Every nudge-era encounter reached the stage with no cast at all, and not because
nobody had authored one. `withDefaultSupportBundle` keys on the template-id prefix,
the `encounter.*` space had no entry, and **0 of its 191 templates declared a
`supportBundle` of their own** — so the failure was structural, upstream of anything
an author could have written. `{cast:*}` placeholders in that family had nothing to
resolve against by construction.

The prefix lookup was right for the families it was built for: `tg.` is the thieves'
guild, `cg.` the city guard, `ts.` the temple — each prefix names a place with people
in it. `encounter.` names nothing. One flat default for that family would have put a
taproom keeper on a battlefield.

So `encounter.*` resolves its default by **setting class** instead — the THR-884
envelope, which is exactly the axis deciding where a scene happens and therefore who
is standing there. Eight classes, one small bind-only cast each, drawn from the roles
worldgen actually seeds at those subtypes (`LOCATION_ROLE_ROSTERS`) rather than from
the full 56-role vocabulary — a reuse list naming roles no roster places is a spec
that can never bind.

**A template spanning several classes gets nothing.** 131 of the pre-envelope
`encounter.*` templates are drawable at two or more classes and 15 at none; picking
one anyway would stamp them with a cast belonging to a place they are usually not in,
which is the placeless-prose failure the envelope vocabulary was written to end.
Coverage lands at 45 of 191 — every envelope-declaring template plus the 37 that sit
unambiguously in one class — and the other 146 are left exactly as they were.

Bind-only throughout: every spec stays `pre-seeded`, so a default attaches an NPC
already standing at the anchor or stays unresolved. Zero world population added.

One test changed beyond the new ones. `prerequisiteTemplateResolution` asserts the
pool lookup and the unified lookup differ only by the transforms registry assembly
applies, and it named just one of the two — `actorAffinities`. `supportBundle` is the
other, and until now it had never touched a template that also lives in an encounter
pool, so the omission was invisible. It is named now.

No deferrals: the 146 uncovered templates are a stated design decision, not debt.
Narrowing one to a single class with `settings` is the authoring move that covers it.
