# THR-1297 slice 3 — ownership becomes its own edge

**2026-08-27 · Proactive Agent Actions, plan doc 2/6 · slice 3 of 6**

A mortal can now *own* a place, and that fact is written in exactly one module.

## What shipped

**A new `owns` edge, beside `controls` rather than instead of it.** The plan called this
decision out as the one the review demanded be made explicitly, and the inventory is why
it went the way it did: the assumed property firewall inside `controls` does not exist.
Exactly one of ~30 production read sites discriminates by any property at all,
`influence` is written and never read, and widening the edge would have broken seven
faction-territory consumers outright plus five that take `[0]?.source` and would have
become nondeterministic. One of those is `battleAftermath`'s power vacuum, which on a
razing would have deleted an agent's holdings along with the fallen faction's land. The
distinction the two edges now carry is **title versus jurisdiction** — a faction
*controls* the hex its banner flies over, a person *owns* the mill — and both can be true
of the same ground, which is exactly why one edge could not carry both.

**`src/engine/holdings.ts` is the only writer of all three objects** — the world object,
the `owns` edge, and the bearer-side attachment face. `grantHolding` / `transferHolding` /
`releaseHolding` / `razeHolding`, plus a reconcile. The edge is the authority and the face
is bookkeeping, the same doctrine the company roster follows against `member_of`.

**Both un-flagged agent writers migrated.** The great work an agent builds
(`encounterAftermath`'s `spawn_unique_location`, `via: 'creation'`) and the two authored
templates `action.iron.conquer` / `action.shadow.establish-network` — the latter routed
through `grantHolding` from *inside* the generic `add_edge` executor, so content-authored
ownership obeys the single writer too. Without that a content-authored edge would have
carried an empty property bag, violating its own `requiredProperties`, and no face at all:
the place a character had just taken would never have appeared on their sheet.

## Two findings worth recording

**Seize needed a new graph primitive, and the atomicity test is what found that out.**
The first draft removed the incumbent's edge and added the winner's. The test — which
wraps every graph mutator and samples at *every* mutation rather than at the endpoints —
went red immediately: there was an instant with no owner at all. Add-then-remove has the
mirror problem, an instant with two. So the edge is **retargeted** through a new
`WorldGraph.retargetEdgeSource`, written because the obvious `updateEdge` route rewrites
the edge record without touching the `outgoing`/`incoming` adjacency maps and would have
orphaned it silently, with no error. All ~30 existing `updateEdge` callers pass
`properties` only, so nothing depended on that behaviour — but the next caller who needed
a retarget would have discovered it the hard way. Falsified 2-of-18 red against the
two-step implementation, which is the plan's stated kill criterion for this slice.

**The anchor-catalog gate caught the new union members, and then mis-parsed my comment.**
Adding `owns` and `holding` correctly failed `generate-anchor-catalog` until both were
classified — the enforcement working as designed. But `parseUnionMembers` strips *line*
comments and not *block* comments, so a multi-line JSDoc placed inside a union body is
read line-by-line as union members, and the error named my own prose as unclassified
members. Rationale now sits above the union; impediment logged.

## The invited veto, taken

Home-ground scoring on your own holding ships, as the handoff specified it would unless
vetoed. It closes a real gap the inventory found: territory read faction control and
nothing else, so an owner standing on their own land under someone else's banner was
scored as an *enemy* there, and anyone factionless could never be at home anywhere. It
reuses `FACTION_CONTROL_BONUS` rather than minting a second number, overrides a hostile
faction verdict, and names the **owner** on the factor line — "Kael holds this ground."
