# THR-1107 — encounter prose that reads correctly for a he/she agent, and a guard that can see the whole class

**Date:** 2026-08-14 · **Lane:** `tb-opus-pickup` · **Worktree:** `infallible-wozniak-e65e78` · **Issue:** [THR-1107](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1107)

## What shipped

**41 prose lines repaired across 15 encounter templates, and a regression guard that took the corpus from 47 agreement defects to 0.**

`{they}` is an actor-bound pronoun token and `{s}` is the present-tense agreement suffix that must accompany a verb following it. A line authored `{they} stop` renders correctly as *"they stop"* in the they/them arm and ungrammatically as **"she stop"** in the he/she arm. The player reads the broken form. The exemplar case is the one that matters most: `encounter.trial_by_combat` is the THR-1036 worked template that every THR-1101 batch calibrated its register against, and it had been rendering *"she stop"* the whole time.

## The population was 47, not 21 — and the gap is the finding

The ticket enumerated 21 lines. Re-derivation (which the ticket explicitly required over trusting the snapshot) found **47 defect occurrences across ~41 distinct source lines**. The difference is not drift; it is three separate blind spots in the instrument that produced the 21, two of which were already known and one of which was not.

**1. The lowercase anchor** (known — flagged in THR-1101 batch 13's reconciliation comment). A subject match anchored on lowercase `she`/`he` cannot see a sentence-initial capitalised `{They}`. Four lines were invisible for exactly this reason: `seal_the_breach[1].failure` (*"She are thrown clear"*), `mystical_vision_quest` (*"She return with the outline"*), `offer_small_prayer[1].atCost` (*"She were still on her knee"*), `confront_the_unknown[1].critF` (*"She walk out whole"*).

**2. The adverb truncation** (known — same comment). The predicate as written stops at the first word after the subject, and classifies an adverb there as a false positive. That is correct for the adverb and wrong for the sentence, because the bare verb sits one token downstream (*"she actually want"*). The guard now scans past `-ly` and a closed set of temporal adverbs before testing the verb.

**3. The field list** (new). The sweep inherited from THR-1036 enumerates **seven field names**. It therefore never looked at `factorLines[].text`, `traitVariants[].factorLine`, or any part of a nudge card — `fiction`, `effectLine`, `bandProse.*`. All of those are player-facing prose, all of them carried instances of this defect, and together they account for **19 of the 47**. A hand-maintained field list goes vacuous the moment content grows a new prose field; the nudge-card system (THR-885) is exactly such a growth, and it arrived after the list was written. **The guard walks every string on the template recursively instead**, which is the ticket's predicate ("every prose field on a template") read literally and cannot drift.

## `{s}` is not a universal repair — four classes, not one

The ticket's body notes that *to be* / *have* have no in-place fix. Working the corpus found the rule is broader, and getting it wrong trades this defect for a spelling one. `{s}` appends a bare `s`, so it is correct **only** where the third-person singular is base+s:

| class | example in this corpus | `{s}` would render | wanted |
|---|---|---|---|
| sibilant (`-s -x -z -ch -sh`) | `reach`, `finish` | "reachs", "finishs" | reaches, finishes |
| `-y` after a consonant | `carry` | "carrys" | carries |
| `-o` | `go`, `do` | "gos", "dos" | goes, does |
| irregular | `are`, `have`, `were` | "ares" | is, has, was |

Note the last row ends in a vowel and so slips past the morphological test entirely — the guard needs **both** a suffix-shape rule and an irregular set, and a unit test pins that they are disjoint and cover different ground. That test caught its own first draft asserting `are`/`have` against the suffix regex.

Lines in the unsafe classes were repaired by dropping the pronoun subject and naming the actor or the thing (*"The recoil throws {them} clear"*, *"before the scree is crossed"*, *"there is little in the pack"*), which is the remedy THR-1101 batch 13 established for `carry`.

## The guard, and why it should survive contact with someone it inconveniences

The ticket's Done-when asks for a guard that distinguishes genuine hits from the past-tense and adverb false positives, *"or it will be softened by the first person it inconveniences"*. The distinction lives in four named sets, each carrying its own rationale in the file: intervening adverbs, non-verb followers, modals/auxiliaries, and irregular past forms. Widening one is a claim about English, not a way to quiet a failing line.

Written **red first**: 47 defects on the pre-fix tree, 0 after, with the nine classifier unit tests green throughout — so the guard is known to fail, which a guard that never saw red is not. The classifier is pinned in both directions independently of corpus population, so it cannot become vacuous if the corpus later empties.

**One limit is recorded rather than hidden.** Verbs identical in present and past (`put`, `set`, `read`, `cut`, `hit`, `cost`…) are treated as past, so the guard **under-reports** on them by design. Flagging them would report a false positive on every correct past-tense use, which is precisely the pressure that gets a guard softened until it means nothing.

## Reconciliation with the enumerated 21

15 of the 21 survived to this pickup and are fixed here. 5 were already fixed by THR-1101 batches 12 and 13 (`starborn_vigil[2].failure`, `investigate_anomaly[0].failure`, `arcane_resonance_study[1].critF`, `hermits_wisdom[1].scene`, `garrison_gossip[1].success`). 1 — `delve_into_depths[1].success` (*"she go"*) — was already clean at pickup and is not attributable to a named batch. The remaining 26 occurrences are the three blind spots above.

## Out of scope, deliberately: three exports with no production reader

`ENCOUNTER_SYSTEM_CONNECTIONS` carries the same defect (*"{they} carry back"*, *"{They} are no longer"* at `culture_transformation`), as do sibling structures in `CULTURAL_ENCOUNTER_OVERLAYS` and `ENCOUNTER_INSPECTION_VIGNETTES`. All three were checked for consumers: **every importer is a test file** that counts or shape-checks them; no production path renders any of them. They are outside the ticket's predicate (which is scoped to *templates*) and outside the player-facing claim this guard makes, so they are reported here rather than swept — fixing prose no player can reach, while tests assert its shape, is the "green tests on a dead contract" pathology, and the honest ticket is a reachability question, not a grammar one.
