# Current Focus — THR-1475: a condition says what it does

**Shipped 2026-09-12.** Engine · Content · UI.

Director feedback on the [THR-1220](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1220) slice, Snow on the Pass aftermath: *"there is still no information on the tool tips or click throughs on the scars describing their in game effect."*

## The defect was one missing reader

Hovering `SCAR · EXHAUSTED` drew `plainRegisterBody` — the template's `description`, *"Pushed beyond their limits. Everything takes more effort."* That is a mood. The effect was one field away the whole time (`domainContributions: { iron: -0.04, eye: -0.04, stone: -0.04 }`) with **no words-producer reading it**. THR-1122 correctly kept every numeral off the tooltip, and the effect went out with the numbers rather than being restated as words.

`conditionEffectLine(node, grant?)` in `aftermathWords.ts` is that producer, and both surfaces read it through one id-keyed door (`resolveConditionEffectLine`) so the hover and the sheet cannot drift into two readings of one state.

## The ladder is calibrated to what the number does, not to how it looks

`domainContributions` are raw-score terms walked by `computeRawScore`, then put through `sigmoid(midpoint 10, k 0.4)`, whose steepest slope is `k / 4 = 0.1` capability per raw point. So 0.04 moves capability by under half a point in a hundred, and 0.10 by about one. **Every shipped condition sits in 0.04…0.10, so every one reads `slightly`** — and that is the honest word, not a ladder bottoming out. The upper rungs (`somewhat` 0.15, `markedly` 0.40, `far` 1.00) protect the next author: a condition written at −0.5 is a different animal and must not still be called slight. The perturbed test arm is what makes that a gate rather than a comment — without it, `slightly` asserted over the shipped corpus would also pass against a function that returned `slightly` unconditionally.

A magnitude word is **not** this reading's answer to *"how much?"* — the Law 13 amendment of 2026-08-12 rules an adverb out for that. The term is, stated in days via `durationLabel`.

## Three conditions got no line, and that is the finding

`under_watch`, `standing_welcome` and `tended_shrine` have **neither substrate**. The search that proved it was for *any* engine read of the trait id: all three appear only as something content writes and as a chip `stateNoun` that names them. `under_watch`'s own definition claims *"Its reader is the gate (`requiredTargetTraits`)"* — and every shipped `requiredTargetTraits` gate names a `trait.reputation.*` trait, none of these.

The obvious sentence for `under_watch` is *"Quiet work here is likelier to be seen"* and it is **false**. A chip may not promise what the engine cannot enact (director ruling 2026-09-12), and that rule does not loosen when the unenactable promise would have made a gate pass. So they are listed in `CONDITION_IDS_WITHOUT_EFFECT` with their reasons, and [THR-1483](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1483) owns giving them an effect or retiring them. The exemption is **two-sided**: the test also asserts every member genuinely has no substrate, so adding one fails until the id is removed from the list.

## Two defects found by asking the data how long a condition lasts

**`shaken` was permanent.** THR-1472 added the template and its writers but no `CONDITION_DURATIONS` row, and **the fallback is permanence, not a default** — `CONDITION_DEFAULT_DURATION_TICKS` is `0`, and `0` means indefinite (the edge omits `ticksRemaining`, the only field `decayConditions` counts down). `slice.snow_on_the_pass` grants it with no `durationOverride`, so the slice Christian reviewed was handing a mortal a permanent loss of nerve. Fixed: `CONDITION_SHAKEN_DURATION = 18`, mirroring `inspired` deliberately — buoyed nerve and lost nerve are the same register read from either end. A new test arm requires a row for every personal condition, because the silent failure mode is permanence.

**The ellipsis cost a character nobody budgeted.** `plainRegisterBody`'s clipped branch returned `budget + 1`, which is why the shipped corpus measured a 161-character body against a 160-character ceiling. It went unnoticed because the only existing trim test exercises the sentence-boundary branch, and one character over did not yet collide with the Law 18 gate at 200. Composing an effect line into the same budget is what made an exact ceiling load-bearing. Swept now at every budget from 2 to 200.

Also filed: [THR-1484](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1484) — two live condition-grant paths write different field names for the same total (`durationTicks` vs `totalTicks`) against one reader, so a phase-granted condition renders no Duration row.

## The body is the half that gives way

`composeTemplateTooltipBody` reserves the effect line's room out of `ATTACHMENT_TOOLTIP_MAX_TOTAL_DESC` (200, the Law 18 ceiling `tooltipValidation.test.ts` sweeps) and trims the *body* to what is left. The ordering is deliberate: the flavour prose is already on the sheet, and the effect line is the information this ticket exists to deliver — trimming the new thing to preserve the old one would reintroduce the defect quietly, the way THR-1122 lost it the first time. Shipped data does not come near the ceiling (worst case 158 of 200), so the squeeze is tested by calling the composition directly rather than through the corpus, which would be a probe that passes because nothing exercised it.
