### THR-1212 slice 4 — the reachable-consumption ledger (2026-08-28)

**A grep for readers said all three of our known dead writes were healthy. This is the artifact that disagrees.**

`generate-consumption-ledger` emits `Docs/canon/consumption-ledger.generated.md`: all **89** members of the two write vocabularies — 45 aftermath effect kinds, 44 `GraphOp` ops — with what each writes, who reads it, and **what that reader does**. Distinction 2 of the typed-state program epic (acted-on vs merely recorded) stops being a thing people remember and becomes a build step.

**The design is one inversion.** A row never states its verdict. It records its consumers, each tagged `acts` / `spawns` / `tally-point` / `reports`, and `classifyRow` derives `acted-on` / `dormant-hook` / `bookkeeping` / `write-without-consumer` from those tags. An author filling a row in honestly — *"its only reader renders a sentence"* — gets the finding whether or not they were looking for one. That is the only reason the plan's falsification check could mean anything: had the verdict been an authored field, passing it would have proved nothing except that I knew the answer.

**Falsification check — PASS, unprompted.** The criterion was that the ledger's first honest run must flag `followOnTags` on its own or the design goes back for rework. It reported exactly one: `1 write-without-consumer — clearance_gate_tag`. **A reader search could not have found it**, and that is the point — the field *is* read, by `gateFollowOnSentence`, which turns each new tag into a `future_hook` chip reading *"A follow-on thread was seeded"*. What refuses that answer is the settled taxonomy, specifically Christian's ruling that hooks are **not passive** (THR-1161): a dormant hook spawns a real entity carrying firing metadata, and `followOnTags` spawns nothing. It is a promise with no mechanism behind it — three tag conventions rotting in one field, now with a tombstone for slice 6 to cite.

**The guard caught 9 of my own 89 rows.** `assertConsumerSitesResolve` reads every cited file and fails unless the cited symbol is in it; the first run rejected four consumer files or symbols I had written from plausible memory rather than from the tree — including a `has_condition` edge type that exists nowhere in the codebase (conditions ride `has_trait`). That is the guard working on its author, and it is the reason the surviving 80 rows are worth reading. `assertWriteWithoutConsumerIsDeferred` reuses the interface map's LEAKED→Deferral rule verbatim: a dead write may ship while it is being retired, never silently.

**Two argument-level dead writes recorded, and deliberately not classified dead** — flattening a live row to "dead" would be as false as the reverse. `unlock_action`'s `revealStyle` (`'card_flight' | 'silent'`) reaches the trace payload and stops there, so an author choosing `silent` gets a card flight anyway; `attune_artifact`'s `attunedSphere` stamp appears nowhere in `src/` outside its two writers. Both live in the ledger's Notes — the Grateful Kin shape (THR-1175) one level down, which is what the operand column exists to make visible.

**One planned wiring item deliberately deferred rather than forced.** The interface-map badge extension was scheduled to ride this PR, but absorbed ruling 3 defines the new content-claims badge *by pointer to the UL entry* — and no UL entry exists. A badge pointing at nothing is the drift that registry exists to catch, so it lands with the entry instead: **THR-1316** (UL-proposal: `WorldRef` + the three violation classes), filed with its coordination block as first comment.

Both guards falsified before the verdict was trusted (a member with no row fails by name; a dead write with no ticket fails by name), each arm confirmed to have applied first. 21 new tests. Registered in both freshness registries in the same PR that adds the generator — this artifact's claim is *"these 89 writes were checked"*, and a stale copy makes a checked-sounding claim about code that has since moved.

**Remaining on THR-1212:** item 5 (no-op gate contract test on a seeded world) and item 6 (the `followOnTags` retirement, which now has its evidence).
