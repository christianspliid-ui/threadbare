# Current focus — yield and leverage (THR-1439)

**2026-09-08 · shipped.** The fourth and last band of the undertakings wayfinder map ([THR-1396](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1396)), in the order set on THR-1399: readers → dormant kinds → people-things → **yield and leverage**. The band that opens no new subsystem and instead makes the economies the other cells already touch worth touching.

## What shipped

**`use × Location` is the active harvest.** Holding court, taxing a market, drawing a tithe by hand. The lump scales on the town's prosperity band and on how many of its Places actually produce (commerce class, capped at four). It is banked through **`bankWealth`** — the funnel extracted from `payHoldingIncome` in the same PR — so the active harvest and THR-1428's passive tithe stamp **one cause vocabulary** rather than two spellings of the same idea, and the Means tooltip reads the new `draw_yield` cause as *a tithe drawn by their own hand*.

**The costs are paid on every band and the lump only on the good ones.** The town loses prosperity and the holder loses standing *with the town* whether or not the work lands. That is what makes a harvest a risk rather than a button, and it is the band's load-bearing arm: a semantic that paid on every band would pass any test that only ever ran a success, so the test asserts a `failure` band moved prosperity and standing while moving no wealth. An *absent* band takes the failure arm too — the instant path has no checkpoint to read and must never quietly pay a full harvest. A town can be drawn once an income interval, by eligibility cooldown.

**`change:raise × Route`** writes volume onto the lane's `trades_with` edge and restarts its decay clock, because expansion is activity. The toll scales on volume, so raising it raises somebody's income the same interval.

**`control:seize × Agreement` is theft.** `retargetEdgeSource` moves the `knows_secret_of` edge from holder to thief — the id stays, so every reader keyed on it survives — and **the holder loses it, never a copy**, or theft would be free. Motive-gated like every seize: opportunism does not get to rob people, and the generated-world test asserts the refusal before the grievance as well as the offer after it.

**`use × Standing` mints a favour**, gated at Respected rather than at `Accepted` (0.5 — the neutral default every stranger carries, so a stranger owes nobody anything). The standing is spent even when the call goes unanswered, because asking is what costs.

**The Agreement kind now has two classes.** Its shape widened to `edgeTypes: ['knows_secret_of', 'owes_favor']` with a liveness discriminator, one handle per ordered pair and the mark winning. An edge object is held by its source — a mark by its holder, **a favour by the one who owes it** — which is right for `seize` and wrong for `use` and `destroy`, so those two carry `ownershipOverride: 'any'` and the eligibility hooks gate per class: a mark is pressed and exposed by its holder, a favour redeemed and forgiven by its creditor. The favour class therefore gets its **whole life cycle** from this one band, and that closes a gap the rulebook had flagged as honest since 2026-08-03 — `owes_favor` was reachable only through template metadata no standard seed reached, **0 edges through tick 167**. It now has a producer, not only a writer.

**The eligibility hook gained the world's `tick`**, additively. A cooldown is a precondition about the state of the world exactly as much as a company's cohesion is, and it cannot be read off the graph alone; every hook written before this ignores the extra argument.

**And the leverage strand reached the player for the first time.** Computed on the card since THR-30, its only renderers were the unmounted `AgentDetailPanel` (impediment #981) and the debug tab — so `BondsTab`'s *Agreements* placeholder became one row per live mark and favour, in words: *taken from* on a stolen secret, *called in* on an asked-for favour, no numerals (Law 4), gated at `known` like the Relationships rows above it.

## What the measurements changed

**Three tests were asserting the candidate walk's rotation phase rather than their own claims.** `rotateForTick` offsets the walked list by `% items.length` and the per-actor ceiling then cuts it, so *which tick* a given cell is reachable on is a function of how many cells the division rule derives — and every ticket that adds one moves it. THR-1438 added two and hit this; this band added four and hit it again. Rather than re-pin the new numbers, the three now assert what they are about: `divisionRule` asserts the ceiling is not exceeded and the per-ambition cap is, and `peopleThingsCells` reads its board across a full rotation cycle. The claim ("a commander's death puts the claim on the board") is unchanged and still falsified by its own before-arm; it is simply no longer coupled to an arithmetic accident.

**The census records all four cells as `no_owned_object` at 150 ticks on both seeds** — a young world has claimed no Location and no lane, which is a supply fact about the *claiming* cells (THR-1428 recorded the same limit). So reachability is proved on a generated world instead, with the holdings made through the world's own writers: `yieldBandCells.test.ts` grants a real holding, mints a real mark, walks a real standing up through `applyReputationWithDelta`, and reads the real board.

Three setup facts that cost time and are worth recording: `ownershipOf` answers `own` only when **every** owner is the actor, so granting a mortal a town the civic guard already `controls` leaves the object reading `other`; `resolveDurableActorLocation` answers with a mortal's **durable origin**, not where they stand, and the board measures range from `located_at`; and the board's `no_object_in_range:<type>` rejection string is emitted for *any* empty target set — the trace, not the string, distinguishes `no_object_exists` from `no_owned_object`.
