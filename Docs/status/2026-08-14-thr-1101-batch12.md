## The vagueness figure is 2, adjudicated, not 0

Applying the strictest `outcome` scope to all 71 lines — stricter than the scene fields owe — leaves two hits, both the word `way`, both in **scene** prose, both in simile position: *"the way a room goes quiet when a stranger walks in"* and *"the way water runs under limestone"*.

These are reported as adjudicated rather than fixed. THR-899 scopes `way` to outcome prose precisely because banning it in scene prose "buys nothing but contortion", and rewriting either simile would produce a worse sentence to satisfy a detector that was deliberately scoped not to fire there. A third hit — *"There is a way through it"* — **was** rewritten to *"a passage through it"*, because that one had a concrete noun waiting behind it and the line improved. The distinction is the inheritable part: rewrite when the plain alternative is at least as plain, adjudicate when it is not.
