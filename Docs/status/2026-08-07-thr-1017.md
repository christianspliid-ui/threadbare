**The aftermath's auto-interrupt was gated on a clock that its own pause had stopped (THR-1017, 2026-08-07).** Seam B of THR-1005, and the half that matched Christian's original report: *"the aftermath is not popping up by itself... There were other modals waiting in the screen."* Three prior passes could not reproduce it because every one of them tested a **cleared** screen — they dismissed the opening action-card modals before driving the encounter, which is precisely the state in which the defect cannot occur. The mechanism is not slot occupancy, as the ticket's working hypothesis guessed. Closing a veil arms `interruptSuppressedUntilTick = tick + 1`, a one-tick debounce so the beat just dismissed cannot immediately re-pop; it clears on exactly one condition, `tick` reaching it. But **every path that arms it also leaves the sim paused**, because `useInterruptAutoPause` (THR-668) holds `running === false` while *any* interrupt surface is open — so with a second surface stacked behind the veil (the `beat.spine.opening` action-card modal, which auto-enters for spine beats and which Christian had not dismissed), closing the veil does not resume the sim. The clock stops, and a tick-gated window whose clock is stopped by the very state that armed it never expires. The scan bailed at its first line forever; the aftermath was generated, queued, and starved, reachable only by the thread badge — which still worked because `handleOpenEncounterBadge` calls the open handler directly and never consults the window. That asymmetry *is* the bug report: the badge became the primary route, which **Law 39** forbids in as many words.

**Measured, paired, on one route** (`?view=game&seeded&size=medium&forceencounters`, `beat.spine.opening` fired and left stacked, `tg.senior.jewel_heist` driven by `__DEBUG.tick`). At the moment of the close: veil + beat modal up, aftermath queued, 4 unresolved pause-tier notifications, `simRunning: false`, tick frozen at 12. Then the veil is closed and the clock is left frozen — the real paused-sim condition. **Before:** `modals: [AscendantBeatModal]`, `aftermathOpen: false`, nothing opened for as long as the tick stood still. **After:** `modals: [EncounterVeil, AscendantBeatModal]`, `aftermathOpen: true`, at the same frozen tick 12 and the same `simRunning: false`. The causal half was confirmed separately against the unfixed build: a single `__DEBUG.tick(1)` — the tick a paused sim can never take — opened the starved veil immediately.

**One method note worth carrying forward, because it is what makes this class of defect invisible:** `window.__DEBUG.tick(n)` advances the tick *even while the sim is paused*, so any A/B that ticks between its arms silently repairs the frozen clock and both arms pass. A first run of the paired script did exactly that and reported the aftermath auto-opening on the unfixed build. Only a "close, then do **not** tick" protocol discriminates (impediment #471).

**The fix is scoped to the scan, deliberately not to the shared predicate.** `isEncounterAutoOpenSuppressed(interruptsSuppressed, simRunning)` in `encounterNotificationRuntime.ts` returns the window only while the clock that measures it is running; `GameView`'s auto-open effect consults it instead of `interruptsSuppressed` directly. Folding the `running` term into `interruptsSuppressed` itself was rejected: that predicate also gates the render conditions of the vignette, story-beat, premonition and beat-offer surfaces, and pausing for a surface that cannot render is the "invisible gate" failure THR-668 warns about in its own comment. Dropping the window when paused is safe because it was always the blunt half of a two-part guard — the precise half, `suppressedEncounterNotificationId`, is keyed on the notification **id** rather than on time and is therefore unaffected by a stopped clock; the just-closed record stays held back either way, and a regression test pins that. Behaviour on a cleared screen is unchanged: while `running` is true the one-tick debounce applies exactly as before. Five tests added, including the stacked-modal case the three prior passes' own findings identified as the coverage gap.
