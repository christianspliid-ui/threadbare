## What changed

**The registry stops being empty.** Slice 2 shipped `UNDERTAKING_KIND_ROWS` as `[]` on
principle — a row must name a reachable, motive-gated destroy, and the corpus held exactly
one destroy verb in 43. Five rows now land *with* their destroys: intelligence cache,
leverage mark, masterwork, chart, network. The rule slice 2 stated is honoured rather than
relaxed: **until a kind can be undone, it is not a kind.** Tiers 2 and 3 stay absent rather
than stubbed, for the same reason the array was empty before.

**A seventh behaviour family.** `wanderer-explorer` had a declared union member, a glyph and
a colour for a long time with no pack behind it — which is why the field survey measured 700
of 798 idle decisions happening at lairs: no template in six packs named a wilderness subtype
as a target. `wandererStrategicPack.ts` names 23 of them. Live on seed 42: 34 wanderer
undertakings completed in 150 ticks.

**Six graph ops, all writing into economies that already have consumers.** `spawn_clue`
(the ruins layer converges it), `seed_knows_of`, `mint_treasure_map` (the consumption sweep
already spends it), `mint_leverage_mark`, `press_the_mark`, `mint_masterwork`. The
discipline throughout: a find only the finder can read is a score, not a kind.

**The first `remote: true` in the corpus.** `strategic_extend_reach` — reaching into a far
town through people you command is the case §6 drew the remote line around, as against
walking four hexes, which is travel.

**Three emptiness pins restated, not relaxed.** The `payoffValue`, `remote` and frozen-desire
pins all fired exactly as their authors intended. The third is the one that mattered: the
undertaking desire multiplier had been a hard constant, so the board ranked every undertaking
with **no personality signal at all**. Measured after authoring `motivations`: **51 distinct
desire values** on the same 60-tick seed-42 sample that previously produced exactly 1. That
is the repair THR-1301 has been waiting on.

## Four findings worth recording

1. **The plan's §7 locality assumption is false, and the measurement is unambiguous.** §7
   authored `requiresLocation: true` for the chart verbs reasoning that "targets are the
   wilderness features around where the idle agents already stand". They are not:
   `findValidTargets` scans **every** location in the graph (868 on a medium map) and slices
   eight, with no proximity filter anywhere in the path — all three agents picked the same
   distant site. Two arms differing only in that flag: at `true`, 0 completions and the
   `chart_find` kind produced nothing at all; at `false`, 14 completions, 3 maps, 4 clues.
   Shipped `false`, which is the remedy the plan's own kill criterion prescribes.
2. **Selection and resolution can disagree silently, and it reads as a dead verb.**
   `press_the_mark` refuses without a held `knows_secret_of` — correct, and unit-tested —
   but its target rule picked any co-located clerk, so it completed 3 times against 3
   strangers and minted 0 debts. The guard was working perfectly and the arc still could not
   connect. Fixed with a `withEdgeFromActor` filter on the target rule: selection now knows
   what resolution requires.
3. **A corpus-wide invariant caught an exception I tried to justify.** `strategic_burn_the_mark`
   shipped first as `verb: 'destroy'`, on the reasoning that the registry's D column means
   *counter-play* so a self-spend could be an ungated destroy. The registry test refused it —
   its rule is corpus-wide, not row-scoped — and it was right: an ungated destroy is
   offerable against anyone for no reason, with only authoring discipline keeping it aimed
   at the actor's own marks. It is a `change` verb now.
4. **Two artifact writers minted unrenderable items.** Both used `subcategory: 'tool'` and a
   string `tier`; neither exists (`PossessionSubcategory` has seven members, `AttachmentTier`
   is numeric 1–4). Nothing threw — the art resolver just returned `null` forever, so the
   items would have rendered as blank plates everywhere a possession is shown. Caught by the
   seeded-world art-coverage test, and *only* because that world happened to mint a chart;
   the masterwork carried the identical bug invisibly. Now asserted at both writers, where it
   does not depend on which seed exercises them.
