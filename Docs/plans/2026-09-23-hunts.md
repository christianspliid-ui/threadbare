# Hunts — a mortal takes up the work of killing a monster

*The beast that scarred her is still in its den. A hunt is how she goes back: learning how it moves, and walking in when she's ready. It's the deliberate version of "you fight what you walk into".*

## Why this is load-bearing

Plan doc 3 gives the lair's beast an **encounter** hunt (`monster.hunt.named_elite`), offered to whoever is already standing at its den. Nothing lets a mortal go after a beast **from afar, for a reason**, which is what undertakings are for: Christian framed this effort "at the level of encounters and undertakings", and undertakings are how mortals pursue long works (the six verbs × world objects grid).

"Hunt" therefore names two things, deliberately linked:
- the **encounter hunt**: plan doc 3, at the den, for whoever is there;
- the **undertaking hunt**: this doc, `cell.destroy.monster`, the long work whose payoff is a confront at the den.

Three drives have no verb until this lands:
- plan doc 1 writes a `blood_drawn` grudge toward a monster that scarred someone;
- THR-1536 routes a killing's grievance to the dead's living bonds, so a monster's victims leave heirs who want it dead;
- a den near home is a threat.

Decisions come from the closed map (THR-1258): THR-1533 (hunts, with tonight's refinements), THR-1268 (a monster is a class of Mortal), THR-1266 (`blood_drawn` toward monsters) and THR-1532 (secrets as advantages).

## Engine pillar

### Systems design

**1. The `monster` undertaking object type** (THR-1533). Definition:
- `shape: { nodeType: 'actor', discriminator: isLiveMonster }`, where `isLiveMonster(node) = isMonster(node) && !isAgentGone(node)` (plan doc 3's predicate plus the standard liveness read, `groupQueries.ts:459-462`). The dead are never a hunt's object;
- `ownedVia: []` and **`selfOwned: true`**, the MORTAL precedent: the ownership gate asks about the monster itself, so `destroy`'s `'other'` rule does not refuse it as unowned (`undertakingResolver.ts:47-49`);
- `tierOf`: `HUNT_TIER_BY_LAIR` (2 at a major lair, 3 at legendary);
- `eligibility` refuses `hunter_gone` for both verbs when `isAgentGone(actor)`: the decision phase picks actors without a `deceased` check (`phaseAgentDecision.ts:418-420`), and a retained corpse keeps its `pursues` edge, which the new avenger profile would otherwise act on. (Whether the dead reach the decision phase at all is a pre-existing question, not this doc's.)
- `lexicon: 'iron'`;
- `harmOnDestroy: 'named_death'` (what killing a beast is, so the contract's counter-play block and `HARM_ON_DESTROY` stay whole), **with its payoff deferred on the `destroy` verb**. Verb entries are functions or `{ mode }` (`undertaking-objects.ts:227-234`), so the flag lives on the type as an optional `deferredPayoffVerbs: ['destroy']`, is carried onto the synthesized cell template as `deferredPayoff: true` (`undertaking-cells.ts:253-294`), and is read at the completion writes (`strategicActionLifecycle.ts:1085-1117`), which record `payoffDeferred: true` in the completion history and its trace, so a skipped harm or grievance write is inspectable (NFP #2). Completing a hunt harms nobody yet, so a deferred-payoff completion writes **no outcome node** and **does not satisfy the grievance** it answers. The grievance closes through its existing `grievance_culprit_eliminated` milestone (`ambition-templates.ts:1297-1298`) when the beast actually dies (plan doc 3's M3 writes `slainBy`). A hunter whose confront breaks off can therefore take the work up again. **The claim is about the hunt's own completion only:** a vendetta can still close 2-of-N through its reach milestones (`ambition-templates.ts:1137`, `:1302`), as today. **And a beast's grievance is not spent on the wrong target.** Completion finds the grievance to satisfy by ambition template alone (`grievanceLifecycle.ts:471-480`, used at `strategicActionLifecycle.ts:1066-1117`). So an avenger whose culprit is a beast, finishing any other vengeance project first, would close the grievance and with it the hunt's `'grievance'` reason. H2 guards that: a grievance whose culprit `isMonster` is satisfied only by the culprit's death (`grievance_culprit_eliminated`), never by another project's completion. Before plan doc 1, no grievance could name a monster, so no existing grievance changes;
- `CELL_FAMILY_BY_TYPE.monster = 'warlord-expansion'`, the martial family that already works companies and armies. It governs **tracking** only: for `destroy`, `COUNTER_PLAY_FAMILY` wins (`undertaking-cells.ts:257`, `:272`), as for every counter-play verb.

Two verbs:
- **`observe` (track):** the milestone work of learning the beast. `observe` is not motive-gated. On completion, **one named helper**, `recordHuntTracking(graph, hunterId, monsterId, tick)`, makes both writes, so a future divination spell has a single writer to call:
  - `mintLeverageMark(graph, hunterId, monsterId, HUNT_TRACK_SECRET_TYPE, HUNT_TRACK_MARK_MAGNITUDE, tick)`, with the existing `'hidden_weakness'` secret type;
  - `monsterState.temperShown = true`, so plan doc 4's lair card reveals the temper.

  **One secret per hunter per beast.** `mintLeverageMark` refuses `mark_already_held` even when the held mark was revealed and spent (`strategicGraphOps.ts:688-692`), so the type's `eligibility.observe` refuses `already_tracked` when the hunter holds any `knows_secret_of` edge toward the beast. A spent secret is spent.

  Plan doc 2's advantage reader turns that live mark into "Their secret" (+0.10 on the first clash where the hunter is behind, then spent through the reveal path).
- **`destroy` (hunt):** the work of going to kill it. Its completion plants the confront (§2) and writes nothing else. Tracking first is not required; it is how a hunter earns the secret advantage. **Company help needs no gather step:** plan doc 2's Company advantage already counts the hunter's company members on the hex as allies (THR-1271), so a hunter who brings friends has them.

**The mark's other lives**, for a monster subject:
- **pressing it** (`use × Agreement`) would make a beast owe a favour: the Agreement type's `eligibility.use` refuses a monster subject (`no_favour_from_beasts`);
- **theft** (`control:seize` on the mark) is accepted: a stolen account of how the beast moves is still true;
- **autonomous revelation** and **decay** are accepted: the mark stops being live, and the advantage is simply absent (fail-soft). Revelation also writes `relates_to` edges from the beast (`secretsFavorsConsequences.ts:105-114`); that is accepted too, as the ordinary shape of a revealed secret.

**2. The confront, planted as an appointment** (THR-1519's path). The `destroy × monster` row in `UNDERTAKING_CELL_APPOINTMENTS`:
- **The meeting query** is `{ kind: 'encounter_template', tags: ['#lair_confront'] }`. H2 seats `#lair_confront` and puts it on `fight.lair.confront` alone; a single bearer is allowed because this query reads it (the runtime-reader rule, `Docs/canon/content-objects.md`).
- **The opponent.** `fight.lair.confront` fights its target, and a seed targets the mortal it was planted on (`unifiedAction.ts:1403`). The payoff's new optional **`inheritSiteAsTarget: true`** makes `maybePlantAppointmentPayoff` set the seed's `inheritedTargetId` to the candidate's `targetNodeId`, the monster. That target belongs to the **kept branch only**: the missed rewrite (`encounterSeeding.ts:618-628`, which spreads `...seed`) drops `inheritedTargetId` when the planted appointment carries `inheritSiteAsTarget`. **The payoff's flags ride on the planted appointment**, because the seed's later readers (the window-closed rewrite, `computeAppointmentPull`) see the `PlantedAppointment`, not the payoff: `PlantedAppointment` (`src/types/unifiedAction.ts:1460-1468`) and `PlantAppointmentInput` (`appointments.ts:279`) gain the optional `inheritSiteAsTarget`, `pullMult` and `requirePlace`.
- **The creditor.** The planter treats a non-faction actor site as the counterparty (`strategicActionLifecycle.ts:2259-2262`). It excludes `isMonster(site)`, so the lair is the creditor and no favour is owed to a beast.
- **A real missed sequel: "The trail went cold."** The appointment substrate holds that *a meeting that cannot be missed is not a promise*: `readPlantedAppointment` rejects a seed with no `missed` branch (`appointments.ts:96`), three types require it, and the CI-gated contract rejects a payoff without one (`undertakingContract.ts:453-454`). So the hunt keeps the rule and authors its missed branch: `missed: { query: { kind: 'encounter_template', tags: ['#hunt_trail_cold'] }, seedLabel: 'The trail went cold.' }`. It fires wherever the hunter is when the window closes, like every missed branch, and the favour is broken as usual (`breakAppointmentFavour`, `appointments.ts:373`). A broken promise does not block a new hunt (the refusal below reads only live favours), so the hunter may take the work up again. The sequel is one new, small template (§ Content pillar), not the Underking's Court (`#court_errand`), which is wrong for a beast.
- **The confront never fires placeless.** `maybePlantAppointmentPayoff` pushes a placeless seed even when the promise was refused (`strategicActionLifecycle.ts:2282-2295`: `over_max`, `APPOINTMENT_MAX_PER_MORTAL` = 3, `movement-content.ts:306`; or `place_unresolved`), and the seeding site fires a seed whose place was lost (`encounterSeeding.ts:588-600`) placeless too. A placeless confront would fire wherever the hunter stands, and M4's `hunt_appointment` skip could not recognise it. So the payoff's new optional **`requirePlace: true`** (the hunt sets it) means: a refused plant pushes no seed (`maybePlantAppointmentPayoff` returns `false`), and a seed whose place is lost is dropped rather than fired. The refusal and the drop are traced as today's planter and place-lost traces. The hunt is simply not paid off, and the hunter may take it up again.
- **The pull.** The payoff's new optional `pullMult` is carried to the planted appointment and multiplies `computeAppointmentPull` (`appointments.ts:251`): `APPOINTMENT_PULL_WEIGHT × pullMult / (1 + dist)`. The hunt sets it to `HUNT_APPOINTMENT_PULL_MULT` (1.0).
- **The travel budget.** The default appointment is due 18 ticks after completion (`UNDERTAKING_APPOINTMENT_DELAY_TICKS`, `strategic-action-constants.ts:175`), and a hunter farther away than that is in the `lost` regime (`appointments.ts:194`), where the pull is 0 (`:249`). Far beasts are exactly the case the scan cap exists for, so the hunt row sets its own **`delayTicks: HUNT_APPOINTMENT_DELAY_TICKS` (48)**. When misses come from the `lost` regime, the delay is the lever, not the pull.
- **One confront per beast per hunter.** `project_already_active` sees only active projects (`strategicActionCandidates.ts:274-280`), so a completed hunt could be followed by a second hunt and a second confront. The hook cannot see seeds (its signature is `(graph, actorId, handle, tick)`, `undertaking-objects.ts:300`, and candidate generation receives no `pendingEncounterSeeds`), but it can see the **promise the planter always writes**: an `owes_favor` edge from the hunter carrying `appointment: { seedId, locationId, dueTick }` (`appointments.ts:325-338`). So `eligibility.destroy` refuses `hunt_confront_pending` while the hunter holds an appointment favour, neither redeemed nor broken, whose `locationId` is this beast's lair. That is exact because a refused plant writes no seed at all (above), a kept appointment retires the favour (`encounterSeeding.ts:511`) and a missed one breaks it.
- **If the monster dies before the confront:** the seed's inherited target is still a node, since `evaluateEncounterSeeds` only checks presence (`encounterSeeding.ts:407`). Plan doc 2's rule, that an opponent who is dead, missing or the fighter never fights, ends the confront `opponent_gone` at its first step. The hunt is over, and because the discriminator excludes the dead, no new hunt forms against the corpse.
- **M4 gives way.** A mortal keeping this appointment arrives holding the seed. H2 adds the matching skip to M4's trigger (`skipped: 'hunt_appointment'`), beside plan doc 3's own `arriving_for_hunt` skip.
- **So does the encounter hunt at the den.** A hunter who arrives early and waits could otherwise draw plan doc 3's `monster.hunt.named_elite` against the same beast. H2 extends M2's `requiresLiveMonster` draw gate: it also hides that encounter from a mortal holding a live hunt appointment favour at the lair. With both, the kept branch's fight is the only one.

**3. Who is offered a hunt, and why they may take it.**

*Offered* (the candidate walk, §Substrate):
- **Every living beast is in view.** The object scan cuts each type to the eight nearest before any gate runs (`strategicActionCandidates.ts:611`), and a scarred mortal's beast is rarely among their eight nearest monsters. So the `monster` type reads its own cap, **`STRATEGIC_TARGET_SCAN_CAPS.monster` = `HUNT_TARGET_SCAN_CAP` (64)**, looked up as `STRATEGIC_TARGET_SCAN_CAPS[rule.objectTypeId] ?? STRATEGIC_TARGET_SCAN_CAPS.object` (the map is typed `Record<string, number>`, `strategic-action-constants.ts:999`, and its doc comment, "keyed by `StrategicTargetRule['type']`", is updated to say so). The per-cell cap counts only accepted candidates (`strategicActionCandidates.ts:324`, `:478`), so refused beasts never crowd out the reasoned one. The cap is set at least as high as the living-monster count on every map preset (THR-1268 measured 14–26 elites on a medium world). H2's CLI step also prints the living-monster count on a **large** and an **epic** map at 300 ticks, so the cap is checked against the biggest presets. The scan is bounded by the number of living monsters, not the map;
- **`monster` joins `KINDS_BY_REACH.iron`.** The division rule reads a mortal's **two leading** reaches (`divisionRule.ts:23-28`). So a vengeance ambition (verbs destroy, change:lower, control:seize), a devotion ambition (change:raise, create, destroy; `division-rule-tables.ts:29`) or a survival ambition (observe, change:raise, destroy) whose two leading reaches include Iron derives `cell.destroy.monster`; survival derives `cell.observe.monster` too, and so does discovery with Iron. The only survival ambition with a profile today is `ambition_escape_cursed_land` (`ambition-templates.ts:580-592`); `ambition_protect_the_home` has none, and giving it one is out of this doc's scope. The den near home is reached mostly through the threat door (below) on devotion and vengeance mortals;
- **`monster` joins `ALL_UNDERTAKING_KINDS`**, so Eye's observe-anything rider lets any Eye-reach mortal track a beast;
- **hand lists:** `cell.destroy.monster` and `cell.observe.monster` join the `strategicProfile.cells` of `ambition_seek_revenge` (the vendetta, beside the plot's `cell.destroy.mortal`).
- **`ambition_avenge_fallen` gains a full `strategicProfile`** (`behaviorFamily`, `preferredVerbs`, `templateIds`, `reachEmphasis`, all required, `strategicAction.ts:1094-1106`) with the two hunt cells in its hand list. Today it has **no** profile (`ambition-templates.ts:1263-1325`), so the board skips it entirely (`strategicActionCandidates.ts:225`), and a grieving heir from THR-1536 pursues nothing. **Under `cells`, a profile also gives every avenger, whether grieving a plot or a fight, the vengeance family's cells over the kinds of their two leading reaches** (the division rule, `:234-235`: destroy, change:lower and control:seize against armies, companies, locations and now monsters for Iron, and so on). **It does not give them the plot:** the division rule never derives `cell.destroy.mortal` (`KINDS_BY_REACH` and `ALL_UNDERTAKING_KINDS` hold no `mortal`, `division-rule-tables.ts:36-51`), and the hand list is the two hunt cells only. Giving avengers the plot is a separate design question, left as fog. The widening is decided here under the 2026-09-11 delegation, because an avenger with no way to act is the defect, and it is recorded on THR-1533 with a veto invitation when this plan merges. H2 measures it (§ Slices: the census), and a kill criterion bounds it.

*Admitted* (the motive gate). `destroy` is motive-gated (`MOTIVE_GATED_VERBS`). The monster type opens it through the existing **`gateExemption.destroy`** door, which returns the reason, or `null` to leave the gate to its social motives:
- `'blood_drawn'`: the hunter holds a `blood_drawn` or `grievance_cooled` `hostile_to` toward the monster. Once plan doc 1 has added `blood_drawn` to `GRUDGE_PROVENANCE`, the gate's own grudge motive admits this as well; the door makes the reason legible either way;
- `'grievance'`: the hunter holds an active grievance, a `pursues` edge whose `culpritAgentId` is the monster (THR-1536);
- `'threat_radius'`: the monster's lair lies within `HUNT_THREAT_RADIUS_HEXES` of the hunter's home (plan doc 1's home rule: the residence's `originLocationId` settlement, else its `positionId` settlement, by `LOCATION_CLASSES.settlement`).

The board records `gate_exempt:<reason>`, so every hunt is inspectable for why it formed. Faction bounties are fog in the v2 layer.
