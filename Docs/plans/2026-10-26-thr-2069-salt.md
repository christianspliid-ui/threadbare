# Salt — THR-2069

*The carrier's mule train crosses the ford before the fog lifts, and next morning Hesk Crossing has salt to cure its winter meat. On the steppe, the pans at Dry Mere send salt down three roads.*

## Why this is load-bearing

The game talks about salt everywhere and has none. Two smuggling scenes are about running salt past the excise (`smugglers-ford.ts:21, 43`; `encounter.smuggle_goods`, `encounter-content.ts:3474`), and the [money-encounter outcome table](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1872) gives them a *salt stock+* row that [mortal wealth S3](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1953) could not build: *"no `salt` resource exists"* (`Docs/status/2026-10-24-thr-1953.md:9`). The shipped economy plans use salt as their example good in player text: *"Holds the region's salt"* ([war aims](2026-10-15-thr-1933-war-aims.md) § UI), *"yields salt"* ([faction purse](2026-10-15-thr-1931-faction-purse.md) § UI). The [mortal economy audit](../audits/2026-10-14-mortal-economy-audit.md) counts about 155 prose mentions of salt, and six terrains with empty resource tables. Law 45 says the game never promises what the world will not do. This plan adds the good and builds the ford's salt row; Smuggle Goods' half closes as no write (Decision 4). It is the *missing `salt` good* row of [Money encounters, later rows](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1954).

## Decisions

Decided by the design lane under delegation (process.md rule 4). Each can be vetoed on its own.

1. **Add salt; do not re-pick the good.** Changing the scenes to an existing good (fish, ore) would rewrite two finished scenes and break about 155 lines of prose, and the shipped economy text would still name a good that does not exist. *Veto:* "the smugglers should carry something we already have".
2. **Salt is a trade good, not food.** It is classed **strategic**, like ore and timber, with value 1.0 (between stone 0.8 and ore 1.1). So a town short of salt is not hungry; it reads *"Salt is dear here"*. Its spheres are **matter** and **order** (the keeping of things). Being non-staple, Reveal the Vein can call up a salt pan. *Veto:* "salt should count as food".
3. **Salt comes from the open dry lands and the shore.** Salt pans and dry lake beds are on the **steppe** and the high **plateau**, which is where towns stand today. The **deserts** and the **coast** also get salt: no town stands there on medium maps today, but larger maps and founded towns reach them. Not on mountains: mountain towns already carry stone and ore, and salt everywhere would never be scarce or worth holding. Measured: 14–21 towns a world have salt, and it rides 14–19 of about 60 roads. *Veto:* "salt should be mined in the mountains too".
4. **Smuggled salt arrives at the ford's hamlet.** The Salt Train at the Ford is about a hamlet that *"has gone short of salt"* (`smugglers-ford.ts:8`). When the train gets over, the hamlet gains salt stock: a big gain on a critical success, a small one on a success or costly success. This holds on both paths, whether the mortal leads or reads the watch for the carrier. A failure brings nothing, because the excise took it. A town with no salt of its own then has a little, and it counts as a salt place in the region. So when the town is not the monopoly holder's, smugglers **dent the holder's share**. *Veto:* "smuggled salt should not count toward a monopoly".
   **Smuggle Goods gets no salt row.** Its salt is bought in the town (*"a yard behind a tannery"*, `encounter-content.ts:3504`) and sold *"at a mill outside the walls"* (`:3547`). It leaves the town, so crediting the town would invert the scene. This closes that half of the outcome research's row as *no write*.
5. **The Salt Monopoly scene is unchanged.** It is about *"the duke's salt licence for ten years"*: a charter, not the regional monopoly, and it fires anywhere urban. Tying it to salt places is a separate design and is not on the later-rows list.

## Engine pillar

No new code path. Data only, in tables the existing readers already walk:

1. **`RESOURCE_DEFINITIONS.salt`** (`src/data/resource-content.ts`, after `peat`): `name: 'Salt'`, `terrains: ['steppe', 'plateau', 'desert', 'rocky_desert', 'sand_dunes', 'coast']`, `baseQuantity: [20, 60]`, `sphereAffinities: ['matter', 'order']`, `renewable: true`, `renewalRate: 0.2` (pans refill, slowly), `description: 'Salt from pans, licks and dry lake beds — for curing meat and keeping the winter.'`
2. **`TERRAIN_RESOURCE_TABLE`** rows (each terrain stays ≤ 3 goods): `steppe` 40–70, `plateau` 45–75, `desert` 40–75 (was empty), `rocky_desert` 30–55, `sand_dunes` 25–50 (was empty), `coast` 15–35.
3. **`RESOURCE_CLASSES.salt`** (`src/data/resource-classes.ts`): `category: 'strategic'`, `primarySphere: 'matter'`, `baseValue: 1.0`, `scarcitySensitivity: 0.6`.
4. **`RESOURCE_ICONS.salt`** (`src/types/resource.ts`): `'⚪'`. Only the test reads it today. It follows peat's plain disc, not a salt cellar, so the icon reads as period.
5. **Not touched:** `DEPOSIT_YIELD_CHANNEL` (salt is not a building material), `STOCK_DEPOSIT_GOODS` (prospecting's `deposit` rows stay ore and stone), `GUILD_PLACE_AFFINITY`, and `world-model.json` (CMS-only, frozen at 8 goods since 2026-03-08).

**Determinism.** Seeding draws one quantity per terrain row in order (`resourceSeeding.ts:47-55`). So the new rows shift the resource stream, and every seed's world changes slightly: on the prototype the lane counts moved 58→60, 63→67, 60→62. Same seed still gives the same world. A seeded test that pins a world's resource or lane figures may need its expectation updated. The build PR names each one and why it moved.

No new node, edge, phase or PRNG call.

## Kill criteria

- Salt leads more than half of all roads' cargo on any of the three seeds. Then it crowds the other goods off the manifest (cap 4): lower `baseValue` to 0.85 (still above stone and grazing at 0.8) and re-measure.
- Fewer than 10 salt places on a seed. Then the dry lands are rarer than measured: add `hills` only if it has a free slot (it has none today), otherwise widen the plateau and steppe quantity floor.
