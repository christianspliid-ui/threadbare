# Encounter Pipeline: Soul Ferryman
> Scale: short | Slug: soul-ferryman-opus | Pass: editorial
> Date: 2026-04-05 | Pipeline version: 1.0

---

## 1. Prose Quality

### Opening Paragraph

The opening paragraph is excellent -- possibly the strongest single passage authored in the encounter pipeline so far. The sentence "The fog sat on the river like a hand pressed over a mouth" is a precise, unsettling image that establishes the Silt as an agent, not a backdrop. The description of Vesik builds through physical erosion ("the damp had found its way into his joints and eaten whatever softness had once lived there") rather than exposition. The courier's arrival is woven into the same breath as the setting. The paragraph earns its length because every clause does double work: atmosphere and characterization, worldbuilding and tension.

No rewrites needed on the opening.

### Branch A Paragraph (Break the Bargain)

Strong. The contract-as-rope metaphor is concrete and original. "The god found the rope. The god cut it." -- three sentences, escalating brevity, correct instinct. Vesik dropping the pole, the pole trailing "its river-weed like hair," the punt rocking -- all sensory, all specific. The closing image ("whether waking was mercy or punishment") lands the thematic ambiguity without stating it outright.

One weakness: the paragraph front-loads the god's action and then lingers on Vesik, but Diehl's reaction is compressed to a single clause ("Diehl stepped back from the waterline"). For an encounter where Diehl's wholeness is a consequence of this branch, his moment of witnessing deserves one more sentence. Not a blocker, but a missed beat.

### Branch B Paragraph (Steady the Courier)

Also strong, and arguably the more difficult paragraph to write because the intervention is subtler. "The god leaned into the courier's fear the way wind leans into a candle -- not enough to extinguish, just enough to steady" is precise. The toll-naming scene works: Vesik reading Diehl's face "the way a hand moves across a page of text" is a good image, though it is the second hand-simile in two paragraphs (the opening uses "a hand pressed over a mouth"). Consider varying the vehicle.

The closing sentence -- "the near bank disappeared behind them like a sentence left unfinished" -- is the best line in the encounter. It earns the literary register because it is also literally true: the encounter's moral question is unfinished, deliberately.

### Aftermath Paragraphs

**Branch A aftermath:** Solid. "Travelers who had been crossing at this point for years stood at the waterline and stared at the empty river as if something had died" -- good. "The river does not make refunds" -- a strong closing beat that avoids sentimentality. Diehl leaving his horse and walking to the ford is a quietly devastating detail; it shows him absorbing a consequence without commentary.

**Branch B aftermath:** The strongest aftermath prose in the encounter. "The sudden lightness of a man who has set down a weight he did not know he was carrying and discovered he needed it" -- this is the encounter's thesis stated as physical sensation, which is exactly right. "The private engine that had driven him" -- good. "The fog closed over the crossing like a mouth" -- echoes the opening's "hand pressed over a mouth," completing a frame. This is well-crafted.

### Outcome Ladder Prose

The outcome ladder entries are functional but noticeably less authored than the main paragraphs. They read as detailed narrative summaries rather than scenes. This is acceptable for the outcome ladder format (which serves as authoring direction for the runtime resolver, not as player-facing text), but two entries deserve attention:

**Branch A, Critical Failure:** "He calls out to Diehl in a voice that is not quite his own, offering passage for free -- just this once, just step aboard." This is the strongest outcome ladder entry because it shifts from summary to scene. The other entries should aspire to this density.

**Branch B, Success at Cost:** The toll "Your memory of being loved" is devastating and specific. But the sentences that follow ("He cannot remember his mother's face, or the particular way his sister used to grab his hand when she was small") read as catalog rather than scene. Consider:

[EDITORIAL REWRITE]
> Original: "When he reaches the far bank, he cannot remember his mother's face, or the particular way his sister used to grab his hand when she was small, or the warmth of any embrace he has ever received."
>
> Suggested: "When he reaches the far bank, he stands for a moment as if expecting someone to be there. No one is. He thinks about this and feels nothing, which is new, and does not understand why the nothing has a shape."

The rewrite removes the list (which tells the reader what was lost) and replaces it with a felt absence (which shows the loss from inside). The specifics of mother's face and sister's hand are more effective when the reader infers them rather than having them cataloged.

### Pressure Knot Prose

The pressure knot (Section 3) is well-written but walks a line between authored prose and design-document exposition. The first paragraph is scene-quality. The second paragraph ("Vesik does not charge coin. He charges something else.") is exposition disguised as narration -- it works, but it is flatter than the opening paragraph. The third paragraph ("He is not a sorcerer") returns to strength. The fourth paragraph introducing Diehl is efficient but plain compared to the opening's prose register.

No rewrite required, but the author should note that the pressure knot's quality is uneven: paragraphs 1 and 3 are scene-quality, paragraphs 2 and 4 are briefing-quality.

## 2. Branch Seduction Audit

### Branch A: Break the Bargain

**Fantasy of interference:** The god who breaks chains. The god who looks at a system of quiet predation and says *no more*. This is the abolitionist fantasy -- ending an economy that feeds on diminishment, regardless of the practical cost.

**Why would a god choose this?** Because the toll is wrong. Not dramatically wrong, not catastrophically wrong, but structurally wrong -- it strips selfhood from travelers as a cost of crossing a river. A god who breaks this bargain is declaring that no geography justifies soul-erosion. This is a statement about what the player's world permits.

**What value does it protect?** Human wholeness. The irreducibility of selfhood. The conviction that there should be no price of passage that costs you a piece of who you are.

**Seduction strength:** HIGH. The fantasy is clear, emotionally resonant, and carries real cost (the crossing closes, Vesik is adrift). A god who chooses this is not being naive -- the downstream consequences are stated plainly. This is principled interference, not rescue fantasy.

### Branch B: Steady the Courier

**Fantasy of interference:** The god who accepts the world's terms and helps someone navigate them. The god who does not abolish the toll but ensures the traveler can bear it. This is the pragmatic steward fantasy -- the world contains ferrymen and tolls and fog, and a wise god helps their people through rather than tearing everything down.

**Why would a god choose this?** Because Diehl needs to cross. The letters matter. The garrison matters. The ford is four days away. The ferryman's toll is not new -- it has operated for thirty-one years and travelers have survived it. Destroying the crossing to save Diehl from a toll he can survive is disproportionate. A god who steadies the courier is saying: *I will not remake the world every time it is imperfect. I will help my people endure what the world asks of them.*

**What value does it protect?** Pragmatic continuity. The acceptance that a world full of small costs is still navigable, and that destroying every imperfect system creates its own harm. Also, implicitly, Diehl's agency -- the god steadies him but lets him choose.

**Seduction strength:** HIGH. This is the harder branch to make seductive (pragmatism is less dramatic than liberation), and the draft succeeds. The key is the outcome ladder's specificity about what Diehl loses -- the toll is not abstract, so accepting it feels like a real decision, not passivity.

### Asymmetry Assessment

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Moral asymmetry** | PASS -- Neither branch holds moral high ground. Breaking the bargain is merciful but destructive. Steadying the courier is pragmatic but ratifies predation. |
| **Dramatic asymmetry** | PASS -- Branch A has the more dramatic scene (contract snapping, fog thinning, Vesik weeping), but Branch B has the more intimate drama (Diehl feeling the toll take hold). Different registers, comparable weight. |
| **Information asymmetry** | PASS -- Both branches give the player equivalent information about consequences. Neither hides its costs. |
| **Prose asymmetry** | PASS -- Both branch paragraphs are authored at comparable quality. Branch B's closing sentence is arguably stronger, but Branch A's contract-rope sequence is equally effective. Balanced. |
| **Aftermath asymmetry** | PASS -- Branch A produces a larger world-state change (crossing closes) but Branch B produces a more personal consequence (Diehl diminished). The scale of consequence differs, but the *weight* is comparable because the Branch B aftermath prose is so effective at making Diehl's loss felt. |

No asymmetry failures detected. Both branches are genuinely seductive and neither reads as the "correct" answer.

## 3. Branch Count Assessment

**Declared count:** 2
**Declared scale:** short

Two branches is correct for this encounter. The encounter's strength is its binary clarity: break the system or work within it. A third branch would need to introduce a genuinely distinct divine posture -- and the design space does not support one without contrivance. The obvious candidate for a third branch ("negotiate a better toll with the river") would collapse into a variant of Branch A (it is still breaking the existing bargain) or introduce a mystical negotiation scene that would inflate the encounter to medium scale.

The author's restraint here is correct. Two branches, both earned.

## 4. Scale Discipline Check

**Declared scale:** short
**Actual footprint:**
- One beat
- Two branches
- No second intervention pass
- No aftermath reaction choices
- One opening paragraph, two branch paragraphs, two aftermath paragraphs

This is a well-disciplined short encounter. The scale justification (Section 2) is honest: the encounter is a liminal moment, not a crisis. The pressure knot sets up a standoff, the player makes one choice, consequences follow. No inflation.

One concern: the outcome ladder has five tiers per branch (ten total), which is thorough for a short encounter. This is not a scale violation (the outcome ladder is authoring infrastructure, not player-facing branching), but the author should confirm that the runtime resolver can distinguish five tiers cleanly for an encounter with this modest a mechanical surface. If the outcome ladder collapses to three distinguishable tiers in practice, the author has written two phantom tiers.

**Verdict:** Scale discipline maintained.

## 5. Inspiration Anchor Honesty

Four anchors are declared: Thematic Pillars (Compassion vs. Power), Anti-Patterns, Ordeal Archetypes (Impossible Bargain), and Character Archetypes (Wandering Scholar-Monster).

**Did they actually change the encounter?**

- **Thematic Pillars (Compassion vs. Power):** YES -- this is the encounter's primary axis. The entire branch structure is Compassion (break the bargain, free the ferryman) vs. Power/Pragmatism (steady the courier, accept the toll). The secondary axis (Sovereignty vs. Consumption) is visible in the framing of the river's contract as parasitic logic. Both pillars are load-bearing.

- **Anti-Patterns:** YES -- the anti-patterns shaped what the encounter is NOT, which is equally important. The ferryman is not a villain (anti Dark Lord). The toll has real consequences (anti Consequence-Free Magic). Neither branch is clean (anti Clean Moral Binary). The situation pre-exists the player (anti Player as Savior). These exclusions are visible in the finished encounter, not decorative citations.

- **Ordeal Archetypes (Impossible Bargain):** YES -- the encounter's structure is precisely an impossible bargain. Every option costs something irreplaceable. The terms are honest. The tension is cold clarity. This archetype shaped the emotional register of the entire encounter (no dramatic confrontation, no hidden trick, just a stated price and a decision).

- **Character Archetypes (Wandering Scholar-Monster):** PARTIALLY -- Vesik has the isolation and strangeness of this archetype, but the "scholar" dimension is thin. He knows the names of the drowned and speaks to the current, but the encounter does not dramatize this knowledge. He is more "mechanism-monster" than "scholar-monster." The archetype pointed the author toward Vesik's characterization but did not fully arrive in the finished text. This is an honest partial fit, not a misattribution.

**Verdict:** Three of four anchors are fully load-bearing. The fourth (Wandering Scholar-Monster) is partially realized. Acceptable for a short encounter -- the archetype influenced Vesik's characterization even if the scholarly dimension is understated.

## 6. Aftermath Payoff

**Branch A:** Lands. The aftermath is actor-centered: Vesik sitting on the bank with his knees drawn up, Diehl walking to the ford on foot, travelers staring at the empty river. The closing line ("the river does not make refunds") is thematically precise and avoids both sentimentality and cynicism. The aftermath connects to future systems (bridge-building, Vesik's reintegration) without over-promising.

**Branch B:** Lands hard. This is the stronger aftermath. Diehl's experience of loss -- "the sudden lightness of a man who has set down a weight he did not know he was carrying and discovered he needed it" -- is the encounter's best piece of writing. The closing image (fog closing like a mouth) completes the opening's frame. The aftermath is entirely Diehl's experience, which is correct: the courier is the person who changed.

Both aftermaths are actor-centered, specific, and avoid the "briefing summary" failure mode. They read as scenes, not reports.

**One note:** The aftermath for Branch A gives Diehl a quietly powerful moment (leaving his horse, walking to the ford) that arguably makes him *more* interesting in the branch where nothing happens to him than in the branch where he is the primary subject. This is not a problem -- it is good writing -- but the author should be aware that Branch A's Diehl has more agency in the aftermath than Branch B's Diehl, which is thematically appropriate (in Branch A he is whole; in Branch B he is diminished).

## 7. Dilemma Energy

The encounter generates genuine dilemma energy through three mechanisms:

1. **Incommensurable goods.** Branch A protects human wholeness; Branch B protects practical continuity. These values cannot be ranked on a single scale. A god who values liberty will choose A; a god who values functioning infrastructure will choose B; a god who values both will agonize.

2. **Stated costs on both sides.** Breaking the bargain closes the crossing (real logistical harm). Steadying the courier ratifies soul-erosion (real moral harm). Neither cost is hidden or understated. The encounter does not trick the player into a bad outcome.

3. **The ferryman is not a villain.** If Vesik were evil, Branch A would be obviously correct. Because he is a man trapped in a contract he made when he was desperate, breaking the bargain is also an act of destruction toward him. The player must weigh Vesik's freedom against Vesik's loss of purpose, which complicates what should be a simple liberation.

**Dilemma weakness:** The encounter slightly underserves the pragmatic case for the crossing's *continued value*. The draft establishes that the ford is four days downstream and the crossing has operated for thirty-one years, but it does not dramatize what happens to the community if the crossing disappears permanently. A single sentence in the pressure knot -- about a village that depends on the crossing for trade, or a healer on the far bank that people need to reach -- would strengthen Branch B's case without inflating the scale. As written, Branch B's pragmatism rests on "the letters need delivering," which is a thinner stake than "the crossing serves a community."

This is a "should fix" rather than a "must fix." The dilemma still functions without it, but the community-dependency stake would make Branch B's seduction even stronger.

## 9. Verdict

**PASS WITH REVISIONS**

This is a strong encounter. The prose is well above the quality floor, the branch structure is genuinely dilemmatic, and the aftermath paragraphs land with real emotional weight. The Bargain / Bind / Break template is correctly applied. The scale discipline is clean. The inspiration anchors are honest. Both branches offer distinct, seductive fantasies of divine interference.

## 10. Revision Summary

### Must Fix

None. No structural or prose-quality failures that would block implementation.

### Should Fix

1. **Strengthen Branch B's community stakes.** Add one sentence to the pressure knot establishing that the crossing serves a community beyond Diehl's immediate errand -- a village, a trade route, a healer on the far bank. This thickens the pragmatic case for letting the toll continue and prevents Branch B from resting entirely on "the letters need delivering." (Sections 3 and 7)

2. **Revise Branch B, Success at Cost, toll consequence.** The list of what Diehl cannot remember (mother's face, sister's hand, warmth of embraces) is effective but reads as catalog rather than felt experience. Consider the editorial rewrite offered in Section 1: replace the list with a moment of felt absence. (Section 9, outcome ladder)

3. **Vary the hand simile.** Branch B's prose uses "the way a hand moves across a page of text" for Vesik reading Diehl's face, which is the third hand-as-vehicle image in the encounter (after "a hand pressed over a mouth" and "the way a hand reaches into still water"). The first and third are strong; the second should use a different vehicle to avoid pattern fatigue. (Section 11)

### Consider

1. **Give Diehl one more sentence in the Branch A paragraph.** His reaction to the contract breaking is compressed to a single clause. One sentence showing his response to the fog shifting -- not understanding what happened, but feeling it -- would give the Branch A paragraph a more complete witness structure. (Section 11)

2. **Note the pressure knot's uneven prose register.** Paragraphs 1 and 3 of the pressure knot are scene-quality; paragraphs 2 and 4 are briefing-quality. If the author has time, raising paragraphs 2 and 4 to match the surrounding prose would make the pressure knot uniformly strong. (Section 3)

3. **Confirm the outcome ladder's five-tier resolution is mechanically distinguishable.** Ten outcome tiers across two branches is thorough, but the runtime resolver needs to produce detectably different results at each tier. If the mechanical surface of this short encounter cannot distinguish Success from Success at Cost in practice, the author has written phantom tiers. (Section 9)

4. **Concept art recommendation.** The draft does not include a concept art suggestion. For a short encounter this is not a blocker, but the scene is highly visual -- the fog-shrouded limestone river notch, the gaunt ferryman on the dark punt, the courier at the waterline -- and would benefit from a concept art direction note. Suggested subject: Vesik standing on the punt in the fog, pole in hand, seen from the near bank at Diehl's eye level. Palette: grey-white fog, dark wet wood, limestone ochre at the edges. Mood: liminal, still, oppressive.
