The design block honours the brief's binding row. It has star 0.74 → star 0.82, a decline arm at 0.76 inside the 0.72–0.85 band, a `drive` + `place` hand, and the Kindled Ambition card used once. 1. **Every aftermath overview repeated its afterimage** (trigger 22, seam). Nine of the ten arm overviews restated the step-1 afterimage directly above them, often word for word. For example, "The duke believed the warning and thanked them before the whole court" was followed by "The duke believed the warning and thanked {actor} before the whole court." The overviews now carry the *next* fact.
2. **Both critical_failure overviews were false on the step-0 route.** A step-0 critical_failure ends the action. The draft's own self-audit says the arm critical_failure page renders there, but both overviews narrated a step-1 telling that never happened ("The duke had {actor} put out of the hall"; "{actor} let the hard star slip in front of the whole court"). They have been rewritten to be true on both routes. The positive one also never linked "curse" to the Plague Scare chip under it. It now states the plague.
3. **Fragment conflict, plus a detector hit.** The Counsel Caution critical_failure fragment had the mortal read the star "the way the court would want it", which is a kind reading. The base band on that outcome reads it as a sign *against the town*. Because both render together, they contradicted each other. `way` is also a natural indefinite, banned in outcome fields (trigger 15).
4. **Card-fragment echoes of the base band and the spine.** Kindle Duty success_at_cost repeated the base afterimage's "the cathedral's watchmen saw them". Trip the Flatterer critical_failure repeated the spine's "the court cheers every line". Light the Sign critical_failure repeated the afterimage's "curse on the heir".

## 1. Prose Quality

**Opening (P1 setting + two spine paragraphs).** It reads plainly and as a report, but the skeleton is mis-split. P2 ("The cathedral's astrologers have cast… the court is celebrating it.") carries the situation and **no complication**. The complication (the unasked reader, the hard star) and the problem are both packed into P3, which also names `{actor}` three times in four sentences. "rise over the town" is also class scenery in an urban envelope that includes cities.

[EDITORIAL REWRITE]
> {actor} arrives in {location} on the day the duke's heir is born.
>
> The cathedral's astrologers have cast the child's nativity. It promises a long and lucky life, and the court is celebrating it. {actor} was not asked. At the hour of the birth, they saw a hard star rise over {location}.
>
> Tonight {actor} reads the same sky to learn what the star means for the child. The court will hear nothing against the cathedral's chart without proof.

That is 12 + 38 + 26 = **76 words**. P2 now ends on the complication and P3 holds only the test and the obstruction stake.

**Step-1 spines.** Both are good. They are plain, they name the counterparty, and they state the stake ("before the court shouts it down"; "keep the first year out of it"). The negative spine's "{actor} must give a true reading of the child's later years" is true on every step-0 route, and its carryover line ("They are not sure which year to leave out") covers the failure route honestly. No rewrite.

**Afterimages.** Step 0 is strong: each band is one fact, and the gradient from "the month it comes" down to "could not say what kind" is exactly what the carryover lines then price. Step 1 had three weak lines:
- positive failure "The court shouted the warning down" echoes the spine's "shouts it down". [EDITORIAL REWRITE] "The duke would not hear the warning out, and sent them from the hall."
- positive success "the best healers in town" uses class scenery. [EDITORIAL REWRITE] "sent for healers that night."
- positive success_at_cost "named them an enemy of the cathedral" is retold by its overview. [EDITORIAL REWRITE] "The duke believed the warning, but the first astrologer would not look at them again."
- negative critical_failure says "court" twice in one sentence. [EDITORIAL REWRITE] "They let the hard star slip, and the court took it for a curse on the heir."
- step-0 critical_failure "a sign for the town" uses class scenery. [EDITORIAL REWRITE] "a sign against {location}".

**Ambition `narrativeHook`s** asserted "a sickness in … first winter" on both arms. That is false after a step-0 failure, which only knew "a hard year", and the ambition reaches the sheet. [EDITORIAL REWRITE] positive "Read a hard first year in the duke's heir's stars, and means to see the child through it."; negative "Kept the duke's heir's hard first year to themselves, and means to be there when it comes."

## 2. Branch Seduction Audit

- **Warn the duke (courage).** A courageous mortal takes it because the child is in danger and only they know. The god gets the most to play here: two specials, one trips the opposition and one makes the sky itself testify. The arm protects the child and the truth told to power. It pays more (a favour from the duke, +0.08) and risks more: the master's name is staked in front of the court, and a rival is believed instead.
- **Keep the star quiet (prudence, fallback).** A prudent mortal takes it because a court in a panic of loyalty boos doubters out of the hall. The arm is a real master test of its own: tell a true but partial reading to the cathedral's first astrologer's face. It is deal-only, so the god watches more than it shapes, which suits a mortal who chose to hide. It protects the reader's name and the court's joy. It pays the same drive with no favour and a smaller standing gain.
- The asymmetry is real and neither arm is obviously right. Both arms carry the same Plague Scare on failure, so prudence does not buy safety, only a smaller stake. **KEEP 2.**
