## 1. Inspiration Anchors

**Foundation references:**

- **Tonal Bible** -- "Cultural Mosaic with Internal Contradictions" shaped this encounter's district. The community that distrusts outsiders is not monolithically xenophobic; they are a neighborhood that lost people to a plague misattributed to foreign traders two generations ago. The distrust is a wound, not a character trait. The Tonal Bible's "Wonder Layered Over Grief" also informed the healer's presentation: she arrives carrying genuine skill and real compassion, but the district's refusal is also genuine and historically grounded.
- **Thematic Pillars** -- "Compassion vs. Power" is the central axis. The player-god must decide whether to spend divine influence opening a door for someone who may not be welcomed even after entry. "Order vs. Freedom" is secondary: the ward-gate is a local institution that protects the district's autonomy, and overriding it is a meaningful act even when it serves mercy.
- **Anti-Patterns** -- Actively avoiding #8 (Helpful Exposition NPC): the healer is not here to deliver lore. She has her own Maslow-driven need (a dying patient she cannot reach without shrine herbs). Avoiding #9 (Clean Moral Binaries): the gate-warden is not wrong to enforce the boundary. The district's caution has kept them safe before. Avoiding #10 (Player as Savior): the healer can eventually find another source of medicine without divine help; the intervention accelerates and reshapes, it does not rescue from oblivion.
- **Event Archetypes -- "Plague Wind"** -- The Seasonal Plague archetype shaped the backstory pressure. The district's ward-gate exists because of a historical plague event that taught them outsiders bring contamination. This encounter is a residual scar from that older crisis, not an active epidemic. The archetype contributed the idea that disease events reveal community character: who cooperates, who locks the door, who remembers.

**Dilemma Library:** Not consulted -- this is a short linear encounter where the player makes no branching choice. The dilemma energy lives in the outcome ladder, not in a choice fork.

**Anti-patterns actively avoided:**
- Monolithic culture (the district has internal disagreement about the ward policy)
- Helpful exposition NPC (the healer wants medicine, not to educate)
- Player as savior (the healer has fallback options; divine help changes the *terms*, not the *possibility*)
- Generic fantasy aesthetics (the ward-gate is a specific local institution with cultural memory baked into its architecture)

## 2. Scale Justification

This is a **short** encounter because it represents common world texture rather than a story-central crisis. A wandering healer blocked at a neighborhood boundary is the kind of friction that makes a living world feel populated and contested, but it does not justify multiple beats, branching choices, or large rewards. The encounter's value is atmospheric and relational: it seeds a reputation thread with the district, it gives the player a small but real act of compassion-versus-respect-for-local-authority, and it establishes the healer as a named NPC who may recur. One sharp intervention, one outcome ladder, modest consequence. This is correctly sized.

## 3. Pressure Knot

A woman named Maret has been walking the frontier for six years with a mule loaded with dried herbs and poultice kits. She is not famous. She is competent, quiet, and occasionally miraculous in the way that anyone who has treated five hundred infections becomes miraculous. She has arrived at the Thornwall Ward of a settlement where a child is sick with bone-fever -- a treatable condition if she can reach the shrine garden inside the ward, where moonwort grows in the shadow of the altar stones. Moonwort does not grow wild outside shrine precincts.

The ward-gate is closed to outsiders. It has been closed to outsiders for nineteen years, since a group of foreign merchants introduced a lung sickness that killed thirty-one people in a single wet season. The gate-warden, a man named Jorik, lost his mother to that sickness. He is not cruel. He is thorough, and he remembers.

Maret has asked. Jorik has refused. She is sitting on a stone bench outside the gate with her mule, waiting for something to change. The child's family can see her from a second-floor window. They cannot override the warden. The district assembly meets in four days. The child may not have four days.

The player's agent is present in or near this ward.

---

## 4. Intervention Fantasy

The player is a god noticing a small knot of suffering that local institutions are too slow to untangle. The fantasy is intimate and precise: you see a competent healer, a frightened family, and a gate-warden doing his job correctly according to rules that exist for real reasons. The intervention is not smashing the gate or commanding the warden. It is *nudging* -- tilting the social balance just enough that someone acts differently than they otherwise would. The player is threading a needle between mercy and respect for a community's autonomy. The compelling part is the smallness: this is not world-shaking, it is world-*tending*. A god who notices a sick child and a locked gate and chooses to lean on the scales.

## 5. Cast and World Objects

**Primary NPCs:**
- **Maret** -- wandering healer, mid-forties, weathered, patient. Carries a mule loaded with medicine. Needs moonwort from the shrine garden. Not affiliated with any guild or temple. Her reputation precedes her in some villages but not here.
- **Jorik** -- ward gate-warden, late fifties. Lost his mother to the lung sickness nineteen years ago. Enforces the outsider ban with genuine conviction. Respected in the district. Not a villain.

**Supporting NPCs:**
- **The sick child's family** -- visible from the upper window. The mother (unnamed) has already spoken to Jorik and been told to wait for the assembly. She is frightened but has not broken any rules.

**Involved factions/communities:**
- **Thornwall Ward** -- a residential district within a settlement. Self-governing on local matters via a ward assembly. The outsider ban is ward policy, not settlement law.

**Required places:**
- **Ward-gate** -- the physical boundary. A heavy wooden gate with a warden's alcove.
- **Shrine garden** -- inside the ward. Contains moonwort. Tended by a local shrine-keeper (minor NPC, not central).

**Required reward objects / burdens / attachments:**
- On success: minor positive reputation with Maret (she remembers who helped). Minor reputation shift with Thornwall Ward (positive or negative depending on outcome tier).
- On failure: the child's condition worsens (narrative burden on the district; Maret moves on to find moonwort elsewhere, slower).

**Reputation / social-state channels:**
- Thornwall Ward disposition toward the player's agent (local community trust)
- Maret's personal disposition toward the player's agent (individual NPC bond)

## 9. Outcome Ladder

**Critical Success:**
Jorik is moved -- not overridden, moved. Something shifts in the social fabric: a neighbor vouches for Maret unprompted, or Jorik remembers his mother would have wanted the child treated, or the shrine-keeper opens a side entrance and claims precedent. Maret enters the ward with the community's uneasy but genuine consent. She gathers moonwort, treats the child, and is invited to stay overnight. The ward-gate policy is not overturned, but a quiet exception is made and remembered. Maret gains real trust with the ward. The player's agent gains minor positive reputation with both Maret and Thornwall Ward. The family is grateful in a way that carries forward.

**Progress:** Maret treats the child. Moonwort gathered. Ward relationship intact.
**Spent:** Minor divine essence.
**New opening:** Maret may return to this ward. The exception creates a precedent that future encounters can reference. The family owes a debt of gratitude.

**Success:**
Maret is permitted through the gate under escort. Jorik insists on watching her the entire time. The atmosphere is tense but functional. She gathers moonwort, treats the child competently, and leaves before nightfall. The ward remembers that an outsider was let in and nothing terrible happened, but Jorik is uneasy and the next request will be harder, not easier. Maret is grateful but notes the hostility.

**Progress:** Child treated. Moonwort gathered.
**Spent:** Minor divine essence.
**New opening:** Maret remembers the agent. Thornwall Ward's outsider policy is unchanged but tested. No lasting social warmth.

**Success at Cost:**
Maret gets through the gate, but Jorik's authority is visibly undermined in the process. The ward-gate was opened over his objection, and the district noticed. Maret treats the child successfully, but Jorik's standing in the ward assembly drops. He becomes suspicious of the player's agent and of divine interference generally. The ward's internal politics are now slightly destabilized -- the faction that supports the outsider ban feels disrespected, and the faction that wanted the healer in feels emboldened but anxious about having forced the issue.

**Progress:** Child treated. Moonwort gathered.
**Spent:** Minor divine essence. Jorik's trust. Ward social cohesion.
**New burden:** Jorik is now a low-grade antagonist toward the player's agent. The ward assembly's next meeting will be contentious. The cost of mercy was paid in someone else's authority.

**Failure:**
The social fabric holds against the intervention. Jorik stands firm. The neighbors do not break ranks. Maret is turned away. She leaves to find moonwort elsewhere -- a two-day detour to a wild shrine in the hills. The child's condition worsens before Maret returns, but the child survives (bone-fever is slow). The ward remembers that a god tried to push them and failed. Maret is disappointed but not broken. The player's agent loses minor reputation with Thornwall Ward (they tried to override local authority) and gains nothing with Maret (she does not know the intervention happened).

**Progress:** None immediate. Child treated eventually, but later and with more suffering.
**Spent:** Minor divine essence. Minor reputation loss with ward.
**New burden:** Thornwall Ward is now slightly more resistant to future divine interference. The outsider ban feels validated.

**Critical Failure:**
The intervention backfires visibly. Jorik perceives the divine nudge as exactly what it is -- outside manipulation -- and becomes openly hostile. He rallies the ward against both Maret and the player's agent. Maret is driven away from the gate with suspicion that she was a divine agent herself (she was not). The child's family is shamed for having consorted with outsiders. Maret loses her welcome in this entire settlement, not just this ward. The player's agent takes a real reputation hit with Thornwall Ward and a minor one with the broader settlement.

**Progress:** None. Situation worse than before.
**Spent:** Significant reputation. Maret's access to the settlement. The family's standing.
**New burden:** Thornwall Ward is now actively hostile to outside healers. Maret must find a completely different route. The family is isolated within their own community. The ward-gate policy hardens.

## 10. Sample Opening Paragraph

The healer had been sitting on the bench outside the Thornwall gate for three hours when the god noticed her. She was not dramatic about it. Her mule stood tethered to the bench's iron ring, its panniers half-open, bundles of dried comfrey and willow-bark visible in neat rolls. She had her hands in her lap and she was watching the gate the way a woman watches rain she cannot stop. Above her, in a second-story window framed by cracked plaster, a woman held a damp cloth to a child's forehead and looked down at the healer with the particular stillness of someone who has run out of things to try. The gate-warden stood at his post in the alcove beside the heavy oak doors, arms folded, jaw set. He was not enjoying this. He was a man who had buried his mother nineteen years ago because someone from outside the ward had brought a sickness through these same doors, and the rules he enforced were written in a language older than policy. They were written in grief. The healer knew it. She had not argued. She had simply sat down and waited, as if patience were a kind of medicine too, and perhaps it was, but the child upstairs was five years old and bone-fever does not wait.

---

## 11. Linear Continuation

The god leaned into the moment the way wind leans into a flame -- not enough to extinguish, just enough to bend. Inside the ward, something shifted. A neighbor who had been watching from her doorway for the past hour crossed the square and spoke to Jorik in a voice too low to carry. She touched his arm. He looked at her, and then at the bench where Maret sat, and his jaw worked like he was chewing something he could not swallow. The neighbor said something else. Jorik closed his eyes. When he opened them, he pulled the iron bolt and swung the ward-gate six inches open -- no wider -- and looked at the healer with an expression that was not welcome and was not refusal but was something more honest than either. "The shrine garden," he said. "Moonwort. Nothing else. I walk with you." Maret stood and gathered her satchel without haste, as if she had always known the door would open, and perhaps she had. The mule stayed at the bench. She followed Jorik through the narrow gap in the gate and into the Thornwall Ward, and overhead the mother in the window pressed her hand against the glass and did not breathe.

---

## 12. Aftermath Paragraph

By evening, the child's fever had broken. Maret had ground the moonwort into a paste with something from her own kit -- a grey powder she did not name -- and applied it to the joints where the bone-fever settled. The child slept. Maret packed her satchel in the shrine garden while the shrine-keeper watched her from a distance that was not hostile but was not warm. Jorik waited at the gate. He had not spoken to her since the walk in. When she passed through the ward-gate on her way out, he pulled it shut behind her with more force than was necessary, and the bolt rang like a bell in the quiet street. Maret untethered her mule. The family did not come down to thank her; the mother would find her later, at the crossroads market, and press a bag of dried apricots into her hands without speaking, and Maret would accept them the same way. In the ward, Jorik sat in his alcove and stared at the closed gate and thought about his mother, who had also been kind to strangers, and who had died for it, and who would have opened the gate three hours earlier than he did.
