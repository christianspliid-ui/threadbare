**Verdict: PASS WITH REVISIONS.** The game design underneath is sound: the crux is one plain sentence, the choice stake is real (price high and the fellowship cannot pay, low and the merchant wins), the appointment is wired the way the boundary survey proved it, the consequence hand is honest, and every chip noun is a sheet word. - **Trigger 22** (seam echo): opening → step-1 spine.
- **Trigger 17 / gate 8**: two effect lines whose mechanism does not hold (Stretch The Morning; Uncover Hidden Tallies).
- **Trigger 13 / any-subset rule**: a step-0 afterimage that contradicts a card in the same hand.
- **Trigger 26**: the step-1 success turns on a rule the spine never states.
- **Trigger 35**: a conflict on the success_at_cost page, a conflict on the failure page, and a BOND chip that retells the overview.

## 1. Prose Quality

**Opening (P1 + step-0 spine).** Good. Arrival with graph names per class, events with costs already paid (the lease is up, bids are taken today, a richer rival), and one stake shape (choice) stated as two plain costs. The recount is honest: 13 + 32 + 33 = **78** on the rural P1 (the urban P1 is 12). "The abbey's" appears three times in P2–P3; I tried cutting one ("its cellarer") and it made the cellarer the mill's, not the abbey's. Clarity beats compression: kept.

**Step-1 spine.**

- **Seam echo (trigger 22).** P2 ends "a grain merchant with more money than the fellowship"; the step-1 spine opens "offers more rent than the fellowship can pay". Same comparative, same noun, across the seam. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** "{cast:merchant} bids first, and offers a rent the fellowship cannot match."
- **The win is not grounded (trigger 26).** Every success afterimage turns on the merchant being unable to say where his flour came from, and the cellarer striking his bid for it. The spine's rule says only that the mill goes to a bidder "who has dealt honestly with the abbey". A merchant who cannot answer a question has not been shown to be dishonest, so the player cannot see why the bid is struck. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** "…the highest bidder who can show he has dealt honestly with the abbey". Now the burden is on the bidder, and failing to account for the flour is failing the rule. It is a rule, not the answer: *who sold* the flour stays behind the roll.
- "Nobody will say how a merchant can bid so much for a mill whose books show so little." Good. This is the mystery the knowledge record pays, stated plainly. It does not front-load the answer (rule 29).
- The closing stake ("If the fellowship wins … If it loses, {location} will think less of {actor}") is stated as a fact and a cost. Doctrine v2 reversed foreshadow-never-announce; this is the lawful form, and the boundary survey shipped the same shape. Not trigger 25 (no "pass and X / fail and Y" outcome mechanics, only the stake).

**Afterimages.**

- Step-0 success_at_cost: "They found the gap for last year only. The older tallies had been burned." With **Uncover Hidden Tallies** in the hand (a card whose whole act is bringing older tallies down from the loft), this base text contradicts a card the player may have just paid for. Base text must read correctly with any subset of the hand active. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** "They found the gap, but only after the cellarer had opened the bids." The cost moves to time, which also agrees with Stretch The Morning's success_at_cost fragment ("then came out wanting the book back at once").
- Step-1 success_at_cost: "…but held the fellowship to the rent he had offered." The overview then says "short of coin all year to pay *that* rent". On the step-0 path into this band (step-0 success_at_cost, step-1 success), no rent was ever "held", and "that rent" has no antecedent on the page. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** afterimage "The cellarer struck {cast:merchant}'s bid, but only after the fellowship had raised its own twice." Overview "…to pay its rent." See § 6b.
- Step-0 critical_failure: "said the figure aloud where the merchant could hear it". This is a clean, readable game loss, and it explains why the action ends (the merchant now knows the fellowship's ceiling). Kept.
- Step-1 critical_failure: "{actor} accused {cast:merchant} of cheating the abbey and could not prove it." Kept. It is the expert overreaching, which is the right extreme for expert work.

**Band fragments.**

- **Uncover Hidden Tallies** (all four): "A bundle of notched sticks … matched the miller's tallies" / "one notch for every sack the old miller ground". The P3 has already put the old miller's tallies on the table, so finding *a second copy of the same tallies* proves nothing new, and the success fragment ("matched the miller's tallies") says the card did nothing. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** the card finds *older* tallies, so the gap can be checked across years: CS "A bundle of older tallies lay forgotten in the loft, and the gap ran back for years." · S "…and they showed the same gap year after year." · NM "…but mice had gnawed most of them." · F "…but nobody could say which years they counted."
- "nobody" in the Uncover failure fragment and the missed sequel's overview: `nobody` is not in the natural-indefinite list (`someone · somewhere · things · stuff · thing · way · ways · nothing · anything · whatever`). It names an absence plainly. Lawful.
- **Swell The Rival's Pride** failure: "{cast:merchant} boasted only of his money." I first read it as thin. It is right: the card fired, and the vanity went to the wrong subject. Kept.

**Aftermath overviews.** See § 6b.

## 2. Branch Seduction Audit

Linear. The seduction lives in the hand, which answers four different questions:

- **Uncover Hidden Tallies** (matter, Cache): the evidence. More proof, found in the mill itself.
- **Stretch The Morning** (time, Signature): the clock. A longer hour with the books.
- **Read Out The Rule** (order, Signature): the opposition's own law, said aloud so everyone hears the clause that will undo the rich bidder.
- **Swell The Rival's Pride** (spirit, Kindled Ambition): the rival's vanity, which makes him talk. This is the card that says something about the god: it wins by working on a man's want, and its critical_failure fragment shows the price (the fellowship's men shout him down and the bidding closes early).

No card dominates. Price and delta track (1/0.09 against 2/0.11–0.12).

## 6. Aftermath Payoff

Centred on the actor, with faces. The cellarer seals; the merchant is struck; the old miller is the answer to the mystery. Failure is cool and costs standing only. After revision, both failure bands say plainly why an expert's loss costs more ("it sent for an expert so that would not happen"; "{location} remembers an expert's mistake longer than a stranger's").

**Stretch The Morning (trigger 17).** "Keep the cellarer at prayers an hour past the bell" is a hand on the cellarer's will (mind), not on time, and the card is labelled a time Signature. The mechanism does not match the sphere it is priced in. **[EDITORIAL REWRITE]** "Make the hour of prayers run long, so the cellarer leaves the abbey's book open to them with nobody waiting." Now the god stretches the hour; the cellarer behaves as anyone would. The fragments ("stayed at prayers an hour past the bell", "came out of prayers early") read correctly against it. Name words (stretch, morning) do not recur in the effect line.
