# Encounter Pipeline: Flawed Steel
> Scale: medium | Slug: flawed-steel | Pass: revised
> Date: 2026-04-04 | Pipeline version: 1.0

## 1. Inspiration Anchors

### Foundation References

**Tonal Bible** -- Two principles shaped this encounter most directly. *Nostalgic Form, Adult Substance* provides the familiar shape: the wronged forge-master, the mercenary threat at the gates, the apprentice caught between ambition and shame. These are recognizable fantasy beats. The adult substance underneath: the forge-master's reputation is the settlement's defense industry, the apprentice's betrayal was born from economic desperation the guild system created, and the mercenaries have a legitimate grievance -- they paid for weapons that will get their people killed. *Wonder Layered Over Grief* applies to the forge itself: the encounter should open with the beauty of craft before the rotten core is visible. The hot iron, the shaped metal, the years of mastery -- and then the discovery that this mastery was being hollowed out from inside.

**Thematic Pillars** -- *Compassion vs. Power* is the central tension. The forge-master has the power to punish her apprentice and the skill to potentially remedy the situation, but compassion for an apprentice she trained since childhood pulls against the need to satisfy armed mercenaries. *Order vs. Freedom* shapes the mercenary demand: they want either contractual fulfillment (order) or blood-price compensation (a kind of brutal freedom from obligation). *Legacy vs. Reinvention* matters for the apprentice, whose betrayal was an attempt to escape the guild system that bound her to a craft she had come to resent -- or perhaps to fund an escape she never got to make. The *Sovereignty vs. Consumption* canonical reframing is present in how the settlement's dependence on the forge-master's reputation has turned her skill into infrastructure. She is not just a craftsperson; she is the thing that keeps the settlement's defensive supply chain functional. Her identity has been consumed by her usefulness.

**Anti-Patterns** -- Actively avoiding: *Clean Moral Binaries* (#9) -- the apprentice is not a villain, the mercenaries are not thugs, and the forge-master is not a pure victim. The apprentice sold flawed weapons out of a mixture of greed and genuine financial desperation; the mercenaries are professionals whose soldiers will die carrying bad steel; the forge-master failed to notice the sabotage because she was too proud to inspect her apprentice's output. Also avoiding *Player as Savior* (#10) -- the settlement has already begun its own response before the god intervenes. The militia is at the walls. The guild elders are arguing. The god's intervention shapes which pressures win, not whether anyone acts at all. Avoiding *Helpful Exposition NPCs* (#8) -- nobody explains the situation neatly. The god perceives the knotted threads directly.

**Dilemma Content Library (TB-038)** -- This encounter is not an origin dilemma, but the library's structural insight shaped the choice set: choices should reveal what kind of god the player is willing to be, not just what tactic they prefer. The axiological pair most relevant is **Mercy vs. Ruthlessness** (Iron reach) and **Honesty vs. Cunning** (Shadow reach). The two intervention paths correspond to these axes: one path prioritizes justice and transparency (reveal the full truth, hold the apprentice accountable publicly, force honest reckoning), while the other prioritizes pragmatic resolution (manipulate the mercenary captain's perceptions, broker a deal that conceals the worst of the damage, preserve the forge-master's reputation through managed truth). The dilemma library's insistence that choices foreshadow claim is also relevant -- whichever path the god chooses, the forge-master becomes more dependent on divine favor, and that dependency is the beginning of consumption.

### Variety References

**Event Archetype: Betrayal Revealed** -- The structural DNA of the encounter. The betrayal reframes past events (previous weapon sales are now suspect), creates cascading suspicion (who else knew?), and forces a decision between revenge, mercy, and exploitation. The archetype's note that "discovering one betrayer makes everyone suspicious" shaped the aftermath: regardless of resolution, the guild will begin inspecting other apprentices' work, and trust within the settlement's craft infrastructure will take time to rebuild.

**Ordeal Archetype: The Builder's Dilemma** -- The forge-master is a maker whose creations have already gone into the world and cannot be recalled. The flawed weapons are already in the hands of soldiers who trust them. This is not just a scandal; it is a builder's nightmare -- the thing she made (or allowed to be made under her name) will kill people she will never meet. The ordeal's tonal note applies: "The maker knows what will happen. They build anyway."

**Ordeal Archetype: Negotiation Under Pressure** -- The mercenary captain's deadline structures the encounter's urgency. Negotiation here is not abstract diplomacy; it is words spoken while armed soldiers wait outside a gate. The archetype's hook -- "the other side is actually right" -- is central. The mercenaries have a legitimate grievance.

**Character Archetype: The Guild-Bound Artisan** -- Directly shapes the forge-master. Her identity is fused to her guild standing; her skill is both personal pride and political currency. The archetype's tonal note is precise: "honest labor and pride in work, shadowed by institutional gatekeeping."

### What the Inspiration Actually Changed

Without the Betrayal Revealed archetype, this encounter would have been a simple negotiation between forge-master and mercenaries. The archetype pushed the betrayal into the center: the mercenaries are the pressure, but the apprentice's choices are the wound. Without the Builder's Dilemma, the encounter would focus only on the present crisis. The ordeal archetype added the haunting question of weapons already deployed -- the flawed steel that has already been carried into campaigns the settlement knows nothing about. Without the Dilemma Library's axiological framing, the two branches would have been "help" vs. "don't help" rather than two genuinely different philosophies of divine interference.

## 2. Scale Justification

**Medium** is correct for this encounter. The stakes are real but local: a settlement's defensive supply chain, a forge-master's reputation, a mercenary company's justified grievance, and an apprentice's future. The encounter does not reshape regional politics or trigger faction-wide consequences; it reshapes a community's internal trust and one professional relationship. Two to three beats are sufficient to establish the pressure, let the player intervene, and land a meaningful aftermath. The reward weight is moderate -- reputation with local factions, a potential ally or enemy in the mercenary captain, and a forge-master whose future relationship to the god depends on how the crisis was handled. This is the kind of encounter that builds world texture and gives the player a sense of local authorship without claiming to be a signature epic.

## 3. Pressure Knot

The forge-master discovered the betrayal three days ago when a returned mercenary scout brought back a shattered longsword and laid it on her anvil without a word. The blade had snapped at the tang -- the steel mixed with inferior ore, the tempering rushed, the flaw invisible to anyone who hadn't spent thirty years reading the grain of metal. She recognized the work immediately: her apprentice Dalla's hand, but not Dalla's training. Dalla confessed that night. She had been selling a second run of weapons to a mercenary outfit called the Greycloaks for the past season, using the forge's stamp but cutting the alloy with cheaper stock to keep the profit margin. The Greycloaks paid well and asked no questions, and Dalla needed the coin -- her brother owed debts to someone the settlement's law could not reach.

The Greycloaks are now camped outside the settlement's eastern gate. Their captain, a woman named Torve Ashgrip, has sent a formal demand: either the forge produces replacement weapons for every blade sold under Dalla's arrangement -- forty-seven swords, sixteen polearms, and a dozen fighting knives -- or the Greycloaks will take blood-price in the amount they calculate their soldiers' lives are worth. Torve is not bluffing. Three of her scouts have already died carrying Dalla's steel into skirmishes they should have survived. The Greycloaks have a reputation to maintain; if word spreads that they march with bad weapons and do nothing, no armorer will take their contracts seriously again.

The settlement's militia is armed and nervous. The guild elders are meeting and have not emerged. The forge-master has not slept. Dalla is locked in the coal shed, waiting.

## 4. Intervention Fantasy

The player-god perceives this as a tangle of threads pulled taut: the forge-master's pride, the apprentice's desperation, the mercenary captain's cold arithmetic, and the settlement's fear. The intervention fantasy is **shaping which thread breaks first and how the others rearrange around the rupture**. This is not rescue. It is the intimate, specific power of a god who can see which person in the knot is closest to a decision and tilt them toward one truth or another.

The compelling element is precision: the player is not resolving a crisis with a wave of divine power. They are choosing where to apply pressure in a system of human relationships that is already straining. Do they steady the forge-master's hand and help her face the mercenary captain openly? Or do they whisper into the gaps between perception and truth, reshaping what Torve Ashgrip believes about the situation so that a pragmatic settlement deal becomes possible? Either way, the god is shaping a community's reckoning with the discovery that their most trusted institution was compromised from inside.

## 5. Cast and World Objects

### Primary NPCs
- **Maren Ironhewn** -- forge-master, guild elder, settlement's primary weapons supplier. Thirty years at the anvil. Proud, meticulous, and now humiliated by a failure she should have caught. Stone Reach (craft mastery), Heart Reach (mentor relationship with Dalla).
- **Dalla** -- Maren's apprentice of nine years. Talented but economically desperate. Her brother's debts are real; her solution was criminal. Currently locked in the coal shed. Not defiant -- ashamed.
- **Torve Ashgrip** -- captain of the Greycloaks mercenary company. Professional, pragmatic, and furious. She has lost three soldiers to bad steel and she holds Maren's forge responsible. Iron Reach (military command), Gold Reach (contract enforcement).

### Supporting NPCs
- **Dalla's brother (unnamed)** -- exists offscreen as the source of Dalla's financial desperation. His debts are to a criminal network the settlement cannot openly confront.
- **Guild elders** (2-3, unnamed) -- the settlement's craft governance body. They are debating whether to expel Dalla, censure Maren, or handle the crisis quietly.
- **Greycloak sergeant** -- Torve's second, visible at the gate. A physical reminder that armed soldiers are waiting.

### Factions and Communities
- **The settlement** -- a mid-sized community with a functioning craft guild and militia. Depends on the forge for its defensive posture.
- **The Greycloaks** -- a professional mercenary company. Not bandits; they operate on contracts and reputation. Their grievance is legitimate.
- **The craft guild** -- the institutional body that certified Maren's forge and stamps. Their authority is now undermined.

### Required Places and Sublocations
- **Maren's forge** -- the primary location. Anvil, quenching trough, weapon racks, the coal shed where Dalla is held.
- **The eastern gate** -- where the Greycloaks are camped. Visible from the walls.
- **The guild hall** -- where the elders are meeting (referenced, not directly visited).

### Reward Objects, Burdens, and Attachments
- **Reputation: forge-master's trust** -- a local reputation channel. How the crisis is resolved determines whether Maren's stamp carries weight going forward.
- **Burden: guild suspicion** -- if the resolution is public, the guild begins auditing all apprentice work. Institutional friction increases.
- **Potential ally: Torve Ashgrip** -- if the Greycloaks are satisfied, Torve becomes a mercenary contact willing to work with agents the god favors. If unsatisfied, she becomes a regional grievance.
- **Attachment: Dalla's fate** -- Dalla's future (expelled, retained, exiled, or freed) persists as a narrative thread the world can reference.

### Reputation and Social-State Channels
- **Settlement trust** -- how the community views its own craft institutions after the crisis.
- **Mercenary reputation** -- how the Greycloaks view the settlement (reliable or treacherous).
- **Forge-master disposition** -- Maren's personal gratitude or resentment toward the god's intervention.

## 6. Beat Structure

### Beat 1: The Reckoning at the Anvil
**Scene:** The god perceives the forge through divine sight -- the banked coals, the rows of finished weapons bearing Maren's stamp, and beneath the surface, the flawed grain running through dozens of blades that have already left this place. Maren stands at her anvil with Torve Ashgrip's written demand in her hand. Dalla is audible through the coal shed wall, not crying, just breathing. The settlement's militia captain has asked Maren what she wants to do. She has not answered.

**Player action:** The god reads the situation and decides how to intervene. This is the primary choice point.

### Beat 2: The Intervention
**Scene (Branch A -- Forge the Truth):** The god steadies Maren's resolve and tilts her toward public honesty. Maren opens the gate herself, brings Dalla before Torve, and offers a full accounting: what was sold, what was flawed, and what she proposes to do about it. The negotiation is brutal and honest. Torve's anger is real but her professionalism is also real; she can work with someone who admits the fault.

**Scene (Branch B -- Temper the Narrative):** The god whispers into the seams of the situation, reshaping how Torve perceives the scale of the problem. Not a lie -- a managed truth. The god helps Maren present the flaw as a materials defect caught early, not a sustained fraud. Dalla's involvement is downplayed. The Greycloaks receive priority replacement of the weapons most likely to fail, and a contract for future supply at reduced rates. The full truth stays inside the forge.

### Beat 3: The Aftermath
**Scene:** The immediate crisis is resolved (or not, depending on outcome roll). The encounter lands its consequences: Maren's reputation, Dalla's fate, the Greycloaks' disposition, and the settlement's trust in its own institutions.

## 7. Branching Profile

- **Branch depth:** `light`
- **Branch count:** `2`
- **Where branching lives:**
  - `scene prose` -- Beat 2 is entirely different depending on the choice
  - `cast emphasis` -- Branch A centers Dalla and Torve in confrontation; Branch B centers Maren and Torve in negotiation, with Dalla peripheral
  - `outcome ladder` -- different success/failure textures per branch
  - `aftermath` -- different reputation consequences, different Dalla fate
  - `follow-on hooks` -- Branch A seeds a guild reform thread; Branch B seeds a hidden-truth thread
- **Convergence policy:** `stays divergent through aftermath`
- **Primary branching template:** **Reveal / Contain / Displace** (template #6 from encounter-branching-templates.md) -- the hidden truth (Dalla's sustained fraud) can be exposed fully (Branch A) or contained within the forge (Branch B). The third option -- displace -- is deliberately not offered because it would be a weaker branch at this scale. Two strong branches over three uneven ones.
- **Secondary branching template:** **Doctrine Split** (template #1) -- the choice between branches is fundamentally a doctrinal one about what kind of god the player is. Branch A is a god who values honesty and accountability even when it hurts. Branch B is a god who values pragmatic stability and managed outcomes.

### Why These Templates

Reveal / Contain is the structural grammar: the encounter is literally about whether a hidden fraud is exposed or kept contained. Doctrine Split is the moral grammar: the choice reveals the player's philosophy of divine interference, not just their tactical preference. Together they ensure the branches feel different in both structure and meaning.

## 8. Branching Map

### Choice at Beat 1 -> What Changes in Beat 2

**If Branch A (Forge the Truth):**
- Beat 2 scene prose: Maren opens the gate, brings Dalla out, faces Torve directly. The prose is raw and public -- witnesses, shame, professional reckoning.
- Beat 2 cast emphasis: Dalla is present and visible. Her reaction (shame, defiance, or collapse) is part of the scene.
- Beat 2 difficulty: The negotiation is harder because the full scope of the fraud is exposed, but Torve respects honesty. The difficulty axis shifts toward Heart Reach (persuasion through sincerity) and Iron Reach (standing firm under pressure).

**If Branch B (Temper the Narrative):**
- Beat 2 scene prose: Maren meets Torve at the gate alone, presenting the managed version. The prose is controlled and careful -- professional exchange, deliberate omissions, the god's subtle influence visible as a shimmer in perception.
- Beat 2 cast emphasis: Dalla is absent. The scene is between Maren and Torve. The god's manipulation is the invisible third actor.
- Beat 2 difficulty: The negotiation is easier in the short term but carries a Shadow Reach difficulty -- the managed truth must hold. If it cracks, the fallout is worse than honest disclosure would have been.

### Choice at Beat 1 -> What Changes in Aftermath

**If Branch A:**
- Dalla's fate is decided publicly (guild expulsion, mercenary labor-service, or retained under strict probation -- depending on outcome roll)
- Maren's reputation takes a public hit but recovers faster because the response was honest
- The guild begins institutional reform -- auditing other apprentices
- Follow-on hook: guild reform thread (future encounter about institutional overhaul)

**If Branch B:**
- Dalla's fate is decided privately by Maren (quiet dismissal, hidden probation, or something harsher that never reaches the guild's ears)
- Maren's reputation is preserved in the short term but a hidden-mark is planted: the concealed truth
- The Greycloaks are satisfied but have been partially deceived
- Follow-on hook: hidden truth thread (future encounter when the concealment unravels or when Torve discovers the real scale of the fraud)

## 9. Outcome Ladder

### Branch A: Forge the Truth

**Critical Success:** Maren's honesty impresses Torve so deeply that the Greycloaks offer a long-term exclusive supply contract. Dalla is sentenced to work off her debt by serving the Greycloaks as a field armorer for one season -- hard service, but she survives it and returns changed. The guild begins reforming its oversight practices. The settlement's trust in its institutions actually increases because the system worked.

**Success:** Torve accepts the replacement schedule and a reduced-rate future contract. Dalla is expelled from the guild but not exiled. The forge's reputation takes a hit locally but holds with external clients. The guild increases oversight. A clean resolution with moderate cost.

**Success at Cost:** Torve accepts the terms but demands that Dalla be handed over to the Greycloaks for their own justice. Maren agrees -- she has no leverage. Dalla's fate is now in mercenary hands: she will serve as an unpaid laborer until the debt is cleared. The forge's reputation suffers. The guild elders blame Maren for the public spectacle.

**Failure:** Torve rejects the terms. The Greycloaks take a portion of the forge's finished inventory by force -- not a battle, just armed professionals walking past nervous militia and carrying out what they are owed. Dalla remains in the settlement but is now a pariah. Maren's reputation is badly damaged. The guild considers shutting the forge entirely.

**Critical Failure:** The negotiation collapses into violence. A militia member panics. A Greycloak sergeant draws steel. People are hurt. The Greycloaks withdraw but swear a blood-feud against the settlement. Dalla escapes in the chaos and disappears. Maren's forge is closed by guild order. The settlement loses its primary weapons source.

### Branch B: Temper the Narrative

**Critical Success:** Torve accepts the managed version entirely. The priority replacement is completed within the season. The Greycloaks become regular clients at favorable rates. Dalla is quietly relocated to another forge in a distant settlement under a new name. Maren's reputation holds. The hidden truth stays buried -- for now.

**Success:** Torve accepts most of the managed version but remains suspicious. The replacement schedule is agreed. Dalla is quietly dismissed from Maren's service and leaves the settlement. Maren's reputation holds locally. The Greycloaks are satisfied but not loyal.

**Success at Cost:** Torve accepts the deal but something in the managed truth does not sit right. She posts a Greycloak observer at the forge for the duration of the replacement work. Dalla must be hidden more carefully. Maren is under surveillance she did not expect. The concealment holds, but barely, and the stress is visible.

**Failure:** Torve sees through the managed version. She knows she is being manipulated but cannot prove it. She takes the replacement weapons and leaves, but the Greycloaks will never contract with this settlement again. Maren's reputation survives publicly but Torve tells other mercenary companies to avoid the forge. Dalla is expelled quietly. The hidden truth becomes a festering rumor.

**Critical Failure:** The managed truth collapses during the negotiation. Torve realizes the fraud was worse than presented and that the settlement tried to deceive her. She views this as a second betrayal. The Greycloaks demand punitive compensation -- triple replacement or blood-price. The settlement cannot meet the demand. The situation escalates toward the same violence as Branch A's critical failure, but with the added stain of attempted deception.

## 10. Sample Opening Paragraph

The first thing the god noticed was the sound -- not the ringing of hammer on steel that a forge should make in the morning hours, but silence. Maren Ironhewn's forge had gone cold for the first time in eleven years, and the absence of its rhythm left a gap in the settlement's morning like a missing heartbeat. The quenching trough still held water dark with yesterday's scale. Racks of finished blades lined the back wall, each one stamped with Maren's mark, each one a promise the settlement had learned to trust without thinking. On the anvil, where hot steel should have been taking shape, there was only a letter -- Torve Ashgrip's demand, written in a hand steady enough that the ink had not bled once, even on the words that were threats. And through the wall of the coal shed came the sound of breathing: Dalla, who had been Maren's hands and eyes for nine years, sitting in the dark with the knowledge of what she had done settling into her bones like cold iron.

Maren stood with her back to the shed door. She had not turned around since locking it. Her hands, scarred and calloused from three decades of metalwork, hung at her sides with the particular stillness of someone who did not trust what they might do if they moved. Outside the eastern gate, the Greycloaks' cook-fires sent thin smoke above the tree line -- fifty-odd professional soldiers who had marched here not for conquest but for the simple, terrible reason that the weapons they had paid for would break when they were needed most.

The threads of this place pulled taut against each other, and the god could feel each one: Maren's pride, fraying. Dalla's shame, hardening into something that might become defiance if left long enough. Torve's patience, measured in hours. And beneath it all, the question the settlement had not yet asked itself -- how many other blades bearing Maren's stamp had already gone into the world carrying the same flaw, and how many of those blades had already failed in hands that trusted them.

## 11. Branch-Dependent Later Paragraphs

### Branch A -- Forge the Truth (Beat 2 opening)

Something shifted in the forge-master's bearing -- not confidence, not yet, but the particular resolve of someone who has decided that the worst thing is not the truth but the silence around it. Maren Ironhewn crossed to the coal shed, drew the bolt, and told Dalla to stand up. The girl rose with the stiffness of someone who had been sitting in the dark for two days, blinking against the morning light as if it were an accusation. Maren did not speak to her. She walked to the eastern gate, Dalla behind her, and the militia captain -- who had been hoping for instructions that would not come -- stepped aside when he saw the look on the forge-master's face.

Torve Ashgrip was sitting on a camp stool outside her command tent, sharpening a knife that did not need sharpening. She looked up as the gate opened and saw the forge-master walking toward her with a young woman trailing half a step behind, blinking against the light like someone pulled out of a cellar. The forge-master stopped at the edge of the Greycloak camp and said, in a voice that carried without being raised, that she had come to give a full accounting. That the fault was in her forge, under her name, and that the woman responsible was standing beside her. That she would hear whatever terms the captain proposed. Torve set the knife down. She did not smile, but something in her posture eased -- the recognition of someone who had chosen the harder road and could be dealt with accordingly.

### Branch B -- Temper the Narrative (Beat 2 opening)

The god's touch was lighter than breath -- a whisper threaded through the morning air, settling into the gaps between what Maren Ironhewn knew and what she was willing to say aloud. The forge-master's hands steadied. Her jaw set not with the rigidity of confession but with the practiced calm of a craftswoman presenting finished work: here is what happened, here is what I propose, here is why you should accept it. She left Dalla in the coal shed. Some truths held the rest of the structure up the way a tang holds a blade. Pull one and the whole thing comes apart in your hands. This truth -- the full scope of what Dalla had done, the months of it, the number of blades -- was one of those.

Maren walked to the eastern gate alone and asked the militia captain to open it. She carried a ledger and a sample replacement blade, still warm from a night of solitary work at the forge. Torve Ashgrip met her at the camp's edge with the expression of someone who had expected to wait another day. The forge-master presented the situation as she had been guided to frame it: a materials defect in a single ore shipment, caught during quality review, affecting a known subset of the commissioned weapons. Regrettable. Remediable. Already being addressed. She placed the sample blade on the table between them. Torve picked it up, tested the edge against her thumb, and studied the tang where the last one had broken. The steel held. The conversation that followed was professional, measured, and efficient -- two women who understood that commerce runs on the ability to absorb mistakes without burning the relationship that produced them. What Torve did not see, because the god's influence made it easy not to see, was the tremor in Maren's hands when she closed the ledger. The numbers in it were true. They were also incomplete.

## 12. Aftermath Paragraph

### Branch A Aftermath

By evening the Greycloaks had a schedule and the settlement had a wound that would scar cleanly. Maren Ironhewn sat on the step outside her forge with a cup of something she was not drinking, watching the Greycloak camp's fires settle into their nighttime rhythm. The replacement work would take three weeks. Her hands ached with the particular fatigue of someone who had carried a weight with her body that should have been carried by a structure. Dalla was gone -- walked out through the eastern gate under Torve's supervision to begin the season of labor that would become the shape of her debt. The girl had not looked back. Maren had not expected her to. The guild elders had already begun discussing what they were calling "the audit" -- a review of every apprentice's output across every forge in the district. It would be expensive, tedious, and necessary. The settlement would remember this as the season the forge went cold, and what came after. What kind of memory that became depended on what was built from here.

### Branch B Aftermath

The Greycloaks broke camp the following morning, satisfied. Torve Ashgrip shook Maren's hand at the gate and said something about reliability being worth more than perfection, and Maren nodded as if this were a compliment rather than an unwitting irony. The replacement schedule was generous; the reduced rates for future contracts were a concession that would cost the forge, but the forge would survive. Dalla left the settlement two days later, quietly, with a letter of reference that did not mention the reason for her departure. Maren had written it herself, at the kitchen table, with the door closed. It was the kindest thing she had done for the girl, and it was also a lie, and she knew that both of those things were true simultaneously.

The settlement returned to its rhythms. The forge relit. Maren's hammer rang again in the morning hours. But the god could feel, beneath the surface restoration, the hidden weight of what had been contained rather than released. The concealed truth sat in the forge like an ember banked in ash -- not burning, but not cold. Torve Ashgrip would eventually learn how many blades had actually been compromised. Or she would not, and the knowledge would simply live in Maren's hands, in the way she inspected every piece of steel that left her forge with a thoroughness that looked like mastery but felt, to her, like penance.

## 13. Aftermath Reaction Choices

### Branch A Reactions

**"Let the guild learn to govern itself."**
The god steps back and allows the institutional response -- the audit, the new oversight rules, the slow rebuilding of professional trust -- to proceed on its own momentum. This preserves the future thread of guild reform as a community-owned process rather than a divinely managed one. The settlement learns to police its own craft. The god is remembered as the catalyst, not the custodian.
*Future thread preserved: institutional reform. Future thread released: personal relationship with Maren.*

**"Keep the forge-master close."**
The god maintains a thread of connection to the forge-master, offering subtle guidance as she rebuilds her reputation and navigates the guild's new scrutiny. This deepens the divine-mortal relationship: Maren becomes more capable but also more dependent on the god's steadying presence. The forge becomes a place where divine attention lingers.
*Future thread preserved: forge-master as ongoing mortal thread. Future thread released: guild self-governance.*

### Branch B Reactions

**"Let the silence hold."**
The god allows the managed truth to settle into the community's memory unchallenged. The concealment becomes permanent -- or at least as permanent as any lie. Maren bears the weight alone. The god accepts that some interventions leave residue, and this one left a hidden mark in the world that may never surface or may surface at the worst possible time.
*Future thread preserved: hidden truth as latent pressure. Future thread released: honest resolution.*

**"Prepare for the reckoning."**
The god plants a seed of readiness in Maren -- not revealing the hidden truth, but ensuring she is prepared for the day it surfaces. This is the god acknowledging that what was concealed was not destroyed, only deferred. The forge-master begins quietly setting aside resources, building relationships with other mercenary companies, and training a new apprentice with more careful oversight. When the truth comes, she will be ready.
*Future thread preserved: controlled revelation (future encounter hook). Future thread released: indefinite concealment.*
