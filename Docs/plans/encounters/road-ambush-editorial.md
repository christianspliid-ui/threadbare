# Encounter Pipeline: Road Ambush

> Date: 2026-04-05 | Pipeline version: 2.0

## 1. Prose Quality

The prose in this draft is strong. The opening paragraph (Section 10) is the best part of the encounter -- it earns its length with specific sensory detail, rhythmic control, and a clear point-of-view. The driver's blood dripping, the birch bark peeling in long pale strips, the tabards faded to thread-memory -- these are not decorative. They carry the encounter's themes (decay, endurance, institutional failure) in physical detail.

The approach card prose is consistently good. "Shield the Road" and "Turn the Chaos" both read as distinct divine postures rather than mechanical descriptions with flavor text pasted on top. The Step 1 sub-cards maintain this quality: "Bend the Captain" is the standout, with the widening-of-awareness move feeling genuinely divine rather than just telepathic.

### Weak Passages

**Pressure Knot, sentence 2:**
> "Six former soldiers -- they still wear their old company's faded tabards, the dye gone to rust where the stitching has frayed -- have blocked the road with a felled oak and taken positions in the ditch on either side."

This is a solid sentence but the parenthetical about the tabards front-loads information that the opening paragraph delivers better. The Pressure Knot should be tighter than the sample opening -- it is the god's immediate perception, not prose fiction. The tabard detail lands harder in Section 10 where it earns the phrase "insignia was only a memory in thread."

[EDITORIAL REWRITE]: "Six former soldiers have blocked the road with a felled oak and taken positions in the ditches on either side. Their tabards are faded past recognition -- former company men, now road men."

**Branch B aftermath, final sentence:**
> "The sparrows that landed on the spilled grain did not care who had won."

This echoes the Branch A aftermath sparrow image almost verbatim. Reusing the same closing image across two divergent branches undermines the divergence. If both paths end with the same sparrows on the same grain, the branches feel less different than they are.

[EDITORIAL REWRITE -- Branch B closing]: "The waymarker stones stood in their places, indifferent as milestones always are. The thread the god had planted in a broken soldier's mind was lighter than spider-silk and stronger than anything on that road."

**Intervention Fantasy (Section 4), final sentence:**
> "The god chooses: does divine force flow into the defense of the caravan, or does divine force exploit this chaos for the god's own strategic purposes?"

This reads like a briefing summary rather than intervention fantasy prose. The rest of Section 4 successfully conveys the visceral feel of Iron-reach attention. The final sentence retreats into a rhetorical question that sounds like a design document.

[EDITORIAL REWRITE]: "The god chooses where the weight falls. Every raised blade on this road is waiting for permission to land."

**Outcome Ladder, Branch A Critical Success, final sentence:**
> "The waymarker stones seem to stand a little straighter in the afternoon light, as if the road remembers what it was for."

This is borderline. It risks being too whimsical -- stones standing straighter is dangerously close to a fantasy cliche (the land itself responding to good deeds). The Threadbare aesthetic favors worn objects that remain worn, not objects that subtly rejuvenate.

[EDITORIAL REWRITE]: "The waymarker stones caught the afternoon light the way they always had. The road did not know it had been defended. It did not need to."

## 2. Branch Seduction Audit

### Moral Asymmetry
**Branch A (Shield the Road):** The morally comfortable choice. Protect the innocent. But the draft does real work to complicate it -- the cost line about pushing the bandits' desperation down the road to someone else is genuine. Still, this is the branch most players will feel good about choosing.

**Branch B (Turn the Chaos):** The morally uncomfortable choice. Exploit suffering for strategic gain. The draft earns this branch's existence by making the exploitation feel *rational* rather than evil -- the god is not cruel, the god is efficient, and Dragan-as-asset is a genuinely interesting future hook. The moral residue (someone may die of neglect while the god harvests) is properly weighted.

**Verdict:** Slight asymmetry favoring Branch A as the "good" choice, but within acceptable bounds. The draft's self-audit on this point is honest. Branch B is seductive for players who enjoy strategic godhood.

### Dramatic Asymmetry
**Branch A:** The drama is in the defense -- watching the tide turn, the young soldier lowering his sword, Dragan's backward glance. This is satisfying dramatic structure.

**Branch B:** The drama is in the manipulation -- the manufactured pause, the god shopping through wreckage, Dragan's mind cracking open. This is more original dramatic structure. The "god as opportunist" fantasy is less common than "god as protector."

**Verdict:** Well balanced. Branch B is arguably more dramatically interesting, which compensates for Branch A's moral comfort.

### Information Asymmetry
Both branches provide roughly equivalent worldbuilding information (the road's condition, the merchant economy, the bandit company's history). Branch B offers slightly more through the sealed letters and merchant ledger -- but this is outcome-dependent, not guaranteed.

**Verdict:** Acceptable. No significant asymmetry.

### Prose Asymmetry
Branch A's resolution prose (Section 12) is excellent -- the guard finding her footing, the driver wrapping the reins around the brake lever, Dragan's backward glance. Branch B's resolution prose is also strong -- the manufactured stillness, the god's attention wandering to the cracked crate, Dragan's awareness of something watching.

**Verdict:** Both branches receive equal prose attention. No favoritism detected.

### Aftermath Asymmetry
Branch A aftermath is warmer (sparrows feeding, the road quiet, an open question about safety). Branch B aftermath is cooler (the cobweb-thread metaphor, Soraya's efficient grief, the road unchanged). Both are tonally appropriate to their branches.

**Verdict:** Well matched. The tonal difference serves the branches rather than favoring one.

**Overall Branch Seduction:** PASS. Both branches are genuinely seductive for different player temperaments. Neither is a trap choice.

## 3. Branch Count Assessment

Two branches for a short-scale encounter is correct. The draft's rationale for omitting the Abandon branch (Section 7) is persuasive: at short scale, two sharp branches serve better than three where one would be thin. The encounter's Iron-reach focus makes both branches expressions of force (protective vs. acquisitive), which gives the two-branch structure thematic coherence rather than making it feel like a missing option.

**Verdict:** PASS. Branch count is right for scale.

## 4. Scale Discipline Check

Short scale requires: immediate stakes, local consequences, one or two beats, modest reward weight. This encounter delivers on all counts.

- Stakes: a single caravan, a band of six, one stretch of road. No kingdom-level consequences.
- Beats: one branch-selection beat, one resolution beat. Clean two-step structure.
- Reward weight: a trade contact or a potential recruit. Not a faction alliance or a regional power shift.
- Time compression: the encounter resolves in the time it takes a fight to end.

The opening paragraph (Section 10) is four paragraphs long, which is generous for short scale. However, the prose density is justified -- it is establishing the physical space and the full cast in a single scene-setting pass, not padding. The encounter does not try to be bigger than it is.

**Verdict:** PASS. Scale discipline is clean.

## 5. Inspiration Anchor Honesty

Section 1 ends with "What the Inspiration Actually Changed," which is a genuine accounting. Three specific claims:

1. Without Sovereignty vs. Consumption, the bandits would be generic robbers. **Confirmed.** The framing of the bandits as waste product of a military economy is visible throughout -- their faded company tabards, Dragan's maimed hand from a war he fought for someone else, the fact that they are soldiers without a lord. This is not surface dressing; it shapes Dragan's entire character and makes Branch B's recruitment angle possible.

2. Without the Dilemma Library's Justice vs. Mercy axis, Protect would be a simple save-the-merchant action. **Confirmed.** The approach card prose for "Shield the Road" explicitly asks what the god is defending (the merchant? the principle of safe roads? the driver's life?) rather than presenting a clean rescue.

3. Without the Anti-Patterns guard, the encounter would collapse into a smite-the-evil scene. **Confirmed.** The bandits remain human throughout. Dragan's backward glance in the Branch A resolution is the proof -- he is allowed to leave with dignity and complexity, not destroyed.

**Verdict:** PASS. The anchors demonstrably shaped the encounter rather than being retroactive justification.

## 6. Aftermath Payoff

### Branch A Aftermath
The aftermath works. Soraya kneeling beside her driver, assessing the wound with practical competence, then looking down the road with anger competing with fear -- this is character-specific and emotionally grounded. The waymarker stones as patient witnesses is a good recurring motif. The grain-and-sparrows image is a strong closer (though it should not be reused in Branch B -- see Prose Quality above).

The open question at the end -- whether the road is safer or just empty -- is the right kind of ambiguity for a short-scale encounter. It does not over-promise future consequences but leaves a thread visible.

### Branch B Aftermath
The aftermath is more tonally complex and succeeds. The fight evaporating rather than ending, Soraya's efficient grief over the sealed letter, Dragan's band withdrawing without a signal -- all convey the manufactured quality of the resolution. The cobweb metaphor for the divine thread is the single best image in the encounter.

The final note -- the waymarker stones indifferent -- correctly conveys that the road itself is unchanged by the god's exploitation. The world did not notice.

### Reaction Choices
Both branches offer two reaction choices each. For short scale, this exceeds requirements (reaction choices are mandatory at medium+ only). The choices are well-drawn: community recovery vs. divine patronage (Branch A), release vs. maintain (Branch B). Each clearly explains what future threads it preserves or releases.

**Verdict:** PASS. The aftermath lands in both branches.

---

## 7. Dilemma Energy

The central dilemma -- protection vs. exploitation -- is genuine. Neither branch is obviously correct for a god-character. Branch A is morally comfortable but strategically passive (the bandits' root problem remains). Branch B is morally uncomfortable but strategically rich (Dragan-as-asset opens future narrative).

The dilemma is sharpened by the Iron-reach constraint: both branches are expressions of force, which means the choice is not between force and mercy but between two *kinds* of force. This is more interesting than a simple fight-or-talk binary.

The Step 1 sub-choices within each branch add secondary dilemma energy: subtle reinforcement vs. psychological intimidation (Branch A), material extraction vs. strategic recruitment (Branch B). These are lighter dilemmas but they give the player a second moment of meaningful choice within their chosen path.

**Verdict:** PASS. The dilemma has real teeth.

## 10. Revision Summary

### Must Fix
1. **Branch B aftermath closing image** -- the sparrow-on-grain image is reused from Branch A. Replace with a Branch B-specific closer that reinforces the exploitation/thread theme. (See rewrite in Prose Quality.)

### Should Fix
2. **Pressure Knot tabard parenthetical** -- tighten the second sentence. The tabard detail lands better in the opening paragraph; the Pressure Knot should be leaner. (See rewrite in Prose Quality.)
3. **Intervention Fantasy closing sentence** -- replace the rhetorical question with a line that stays in the visceral Iron-reach register. (See rewrite in Prose Quality.)
4. **Outcome Ladder Branch A Critical Success closer** -- the waymarker stones "standing straighter" is too whimsical for Threadbare. Replace with something that lets the stones remain indifferent. (See rewrite in Prose Quality.)

### Consider
5. The Pressure Knot's description of Dragan's maimed hand ("three fingers on his shield hand... the stump has healed badly") could be sharpened. "Healed badly" is vague -- a specific detail (the skin puckered white, or the remaining fingers curling involuntarily) would be more Threadbare.
6. The concept art palette mentions "a single bright point: the crossbow bolt" -- this is good direction but the bolt's fletching color is never specified. A small detail, but the art direction skill will want it.
