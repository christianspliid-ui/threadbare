# Salt — brainstorm companion

Companion to [the plan](2026-10-26-thr-2069-salt.md). It records the options weighed and the tensions, so the plan can state only the decision.

## Add a good, or re-pick the good

- **Re-pick (fish, ore, grain).** This is cheapest in code. But the two smuggling scenes are about untaxed salt and an excise post, and each would need rewriting. The war-aims and purse plans' player text would still name salt, and about 155 prose lines across the content mention it. Rejected under Law 45: the world would keep saying salt and never have it.
- **Add salt.** The resource web is all `Record<string, …>`, and every economic reader keys on the class, so one good is a few table rows. Chosen.

## Which class

- **Staple.** Salt keeps people alive through winter. But staple means food: hunger, famine riots and the harvest's staple writes would all start reading salt, and *Bless the Harvest* would swell it. Salt would join the town's food balance and a salt shortage would starve a town. That is too strong, and it collides with [hunger and unrest](2026-10-16-thr-1934-hunger-and-unrest.md).
- **Luxury.** It yields more to the purse (0.4), but luxury goods are anomaly-rare here, and salt is a common trade good.
- **Strategic.** This is the class of ore and timber: traded, valuable, and something a faction wants to hold. It is not food. *Reveal the Vein* can surface it, since it is non-staple. Chosen.

## Where it is found

- **Coast and desert only** (salt pans by the sea, desert flats). This is the most iconic choice, but the measurement killed it: no place stands on those terrains on three medium seeds, so salt would exist in no town.
- **Mountains** (rock salt). Mountain towns are plentiful (27–57 a world), and every one would get salt, because a place gets every good its terrain lists. Salt would be as common as stone, never scarce, and pointless to hold.
- **Steppe and plateau** (dry lake beds, salt licks), plus the deserts and coast for larger maps. On these three seeds that is 14–21 towns a world, clustered by terrain. Clustered is good for a regional monopoly. Chosen.
- **A presence chance per place** was considered so that salt could also sit on common terrains at a lower rate. Rejected: it needs a new field and a new draw for one good (Law 42), and the terrain choice already gives the spread.

## Quantity

The first try (20–45) gave every salt town an *adequate* tier. No town had a surplus, so no road carried salt. Roads carry only surplus goods. At 40–75, 5–7 towns a world have a surplus and 14–19 roads carry salt. That is visible, and it does not dominate: stone still leads more roads.

## Smuggled salt and the monopoly

A town that gets smuggled salt now has a salt bag entry. The monopoly's bearing index counts any good with quantity above 0, so that town becomes a salt place in the region. When the holder does not hold the town, its share drops. We could have excluded brought-in stock. We kept it instead: smugglers denting a monopoly's hold is exactly their story.

## Which scenes bring salt in

Only the ford. The hamlet there *has gone short of salt*, and the train brings it in. In Smuggle Goods, the salt is bought in town and sold at a mill outside the walls, so crediting the town would invert the scene (the intent judge's run-1 finding). That half of the research row closes as no write. Christian can veto this on its own.

## Tension noted, not solved here

No regional monopoly of any good formed in 30 days on the three seeds. The monopoly chip's example (*"Holds the region's salt"*) may still be rare in play. That is about how monopolies form, not about salt, and this plan does not touch it.
