The Premise

You are a young god.
The world below is already alive.

Thousands of mortals live out their days. A handful carry a thread that, pulled at, will remake the age. You find them. You shape them. You watch what they do with the lives you've touched.

"You did not make this world. You will not outlast it. The threads that pass between — those are yours."
How It Plays

Uncover. Shape. Witness.

Threadbearer is turn-based. Nothing moves until you let it. Each turn is yours.

01 · Uncover

Your world is full of people.

You listen.

A scholar finds a word that shouldn't exist. A swordbearer wakes the morning of a duel she won't survive. A refugee crosses a border that no longer exists.

You pick one. You follow her.

02 · Shape

You don't command.

You whisper a dream.

You send a sign at the crossroads. You place an old coin where she'll find it. You let rain fall on the night she rides out.

She decides what to do with it. She always does.

03 · Witness

Seasons later, she comes home.

Her left hand is hidden.

The scholar has started a heresy. The refugee has founded a new faith in a cold place.

The thread you pulled is now a seam in the world. You choose the next one.

What you get

A world that lives

Threadbearer isn't a game with a world attached. It's a world with a game you discover inside it.

✦

Your world is yours alone.

A new world every time. New peoples, new gods, new cycles. The same seed gives you the same world — so you can share one. No two runs ever play out the same.

✷

Failure is a turn, not a loss.

Your swordbearer loses the duel. She doesn't die on a screen — she wakes in a cell, with a pact she shouldn't have made and a debt that will follow her home. That's not a game over. That's the next chapter.

✵

You whisper. They choose.

You're a god, not a general. No units, no abilities. You send a dream. You plant a doubt. You withhold a sign when a mortal begs for one. They decide. You decide what they get to see.

✹

Prose, not spreadsheets.

No stat blocks. No hit points. A merchant isn't Gold: 7 — she's a shrewd trader whose ambition outruns her caution. The world is told to you. You read your characters the way a novelist reads hers.

Your Protagonists

The uncut gem.

A world has thousands of people. Most live quiet lives — bakers, soldiers, farmers counting rainy springs. A few are different. A few carry something that, pulled at, changes everything.

You find them by paying attention. The apprentice who asked the wrong question. The exile who crossed the wrong border. The child who saw what others couldn't.

You don't save them. You follow them. You pull when the moment matters.

You start with one. You grow to two. At the peak — perhaps five, their stories braiding together like chapters of the same book.
Your Hand

What a god can do.

You can give her a dream the night before she decides. You can send a crow at the right window. You can let the rain fall on the night she rides out. You can place an old coin where she'll find it.

Every whisper costs something. Every whisper can be misread. Some of your best moments will be failures you didn't see coming.

Your reach widens with the age. What starts as a nudge can become a prophecy across a generation — a curse that outlives the one who spoke it.

Dreams, signs, withholdings, visions. A vocabulary of divine hands across a long run.

The Weather

The world doesn't wait.

Kingdoms rise while you watch a single baker. Wars start in languages you aren't listening to. A plague takes a city three seasons' walk from anyone you care about.

Your protagonists live in this weather. The scholar's library is requisitioned. The mercenary stops hearing news from home. The exile finds the border he crossed no longer exists.

You can't stop the storm. You can be a hand at a shoulder when it lands.

Factions, guilds, sieges, trade routes, plagues — a living substrate your protagonists live inside.
"Eight spheres of creation. Four hidden foundations. One age, and everyone alive inside it."
The Cycle

Every age turns.

The world has a shape. It begins, it frays, it ends. The fraying doesn't come at you as a countdown. It comes as texture — harvests that fall short, prophets that stop sleeping, a moon that looks slightly wrong.

You can't stop it. You probably shouldn't try. What you can do is choose whose hands hold the last light, and whose story survives into what comes after.

When the cycle closes, the threads you pulled are the seams of the age that follows.

Seven Unmaking archetypes — each a distinct shape an age can end in. The Breach. The Convergence. The Failing. Each changes how the world speaks as it closes.
Under the Hood

What makes it work

Threadbearer's world is built on systems that talk to each other. Nothing is scripted. Everything is deterministic. The story you get is the story the simulation grew.

A living cosmology

Eight spheres of creation. Four hidden foundations at the edges — old magic, waiting in ruins for someone patient enough to find it. The stars are not decoration.

Turn-based, always

Nothing happens until you let it. The kettle stays warm. Read a scene twice. Walk away and come back. The world is patient.

Seeded and shareable

Every world is reproducible. Share a seed, play the same world. Your choices inside it won't be anyone else's.

Graph-native

Every mortal, oath, curse, and trade route lives in one graph. If the world can feel it, the game can reason about it. That's how stories come out emergent instead of scripted.

Procedural, but coherent

Real climate. Rivers that flow downhill. Mountains that make sense. Every world new. Every world holds together.

Stories with shape

Every age bends toward an ending it already carries. The world's voice thickens as the age closes. You don't race a clock. You live inside one.

"What kind of god will you be — to them?"
