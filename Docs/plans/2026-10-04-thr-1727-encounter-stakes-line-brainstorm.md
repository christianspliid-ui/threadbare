# Brainstorm companion — Encounter stakes line (THR-1727)

Companion to `Docs/plans/2026-10-04-thr-1727-encounter-stakes-line.md`. Records the alternatives and tensions from the attended design session with Christian, 2026-10-04.

## The problem as found

On The Unsafe Bridge, the veil's top carried two texts that did not know about each other: a hand-written summary that restated the scene prose below it, called the mortal "the traveler", and explained the god's levers; and a stock motive line ("Vara was simply here when it started.") with no information in its `chance` variants. Christian asked for a formula that works across encounters.

## Options considered

1. **No summary at all.** Let the scene prose open the screen. Cheapest; but the player then reads a paragraph before learning what is at stake. Rejected by Christian (decision A).
2. **Tightened hand-written blurb.** Style rules on the existing `description`. Keeps authoring freedom but has no shape a validator can hold, so it drifts again. Rejected.
3. **Stakes line from structured parts (chosen).** `[lead], [actor] must [goal] — or [risk].` Short, one shape everywhere, assembled with the real mortal's name, and the parts are reusable on other surfaces.

## Tensions and how they were settled

- **Free second clause vs a fixed "— or" fork.** A free clause reads more naturally in some encounters; the fork always names what the hand decides. Christian chose the fork (decision B). Consequence recorded in the plan: `risk` is the cost of failure, not the alternative option. The first example shown in chat used the bridge's ford as the risk; under the rule, the ford is a choice inside the scene and the risk is going into the river.
- **Opening line only vs also an ending line.** An ending line costs two more authored fields per encounter (past-tense forms) but gives the Chapter Ledger, badge and thread row a finished-story line without new prose. Christian chose the ending line (decision C).
- **Second person.** The `divine` lead ("Led here by your hand") uses "your"; Law 42 allows second person for the god's own actions. The `goal`/`risk` parts never use it, because the stakes are the mortal's.
- **Motive line kept separate vs folded in.** Folding the motive into the lead clause turns a floating stock sentence into the start of the stakes sentence. The `chance` source, previously contentless, now names the place.
- **Result line detail.** Tempting to say what the cost was ("at the cost of her pack"); rejected because the consequence chips own state claims (Law 56) and a duplicate would drift from them. The result line names the ending only.

## Vision premises

No Vision premise changes. The plan serves the existing premise that the player is a god watching mortal stories and must be able to follow each one (Law 55).

## Revision from the Vision audit (same day)

The first draft mapped both failure bands to one `lost` form written from `risk`. The Vision auditor checked the bridge's authored endings: plain failure is the long ford, critical failure is the river. One form would have put "went into the river" in the ledger while the screen said the ford took the day — a false retelling. Revised: `risk` names the worst ending (the stake shown up front), `lost` and `lostBadly` are written from the template's own failure and critical-failure endings, and fork arms that pursue a different goal carry their own past-tense forms.
