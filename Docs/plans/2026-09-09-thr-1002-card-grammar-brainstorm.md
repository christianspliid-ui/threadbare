# Brainstorm — one card grammar (THR-1002)

*Companion to `2026-09-09-thr-1002-card-grammar.md`. What was weighed, in the order it came up, 2026-09-09.*

**How this started.** Christian, 2026-08-06, from a screenshot: the action cards are too verbose, what they do is hard to understand, and playing one gives no feedback he can see — and all cards in the game should share one syntax, layout and language. The ticket sat In Design for three weeks unassigned. Two of its premises had moved by the time it was picked up.

**The two premises that moved.** THR-998 shipped on 12 August: the cast line now reads the effective difficulty and names the scale where the floor capped the price, so the ticket's *"this blocks THR-998"* is history. And Prose Doctrine v2 (25 August) struck the flavour quote by name; THR-1225 deleted `StepNudge.fiction` with a *do not reintroduce* comment and 767 strings. The nudge card's zone 7 is a comment now. So Christian's *"one line flavour on action cards is fine"* from 6 August conflicts with his own doctrine of 25 August, on exactly the axis this ticket unifies.

**Which ruling wins.** Later and broader: the doctrine is game-wide (*prose does the scene, cards do the rules*), the 06 line was a permission, not a requirement, and a card with prose beside a card without it is two grammars. Decided: no flavour on the action card; its one line is the effect line. Rejected: a flavour field on the template — a 464-importer edit for prose the doctrine retired. Rejected: honouring the 06 line by re-adding the quote to *both* cards — that reopens Doctrine v2 by the back door. The handoff says this plainly and invites a veto; if it comes, it is a doctrine change, and the answer is still not a card field.

**The open question the ticket named — what the odds pips measure on a cast.** Law 10, after THR-972: *pips mean only "effect on the odds", everywhere*. A nudge has a delta; a cast has a roll. So the pips do not port, and that is not a gap — it is the law working. The nudge test panel already has the honest reading for a roll: the forecast tier word, `doomed … fated`, from one classifier. The action card's odds zone is that word. Rejected: bucketing the three risk words on the resolved probability — THR-998 measured it collapsing 85% of the list onto one word and rejected it; but the tier word does not collapse the deck the same way, because it differentiates the classes that matter: a soul-verb reads `fated`, a local working `favorable`, a regional one `perilous`, a cosmic one `doomed`, and as the god deepens the local ones spread. Rejected: keeping the three-word risk sentence beside the tier — two vocabularies for one quantity is the THR-972 confusion again.

**One size, one row.** The fan of 160×110 thumbnails that pops a 400×560 portrait is a different layout language from a scrolling row of 210px cards, and the directive says *same rough layout*. Rejected: keep the fan and restyle the thumbnails — the focused frame is where the verbosity lives. Rejected: a focused size at 1.5× — the face is complete at one size, and a second size is a second thing to keep in step. The drawer gets the nudge hand's row and a Cast button; select, then cast — arm then fire, which is Law 48 and the nudge stage's own shape.

**Extract, don't imitate.** The first draft had the action card re-implement the nudge card's zones with the same primitives. That is a grammar by convention, and conventions drift. Extracting the zone stack into `CardFace` makes the grammar structural: both cards render the same component, and the nudge card's snapshot pins that its DOM did not move. The ticket rules out changing the nudge card; a refactor that provably renders it unchanged is not a change.

**The keyword.** The nudge chip says the card's *type* from a build-enforced union. An action's type is its verb — the rulebook's five: Create, Find, Change, Destroy, Control — and `crudType` is already on the template. Rejected: reach as the keyword (it is an icon class of its own, Law 9, and rides in the chip row). Rejected: the sphere (same). The scale is a second, muted chip: it is a real concept the card must not hide (it replaces `{n} hex` and the scale sentence), and the chip has a `muted` prop for exactly a secondary fact.

**Upkeep.** `0.5 force/tick` is a numeral; the nudge card's cost-channel row is the shape. A per-tick fraction is not pip-shaped, so it is banded into three words. Rejected: pips of the per-tick cost — sub-unit magnitudes do not read at 5% steps.

**Detection risk.** Dead on the only live builder since THR-501; its test asserts a number production never makes. Deleted from the face and the slot. The eight authored values are `computeDetection`'s data, not the card's — they go only if that function has no caller, and the executor decides that with a grep, not this doc.

**Feedback: content, not tier.** The measurement: 28 of 30 holdable cards can never reach the modal, and the toast says `Your <name> held.` Rejected: lower the modal gate — a modal per cast turns the aftermath into an interruption, against the core loop. Rejected: a third tier between toast and modal — a new surface for a sentence. Decided: the toast carries the overview the resolver already wrote, and the card wears the fate word on its face — the ending in the chrome of the beginning (Law 37). The gate does not move.

**The agent-hand hole.** Found while measuring: the drawer gates the Effect block on `technicalEffect`, and the agent hand's 44 templates have none because their assembly skips the overlay — so on the surface where most casts happen, THR-728's and THR-998's whole readout never rendered. The new face reads the slot, not the block, so the hole closes by construction; the overlay is applied anyway so the codex has the text.

**Not taken.** Re-pricing template difficulties (THR-736 anti-pattern; THR-998 rules it out). Any edit to the nudge card's rendering. A new node, edge, field or trace type. Emoji-free layer tabs were taken — they are on the same drawer and the taste profile forbids them.
