# Sovereignty vs Consumption — Canonical Doctrine

> **Date:** 2026-04-02
> **Status:** Canonical design doctrine
> **Scope:** The First, threads, claim, sponsorship, legitimacy, quintessence, convergence, ascension, moral framing
> **Authority:** When future design docs conflict with this one on narrative or moral direction, this document wins unless explicitly superseded by a later canonical doctrine.
> **Related:** `2026-03-26-meet-the-first-design.md`, `2026-04-02-agent-success-redesign-roadmap.md`, `2026-04-02-economy-consequence-second-pass-design.md`, `2026-04-02-encounter-redesign-guidelines.md`

## Summary

This document defines the central moral and dramatic truth of **The Fantasy World Simulator**.

The game is not about a benevolent god guiding a chosen hero toward clean triumph.

It is about:

- a god choosing a mortal
- a thread bringing power, sight, luck, and intervention
- success drawing sponsorship, legitimacy, and claim
- the world hardening around that mortal as factions and gods try to define what they are for
- the danger that divine favor is not love, but hunger

The core question is not:

**"Can The First become powerful?"**

The core question is:

**"Can The First become powerful without being consumed?"**

This is the axis that should govern future design decisions for:

- The First's arc
- faction courtship and contest
- sponsorship and legitimacy
- low and high quintessence
- rival god interference
- convergence and charged places
- ascension and end states

---

## The Core Thesis

Power in this world tends toward predation unless it is consciously broken, refused, or transcended.

The gods are not fundamentally separate from mortals.

They are ascendant mortals who rose high enough to gain power over others, and now compete through:

- threads
- sponsorship
- legitimacy
- omen
- manipulation of circumstance
- rivalry over what mortals and places will become

From the mortal side, divine favor can initially feel like protection, destiny, blessing, or meaning.

But the deeper truth is darker:

**divine favor is often hunger, not love.**

---

## The Thread

The thread is the first and deepest sign of claim.

It gives the god:

- line of sight
- line of insight
- the ability to supervise, watch, and intervene

It gives the mortal:

- strange luck
- significance
- access to possibility
- a life that begins to matter more than ordinary lives do

The thread is therefore both:

- gift
- danger

It is the beginning of the entire arc.

---

## The First

The First is called *The First* from the player's point of view because they are the godling's first true mortal thread and first deep investment.

The expected dramatic sequence is:

1. The god chooses The First through the meeting vignette.
2. A thread is formed.
3. The god gains sight and insight into that mortal.
4. The First develops an ambition and begins to pursue it.
5. With grit and divine support, The First achieves enough success to be noticed.
6. A faction or institution begins with invitation and sponsorship.
7. Sponsorship hardens into claim, contest, and possible corruption.

This pacing matters.

The First is not born into faction claim.
They are first born into divine attention.
Faction claim arrives after action, ambition, and early visible success.

---

## Visible Claim vs Hidden Claim

Claim operates on two layers.

### Visible Claim

What the world openly says about The First.

This includes:

- sponsorship
- legitimacy
- titles
- roles
- public significance
- being invited into institutions

These are the front-stage, seductive forms of claim.

### Hidden Claim

What actually binds, distorts, or consumes The First underneath the surface.

This includes:

- dependency
- pressure
- obligation
- symbolic capture
- convergence
- manipulation
- being turned into instrument, vessel, or role

Future systems should preserve this split:
the visible layer attracts the player and mortal alike;
the hidden layer reveals the danger.

---

## The Primary Forms of Claim

The two primary visible forms of claim in this game are:

- **Sponsorship**
- **Legitimacy**

These should be treated as the main public currencies of social and divine power.

### Sponsorship

Narratively:

- an offer
- an opening
- access to a future

It begins attractively.
It says:

- "join us"
- "we can help you become more"
- "you belong somewhere now"

It is seductive because it gives support without appearing, at first, to demand ownership.

### Legitimacy

Narratively:

- public meaning
- right to act
- a recognized place in the world

It is dangerous because the world names The First before The First fully knows what they are becoming.

In this game, legitimacy usually comes **before** true inner completion.
The world names the agent early, and they grow into or against that meaning.

---

## The Seduction and Danger of Claim

The most seductive forms of claim should be:

- sponsorship
- legitimacy

The danger is not merely that they restrict freedom.
The danger is that they turn a person into something load-bearing.

### Sponsorship becomes dangerous when

- it paints a target on the agent
- rivals begin contesting the agent through institutions, agents, and omens
- it curdles into dependency

### Legitimacy becomes dangerous when

- the role matters more than the person's wishes
- rivals target what the person represents, not just the person
- the city, faction, or divine struggle begins reorganizing around them

---

## Dependency

Dependency is not one of the main front-stage currencies.
It is the dark underside of claim.

Dependency should read as:

- diminished confidence
- easier indirect manipulation
- increased need for signs, support, miracles, or institutional scaffolding
- greater risk of being shaped for another power's purposes

Dependency is especially important at **low quintessence**.

It is one of the clearest signs that:

- the agent's usefulness is being extracted
- the fall from grace has begun
- a sponsor or god is no longer elevating the agent, but spending them

---

## Quintessence as Integrity of Self

Quintessence is not merely hit points or magical fuel.

Narratively, quintessence is:

- power
- resilience
- coherence
- integrity of self
- resistance to being hollowed out

### Low Quintessence

Low quintessence should mean:

- reduced confidence
- easier indirect manipulation
- priorities becoming easier to bend
- choices feeling less and less like the agent's own
- possible dependence on signs, miracles, or stronger powers

This is one of the main story meanings of "falling from grace."

### High Quintessence

High quintessence should mean:

- greater sovereignty
- greater resistance to claim and ownership
- harder to define from outside
- harder for any one faction or god to own
- increasing awareness that the gods are parasitic powers using mortals

As a mortal rises in quintessence, they begin to perceive the structure of the game more clearly.

---

## The Earliest Sign of Understanding

The earliest sign that The First is beginning to understand the gods rightly is:

**they begin to feel that divine favor is hunger, not love.**

This realization should be one of the most important shifts in the whole game.

It transforms:

- blessing into appetite
- protection into claim
- destiny into predation

This should not arrive as a dry lore reveal.
It should arrive through:

- lived contradiction
- being used too hard
- seeing sponsorship demand too much
- watching miracles leave damage behind

---

## Charged People

A person feels **charged** when:

- strange timing and coincidence gather around them
- victories and failures alike matter too much
- the world starts bending harder around their life than ordinary lives warrant

In prose, the strongest signals of a charged person are:

- fate-density
- outsized consequence
- the sense that institutions and stories are starting to move around them

A charged person is not just "powerful."
They are becoming a site of interpretation and struggle.

---

## Charged Places

A place feels **charged** when:

- omens, tension, and spiritual density gather
- factions begin maneuvering more openly
- the atmosphere suggests that something is coming

Charged places should first be felt through:

- prose
- behavior
- changed tone
- visible maneuvering

Only after that should the player feel the deeper systemic changes.

The most important charged places in the game should be places where:

- governance becomes contested
- underworld control becomes contested
- local institutions begin trying to define what the central mortal means
- divine struggle is becoming worldly

## Notice, Courtship, and Convergence

### How Factions Notice The First

Factions should usually notice The First through:

- public success within that faction's domain
- the agent's relevant sphere/reach "fit"

This should feel like:

- visible success
- plus a kind of divine "je ne sais quoi"

### How Rival Gods Notice The First

Rival gods primarily notice The First because:

- gods keep tabs on each other's threads
- investment itself draws attention

Then secondarily through:

- public success
- position
- charged-city significance
- faction centrality

### Convergence

Convergence is when too many claims become true around the same mortal or place at once.

It should feel mostly:

- ominous
- dangerous
- like "shit is going to hit the fan"

It is when:

- factions maneuver openly
- roles overtake persons
- sponsorship becomes strategic
- legitimacy becomes dangerous
- divine rivalry becomes hard to ignore

---

## The Canonical Early Tragedy

The first major "wrong power" pattern should usually be:

**the player god itself begins using The First too hard.**

This is a critical doctrine.

The game should not preserve the player's innocence by default.

The player god must be able to become:

- overbearing
- exploitative
- consuming
- villainous

without the game hiding it.

This does not erase external threats.
It means the most morally interesting danger is often internal to the thread itself.

---

## What a Spent Mortal Looks Like

A "spent" mortal is not simply dead, defeated, or exhausted.

A spent mortal should read as one or more of the following:

- empty
- obedient
- saintlike in a hollow way
- bitter
- broken
- used up
- kept alive only as symbol, vessel, or instrument

The central horror is not only suffering.
It is loss of personhood under accumulated claim.

---

## Ascension as Tragedy

Ascension should not be treated as uncomplicated victory.

The tragedy of ascension is:

**to ascend is to risk inheriting the same parasitic logic unless it is consciously broken.**

This is the central moral tension of late-game power.

Without this truth, the game collapses into a simple power fantasy.
With it, ascension remains thematically dangerous and dramatically rich.

---

## Sovereignty

True sovereignty, if it exists at all, should be rare and radical.

The strongest valid forms are:

### Refusal

Refusing all claims.
Walking away from the game.
Breaking the threads.

This is the only straightforward "happy ending."

### Rupture

Rewriting what divine power means.
Sacrificing mortality to ruin the game for the gods.
Breaking the game itself.

### Transcendence

Ascending without becoming parasitic.
Becoming:

- a force of nature
- a concept
- a balancing force

In other words:
transcending the game rather than merely winning it.

---

## The Moral Possibilities for The First

The end states for The First should broadly remain within these families:

- **Consumed**
  Used by the wrong power, hollowed out, symbolic, spent.
- **Villainous Ascension**
  Rises by inheriting predatory logic.
- **Refusal**
  Rejects all claims and walks away.
- **Revolutionary Sacrifice**
  Gives up mortality to break the game for the gods.
- **Transcendent Balance**
  Ascends without parasitism, becoming something closer to principle than predator.

Not every implementation needs to support all of these immediately.
But future design should remain compatible with them.

---

## What the Player Should Feel

This game should let the player feel:

- the thrill of shaping destiny
- the seduction of sponsorship and legitimacy
- the excitement of deepening story when claims become ambiguous
- the dread of convergence
- the horror that their own favor may be hunger
- the temptation to become the villain

The player is not protected from complicity.
They are invited into it.

---

## Interface Doctrine

The interface should remain:

- narrative-first
- language-first
- low-numeric

The player should primarily read:

- tone shifts
- behavioral shifts
- omen
- maneuvering
- changed titles and roles
- escalating social and divine pressure

They should not need to calculate.
The game does the math underneath.
The player experiences the cost through story.

---

## Design Laws

Future systems should obey these laws:

1. **Success should create claim.**
2. **Claim should first feel attractive.**
3. **Legitimacy should become dangerous before it becomes secure.**
4. **Low quintessence should reduce integrity of self, not only strength.**
5. **High quintessence should increase sovereignty and the ability to perceive divine predation.**
6. **The player god can become the villain of The First's story.**
7. **Charged people and places should first be felt in prose and behavior.**
8. **Convergence should feel ominous, not merely climactic.**
9. **Ascension without moral danger is not valid for this game.**
10. **True sovereignty requires refusal, rupture, or transcendence — not mere accumulation of power.**

---

## Usage

This doctrine should guide:

- The First and retinue content
- sponsorship and legitimacy systems
- faction courtship design
- rival god interference
- charged-city and charged-place design
- quintessence story meaning
- convergence mechanics
- return and ascension outcomes

If a future system feels mechanically useful but weakens this doctrine, it should be revised.

This document is the canonical narrative and moral spine for that work.
