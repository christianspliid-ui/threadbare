Encounter Experience — Three Experiments

2026-05-04 · companion to 2026-05-04-encounter-experience-grill-me.md · all three frame the encounter mid-flight on a 1920×1080 viewport

The Bet

Encounters are cards. Each beat is a tarot card turned face-up. The player pulls a coloured thread from a small set of options to lean. The same card-vocabulary represents your divine court — every strongly-threaded agent has a card on the right rail, and you can feel which ones have begun to glow.

Reads as story

Even un-pulled, the column of cards reads like an Arkham log: card 1 resolved with grace, card 2 turned, card 3 face-down. Months later you can scroll a character sheet that is their stack of cards.

This is right if…

…the moment you turn a card you feel a small held breath. If pulling a thread feels like a deliberate weight rather than picking from a menu — and if you can put the deck down without guilt.

The Bet

The encounter is a scene. The cast stands on a dim stage and the player is a god in the rafters who can whisper one tone to one cast member per beat. The whispered figure hears it differently — and so the moment turns.

Reads as story

The script unfolds in stage-direction prose. Even un-whispered, the cast still acts. The journal becomes a small play in four scenes — readable as theatre by anyone who turns to your character's page.

This is right if…

…choosing whom to whisper to feels as meaningful as choosing what to whisper. If a tone of fear into the captain creates a different scene than the same tone into the trader, the bet pays.

The Bet

The encounter is a graph of pressures made visible. Each cast member, item, and trait is a node; each relation a thread with its own colour, thickness, and state. Leaning is editing the system: press a thread, slacken another, weave a third in. You never cut.

Reads as story

After the dice fall, the knot reshapes — one thread snaps, another forms, a node colour shifts. The journal records which thread gave way. Months later you can revisit the page and see the knot as it stood that day.

This is right if…

…seeing a thread go taut feels like reading the room. If pressing a node feels like nudging a current rather than picking an outcome, the bet pays. If the graph reads as ornament rather than insight, it fails.

Comparison at a glance. Each experiment makes a different bet about where the player's attention should sit during a beat — on a card, on a face, or on a system.
