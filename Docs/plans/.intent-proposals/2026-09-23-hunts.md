## scope (what this plan does)

- **The `monster` undertaking object type,** with two verbs:
  - **track** (`observe`): mints a `hidden_weakness` mark (`mintLeverageMark`) and reveals the temper;
  - **hunt** (`destroy`, with `deferredPayoff`): its completion plants an appointment at the lair whose kept branch spawns `fight.lair.confront` against the beast; no harm is written and no grievance is satisfied until the beast dies. Company help rides plan doc 2's Company advantage, so there is no gather step.
- **Offering and reasons.** Hunts are offered through the division rule (`monster` in `KINDS_BY_REACH.iron` and `ALL_UNDERTAKING_KINDS`) and the vengeance hand lists, and admitted through the existing `gateExemption` door for three reasons: a `blood_drawn` scar from the beast, a grievance naming it (THR-1536), or its den within 2 hexes of a settlement home.
- **The world-objects registry** gets a class note (Monster, a class of Mortal), a UL term and a canon row.

It carries out THR-1533, refined from "a `destroy × mortal` branch" to "a `monster` object type". The type is shown as a class of the Mortal kind in the grid and codex (`classOf: 'mortal'`); company and army are kinds, so they are not the precedent the first draft claimed.

## scope (what this plan does NOT do — explicit non-goals)

- No faction bounties (the v2 layer).
- No new world-object kind: Monster is a class of Mortal.
- No new UI; hunts render through the existing undertaking surfaces.
- No change to the plot. Monsters stay excluded from it (plan doc 3).
- No direct spawn from undertaking completion. The confront uses the appointment path, which judges at the place.
