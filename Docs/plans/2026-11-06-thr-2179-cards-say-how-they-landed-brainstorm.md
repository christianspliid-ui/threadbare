# Brainstorm — your cards say how they landed (THR-2179)

Companion to `Docs/plans/2026-11-06-thr-2179-cards-say-how-they-landed.md`. Design lane, 2026-11-06, run dl-20261106T1119.

## The question as filed, and how the measurement moved it

The ticket asked how many of The First's own chapters should give the god a hand, and whether a deal-only step pays off at every band. It started from a registry count (391 of 541 story templates offer no move).

Two measurements changed the shape:

1. **Live, most of The First's chapters already wait.** 72 of 103 story starts (70%) over four seeds. The registry count was mostly scenes The First never draws. THR-1634's slices had already dealt hands into the templates the world fires most. So "most chapters are silent" was not true for the player's own mortal. Twelve templates are, and five carry three quarters of it.
2. **No card's band line is ever shown.** Trying to answer "does a deal-only step pay off at every band" led to the reader, and the reader has no caller. This is not a deal-only problem. It is every card, authored or dealt. It also explains part of warm round 3's PC-8 / PC-2 complaints: the only answer a played card gets is "your cards held it".

## Tensions

- **Pacing vs. consistency with THR-1634.** THR-1634 put a deal on every step. For world-fired templates that cost no world stops, because most are not The First's. For The First's live chapters, every step means more stops: +46% for five chapters. Step 0 keeps it to +19%, and the god still gets a say in how the chapter starts. The cause clause already covers "your cards were on another step".
- **Fallback vs. authoring the missing lines.** 31 library cards have no critical-failure line. Authoring them would be better prose, but it is a content pass across the library, with the Encounter Factory's bar. The fallback is honest: the failure line is still true on a critical failure. It is a floor, not the ceiling.
- **How many lines.** A full hand can commit four or more cards. Four extra sentences under every step result would bury the scene. Two lines, in commit order, keep the god present without drowning the mortal's story. The cause clause still counts the rest.
- **Title or no title.** A bare line ("The odds had been quietly widened…") reads as narration. It could be mistaken for the scene. The card's title in front ties it to what the player chose. Left to the executor to drop the title if it is noisy in play.

## Considered and dropped

- **Reserve the bar for Christian.** The ticket said to reserve it only if the answer changes what the game means. Five chapters asking once does not. "Every chapter asks" would, and is not proposed.
- **Render band prose only for dealt cards.** That would leave 1,310 authored lines dark, for no reason.
- **Put the card line in the cause clause sentence.** It makes one long sentence and loses the per-card voice.
- **Hand on the step with the hardest roll instead of step 0.** Better drama in theory, but it needs a per-template choice. Step 0 is a rule, and it makes the THR-2178 word true by construction.

## Vision premises

The god nudges, fate rolls, the mortal decides (canon). The god's hand must be traceable at every band. Both slices serve that premise and change none.
