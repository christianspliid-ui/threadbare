### The rule-4 re-read found its stopping condition, and that is this run's most useful result

Runs h and i flipped four shelved tickets by re-reading them against `Docs/canon/process.md` § *User review interface* **rule 4** (Christian, 2026-08-12) — on the grounds that a decline whose evidence is *a sentence written under a superseded rule* deserves re-examination. Run i's own caution was that *"the re-read is not a device for emptying the shelf."* This run is the case that proves the caution has teeth.

**[THR-1114](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1114) looked like the next obvious flip and is not one.** Two action templates carry a `sphereAffinity` outside the canonical twelve — `action.secrets.plant_secret` says `shadow` (a *reach* name in a sphere field) and `artifact.nullify` says `void` (neither reach nor Sphere). That the values are *wrong* is settled canon, not a design question, and the ticket's third Done-when — a corpus-wide invariant test pinning every populated `sphereAffinity` to `SPHERE_NAMES` — is unambiguously executor work and is the durable half.

**It stays declined, on a test that discriminates.** Its filing comment routes it away from the queue explicitly: *"Promoting it to the queue as-is would hand an executor a decision they would have to invent, and an invented cosmology alignment is worse than the current honest warning."* That comment is dated **2026-08-14** — **two days after rule 4 existed**. So it is not a decision made under a superseded rule; it is a live, informed judgement by a party that knew the rule and routed the ticket anyway. Re-promoting it would be overruling a current verdict, not correcting a stale one.

**The general form, offered for the retro rather than filed as a ticket:** the re-read is only valid where the decline's *evidence predates the rule it would be re-judged under*. A decline written after 2026-08-12 that still says "this needs a decision" has already been through rule 4. Applying the re-read past that line converts a genuine correction mechanism into a ratchet that empties the shelf of everything anyone ever deliberately held back.
