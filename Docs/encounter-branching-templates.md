# Encounter Branching Templates

> Added 2026-04-04. A reusable branching-template library for encounter authors and agents. Use this alongside the encounter-building checklist to avoid repeating the same branch grammar across every serious encounter.

## Purpose

This document is not a list of plots. It is a list of branching grammars.

Use it when:
- a serious encounter is starting to feel too similar to the last one
- the choices are tactically different but emotionally flat
- the author keeps falling back to the same `interpret / coerce / withdraw` shape
- you want a quick way to vary who becomes central, what kind of cost dominates, or what the world remembers

Hard rule:
- do not copy a template mechanically
- use a template to diversify the encounter's branch profile, not to flatten it into formula

## How To Use

For every serious encounter:
1. Pick one primary branching template.
2. Optionally pick one secondary branching template.
3. State why this encounter uses those templates instead of another.
4. Make sure the choices reveal what kind of god the player is being, not only what tactic they prefer.

Also check scale first:
- `short` encounters should usually use one compact branching grammar or stay mostly linear
- `medium` encounters can combine one main grammar with one secondary effect
- `long` encounters can support a full branch grammar plus a distinct aftermath-thread grammar

Valid branch counts:
- `1` is fine when the encounter is stronger as a single sharp intervention
- `2` is often ideal for `short` encounters and many `medium` encounters
- `3` should be earned, not assumed
- if the third branch is weaker, redundant, or symmetry-driven after revision, cut it

Hard rule:
- do not force a `short` encounter into a `long` encounter's branching density

Each template below includes:
- what it is good for
- what kinds of branches it usually changes
- what failure mode to avoid

## 1. Doctrine Split

**Shape:** The first choice establishes the moral grammar of the entire encounter.

Typical branch logic:
- mercy
- law
- ownership
- truth
- loyalty
- survival

Best for:
- public crises
- famine, justice, triage, heresy, and civil unrest
- encounters where the player’s godhood posture should be visible immediately

Usually changes:
- step 2 scene architecture
- who is central
- what costs are considered honorable
- aftermath meaning

Failure mode:
- one doctrine reads as obviously wise while the others read as decorative

## 2. Custody Triangle

**Shape:** The encounter is about who gets to carry, own, guard, or interpret something dangerous.

Typical branches:
- protect it
- expose it
- privatize it

Best for:
- relics
- evidence
- bodies
- witnesses
- forbidden knowledge

Usually changes:
- object persistence
- witness structure
- follow-on hooks

Failure mode:
- the object becomes just a prop while all branches feel socially identical

## 3. Bargain / Bind / Break

**Shape:** The player can negotiate with the force, contain it, or destroy it.

Best for:
- mystical encounters
- shrine encounters
- hauntings
- living anomalies
- cursed items or entities

Usually changes:
- tone
- shell/state
- price structure
- relic vs scar vs peace aftermath

Failure mode:
- `break` becomes the obviously practical path and `bargain` becomes cosmetic weirdness

## 4. Public / Private / Invisible

**Shape:** The same crisis can be resolved openly, behind closed doors, or by manipulation that leaves no clear public owner.

Best for:
- scandals
- arrests
- ideological conflicts
- elite bargains
- bureaucratic violence

Usually changes:
- witness memory
- reputation channels
- future rumor vs official record

Failure mode:
- only the location changes while the social consequence stays the same

## 5. Protect / Exploit / Abandon

**Shape:** A vulnerable person, community, or opening can be defended, used, or left to fend for itself.

Best for:
- escorts
- body-horror
- frontier hardship
- rescue dilemmas
- ally-origin encounters

Usually changes:
- who becomes central
- whether future memory is gratitude, resentment, or loss
- the moral temperature of the whole encounter

Failure mode:
- `abandon` is framed as non-choice instead of a meaningful divine posture

## 6. Reveal / Contain / Displace

**Shape:** A hidden truth can be exposed, kept contained, or moved somewhere else.

Best for:
- forbidden knowledge
- plagues
- magical contamination
- political secrets
- dangerous discoveries

Usually changes:
- signal/reveal state
- who bears the burden afterward
- where the next encounter will happen

Failure mode:
- all branches still resolve in the same place with the same witness set

## 7. Human Price / Structural Price / Sacred Price

**Shape:** The encounter asks what kind of cost will be paid.

Typical branches:
- one named person pays
- the community/system pays
- the holy order pays

Best for:
- impossible bargains
- scarcity crises
- temple politics
- military sacrifice
- builder’s dilemmas

Usually changes:
- aftermath tone
- visible marks
- who becomes scarred, promoted, or condemned

Failure mode:
- the cost is described differently, but the actual aftermath rewards the player equally

## 8. Center Shift

**Shape:** Earlier choices decide who the later encounter is really about.

Typical branch outcomes:
- authority-centered
- witness-centered
- victim-centered
- rival-centered
- crowd-centered

Best for:
- scenes with multiple strong actors
- situations where memory and testimony matter
- socially dense encounters

Usually changes:
- who gets the best lines
- what the player can credibly influence
- what the aftermath frames as important

Failure mode:
- all roads still become “the authority decides” by step 2

## 9. Debt / Gift / Refusal

**Shape:** Help can come as free care, binding aid, or refusal to intervene.

Best for:
- divine favor
- sponsorship
- first-contact blessing scenes
- patronage politics

Usually changes:
- whether aid becomes claim
- future bond / obligation surfaces
- how the god is remembered

Failure mode:
- the “gift” is secretly just debt with nicer prose

## 10. Endure / Escape / Transform

**Shape:** The player can hold through the ordeal, flee it, or let it change something fundamental.

Best for:
- pilgrimages
- gauntlets
- survival ordeals
- metamorphosis
- deep wilderness or planar travel

Usually changes:
- bodily consequence
- landscape memory
- future capability or scar

Failure mode:
- all three branches preserve the same identity and only change flavor

## 11. Lawful Lie

**Shape:** The player can preserve order honestly, preserve it dishonestly, or let it break.

Best for:
- courts
- religious authority
- sieges
- resource distribution
- diplomatic scenes

Usually changes:
- legitimacy
- hypocrisy burden
- later revelation hooks

Failure mode:
- the lie branch feels like a clever optimal solution with no moral residue

## 12. Aftermath Thread Templates

Use these to vary post-summary choices so the player is not always choosing the same kind of follow-up.

### Keep the witness alive
- preserve a testimony thread
- good for rumor, memory, and social fallout

### Keep the institution alive
- preserve a law, shrine, office, or custom
- good for order/legitimacy stories

### Keep the wound alive
- preserve blame, rivalry, debt, or unrest
- good for darker follow-on pressure

### Keep the object alive
- preserve a relic, ledger, mark, or tool
- good for salvage, knowledge, and corruption arcs

### Let it diffuse
- let the settlement, district, or landscape absorb the consequence
- good when the player should feel restraint rather than ownership

## Pairing Advice

Good primary/secondary pairs:
- `Doctrine Split` + `Center Shift`
- `Bargain / Bind / Break` + `Human Price / Structural Price / Sacred Price`
- `Public / Private / Invisible` + `Reveal / Contain / Displace`
- `Debt / Gift / Refusal` + `Aftermath Thread Templates`
- `Protect / Exploit / Abandon` + `Endure / Escape / Transform`

Avoid:
- stacking templates that only produce tonal variation
- picking two templates that both still collapse into the same public accusation scene
- giving a `short` encounter two large templates just because the system technically supports more

## Editorial Questions

Use these in the editorial review:
- Does the chosen template actually show up in the branch grammar?
- Could I swap the branch labels with another encounter and barely notice?
- Which branch is currently weakest or most obviously “wrong”?
- Does the template change who matters, what is possible, and what the world remembers?
- Are the aftermath choices using a different grammar from the main-scene choices?

## Hard Rules

- A serious encounter should normally use one named branching template, and often one secondary template.
- If the branch grammar feels too similar to the last proving encounter, change the template before implementation.
- If the template only changes tone and not consequence, the encounter is underbranched.
- If the player’s choices do not reveal a divine posture, the encounter is underauthored.
