**Every multi-step encounter crashed the tick the moment it reached a fork (THR-924, 2026-08-01).** `UnifiedActionTemplate.steps` is `ActionStepOrBranch[]`, and an `ActionStepBranch` carries its `reach` on each *variant*, never on itself. The contract adapter's fallback path asserted that union away — `template.steps as readonly ActionStep[]` — then read `step.reach` off every entry, so a branch reached `defaultPoleForReach` with `reach === undefined` and `MORAL_AXIS_POLES_BY_REACH[undefined][0]` threw. **The cast is the whole defect:** the compiler knew the shape and was told not to look. **The blast radius was far wider than the fork-bearing template**, because `phaseAscendantHandFilter` adapts *every* pending encounter notification in one unguarded loop — one fork-bearing encounter anywhere in the world threw out of the phase and the orchestrator caught it as "Tick crashed, returning previous state", reverting the tick for every other pending encounter too. That is why `snow_on_the_pass` and `shrine_offering` were both reported as crashing despite adapting cleanly in isolation: **one root cause, four symptoms**, and NFP #4's fail-soft tick loop turned a hard crash into an encounter that merely never advanced. **The ticket's own hypothesis was wrong and checking it first was the whole job** — it proposed a clobbered `reach` or a stale module-graph import of `MORAL_AXIS_POLES_BY_REACH`; enumerating the five repro templates showed two carrying a step with no `reach` at all, which named the branch union in one step (the verify-the-noun-before-the-verb protocol, paying for itself). The adapter now resolves a branch to its `fallback` — the arm `resolveStepDefinition` picks before any choice is recorded, and the idiom already used by `encounterCache`, `encounterEventNode`, and the encounter-stage adapters — and `defaultPoleForReach` degrades to a pole rather than throwing, since it runs where nothing may throw. **Only templates *without* encoded contract metadata ever walked the broken path**: one carrying `__encounter_contract_v1` in `illustrationAlt` is decoded verbatim, which is why the shipped `reputation.*` forks were unaffected while the vertical-slice ones were not — so the regression test splits on that predicate rather than asserting one beat count for both paths. **Falsified in all three registers rather than asserted:** reverting the fix turns the new phase-level test red with the exact reported stack, and in a live browser on the ticket's own `?spawn=` URL `__DEBUG.tick(8)` reports `ticksRun: 8, tick: 0` — eight ticks run, world frozen at zero — against `tick: 8` with the fix. The 30-tick CLI smoke is a no-regression gate only and is **not** evidence here: it behaves identically with the fix reverted, because the headless path never builds the notification shape that reaches the crash.


**A detector that failed the sentence it was built to protect (THR-899, 2026-08-01).** The vagueness lexicon ran one flat ~35-term list over all of a template's prose, which conflated two different faults under one word. Christian's canonical example of *correct* prose — "someone is asking around after the agent, and not in a good way" — tripped it, and content carries contortions written only to dodge that: "the stranger is asking the room for them by name". **A detector that fails the reference sentence is measuring the wrong thing.** Terms are now split by intent and enforced by field class: **evasive** (hedges, nominalised placeholders, `something`) banned everywhere; **natural indefinites** (`someone`, `way`, `nothing`, …) enforced in **outcome prose only** — band text, fragments, afterimages, `narrativeTemplates.success`/`.failure` — where an indefinite withholds a result the player has no other source for; **intensifiers** demoted to a warning that fails nothing. `countVagueness(text, fieldClass)` defaults to the strictest scope, so an unthinking caller gets the conservative answer rather than a silent loosening. **The two-list era is closed structurally, not by discipline** (THR-877): `nudgeAuditDetectors.ts` is the single authority and `VAGUENESS_LEXICON` *derives* from it, so the divergence that cost THR-868 a full rewrite cycle cannot recur without someone deleting the derivation. **The blast radius was measured before adoption, as THR-877 demanded, and the answer was not the expected one** — the rescope nets *tighter*, not looser, because `thing`/`way`/`nothing`/`anything`/`whatever` sat on the old authoring list and the audit path never matched them: over 680 templates, 1100 → 997 hits, 13 loosened, **30 newly failing** (THR-929), none of them CI-gated and all 7 `WS5_MIGRATED` templates clean. **One walk, not two** — the classed collector emits fields in the original sweep order and `collectTemplateText` joins them back, so word counts, abstraction density and the not-X-but-Y sentence split are byte-identical and every "before" score already quoted as WS5 evidence stays comparable. Every loosening assertion is paired with a tightening one across three suites, because a rescope that only relaxes can go inert and still read green.

**Three gates that exist to validate documentation were skipped on documentation-only PRs (THR-909, 2026-08-01).** `ci.yml`'s `detect` job computes `code` with `predicate-quantifier: every`, so a docs-only PR yields `code == 'false'` and `Test · Typecheck · Build` is skipped — and that job is where `check:impediment-ids`, `check:generated-freshness` and `check:wiki-freshness:blocking` all ran. **All three police documentation, and all three were absent on the only PR shape that can trip them.** Measured on PR #1096, which carried two rows with id `282` from the moment it was authored while CI reported `skipping`, and again on PR #1173, which documented the defect while demonstrating it. **This is not the THR-768 case** — there `detect` *died* and the required check recorded `skipped` while branch protection accepted it; here `detect` succeeds and `code == 'false'` is correct, so the skip works as designed and the defect sits upstream of that guard: doc gates were placed in a job gated on **code** changing. A new cheap `Docs gates` job (`npm ci` plus the three scripts — no test suite, no typecheck, no build) runs them on docs-only PRs. **The `docs` predicate needed its own paths-filter step rather than a second filter in the existing one:** `predicate-quantifier` is a step-level input and the two predicates need opposite quantifiers, so a merged `docs` filter would have demanded a single file be simultaneously `*.md` **and** under `Docs/` **and** under `.planning/` — a filter that reads as working and can never fire. **Gated on `docs && !code`, so exactly one job runs the three gates, never both and never neither** — on a mixed PR the existing job already covers them, and closeout commits touch `Docs/` almost every time, so firing both would duplicate them on nearly every code PR. **Verified by injection rather than assertion:** a duplicate impediment id fails `check:impediment-ids` (exit 1) and a row added without regenerating the dashboard fails `check:generated-freshness`, both run as docs-only changes; the `check` job parses byte-identical before and after, so the fast-docs-PR path is provably untouched. **The half that actually closes the hole is Christian's** (THR-931): auto-merge waits only on *required* checks, so a red-but-not-required `Docs gates` would merge anyway and reproduce the very class — and the job must land on `main` first before GitHub will offer the name.

**A sweep that reported success while three finished PRs sat unmergeable (THR-897, 2026-07-31).** `pull-work` Step 0.8 drains armed auto-merge PRs by matching `mergeStateStatus === "BEHIND"` and running `gh pr update-branch`. A PR at `DIRTY` — a real merge conflict — is not `BEHIND`, so it was skipped, and `update-branch` would not have fixed it anyway. The step's prose *did* name `DIRTY`, but with no mechanism and a log line reporting only what it drained, so every run stepped past it while truthfully claiming success. **Measured during this ticket's own pickup: 3 of 4 armed PRs were conflicted**, the oldest armed 19 hours carrying THR-883's authoring-contract rewrite — the deliverable unblocking 11 content tickets — across three consecutive sweeps that each reported no problem. The new `check:armed-prs` probe classifies (`drainable` / `conflicted` / `waiting` / `indeterminate`) rather than testing one value for equality, and computes conflicting file names read-only via `git merge-tree --write-tree` so the session that picks a PR up starts diagnosed. **Two design points earn their keep. `UNKNOWN` is not "fine"** — GitHub computes mergeability lazily, and PRs #1132/#1166 were observed reading `DIRTY` then `UNKNOWN` minutes apart with no intervening push, so a single-read classifier calls a conflicted PR healthy on roughly every other run; unrecognised enum members classify `indeterminate`, never `waiting`, because `waiting` asserts nobody needs to act. **And a conflict is an agent's job until it isn't** — per THR-608 the first age tier escalates to a *session* (90 min), and only past 12 hours, when ~12 hourly runs have each had a chance, does it become Christian's. **A gate that refused nearly everything it was shown (THR-836, 2026-07-31).** `pull-work` Step 3 demanded three coordination lines — `Suggested model`, `Parallel-safe with`, `Mutex with` — on every candidate's latest comment, with no exemption. Both authors of that block were handoff-path only: the design-session handoff (retired with THR-654) and the orchestrator's T1 **promotion**. The executor files its own deferrals *directly* into `Ready for Dev`, bypassing T1 — so **those issues were born failing the gate**, and it produced one of two outcomes on nearly every pickup: a lost run, or a hand-written reversal re-derived from scratch each time (THR-834/THR-817 zero-comment; THR-804 claimed past it by hand; THR-778 bounced at 05:03 and re-offered at 06:03). The fix separates the two things the gate was conflating. **A block is an artifact of coordination between two parties**; a ticket the lane filed for itself, naming the file it means to change, was never coordinated by anyone, so demanding it is a category error. Step 3 now classifies before deciding: a **self-scoped** ticket (description names a repo path or a backticked file/symbol) is *claimed*, with the executor deriving and posting the three lines under a labelled heading; only an **unscoped** one bounces. **A bounce now also routes** — comment, move to `Todo`, continue to the next candidate in the same run — because refusing without removing from the queue is a spin loop wearing a gate's clothes, which is exactly what re-offered THR-778 hourly until a human moved it by hand. The writer side was closed in the same pass so the derivation is a fallback rather than the norm: CLAUDE.md's Definition of Done now requires the block as the **first comment** on any issue filed straight into `Ready for Dev`, and the orchestrator's rule was widened from "every promotion" to promotions *and* direct filings (its two-write create path produced zero-comment issues with no later step that would ever author one). The rule is **machine-checked, not prose-only**: `classifyCoordinationBlockGap` in `scripts/coordination-block-predicate.ts` is the single predicate both `check:process` and Step 3 cite, reporting `warn` for self-scoped and `error` for unscoped, with 16 tests pinning **both** directions — a predicate that only ever answered "self-scoped" would make the gate vacuous while every test still passed.

**A branch point that could not branch (THR-894, 2026-07-31).** `ActionStepBranch` selects its variant by a recorded `choiceId`, and the only thing that ever recorded one was the retired player-pick (`authoredChoices`) — so a branch authored *today* could only ever resolve down `fallback`, on every seed, forever. The fork existed in the type and nowhere in the world. This generalizes the one shipped personality-driven selector, Meet The First (THR-868), so the **mortal** decides in-encounter forks from who they are, leanable by the god's hand. A branch declares `decidedBy: { axis }` with variants keyed exactly `positive`/`negative`; at the deciding step the engine reads the mortal's **live** axis position (standing profile *plus* accumulated drift), adds the net `poleLean` of the cards the god actually committed, and records the pole through the **existing** choice-history path — so `resolveStepDefinition` reads it as an ordinary choice and no second branch-resolution route exists to drift out of sync. **Generalizing by copying the meeting's summing loop was the obvious move and the wrong one** — that is the `assignAmbitionToActor` failure (THR-885), two implementations of one rule that diverge the first time either is tuned — so the arithmetic was extracted to `encounters/poleLean.ts` and the meeting now *calls* it, its 49 tests green untouched. **The loop closes on drift:** the decided pole writes through `applyDriftMagnitude`, the same accumulator `phaseChoiceResolution` uses, so decay, threshold crossings and the `archetype_drift_register` reveal all see it with no parallel store — a mortal who keeps taking the cunning fork measurably becomes likelier to take it again. **Two decisions are recorded so they are not re-litigated:** the seeded coin is drawn *only* inside the neutral band (drawing it unconditionally would desynchronise two runs differing solely in how convinced a mortal was), and the meta pair `courage_prudence` has no canonical `${reach}_axis` id, so it keys drift on its own pair name — canonical readers simply do not find it, which keeps a *designed* authoring axis decidable rather than an edge case that refuses. **A card's lean counts only on its own axis**, and a deciding step dealing no card on the branch's axis **warns** rather than fails: legal (the mortal decides alone), but far more often it means a card names the wrong axis and abstains silently — the THR-844 shape. Variant keys are validated at build time for the same reason. **Zero content ships here** (THR-883 owns the first choice-designed encounters), so the interface-map row is badged **LEAKED** with its remediation ticket rather than LIVE: a green badge has to mean traffic, not intent (THR-614).
