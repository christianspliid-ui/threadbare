# Game Design Laws — the rules every system keeps

> **Status:** binding. Opened 2026-10-14 at Christian's direction, in chat: *"fix and add the relevant game design principles to our relevant game design laws."* Chapter I came out of three videos he shared from the [candlesan](https://www.youtube.com/@candlesan) channel on loot tables, the marble bag, and ways to control randomness, checked against Threadbearer's own code and measurements. Chapters II–VII were collated the same day ([THR-1891](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1891)), also at his direction: *"if we dont have any game design laws we should make a place for them."* They gather fundamentals the game had already decided, scattered across the rulebook, canon, the Vision notebook and his dated rulings. Nothing in them is new: every [E] law cites the ruling or settled decision it comes from, and every [P] law cites the rule of play it was lifted from.
>
> **What this file is:** the numbered law book for *how the game behaves*, the sibling of the UI Laws ([`Docs/design-system/laws.md`](../design-system/laws.md)), which govern how the game is shown. A law is checkable: a reviewer with a trace, a test or a measurement and this file can say "Law 2 violated" without re-deriving intent.
>
> **What it is not:** every rule of play. The [rulebook](../canon/rulebook.md) holds the rules; these laws are the fundamentals the rules must keep, and a rule that breaks one is a finding. How the game is shown stays in the UI Laws (words, never numerals, is UI Law 13; a consequence chip needs a real state write, UI Law 56). Engineering decisions (the graph, determinism, fail-soft) stay in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md)'s load-bearing decisions and Non-Functional Priorities.
>
> **Provenance marks:** **[E]** = established and binding, with source: a dated ruling by Christian, a Vision non-negotiable, or a settled decision recorded in CLAUDE.md. New proposals enter as **[P]** and are followed until Christian rules; his ratification (chat, dated) flips them to [E].
>
> **Loaded by:** the `game-design-laws` skill ([`.claude/skills/game-design-laws/SKILL.md`](../../.claude/skills/game-design-laws/SKILL.md)), on every design *or implementation* pass that changes how the game behaves: odds, rewards, progression, failure, the god's powers, economies, clocks. `Docs/canon/design-governance.md` names this file in its checklist, and the review gate's rubric cites it. Ticket Done-whens cite it by law number; a law does not need restating per ticket.
>
> **Chapters:** I Randomness and luck (1–8) · II The god and the mortal (9–15) · III The odds (16–21) · IV Failure (22–26) · V Time and the run (27–32) · VI Power, growth and the economy (33–39) · VII Building systems and content (40–45).

## I. Randomness and luck — Christian's direction, 2026-10-14

1. **[E] A number the designer sets is the number the player gets.** An authored chance (a rarity curve, a tier share, a crit rate) must be the rate players actually experience. If how many items exist, an encounter's filters, or what the mortal already owns can move that rate, that is a bug, not tuning. Check the experienced rate, never only the authored weight. *Motivating instance:* the reward draw set Legendary on a success at 5%, and live play paid about 1.5%. A critical success was set at 10% and paid about 4%, and a success's Mythic was set at 15% and paid about 7%. (Measured 2026-10-14 over 5,356 live draws; plan [`2026-10-14-luck-protection-and-tier-first-loot.md`](../plans/2026-10-14-luck-protection-and-tier-first-loot.md).)

2. **[E] Roll the class, then the thing.** When a draw has classes with their own odds (rarity tiers), roll the class first from its own table, then pick inside it. Never multiply a class's share into every item's weight. If you do, each item added to a class quietly raises that class's odds and lowers every other class's. *Established instance:* `drawTierFirst` in `src/engine/rewardPool.ts` is the one reward draw; the flat `drawFromPool` is for untiered pools only. *Established instance:* inside the rolled tier, `drawTierFirst` rolls the reward kind (condition, possession, power) by the recipe's share among the kinds present, then the item — tier, then kind, then thing (THR-1997, decided on THR-1890). *Remainder:* a kind with nothing at the rolled rarity yields to the kinds that have one, so about 13% of multi-kind prize draws still land off the recipe's shares, against 26% before; the rarity mix is unchanged.

3. **[E] The rarest tier's chance is pinned.** Adding a new lower tier, retiring an item, or an encounter having nothing at some tier never changes the chance of the rarest tier. When the rolled tier has nothing eligible, the draw falls one tier *down*, and climbs only when nothing below is eligible. Leftover weight never goes to Legendary. *Instances:* `drawTierFirst`'s cascade; `seedRarityTier` (world generation), which used to hand any shortfall in a distribution to Legendary.

4. **[E] Random is not fair, and the player's own mortals get fair luck.** Rolls on a mortal the player holds by a thread (the First, and any later threaded mortal) come from a **marble bag**: each bag holds every slice of the d100 once, dealt in a seeded order. Each bag lands once in every tenth of the range, so the mortal's luck evens out to the odds it was shown instead of drifting: at steady odds a bag delivers them exactly, and over a run the success rate tracks the forecast. The long losing streaks plain dice allow are cut. Everyone else, including rival gods' mortals and the background world, rolls plain dice: nobody watches those rolls, and real swings are the world's texture. *Instance:* `src/engine/luck.ts`, used at the three places a mortal rolls (encounter steps, fights, undertaking checkpoints).

5. **[E] Choose the protection by the problem.**
    - **Marble bag:** a repeated event at known odds, such as a threaded mortal's step rolls.
    - **Predetermination:** one outcome the player tracks, with a worst case to rule out. The Legendary drought is the instance: each cycle fixes in secret, up front, the win by which the next Legendary will have arrived (between the 30th and 50th win today). Luck can bring it sooner.
    - **Progressive probability** (the odds rise after each miss): never on odds a chooser can see. Mortals pick challenges by their forecast (the 50–65% window). A rising bonus would be spent on a harder challenge instead of ending the streak.
    - **Fixed limit** (a visible "guaranteed by the Nth try"): only as a deliberate, visible feature, and designed so that saving the guaranteed result for the best target is either impossible or intended. Adopting one is Christian's call.

6. **[E] Protection stays lighter than the player's own tools.** Luck protection removes only the long streaks. Short bad runs stay, because answering bad luck is what the nudge cards are for (the `no_crit_fail` and `floor_at_cost` riders). If a protection ever makes a card pointless, the protection is too strong.

7. **[E] Protection is invisible unless it is the feature.** No surface names the bag or the deadline, and the forecast shows the true odds. Making a protection visible is a design decision with its own law-5 review, not an implementation detail.

8. **[E] Luck's memory is game state: seeded, saved, traced.** Store it as counts on the holder (the bag's order and the deadline are re-derived from the world seed, the holder's id and those counts), never as a stored shuffled list. A protection draws on its own named stream and never changes how many values a shared stream consumes. Every protected roll and every Legendary cycle emits a trace (`luck.bag_roll`, `luck.legendary_cycle`). (NFP #2 Inspectability, #3 Determinism.)

## II. The god and the mortal — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

9. **[E] The god influences; it never commands.** Everything the player does to a mortal bends odds, spends essence or leaves a mark. Nothing moves a mortal like a piece, speaks for them or decides for them. The god's reach is as wide as its spheres (matter, minds, dreams, fates); what stays forbidden is an instruction to the mortal. A design where "the player clicks and the mortal does X" is reframed as an intervention with an uncertain outcome, or it is dropped. This holds at every scale: the god never orders an army or a company, and its seat in a war is the intervention seat. *Why:* the game's moral weight lives in the distance between god and mortal; a god who can command has nothing left to choose. *Source:* Vision `02-non-negotiables.md` §1; Christian, 2026-08-18, that the old "physics of the scene" framing over-constrained the nudges ([THR-1178 brainstorm](../plans/2026-08-18-thr-1178-nudge-library-completion-brainstorm.md); "influence, never authorship" in [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md)); rulebook §9 and §10.5.

10. **[E] Fate picks the band; the player never picks an ending.** A card moves the odds, adds a rider or leaves something behind. No card, keyword or rider chooses how a roll comes out, and no screen asks the player to choose between two futures for a mortal. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-07-25/26 ([THR-772 program plan](../plans/2026-07-26-nudge-model-encounter-system.md): "the player must never pick an ending"); [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md) § Rejected approaches (choosing between authored futures); rulebook §7 (no card keyword picks an outcome).

11. **[E] Forks belong to the mortal.** When a story branches, the mortal's own values decide the branch; the god's committed cards only lean on it. A god who plays nothing still gets the choice the mortal would have made alone, and only a mortal with no conviction either way falls to chance. Taking a fork moves the mortal toward that pole. *Why:* the player can shape who someone becomes, never answer for them. *Source:* Vision `02-non-negotiables.md` §1 ("forks are decided by the mortal", director-approved sweep 2026-08-28, THR-1335); rulebook §7 (`decidedBy`, THR-894/THR-898).

12. **[E] The thread runs both ways.** The mortal answers the god (awe, devotion, doubt, defiance, prayer, refusal), and that answer is part of play the player can see. A bond that only carries the god's hand outward is half built. *Instance:* the bond with The First always forms and fate picks how it is received; a defiant First bonds and defies. *Source:* Vision `02-non-negotiables.md` §1 (the two-way thread, added 2026-05-11, THR-402); rulebook §7, *Meet The First*.

13. **[E] The world does not wait for the god.** Mortals pursue their own goals, factions go to war and trade, rivals scheme, whether or not the player acts. The player's pleasure is watching a living thing and choosing what kind of god to be toward it, not steering it, and there is no correct intervention at a given moment. *Source:* Vision `00-north-star.md` ("the pleasure is witnessing, not steering"; a good run "is not a puzzle with a right answer"); Vision `03-design-tensions.md` §2 (emergence produces the situations).

14. **[P] The god bends a mortal's odds; it never lends its own competence.** The probability floor clamps before the god's cards are counted, so a mortal far outside its reach reads *doomed* however much the god spends. To make that attempt go differently, change the mortal (thread them, grow them, wait) or choose another. *Why:* reaching the floor is the game saying the attempt was never the god's to fix from above. *Source:* rulebook §7, *There is a floor your nudges cannot lift a mortal off* (`PROBABILITY_FLOOR`, measured in [THR-821](../audits/2026-07-27-thr-821-nudge-headroom.md)). Shipped, but no ratification found: proposed.

15. **[E] A mortal's moments stop the world only for a player who asked.** Threading or following a mortal is what lets its moments interrupt; un-following mutes them. Everyone else's news waits as a badge or a chronicle line. *Why:* interrupts the player did not ask for train them to dismiss interrupts. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-08-26: "every interrupt is one the player asked for" ([review record](../audits/2026-08-26-proactive-agent-actions-review.md) §2.1); rulebook §9 (*Threads are your intelligence*) and §10.8.

## III. The odds — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

16. **[E] One curve for everyone.** Every roll, whether a peasant's, a master's, a beast's or a god's, runs the same path: capability against difficulty, a sigmoid to a chance, a d100, the five-band ladder. No second dice system and no special-case resolution; fights, spells and divine workings ride the same curve. *Source:* settled decision ("Sigmoid → d100 for resolution. One system, no special cases", [`architectural-decisions.md`](../../.claude/skills/state-of-game-design/reference/architectural-decisions.md)); rulebook §7.

17. **[E] The player sees the stakes before paying, in words.** Before any spend the player can read what the step tests, a difficulty word, the named lines for and against, and a forecast word, and the forecast word moves as cards are toggled. How the words look is the UI Laws' business; that they come *before* the spend is this law. *Source:* THR-772 ruling 1, Christian 2026-07-26 ([program plan](../plans/2026-07-26-nudge-model-encounter-system.md): "odds legible, words only"); Christian, 2026-07-24 ([THR-728](../plans/2026-07-24-thr-728-player-cast-variance.md)): a divine working names its stakes before it is cast.

18. **[E] The forecast is the roll.** Anything that shows a chance (the forecast word the player reads, the forecast a mortal chooses with, a fight's odds) is computed by the same call the dice use, with the same modifiers, floors and named lines. A forecast that differs from the roll is a bug, however small. Sibling of Law 1: that law keeps authored rates honest, this one keeps shown odds honest. *Motivating instance:* in September mortals chose challenges with a planner that skipped the scale adjustment and inflated difficulty, so the odds they chose by were not the odds they rolled ([THR-1575](../plans/2026-09-24-thr-1575-forecast-window.md), cause 4). *Source:* rulebook §7, *A mortal takes on a challenge…* (`[IMPL]` THR-1581 under Christian's ruling of 2026-09-24: "the forecast a mortal chooses with is the chance the dice will roll"); rulebook §7, *The odds shown are the odds rolled* (THR-1543).

19. **[P] The price is paid before the dice, never after.** Committing a hand spends its essence up front; if the pool cannot cover it, the commit is refused and the cards come back. The player is never told after a roll that a card they watched move the odds did not apply. *Source:* rulebook §7, *The price is paid before the dice* (`spendNudgeEssence`). Shipped, but no ratification found: proposed.

20. **[E] A mortal takes on what it can win about half the time; who it is decides which.** A mortal engages its own challenges at a forecast of about 50–65%. Skill picks the challenge, not the success rate: success stays level across the world while what a mortal attempts grows with it, and that is what progression looks like. Boldness and caution shift the window a little around the same centre. Long odds are the god's to impose; fate, promises and forced arrivals do not pass through the window. The bands themselves are tuning ("a constant we tweak as we search for a good game", Christian 2026-09-26); level success and rising difficulty are the law. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-09-24 ([THR-1575](../plans/2026-09-24-thr-1575-forecast-window.md)): "who a mortal is should count a lot".

21. **[P] The same god at the same step is dealt the same hand.** Nothing in a hand is shuffled: the cards dealt from a god's repertoire follow from who the god is, what the step is about and what the god has earned, so a reload changes nothing. Variety comes from who the god is becoming and where it stands, never from a hidden die. Luck lives in the roll (§ I), not in the offer. *Source:* rulebook §7, *A hand is part the scene's and part your own* (`dealHand`, THR-1247; Christian approved the dealt-hand framing in chat 2026-08-24/25, [plan](../plans/2026-08-25-thr-1247-dealt-hands.md), but the no-shuffle rule was the design's own). Proposed.

## IV. Failure — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

22. **[E] No outcome is dead air.** Every failure leaves a story artifact the world acts on: a complication, a mark, a seed, a scar, a grudge. "You failed and nothing happened" is a content bug, not a balance setting. *Source:* Christian's creative verdict, 2026-07-03 (THR-571, recorded in the [outcome-ladder plan](../plans/2026-07-03-encounter-balance-outcome-ladder.md): failures become pressure, complications and branching, and no outcome reads as dead air); rulebook §7 (*failure is plot, not punishment*).

23. **[E] The god's hand shows at every band.** A card that bent the odds is paid off in prose at every band it can land on, misfires included; a nudge that backfires is prime story. A card that vanishes on a loss makes failure read as punishment. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-07-25/26 ([THR-772 program plan](../plans/2026-07-26-nudge-model-encounter-system.md): the nudge is paid off "at every band — including the misfire bands"); [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md) § Rejected approaches (a nudge with no failure-band payoff).

24. **[E] Failure never costs skill, and never loops.** A failed attempt still teaches. Failure's price is conditions, standing, essence, quintessence and story, never Reach. A failed challenge is not retried straight away, and a mortal on a losing run looks for something it can win. *Why:* early builds had both traps, mortals retrying the same content without getting anywhere and mortals losing reach to penalties until they were stuck. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-09-24 ([THR-1575](../plans/2026-09-24-thr-1575-forecast-window.md) § the two failure modes).

25. **[E] Heavy loss takes a mortal out of the story, not out of the world.** Failure wears quintessence; worn far enough a mortal goes Broken, out of the story for a while with a road back. Wearing alone never kills; death stays rare and keeps its own rules. A lock-out with no way out is stun-lock, not story, so a lock-out ships only together with its road back. *Source:* THR-772 ruling 5, Christian 2026-07-26 ([program plan](../plans/2026-07-26-nudge-model-encounter-system.md): heavy loss breaks the mortal for a while, death is rare, recovery by rebuild encounters and one costly divine restore); rulebook §7 *Broken* (`BROKEN_GATE_ENABLED` stays off until the rebuild encounters exist).

26. **[E] A paid divine working never hard-fails.** The god's castings roll the same ladder as mortals, but a losing roll is caught at success-at-cost: the working lands crooked and the world charges for it. No refunds; the floor is the compensation. Clean success and the rare surge stay earned. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-07-24 ([THR-728](../plans/2026-07-24-thr-728-player-cast-variance.md)): "Yes, with a safety floor".

## V. Time and the run — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

27. **[E] The world runs in real time and stops for every moment that matters.** An encounter, a choice, a doom stage, a receipt, the Chapter Ledger: each one halts the clock, and when the last one closes the clock returns to the state the player left it in. Reading never costs time. *Why:* time that runs while the player reads turns the weight of a moment into clock anxiety. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-09-27 (the Stellaris model, [the opening plan](../plans/2026-09-27-thr-1605-the-opening.md)); Vision `01-core-loop.md` § Time stops for every moment.

28. **[E] Scan, chapter, aftermath: in that order, one story at a time.** The player chooses whose story to look at; the encounter is the chapter; the aftermath is the pause before the next look, and the world has moved by then. One story is front of stage at a time, and the scan leaves most mortals alone. Consequences compound: what happens now should echo much later. *Source:* Vision `01-core-loop.md` (the order of the beats, "one complex story at a time", "a decision at tick 47 should echo at tick 130").

29. **[E] The game never rations chapters.** How many encounters a run has is mostly the player's doing: more threads woven and more interventions made mean more chapters, with a gentle lean toward more as doom climbs. The answer to many chapters is helping the player hold them (the Chapter Ledger), never a cap. *Source:* Christian's verdict, chat, 2026-07-04 (THR-603, [density plan](../plans/2026-07-04-encounter-density-and-chapter-ledger.md)).

30. **[E] Two clocks press, and one purse pays for both.** Doom runs toward an Unmaking and the Mandate toward a win; essence spent on one is essence not spent on the other. The quiet stretches are never safe, which is what makes attention a spend. *Source:* Vision `00-north-star.md` (two clocks, director-approved sweep 2026-08-28, THR-1335); rulebook §8.

31. **[E] Doom leaves room for one whole journey.** Doom cannot end a world before one threaded mortal has had room for a full character journey. Today that is a tunable floor; later it becomes a proper win condition. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-09-27 ([the opening plan](../plans/2026-09-27-thr-1605-the-opening.md), ruling 3; THR-1646).

32. **[E] A run is not about winning.** The Mandate and Doom are two kinds of ending, not a win and a loss, and both feed the next world. A run is good if it leaves a story the player can tell, whichever clock ran out first. The game does not rank the player. *Source:* Vision `00-north-star.md` (*What a good run is NOT*); Vision `02-non-negotiables.md` §2; rulebook §11.

## VI. Power, growth and the economy — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

33. **[E] A mortal is what it can do, not what class it is.** Capability is Domain Capability across the eight Reaches, fed by training, traits, items and company. No classical stats (STR/DEX/INT) and no fixed classes. *Source:* [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) Rejected Approaches (classical stats); Vision `taste-profile.md` anti-patterns.

34. **[E] Reaches and Spheres are two separate axes.** Reaches are what a power does (eight); Spheres are what fuels it (twelve, in opposed pairs). Neither subsumes the other and they combine freely. Quintessence is not a ninth Reach, and Spirit is a Sphere, not a Reach. *Source:* [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) Load-Bearing Architectural Decisions; [`cosmology.md`](../canon/cosmology.md); [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md) § Rejected approaches.

35. **[E] The god is a mortal grown great, on the same ladder.** The Ascendant uses the same prerequisites and the same capability system as every mortal: Domain Capability tiers and sphere checks apply equally. A god power that needs a special-case entity or a parallel stat is a wrong design. *Source:* [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) Load-Bearing Architectural Decisions (Ascendants use the same prerequisite system as agents).

36. **[E] The purse is small on purpose.** The player cannot afford every intervention it wants, and choosing which one is the play. Every card costs something. *Source:* Vision `00-north-star.md` (a "small supply of divine essence"; any card "will cost"); rulebook §6 (*the economy is tight on purpose*).

37. **[E] Home ground is cheaper and surer.** The god's power loop is Dominion: a place, mortal or faction that leans toward the god's spheres costs less to work and lands more often; threads and powers spread that ground; the opposing spheres, as the cosmology pairs them, push back. Graded, never on or off. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-10-05 (THR-1745, recorded in the [Dominion core plan](../plans/2026-10-11-thr-1748-dominion-core.md)).

38. **[E] A god's powers come from what the world offers and what it has earned.** Most god cards are taken up from the world's offers, paid for in essence earned through the card's own sphere, never handed out on one schedule every god shares. Where a card sits on the ladder is data on the card, so the distribution is tuned by editing data. *Motivating instance:* two different gods used to share about four of their first five cards (THR-1770). *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-10-09 (THR-1770, option B, and "this should all be metadata on the actions"; [offerings plan](../plans/2026-10-13-thr-1811-god-card-progression-and-offerings.md)).

39. **[E] No god grows stronger between runs; the world remembers.** Every run starts the god equally strong, in a new world. What carries over lives in the world (monuments, descendants, relics, the World-Soul's tilt) and in wider choices (more hungers, courts and cards to choose from). Rivals are generated from the World-Soul, never a fixed pantheon. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-10-11 (THR-1773: "World remembers + wider", [plan](../plans/2026-10-14-thr-1813-the-world-remembers.md)); [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) Rejected Approaches (fixed rival pantheon).

## VII. Building systems and content — collated 2026-10-14 (THR-1891)

40. **[E] Game design comes before fiction.** A piece of content is designed as game first (its structure, payoffs, prizes, penalties and consequences) and the story is written inside those choices. A story written first and then fitted to mechanics is rejected. *Source:* Christian, chat, 2026-08-24; [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md) § Rejected approaches (fiction-first briefs).

41. **[E] One vocabulary of effects.** Spells, items, monster powers, nudge cards and encounter endings change the world through one shared set of effects and the same machinery. A feature does not get its own parallel ability system. *Source:* Christian's condition on the fight design, 2026-09-23 (THR-1263, that fights be ready to take spells; recorded as one effect vocabulary in the [fight-block plan](../plans/2026-09-23-fight-block.md)); rulebook §7 (what a card grants is not a separate card language).

42. **[E] Simple frameworks; the richness is in the connections.** A framework (undertakings, encounters, fights) stays simple and scalable, and its complexity comes from how it connects to other systems, not from special cases per kind inside it. Before adding a bespoke case, look for the generic seam. *Source:* Christian, 2026-09-03, approving the undertaking factory pilot: "make sure the undertaking framework is kept systemically simple, scalable and that the complexity comes from how it connects to other systems" ([THR-1392 brainstorm](../plans/2026-09-03-thr-1392-verb-object-undertakings-brainstorm.md)).

43. **[E] One concept per thing the player has to learn.** Before inventing a concept for one surface, use the one the player already knows. The social score between any two parties is reputation, with one word ladder, everywhere. *Why:* "custom concepts are difficult for players to learn and understand" (Christian). *Source:* Christian, 2026-08-23 (THR-1206, [reputation plan](../plans/2026-08-23-thr-1206-reputation-unification.md)).

44. **[E] Knowledge enriches; it never hides.** What a mortal or the god knows can make a scene richer (recognition in the prose, a bonus on the odds), but intel never hides encounters or decides what content exists. *Source:* Christian, project direction, 2026-05-07 (THR-138); [`encounters.md`](../canon/encounters.md) § Rejected approaches.

45. **[P] The game never promises what the world will not do.** Anything that states a consequence (an item's page, a card's grant, a promise made to a mortal) is backed by a system that actually does it, or it is not said. *Instances:* a made item "only ever promises what the world actually does" (THR-1570); a card that promises content the game never built fails the build (THR-885); a mortal below the spotlight gets the grudge without the vendetta rather than "a promise the world could not keep" (THR-1298); before THR-1241 an artifact could promise its bearer would not die, and the bearer died on schedule. UI Law 56 is the same rule for consequence chips, and is ratified. *Source:* rulebook §4, §7, §10.7. Proposed as the general rule.

A law a machine can check gets its check in the same PR that first relies on it.
- **The skill loads this file.** `game-design-laws` is the load for any design or implementation pass that changes how the game behaves. Its law check names the laws a change touches, conforms to or would break, and the result goes in the plan doc or the PR body as one line.
- **Design passes:** a plan that adds a random draw names which law-5 tool it uses (or "plain dice", with the reason) and how Law 1 is measured. A plan that changes any mechanic names the laws it touches and how it keeps them.
- **The laws are canon in the director loop.** When Christian asks for a change to how the game plays, the session first assesses it against these laws. A change that breaks an [E] law goes to him as one plain-language yes/no; an [E] law is never changed as a side effect of one ticket.
- **A rule that breaks a law is a finding.** If the rulebook, a canon page or the code contradicts a law, raise it: fix the rule, or ask Christian whether the law should move. Never quietly reword either side.
- **Change protocol:** amending a law takes one line in `Docs/changelog.md` naming the law number. Laws marked [P] are ratified or vetoed by Christian in chat; the mark then changes to [E] with the chat date as source. A new law enters as [P] with its source; a law with no source does not go in. **Law numbers are permanent**, because code comments and the rulebook cite them: a new law takes the next free number (in its chapter, or in a new chapter at the end), and a retired law keeps its number, struck through, with the date and the reason.
