⛰ The Land

A high flat expanse above the world, wind-scoured and remote. The plateau drops away into dry canyons whose walls show the strata of ages. The air is thin, carrying dust from the east and silence from everywhere else.

✦ The Soul

Energy crackles through the high stone — static charge building before storms, lightning strikes frequent enough that the canyon-folk say the rock remembers each bolt. At dusk, thin golden threads pulse in exposed quartz veins.

Beneath the charge, faint Time pools in canyon depths — the strata glow orange at certain hours, temporal residue from an age when something here measured the turning of the heavens.

⚑ The People

The Children of the Shifting Dry Canyons claim this height — shaped by patience and territorial pride, marking boundaries in hammered iron and bone. Fluid hierarchies, personal honor over written law.

The Iron Covenant holds the gates. Their banners mark every approach to the Forge of Sorrow. The Covenant's grip is administrative — they tax the pass roads and maintain a garrison — but the people's loyalties run deeper than any banner.

At the Forge, the Hollow King operates on careful reciprocity. What he receives, he returns in kind, and nothing more.

◇ The Ruins

Before the Canyon people, the Star-Readers charted the heavens from this vantage. Their name survives in the region: the Storm-Born Reach — a corruption of a Star-Reader phrase meaning "where sky touches stone."

Shattered observatories dot the eastern edge. Star-charts etched in stone floors still glow faintly when Time-residue aligns — a dead culture's instruments measuring a sky that has moved on.

They read the heavens until the heavens read them back.

The eastern observatories have not been fully explored. Something in the lowest level still draws power.
A reconstructed star-chart might reveal patterns the current sky no longer shows.
