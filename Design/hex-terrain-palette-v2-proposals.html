Three approaches to harmonize terrain colors with the Threadbearer UI palette — grounded in nature, warmed toward gold

Mini Map Preview

Same hex layout across all four palettes — a representative terrain mix to judge overall map feel at a glance.

Current (v1)

A: Parchment Wash

B: Golden Hour

C: Tapestry

Design Rationale

A: Parchment Wash (subtle) — Lightest touch. Greens lose ~10-15% saturation, gain a warm yellow bias. Cold tiles shift from pure grey to warm ivory. Browns become slightly more amber. Water desaturates gently toward slate-blue. The map still reads as a vivid nature map, but whites and highlights harmonize with the parchment UI text.

B: Golden Hour (moderate) — The sweet spot. Greens shift significantly toward olive/sage (hue rotates ~10-15 degrees toward yellow). Highlands and deserts align more closely with the UI gold accent (#d4a040). Cold tiles gain a distinctly warm cast like aged paper. Water moves toward muted teal-blue, less "cartoon ocean." The overall feel is a map seen in warm lamplight — still readable as nature, but clearly from the same world as the UI.

C: Tapestry (bold) — Strongest shift. Greens become distinctly olive/khaki — more aged tapestry than satellite photo. All warm tones converge toward the gold/amber range. Cold tiles match parchment-secondary. Water becomes decidedly muted steel-blue. The map reads like a medieval illuminated chart — beautiful and atmospheric, but biome contrast is reduced. Best paired with stronger signifier icons to compensate for lower color differentiation.

What changes across all three:
- Forests shift the most (bright neon green to warm olive) — this is the biggest visual difference
- Deserts/highlands barely change (they're already closest to UI gold)
- Cold biomes gain warm ivory tint (pure white to parchment)
- Water shifts from vivid cyan-blue toward muted dusty blue
- High mountains and broken lands gain warm undertones instead of neutral grey
