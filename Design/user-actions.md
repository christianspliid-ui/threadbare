2. **A subsystem with no callers is invisible to every sweep that looks for wrong behaviour, because it has none.** Seven instances in three days, all closed: THR-779 (61 templates, no draw path), THR-787 (three engine reads, wrong field name), THR-803 (whole chain subsystem, zero production callers), THR-800 (62 refs, no definition), THR-783 (five authoring sites, no reader — and beneath it THR-809, an effect kind whose target definitions are never seeded), THR-784 (four UI consumers, no writer), THR-811 (five gates, one lookup that could not see their operands). **THR-811 is the variant worth naming separately: the callers existed and ran.** What was missing was resolution — every gate read its field off `undefined` and passed. A gate that cannot find its subject is indistinguishable from a gate that approves, and neither the type system nor the suite can tell them apart. Every one was authored or implemented capability that nothing routed to; every one failed silently; every one became visible only when something measured the producing side against the consuming side. **The class detector, not the individual repair, is the deliverable** — the repairs ran 30–82 minutes each once found. THR-783 adds the sharpest corollary: **wiring a dead field proves the wire, not the effect.** Only running the world and looking for the outcome distinguishes "connected" from "working", and here those differed.
