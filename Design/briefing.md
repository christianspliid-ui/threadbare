## Decided for you

- [The god's stake in the mortal economy](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1871/the-gods-stake-in-the-mortal-economy-what-a-god-gains-from-a-thriving) — **your god earns from the economy through its worshippers, not through coin.** A hungry town on your ground prays to you, and you have about six days to answer. Answer it (Bless the Harvest, Open the Markets) and your hold on that place grows. If the town then recovers, its people become a faithful congregation: a small essence source that thrives while the town is fed and withers when it starves. If you ignore the prayer, the rival god answers instead and goes after that town first. Help given to a hungry town off your ground widens your ground a little but wins no congregation. There is no tithe and no fear to collect; Blight hurts through hunger and unrest. Three new cards join the omen market: *Fortune's Favour* and *Ruin* make one mortal richer or poorer, and *Bless the Holding* makes a held place pay its owner more for a season. Claim Resource now pays by what it claims, and Open the Markets' promised thieves' guild gets built or the promise is removed. *To veto, say:* **"the god should be paid by the coin, not the prayers"**, **"every hungry town should pray to me"**, or **"a god should profit from fear"**. *— from the [design lane](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/ops/Docs/ops/design-lane-2026-10-14j.md)*
- [Hunger and unrest](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1869/hunger-and-unrest-what-scarcity-does-to-mortals-and-what-a-town-on-the) — **a hungry town now boils over instead of shrugging.** Today unrest drains away within a day: the four towns stuck in famine for weeks sit at 0 unrest. Now unrest climbs toward what the town lives through, mostly hunger and sieges, and passes four steps: **Restless** (people move to a fed town nearby), **Lawless** (theft and granary riots; the lord's tax income halves), **On the edge** (the town goes over to a better lord next door, and the old lord wants it back), **Rising** (the townsfolk throw off their lord, become rebels the lord will march on, and burn their own finest building). Poor towns simmer but do not boil on poverty alone. In 50-day tests on three worlds, 7–12 towns turned Restless, 0–2 reached the edge, and none rose yet. A hungry town prays to the god whose land it stands on, and that can be a rival; what answering earns your god is decided next, on [the god's stake](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1871/the-gods-stake-in-the-mortal-economy-what-a-god-gains-from-a-thriving). [The prototype branch (never merged)](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/tree/proto/thr-1869-hunger-unrest), [the test output, seed 42](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/proto/thr-1869-hunger-unrest/scripts/proto/hunger-unrest-sketch.seed42.no-impulse.output.txt). *To veto, say:* **"poor towns should riot too"**, **"towns should never change hands without a war"**, or **"a rising should be its own kind of faction"**. *— from the [design lane](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/ops/Docs/ops/design-lane-2026-10-14i.md)*
- [Undertakings in the economy](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1873/undertakings-in-the-economy-what-founding-building-and-trading-should) — **what mortals build now stays built and pays.** Founding a town: the founder's faction rules and taxes it, and the founder keeps a freehold (a founder with no town-holding faction rules it alone). Raising a building: it is the town's next building, picked by [the settlement economy](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1923/plan-doc-the-settlement-economy-towns-grow-from-surplus-build-to-their) from what its land can support, not a workshop every time; the builder holds it. Trade: a trade run pays the trader by what the road carries, and whoever opens a road collects its toll. Ambitions now ask for things their own undertakings can make (today Found Anew wants an estate and Rebuild from Ashes wants a camp, and neither undertaking can build one); Dominate Regional Trade also counts roads held and a Wealthy purse. A monopoly is a faction's goal, never one mortal's. *To veto, say:* **"a founder should be a lord in their own right"**, **"mortals shouldn't get rich from trade runs"**, or **"let mortals build any building they like"**. *— from the [design lane](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/ops/Docs/ops/design-lane-2026-10-14h.md)*
- [Economic war aims](https://linear.app/threadbare/issue/THR-1868/economic-war-aims-should-factions-fight-and-expand-for-staples-trade) — **factions now fight for things worth having.** An army marches only on an enemy; a faction with no quarrel keeps its army at home. It defends its own towns and sworn towns first, then goes for the richest town in reach (what the town and its fields would pay its owner, less for far towns). Monopolies become regional: most of a resource within 8 hexes, not 60% of the whole world (which never happened); that gave 3 monopolies from day one. A monopoly pays its holder more and lets it **embargo** an enemy's towns in the region, and enemies want to break it: 11 of 15 new war targets in the test would. In the 50-day test, 19 of 20 armies today marched on factions they had no quarrel with; the new targets pay 43% more. [The prototype branch (never merged)](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/tree/proto/thr-1868-war-aims), [the test output](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/proto/thr-1868-war-aims/scripts/proto/war-aims-sketch.output.txt), [the monopoly comparison](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/proto/thr-1868-war-aims/scripts/proto/war-aims-monopoly-alt.output.txt). *To veto, say:* **"armies should fight whoever is closest"**, **"monopolies should be world-wide and rare"**, or **"factions shouldn't embargo"**. *— from the [design lane](https://github.com/christianspliid-ui/threadbare/blob/ops/Docs/ops/design-lane-2026-10-14g.md)*
