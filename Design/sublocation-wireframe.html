Reading order = story order

Active sublocations with agents sort to the top. The player's eye naturally goes to where the action is. Empty sublocations collapse below — visible but not competing for attention.

Each agent row = a narrative beat

Agent name + encounter name + step progress tells you the whole story in one line. "Kael the Devoted — Rite of Ascension — step 2/4: Trial of Faith" is a sentence, not a data table.

Step dots show momentum

Green = completed, gold = current, grey = pending. The player can see at a glance how deep into a chain each agent is. More green = closer to transformation.

Divine sublocations feel different

Subtle purple tint + "divine · temporal" badge. The player knows they placed it; agents don't. The "dissolves when encounter completes" hint reminds the god of the arrangement's fragility.

Available encounters = latent possibility

Muted list under each active sublocation shows what could happen. "Consecrate the Altar — easy" tells the player: if another agent arrives here, this is what they might try. It's the god surveying their domain.

Empty sublocations = worldbuilding

"Barracks — No agents present" tells the player this place exists but is quiet. An empty barracks after a war is a story. The player might decide to lure someone there.
