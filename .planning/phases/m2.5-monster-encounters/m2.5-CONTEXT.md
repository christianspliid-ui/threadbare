# Phase M2.5: Monster Encounters Integration - Context

**Gathered:** 2026-03-30
**Status:** Ready for planning

<domain>
## Phase Boundary

Fold TB-051 (Monster Encounters) into the existing conflict layer. Monsters as wilderness threats: territorial creatures on the map, hostile to armies and agents alike. Province danger gradient drives monster density. Monster lairs as locations that can be cleared. Armies and agents can be sent to clear monster threats. Monster factions participate in the same systems as civilized factions.

</domain>

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### Monster Identity & Nature
- **Layered system:** Primordial baseline fauna + sphere corruption as modifier. A cave bear is dangerous; a Death-touched cave bear is terrifying. Sphere adds flavor, terrain sets the base.
- **One archetype per sphere** (~9 base monster types at launch). Each sphere gets a signature creature: Force → war beast, Death → undead, Nature → primordial, etc. Terrain determines WHERE they appear, sphere determines WHAT they are.
- **Naming:** Named elites use descriptive titles from the prose system ("The Blighted Matriarch", "Goreclaw the Relentless"), not generated fantasy names.

### Monster Tiers
- **Generic tier (encounter-only):** No graph nodes. Generic monsters exist only as encounter templates that fire based on hex danger level and lair proximity. The lair is the graph presence — generic monsters are its output.
- **Named elites (stationary lair bosses):** Graph actor nodes attached to their lair. They don't wander — you go to them. Creates deliberate quest targets.
- **Monster factions (full faction system):** Monster factions are faction nodes with ambitions, reputation, armies — identical to civilized factions. They participate in the same encounter/conflict pipeline. Maximum reuse of existing systems.

### Monster Faction Behavior
- **Sphere-specific goals:** Each sphere's monsters have different ambitions. Death monsters want to kill/corrupt. Force monsters want to dominate territory. Mind monsters want to infiltrate. Drives varied monster behavior.
- **Full armies:** Monster factions can raise hordes (army actor nodes) that march on settlements. Uses the existing army system. A Death faction raises an undead horde; a Force faction assembles a beast pack.
- **Proactive attacks:** Monster factions with expansion/destruction ambitions will target nearby settlements. Their armies march on cities like civilized armies. Creates constant pressure on civilization.
- **Sphere-aligned coexistence:** If a civilized faction's sphere alignment matches a monster faction's, there's a truce or uneasy coexistence. A Death-aligned cult might tolerate undead. Emergent storytelling.

### Sphere Feedback Loop
- **Bidirectional:** Strong sphere energy attracts/creates monsters, AND monster lairs increase local sphere pressure. Unchecked monster lairs become escalating threats as the sphere-monster feedback loop amplifies.
- **Root cause mechanic:** Players can't just clear lairs — they need to address underlying sphere pressure to prevent reinfestation. Connects to the sphere affinity system from Phase 10.

### Divine Interaction
- **Full divine targeting:** Monster lairs and named elites are valid targets for divine actions via the ActionDrawer. Bless a beast to make it your champion, smite a lair to weaken it, use sphere-matched intervention during monster encounters. Monsters are first-class entities in the divine action system.

### Danger Gradient & Spawning
- **4 danger zones:** Capital (safe, no monsters), Heartland (very low risk), Borderland (moderate risk), Wilderness/unprovinced (high risk). Province roles already exist as Uint8Array (0=capital, 1=heartland, 2=borderland); unprovided hexes are implicit zone 4.
- **Worldgen + escalation:** World generation places sparse initial lairs based on terrain + sphere affinity. Over time, unchecked lairs spawn adjacent lairs, and high sphere pressure creates new ones. The world gets more dangerous if ignored.
- **Moderate density at worldgen:** Most wilderness/borderland areas have at least one lair nearby. Creates clear safe-vs-dangerous contrast from tick 1.
- **Slow escalation:** New lairs appear every ~20-30 ticks if conditions are met. Player has time to react. Escalation is a strategic concern, not an emergency. (All rates as named constants — NFP #1.)

### Lair Mechanics
- **3 tiers that upgrade:** Minor (generic encounters only) → Major (has a named elite) → Legendary (monster faction capital, raises armies). Lairs start minor and upgrade if left unchecked. Neglected minor lairs become major problems.
- **Don't block settlement:** Lairs threaten settlements and suppress prosperity but don't hard-block settlement placement. Players can settle in dangerous areas but pay a cost. (Deviation from roadmap wording which said "must be cleared before settlement".)
- **Cleared lairs become explorable sites:** Cleared lair transitions to 'cleared_lair' location subtype. Can be settled, claimed by a faction, or becomes a resource site.
- **Reinfestation possible:** If underlying sphere pressure remains high and the cleared site is unoccupied/unsettled, monsters can reclaim it over time. You need to HOLD territory, not just clear it.
- **Lair icon on hex:** Distinct lair signifier on the hex using existing location icon layer — monster-themed, sphere-tinted.

### Clearing Lairs
- **Threat-appropriate response:** Small lairs (minor tier) can be cleared by capable agents via Adventuring Guild quests (existing quest framework). Major/legendary lairs require faction army ambitions — army sent to destroy it. Named elite lairs might need both: army to break defenses, hero agent to confront the boss.
- **Faction assessment:** Factions assess threat tier and propose appropriate response. Agents and factions should evaluate what's needed — an elite monster requires an army, a small band requires agents.

### Monster-Army Interaction
- **March attrition + encounter chance:** Armies passing through monster-infested hexes pay Quintessence attrition AND have a chance of encountering a lair's defenders. Double pressure creates real cost to marching through the wild.
- **Full battle system:** Monster horde vs civilized army uses the same battle resolution (momentum, spotlights, aftermath). Monster armies can besiege cities using the same siege mechanics. Maximum reuse of M2 conflict code.

### Claude's Discretion
- Exact sphere-to-archetype mapping (which creature type per sphere)
- Lair icon art direction and signifier design
- Specific encounter template content for monster encounters
- Quintessence attrition rate for monster territory traversal
- Exact escalation thresholds and timing constants
- Monster faction ambition weight tuning
- How sphere-aligned coexistence is computed (threshold matching)

</decisions>

<code_context>
## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets
- **Province role system:** `PROVINCE_ROLE_CAPITAL=0`, `HEARTLAND=1`, `BORDERLAND=2` in `worldgen/types.ts` — danger gradient maps directly to these + unprovided hexes as zone 4
- **Faction system:** Full faction node + ambition + army pipeline from Phase 12 — monster factions reuse this wholesale
- **Army system:** `armyMovement.ts`, `battleResolution.ts`, `siegeResolution.ts`, `battleAftermath.ts` — monster armies use the same code
- **Encounter templates:** `encounter-content.ts` already has wilderness-typed encounters; `faction-encounter-content.ts` has `ag.quest.monster_hunt` template
- **Location icon layer:** HexMapV2 location rendering system — lair icons use this existing layer
- **Sphere affinity:** Hex-level sphere scores from Phase 10 — used to determine monster type and spawn probability
- **Quintessence system:** Army Quintessence degradation already exists — extend for monster territory attrition

### Established Patterns
- **Graph nodes for entities:** All entities (agents, factions, armies, locations) are graph nodes with property bags — monster elites and lairs follow this pattern
- **Encounter-only for ephemeral threats:** Some game events exist only as encounters, not persistent nodes — generic monsters follow this established pattern
- **Faction ambition → army → battle pipeline:** The M2 pipeline from faction ambition to army raise to battle is fully built — monster factions plug into this same flow
- **Location subtypes as strings:** `locationSubtype` is string-typed — adding 'lair', 'cleared_lair' is trivial

### Integration Points
- **World generation:** Lair seeding happens in a new worldgen pass (after sphere affinity seeding)
- **Orchestrator:** New phase for lair escalation/spawn checks (slow cadence, every N ticks)
- **Encounter system:** New monster encounter templates + lair-based encounter generation
- **Army movement:** Attrition modifier when traversing monster-infested hexes
- **HexMapV2:** Lair icons as new location signifier variant
- **ActionDrawer:** Monster lairs and named elites as divine action targets

</code_context>

<specifics>
## Specific Ideas

- Monsters as first-class faction participants — not a separate system, but full members of the existing faction/ambition/army pipeline
- The sphere feedback loop (sphere → monsters → more sphere pressure) is a core mechanic, not just flavor — it creates a strategic problem that can't be solved by just clearing lairs
- Threat-appropriate response scaling: Adventuring Guild quests for minor threats, faction armies for major ones. The game should assess and propose the right tool for the job
- Lairs that upgrade over time create a "neglect penalty" — the longer you ignore a problem, the harder it gets

</specifics>

<deferred>
## Deferred Ideas

- **Bestiary UI** — Player-facing catalog of encountered monsters with type, sphere, known lairs. Acknowledged as its own phase.
- **Monster diplomacy via divine action** — Player brokering pacts between monster and civilized factions. Core system supports it (sphere-aligned coexistence), but explicit divine-action-brokered diplomacy deferred.
- **Mobile named elites** — Currently stationary lair bosses. Future phase could add patrol/territory behavior for named creatures.

</deferred>
