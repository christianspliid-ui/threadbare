### Human Verification Required

#### 1. Organic Shoreline on Coastal Land Hexes

**Test:** Open `http://localhost:5173/?view=hexv2`. Zoom into a coastline area using scroll wheel. Find hexes where land meets ocean.
**Expected:** Land hexes directly adjacent to water should have their terrain color (green for forest, tan/brown for arid terrain) but their ocean-facing edges should be cut by an organic, curved boundary — not a straight hexagonal edge. The boundary should look like a natural coastline, not a grid.
**Why human:** Three.js WebGL stencil clipping cannot be verified by DOM inspection or Playwright. The stencil buffer operates at the GPU pixel level and requires a rendered screenshot from a browser.

#### 2. Water Hexes Show Full Hexagonal Shape

**Test:** At the same coastline view, inspect ocean/lake hexes on the water side.
**Expected:** Water and lake hexes should display as complete, full hexagons with their depth-band colors (deep ocean blues, shallows teal). They should NOT show any clipping or organic edge cutting into them.
**Why human:** Confirming the water mesh has no stencil test applied requires visual inspection.

#### 3. Fog of War on Both Mesh Types

**Test:** Open `http://localhost:5173/?view=hexv2&fog`. Pan around the map to trigger fog visibility changes on both land and ocean hexes.
**Expected:** Both land hexes (stencil-tested) and water hexes (normal render) should respond to fog state changes. Unexplored hexes should be dark; revealed hexes should show their terrain/water color. No hex type should be stuck in wrong state.
**Why human:** Fog routing through `globalToMeshMap` requires runtime state verification — the code path branches based on fog state, and both mesh types must be updated correctly across state transitions.
