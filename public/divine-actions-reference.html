Divine Actions & Prerequisites

Everything you do to the world is one of five verbs — and most of what you could do is quietly withheld until you have earned the right to see it.

Five verbs, and only fiveEvery divine act, however dressed, is one of these underneath.

You never pick a verb from a menu — the game teaches them through the moments themselves, never as filter chips on a wheel. But underneath every intervention you will ever make, there is one of exactly five things a god does to a world. Knowing the five is knowing the whole grammar of your power.

Create

Bring something into being — an artifact, a settlement, a bond between mortals that was not there before.

Find

Perceive and reveal. Search the world, lift the fog, surface what was hidden from you.

Change

A one-shot alteration for a one-time cost. Fire and forget — the world keeps the mark, not the leash.

Destroy

Remove, corrupt, scatter, erase. The unmaking verb, and often the loudest.

The Signature Verb

Control

A sustained grip. Ongoing upkeep, held open turn after turn — and contestable by any rival god who meets the same bar you did.

Why Control is the god-game's signature

The other four verbs resolve and end. Control is the one that stays. It is not a bigger Change — it is a standing commitment that drains essence for as long as you hold it, and every held thing is a visible prize a rival can try to usurp (take from you) or shatter. Choosing to Control is choosing to keep spending, and to be seen keeping it.

You can't shape what you haven't seenFind is the verb that unlocks the others. Perception comes before power.

There is a natural order to the verbs, and it is not arbitrary. Find gates Change and Control. You cannot reshape a mortal's fate, or take sustained hold of a place, before you have perceived it — the fog has to lift before the hand can land. The chain the game leans you toward is Find → Change or Create → Control: see it, touch it, then keep it.

Find → Change · Create → Control

This gate is deliberately soft, not an iron law. It is taught through which actions a target offers, not enforced as a wall that throws you back — you will feel the ordering as the shape of what is available, the way a story withholds a door until you have found the key.

Everything you look at is an action targetFocus a node, and the game asks what a god could do to it here.

Threadbearer has no fixed list of buttons. Instead, every node you focus on in a detail view — a mortal, a location, a sublocation, a hex, an artifact — becomes a target, and the action drawer fills with exactly the interventions that make sense for that thing, in that place, for you. Open a peasant and you are offered peasant-shaped acts; open a ruined temple and the offers change entirely.

The drawer is assembled fresh each time by asking a cascade of questions about the target and about you: what kind of node is it, what particular sort, what does it already carry, which cosmic energies does it answer to, can you afford it, is it in reach. Whatever survives every question is what you may do. There is no slot count and no cap on the imagined space — "what is doable here" is open-ended and grown from data, never a hand-authored list.

Why some cards never appearAn empty drawer is information. The absences are as designed as the offers.

Sometimes you focus a target and an obvious power simply isn't there. That is not a bug — the game hides what you cannot attempt, so that "visible" and "possible" mean the same thing. There are three reasons a card stays dark:

Outside your two domains

Your identity grants exactly two Reaches, fixed for the whole run. A power belonging to a Reach you do not hold never appears — not even as aspiration. This wall is permanent by design.

Sphere misalignment

Many acts answer to a specific cosmic energy. If your sphere alignment does not resonate with what the action demands, it stays hidden until your alignment does.

Not capable enough

Some acts ask a minimum Domain Capability — a tier of competence in the relevant Reach. Below the bar, "can you do this kind of thing?" answers no, and the card waits.

The first of these is worth dwelling on, because it is the one that never changes. Sphere and capability are things you can grow into over a run. A Reach outside your two domains is a door that was walled off when you chose who you were, and stays walled off until the run ends. You are a particular kind of god, not a universal one — and the drawer is where that particularity is felt, turn to turn.

How a casting landsPaying the essence buys the attempt, not the result — but a paid casting never simply fails.

Clearing the prerequisites gets the card into your hand. What happens when you play it is a second question, and the answer is not always the same.

Castings with no difficulty always succeed. The divine.* soul-verbs — Dream, Whisper, the intimate work — along with your reach signatures and pure observation, land every time. That certainty is a statement, not an oversight: a god reaching into a sleeping mind does not fumble.

Every other casting rolls, on the same sigmoid→d100 ladder a peasant rolls on — your Domain Capability in that Reach against the difficulty the act was authored with. A tentative working and a world-shaking one are not priced alike, and the roll is where you feel it.

But the ladder has a floor under it. A paid casting can never outright fail. A losing roll is caught at success-at-cost: the working lands, and the world charges you for the holding — a complication, a scar, a thread pulled tighter than you meant. There are no refunds, because the floor is the compensation. The upside stays fully open: a clean success, or the rare surge, is still earned rather than granted.

You are told the stakes before you commit — and only where there are stakes to tell. A focused card whose price genuinely reaches the roll names the risk in plain words — a steady, an uncertain, or a perilous working. Never a percentage: you are a god weighing an act, not a gambler reading a table. A casting with no difficulty says nothing at all, and that silence is itself the tell.

But the floor described above cuts both ways. Where it guarantees your odds it also flattens them: at the intimate and the near-at-hand scales — most of what a young god can reach — the authored price is capped away before the roll ever sees it, and two acts priced a world apart land on identical odds. A risk word there would be describing a number the world ignores. So the card names the working's size instead — a working the size of one place — because at that scale the reach of the act is the thing setting your chances. As you deepen, more of the authored price survives the floor, the harder words come back on their own, and the card starts weighing risk again exactly when risk becomes real.

Push and resist — the quintessence gambles a mortal can spend to buy better odds or soften a loss — stay mortal. Your economy is essence.

Your two reach signaturesBeyond the shared verbs, each run hands you two headline powers — as story, never from a shop.

The five verbs are the grammar every god shares. On top of them, each run grants you exactly two reach signatures — one headline divine power for each of your two domains. The Warhost for Iron, Rend the Gate for Veil, the Great Work for Stone, and so on across all eight Reaches: the signature is the thing your kind of god is known for.

You do not buy them. A signature is permanently reach-gated — a signature for a Reach outside your domains will never be offered — and it arrives as a moment in your story, not a purchase:

  • Your primary signature is granted at the culmination of the opening spine — Beat 4, "A Path Opens" — alongside your choice of god-path.
  • Your secondary arrives later, when the living world next calls on your deeper domain.
Signatures scale with your sphere power

How hard a signature lands is not fixed. It scales with your sphere power — the same cosmic strength that colours the rest of your expression. A god steeped in their sphere throws a heavier signature than one only lightly aligned; the price rises with the potency, so mastery makes the act mightier, never merely cheaper.

Three states of a power — the path codexThe drawer shows only what you can do now. The codex shows the whole shape of your becoming.

Because the drawer hides what you cannot yet attempt, it answers "what now?" cleanly — but it cannot answer "what could I still become?" That question lives in the codex, a browsable catalog of every divine act. Open it from your character sheet (or the Signatures readout on your dashboard), and each card wears one of three plain marks for this incarnation:

Yours

A power already in your hand — revealed into this run's palette and ready to call. These are the cards the drawer offers when their target and cost allow.

Within reach

In your domains, but not yet earned this run. A path still open to you — through a deepening, a beat's gift, or a milestone. This is your becoming: what you have not done yet, but could.

Another life

A power of a Reach you did not take. Not this incarnation — it belongs to a different kind of god. The codex shows it greyed so the wall is legible, where the drawer simply keeps it dark.

The codex is deliberately kept out of the drawer: your turn-to-turn hand stays clean, holding only what you can actually play, while the fuller map of your identity — held, earnable, and forever-elsewhere — waits one click away. From the dashboard, "what you could still become" opens the codex already filtered to within reach — the shortest read of your run's remaining arc.

Designer Notes

The targeting cascade, the reach gate, sphere-power scaling, and where signatures are authored.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced from the rulebook — this page mints none). Synthesis authority for this page is Docs/canon/rulebook.md §4; the cosmology spine is Docs/canon/cosmology.md. Where any surface disagrees, the rulebook and the UL win on their respective boundaries.

Five verbs as implicit substrate IMPL

  • Create / Find / Change / Destroy / Control are CRUD-ish verbs threaded through UnifiedActionTemplate (src/types/unifiedAction.ts), the single action format since THR-108. They are not surfaced as ActionDrawer filter chips — resolved as implicit substrate, taught through encounter prose (rulebook §4, Open Q resolved 2026-06-23). No "pick a verb" UI exists by design.
  • Control's sustained semantics (upkeep drain, contestation via usurp/destroy reactions, the visible control-effect node a rival can target) are the part still landing — see the essence-control page (W6) for the sustain-model detail. DESIGN for the sustain/contestation half; the verb tag itself is IMPL.

Generalized Action Targeting — the filter cascade IMPL

  • getTargetActionSlots(params): WheelSlot[] in src/engine/targetActions.ts is the core function. It takes a TargetContext for any focused graph node and returns the drawer's slots. There is no fixed slot count; MAX_SLOTS = 20 is only a UI-overflow guard (TARGET_ACTION_CONSTANTS).
  • The cascade runs in this order (file header, code-true): 1 node-type (targetCategories ∋ nodeType) → 2 subtype (targetSubtypes) → 3 traits (requiredTargetTraits) & 3b node-properties (requiredNodeProperties) → reach (requiresReach, below) → 4 sphere (sphereAffinity ∈ accessibleSpheres) → 5 essence (affordable essenceCost) → 6 range (hex distance from avatar) → 7 revelation (narrativeLayer revealed on the target hex). Each rejection increments a per-gate counter for inspectability (NFP #2).
  • 119+ unified templates span the CRUD, encounter, divine, location, attachment, sublocation, and hex categories. The browsable, code-true catalog is the Action Catalog (Deep Reference); its buckets come from ASCENDANT_ACTION_BUCKETS (src/data/ascendant-beat-content.ts:465).
  • Rejected approaches (do not reintroduce): the AgentWheel and any fixed / capped action-slot list. The open-ended data-driven pool replaced both (CLAUDE.md Rejected Approaches).

The reach gate — permanent, set-membership IMPL

  • Templates declaring requiresReach are hidden unless the ascendant's affinity in that Reach meets REACH_GATE_MIN_AFFINITY = 0.2 (src/data/influence-content.ts:92). Below the bar, the slot is dropped and counts.byReach increments. Gate logic: src/engine/targetActions.ts ~L238, shipped in THR-503.
  • This is a permanent set-membership filter, not aspiration: an ascendant's two Reaches are fixed for the run, so a card requiring a third Reach can never surface — distinct from within-run unlock gates (revelation/essence/capability), which a player can grow past.
  • Fail-soft (NFP #4): an unknown requiresReach value logs a console.warn and ungates the template rather than hiding it — a content typo degrades to "visible," never to a silently missing action.

The path codex — three-state legibility, out of the drawer IMPL

  • Slice 3b-tail (THR-613). The design gate (2026-07-18, human-reviewed) resolved against putting "acquirable this run" ghost cards in the live drawer — the flood-control risk under the empty THR-501 starter floor, and the blast radius of touching the getTargetActionSlots filter cascade. The full three-state catalog lives in the Codex instead (?view=codex), reachable pre-filtered from the character sheet. Drawer card population is unchanged.
  • The three states — available ("Yours") / acquirable ("Within reach") / locked_incarnation ("Another life") — reuse the shipped SignaturePathState grammar + copy the Signatures readout uses (src/data/ascendant-bar-content.ts), so the character-sheet readout and the codex can never drift. State logic: codexEntryRunState (src/components/Codex/codexRunState.ts).
  • Correctness callout: the "Another life" lock keys on requiresReach (the real reach gate), never on the card's reach classification tag — so a universal (reach: 'star', no gate) card is never wrongly greyed out. This matches exactly what the drawer hides. The eight invest.* signatures are the only reach-locked cards, and are now catalogued in the codex so the "Another life" state is legible at all.
  • Held vs. within-reach partitions by isActionRevealed (unlockedActionIds + the empty THR-501 starter floor). The domain set comes from getAscendantProgress — the same read the Reaches/Signatures readouts use.

Sphere-power scaling IMPL

  • spherePowerMultiplier(sphereScore) (src/engine/sphereScaling.ts:25, THR-548) linearly interpolates a per-sphere score ∈ [0, MAX_SPHERE_SCORE=10] to a multiplier in [SIGNATURE_SCALE_FLOOR = 0.6, SIGNATURE_SCALE_CEIL = 2.0]. Monotonic non-decreasing; the score is clamped before interpolation so garbage input never escapes the floor/ceil band.
  • scaledEffect(base, mult) scales effect magnitude directly. scaledCost(base, mult) floors the multiplier at 1× — cost never drops below base, so sphere mastery makes an action more potent, never cheaper. This is the general sphere-power helper, reusable beyond signatures.

Reach signatures — authoring & acquisition IMPL

  • The eight headline powers are the invest.<reach>.<name> templates in REACH_SIGNATURE_CONTENT_TEMPLATES (src/data/reach-signature-content.ts), e.g. invest.gold.patronage_network, invest.shadow.brokers_web, invest.star.beacon_of_fate. Per-reach base cost lives in SIGNATURE_BASE_COST; they are catalogued reach-gated in ASCENDANT_ACTION_BUCKETS.
  • Signature magnitude uses SIGNATURE_DEFAULT_BASE_VALUE = 3 (composed default) or SIGNATURE_BESPOKE_BASE_VALUE = 5 (authored SIGNATURE_MATRIX entries with a richer trigger), then scaled by spherePowerMultiplier at fire time.
  • Acquisition is story-driven, not menu-driven: BeatDefinition.grantsReachSignature resolves per-run from ranked domain affinities in resolvePendingBeat. Primary lands at Beat 4 ("A Path Opens"); secondary via the beat.pool.invest.reach_signature pool beat (THR-523).

Rekindle the Thread — the restore verb DESIGN

  • divine.rekindle_thread (THR-773), Spirit, reach: 'heart', scale: 'personal', essenceCost: REKINDLE_ESSENCE_COST (6). Raises a Broken mortal's quintessence to REKINDLE_RESTORE_TO_RATIO (0.6), clears their brokenSince stamp so the state releases immediately, and writes rekindledBy/rekindledAtTick plus a recent_event receipt — the mortal knows whose fire is in them.
  • Unlock-gated, never a starter. Granted by beat.milestone.the_guttering_thread, which fires the first time a mortal the ascendant is threaded to goes Broken. The trigger is the card's own precondition (the same discipline as the_empty_road granting Reunite): the god meets the consequence before being handed the answer to it. Bucket unlockable-generic.
  • It is not an apply_influence verb. Its op is quintessence_restore, which does not decay — the restore is permanent and the mortal can be worn back down normally afterwards. Because it must append the receipt (which needs full GameState), it routes through the resolution-intercept path in unifiedActionResolution alongside plant_trap/reveal_secret, not the graph executor. Implementation: src/engine/rekindleThread.ts.
  • Priced as the exception, not the routine answer — the WS5 rebuild encounters remain the primary road out of Broken. Fail-soft: a missing target, a non-actor target, or a mortal already at/above the restore ratio is a no-op with a reason.

Nudges — the god's other lever inside a scene DESIGN

  • Distinct from the action cards this page documents: a nudge is authored on an encounter step (ActionStep.nudges), not cast from the drawer, and exists only in an attended encounter (AttentionTier === 'story_beat'). It costs essence and shifts the step's named odds; a card may carry a rider that remaps the outcome band after the roll, taking no extra die. The step also authors the panel the hand is played against — ActionStep.purposeLine and ActionStep.factorLines (THR-820), the for/against reading of why the odds sit where they do. Most of that panel is now derived rather than authored (THR-892): the actor's Reach capability and each named world modifier become lines automatically, and the only authored factors that survive are trait lines and ActionStep.carryoverFactorLines, keyed on the previous step's outcome. Full detail on the encounters manual page; engine in src/engine/encounters/nudges.ts.
  • The no_crit_fail rider is the one sanctioned exception to "critical failure survives at every scale" — per-step, essence-priced, authored, never a global gate. Its mirror image is all_or_nothing (The Gambit, THR-885), the only rider that can make an outcome worse: it widens both ends of the band, and sits last in NUDGE_RIDER_PRIORITY so a protective card in the same hand always outranks it.
  • A nudge card may also charge a price that is not essence and leave something behind (THR-885): StepNudge.costs carries a signed detectionDelta (help given openly vs. unwitnessed) and a doomDelta, while StepNudge.grants carries ordinary EncounterAftermathReactionEffect entries — the same vocabulary an aftermath reaction speaks, dispatched by the same applier. That reuse is deliberate: a card that plants an omen reaches the omen system through the door emit_omen already uses, rather than a second path to the same place. Sphere-matched cards take a 1-essence discount (never below 1); full sphere gating stays parked with THR-870.
  • That grants vocabulary gained quintessence_shift (THR-1082), so a card or an aftermath reaction can now author an existential price — the "loss of confidence" shape of consequence — rather than reaching for a reputation delta or an item to stand in for it. The engine has moved quintessence since TB-075, but only ever itself: overchannel, encounter failure, doom. Nothing authored could. The applier does no arithmetic of its own — it queues the same QuintessenceEvent every other producer queues, so clamping, dissolution and loss-prevention stay in phaseQuintessence and an encounter cannot invent a second set of rules for the same quantity. Because StepNudge.grants reuses this union, cards inherit it for free.
  • The last of those six hooks to be wired, The Compulsion (THR-886), is the one that shows what the reuse rule costs when a card does not fit it. Its apparent host, buildCompulsionEvent, wants the decision pipeline's scored candidate list — which exists only for a moment inside phaseAgentDecision and cannot be reached from aftermath, so the card sat inert rather than fake one. The resolution was to change what the card plants: plant_compulsion writes a per-agent encounter-type weight, and a weight is reachable where a candidate list is not. The mortal's own next decision then leans that way through the ordinary scoring path — you steered them, they still chose — with no second choice-screen nested inside a scene already being resolved. Its encounterBias is keyed on the closed EncounterType union, so a misspelled type is a compile error rather than a card that quietly does nothing; author a narrativeHook too, or a successful tilt leaves no trace the player can read.
  • A third per-step lever, authored rather than cast: successMetadata.effects / failureMetadata.effects (THR-783) hang the full aftermath effect vocabulary on one outcome side of a step, applied through the same dispatcher an aftermath reaction uses. It is content, not a player choice — no essence, no drawer, no pick — and it applies to any UnifiedActionTemplate step, divine cards included. The side split is isStepSuccess, which counts near_miss as a success.
  • Whatever a divine card’s ending reports, it reports in words (THR-1004). A card resolves through the same UnifiedActionTemplate pipeline an encounter does, so the same resolver mints its derived EncounterAftermathChange entries — growth, standing, faction standing, reputation tallies, the reward — and those used to be debug-grade template literals printing toFixed(2) magnitudes and raw internal keys onto a player surface. They are now built by src/engine/aftermathWords.ts, which bands each delta into words; numbers stay on the trace. A change also carries concepts, the game concepts its sentence names, so the receipt and the aftermath chips can give each one its tooltip, its EntityVisual tile, and a kind-routed link — the UI Law, enforced at the producer rather than guessed at the surface.
  • A fourth lever, at the end rather than per-step: AftermathVariant.byOutcome (THR-969) keys per-band overrides of the closing overview / changes / reactionPrompt / reactions on the seven-value UnifiedActionOutcome. Because aftermathConfig lives on UnifiedActionTemplate, this is available to a divine card's own template exactly as it is to an encounter's — a working that ends in critical_failure can close on different prose, and offer different exits, than one that ends in critical_success. Before it, aftermath keyed only on the choiceId of one step, so any template shipping variants: {} had a single ending regardless of outcome. Resolution is choice → outcome band → base variant → fallback; an unauthored band degrades to the un-banded variant rather than throwing, and it consumes no new rng — the band is read from the already-resolved outcome.

The Find-gates-Change/Control chain DESIGN
