Divine Actions & Prerequisites

Everything you do to the world is one of five verbs — and most of what you could do is quietly withheld until you have earned the right to see it.

Five verbs, and only fiveEvery divine act, however dressed, is one of these underneath.

You never pick a verb from a menu — the game teaches them through the moments themselves, never as filter chips on a wheel. But underneath every intervention you will ever make, there is one of exactly five things a god does to a world. Knowing the five is knowing the whole grammar of your power.

Create

Bring something into being — an artifact, a settlement, a bond between mortals that was not there before.

Find

Perceive and reveal. Search the world, lift the fog, surface what was hidden from you.

Change

A one-shot alteration for a one-time cost. Fire and forget — the world keeps the mark, not the leash.

Destroy

Remove, corrupt, scatter, erase. The unmaking verb, and often the loudest.

The Signature Verb

Control

A sustained grip. Ongoing upkeep, held open turn after turn — and contestable by any rival god who meets the same bar you did.

Why Control is the god-game's signature

The other four verbs resolve and end. Control is the one that stays. It is not a bigger Change — it is a standing commitment that drains essence for as long as you hold it, and every held thing is a visible prize a rival can try to usurp (take from you) or shatter. Choosing to Control is choosing to keep spending, and to be seen keeping it.

You can't shape what you haven't seenFind is the verb that unlocks the others. Perception comes before power.

There is a natural order to the verbs, and it is not arbitrary. Find gates Change and Control. You cannot reshape a mortal's fate, or take sustained hold of a place, before you have perceived it — the fog has to lift before the hand can land. The chain the game leans you toward is Find → Change or Create → Control: see it, touch it, then keep it.

Find → Change · Create → Control

This gate is deliberately soft, not an iron law. It is taught through which actions a target offers, not enforced as a wall that throws you back — you will feel the ordering as the shape of what is available, the way a story withholds a door until you have found the key.

Everything you look at is an action targetFocus a node, and the game asks what a god could do to it here.

Threadbearer has no fixed list of buttons. Instead, every node you focus on in a detail view — a mortal, a location, a sublocation, a hex, an artifact — becomes a target, and the action drawer fills with exactly the interventions that make sense for that thing, in that place, for you. Open a peasant and you are offered peasant-shaped acts; open a ruined temple and the offers change entirely.

The drawer is assembled fresh each time by asking a cascade of questions about the target and about you: what kind of node is it, what particular sort, what does it already carry, which cosmic energies does it answer to, can you afford it, is it in reach. Whatever survives every question is what you may do. There is no slot count and no cap on the imagined space — "what is doable here" is open-ended and grown from data, never a hand-authored list.

Why some cards never appearAn empty drawer is information. The absences are as designed as the offers.

Sometimes you focus a target and an obvious power simply isn't there. That is not a bug — the game hides what you cannot attempt, so that "visible" and "possible" mean the same thing. There are three reasons a card stays dark:

Outside your two domains

Your identity grants exactly two Reaches, fixed for the whole run. A power belonging to a Reach you do not hold never appears — not even as aspiration. This wall is permanent by design.

Sphere misalignment

Many acts answer to a specific cosmic energy. If your sphere alignment does not resonate with what the action demands, it stays hidden until your alignment does.

Not capable enough

Some acts ask a minimum Domain Capability — a tier of competence in the relevant Reach. Below the bar, "can you do this kind of thing?" answers no, and the card waits.

The first of these is worth dwelling on, because it is the one that never changes. Sphere and capability are things you can grow into over a run. A Reach outside your two domains is a door that was walled off when you chose who you were, and stays walled off until the run ends. You are a particular kind of god, not a universal one — and the drawer is where that particularity is felt, turn to turn.

Your two reach signaturesBeyond the shared verbs, each run hands you two headline powers — as story, never from a shop.

The five verbs are the grammar every god shares. On top of them, each run grants you exactly two reach signatures — one headline divine power for each of your two domains. The Warhost for Iron, Rend the Gate for Veil, the Great Work for Stone, and so on across all eight Reaches: the signature is the thing your kind of god is known for.

You do not buy them. A signature is permanently reach-gated — a signature for a Reach outside your domains will never be offered — and it arrives as a moment in your story, not a purchase:

  • Your primary signature is granted at the culmination of the opening spine — Beat 4, "A Path Opens" — alongside your choice of god-path.
  • Your secondary arrives later, when the living world next calls on your deeper domain.
Signatures scale with your sphere power

How hard a signature lands is not fixed. It scales with your sphere power — the same cosmic strength that colours the rest of your expression. A god steeped in their sphere throws a heavier signature than one only lightly aligned; the price rises with the potency, so mastery makes the act mightier, never merely cheaper.

The targeting cascade, the reach gate, sphere-power scaling, and where signatures are authored.

Five verbs as implicit substrate IMPL

  • Create / Find / Change / Destroy / Control are CRUD-ish verbs threaded through UnifiedActionTemplate (src/types/unifiedAction.ts), the single action format since THR-108. They are not surfaced as ActionDrawer filter chips — resolved as implicit substrate, taught through encounter prose (rulebook §4, Open Q resolved 2026-06-23). No "pick a verb" UI exists by design.
  • Control's sustained semantics (upkeep drain, contestation via usurp/destroy reactions, the visible control-effect node a rival can target) are the part still landing — see the essence-control page (W6) for the sustain-model detail. DESIGN for the sustain/contestation half; the verb tag itself is IMPL.

Generalized Action Targeting — the filter cascade IMPL

  • getTargetActionSlots(params): WheelSlot[] in src/engine/targetActions.ts is the core function. It takes a TargetContext for any focused graph node and returns the drawer's slots. There is no fixed slot count; MAX_SLOTS = 20 is only a UI-overflow guard (TARGET_ACTION_CONSTANTS).
  • The cascade runs in this order (file header, code-true): 1 node-type (targetCategories ∋ nodeType) → 2 subtype (targetSubtypes) → 3 traits (requiredTargetTraits) & 3b node-properties (requiredNodeProperties) → reach (requiresReach, below) → 4 sphere (sphereAffinity ∈ accessibleSpheres) → 5 essence (affordable essenceCost) → 6 range (hex distance from avatar) → 7 revelation (narrativeLayer revealed on the target hex). Each rejection increments a per-gate counter for inspectability (NFP #2).
  • 119+ unified templates span the CRUD, encounter, divine, location, attachment, sublocation, and hex categories. The browsable, code-true catalog is the Action Catalog (Deep Reference); its buckets come from ASCENDANT_ACTION_BUCKETS (src/data/ascendant-beat-content.ts:465).
  • Rejected approaches (do not reintroduce): the AgentWheel and any fixed / capped action-slot list. The open-ended data-driven pool replaced both (CLAUDE.md Rejected Approaches).

The reach gate — permanent, set-membership IMPL

  • Templates declaring requiresReach are hidden unless the ascendant's affinity in that Reach meets REACH_GATE_MIN_AFFINITY = 0.2 (src/data/influence-content.ts:92). Below the bar, the slot is dropped and counts.byReach increments. Gate logic: src/engine/targetActions.ts ~L238, shipped in THR-503.
  • This is a permanent set-membership filter, not aspiration: an ascendant's two Reaches are fixed for the run, so a card requiring a third Reach can never surface — distinct from within-run unlock gates (revelation/essence/capability), which a player can grow past.
  • Fail-soft (NFP #4): an unknown requiresReach value logs a console.warn and ungates the template rather than hiding it — a content typo degrades to "visible," never to a silently missing action.

Sphere-power scaling IMPL

  • spherePowerMultiplier(sphereScore) (src/engine/sphereScaling.ts:25, THR-548) linearly interpolates a per-sphere score ∈ [0, MAX_SPHERE_SCORE=10] to a multiplier in [SIGNATURE_SCALE_FLOOR = 0.6, SIGNATURE_SCALE_CEIL = 2.0]. Monotonic non-decreasing; the score is clamped before interpolation so garbage input never escapes the floor/ceil band.
  • scaledEffect(base, mult) scales effect magnitude directly. scaledCost(base, mult) floors the multiplier at 1× — cost never drops below base, so sphere mastery makes an action more potent, never cheaper. This is the general sphere-power helper, reusable beyond signatures.

Reach signatures — authoring & acquisition IMPL

  • The eight headline powers are the invest.<reach>.<name> templates in REACH_SIGNATURE_CONTENT_TEMPLATES (src/data/reach-signature-content.ts), e.g. invest.gold.patronage_network, invest.shadow.brokers_web, invest.star.beacon_of_fate. Per-reach base cost lives in SIGNATURE_BASE_COST; they are catalogued reach-gated in ASCENDANT_ACTION_BUCKETS.
  • Signature magnitude uses SIGNATURE_DEFAULT_BASE_VALUE = 3 (composed default) or SIGNATURE_BESPOKE_BASE_VALUE = 5 (authored SIGNATURE_MATRIX entries with a richer trigger), then scaled by spherePowerMultiplier at fire time.
  • Acquisition is story-driven, not menu-driven: BeatDefinition.grantsReachSignature resolves per-run from ranked domain affinities in resolvePendingBeat. Primary lands at Beat 4 ("A Path Opens"); secondary via the beat.pool.invest.reach_signature pool beat (THR-523).

The Find-gates-Change/Control chain DESIGN

  • "You can't Update what you haven't Read" is kept deliberately soft: per-template prerequisites plus layer-revelation gating (the revelation gate, step 7 above), not a hard runtime invariant (verdict 2026-06-23, THR-414). An advisory content-lint to flag Change/Control templates that aren't Find-gated is tracked as THR-476.
