Threads, the Court & The First

A god does not command a world — it reaches into a few chosen lives and holds on. Every thread you spin is a two-way bond: you invest, and the mortal, in their own time, comes to feel it.

The thread is a two-way bondYour side is investment. Their side is experience. The game refuses to let you forget there is another half.

A thread is the line of divine attention that runs from you down to a single mortal. It is how your power actually enters the world — not as a decree over everyone, but as a held connection to this life, and that one, and a handful of others. You spin a thread by binding a mortal, and you keep it by paying its upkeep, turn after turn.

But a thread has two ends, and they move at different speeds. On your end there is depth — how much you have invested. On their end there is experience — how the mortal comes to feel the presence leaning on their fate. You can pour everything into a thread and the mortal may still only sense something; recognition of a god is not bought, it is earned by repetition and time.

Your investment

How deep the thread runs — grows with maintained upkeep.
  1. Unaware — a thread barely spun
  2. Curious — the first real hold
  3. Recognized — a lasting connection
  4. Devoted — a deep, costly bond
  5. Enthralled — the mortal is yours

Their experience

How the mortal perceives the connection — moves on its own clock.
  1. Unaware — they feel nothing yet
  2. Intuition — an unnameable pull
  3. Faith — they believe, and name you
  4. Communion — they answer back
Deepening a thread takes maintained time, not a single spend

You establish a thread cheaply, but each deeper level of investment demands continuous upkeep held current over many turns before it will rise. Miss the upkeep and the thread stalls where it is — the mortal drifts, and your grip loosens. Beyond the top level of investment lies a rarer thing still: an Aspect, a mortal who has become a partial aspect of you. That is not another rung on the ladder; it is a separate, permanent apex, and it survives even the mortal's death as lasting myth.

The court — how you hold what you holdThreads are not a flat list. Each has a place in your court, from the one who anchors you to the ones who merely sleep under your eye.

As your threads multiply, they organise into a court — four positions that say what each held mortal is to you. The court is how the game keeps a growing portfolio legible: at a glance you see who anchors your presence, who you keep close, who you are only watching, and who has faded to the background.

the_first

The First

The one bonded mortal who anchors your divine presence in the world — the protagonist of your opening arc.

retinue

Retinue

Close-held. The mortals you have chosen to keep near, tend, and shape with intent.

watched

Watched

Kept in view. A thread exists, but you are observing more than steering — a life you have not yet committed to.

dormant

Dormant

Faded to the background. The bond persists but sleeps, waiting for a reason to matter again.

A thread can exist with no named position at all — a bare connection. The court is the layer of meaning you lay over your threads, and moving a mortal from watched to retinue is a small declaration: this one, I have decided, is mine to tend.

The First and the shape of a lifeYour first bonded mortal is not just a resource — they are the hero of a story that the doom clock is quietly telling.

Among all your threads, one holds a special place: The First, the mortal bonded to you at the start, who anchors your presence and becomes the protagonist of your opening arc. The First does not merely exist in your portfolio — they live a story, an arc shaped after the classic hero's journey, unfolding across five phases.

I

Call

The ordinary life, and the summons that disturbs it.

II

Road of Trials

The testing — a run of challenges that reveal who they are.

III

Crisis

The turning point, where the stakes become personal and sharp.

IV

Ordeal

The deepest trial — the moment that will define the whole arc.

V

Return

The reckoning — the journey resolves and its ripples spread outward.

The doom clock is the storyteller

You do not schedule these beats — the Doom Clock does. As the world winds toward its Unmaking, The First's journey advances in step with it: the closer doom comes, the deeper into the arc your mortal travels. When a beat arrives, the game pulls the camera onto them and offers you a moment to shape — a choice that bends where their story goes next. The final Return needs no choice from you; it resolves on its own, gathering up everything the arc has become and letting it ripple through the world they leave changed.

Coming to know themFeeling a mortal's presence is one thing. Actually knowing who they are is another — and it, too, is earned.

Binding a thread does not hand you a mortal's whole character. At first they are a stranger — a name, a place, little more. What you know about them grows with familiarity, a separate track from how deep your thread runs. Familiarity is the fog lifting: as it rises, the game reveals more of who this person truly is — their traits, their bonds, the buried shape of their character sheet.

You build familiarity by being present in their life:

  • Nearness — keeping your avatar close to them, a slow trickle every turn.
  • Scrying and searching — deliberate acts of looking, worth far more than passive proximity.
  • Binding and deepening the thread — the act of taking a mortal into your court reveals a great deal at once.
  • Shared moments — a dilemma or an encounter you steer them through leaves you knowing them better.

Some gods see more, faster: a god of the Eye learns any mortal quicker than most, while Shadow lays a mortal's hidden traits bare early, and Heart reveals the bonds they carry. What you can know is coloured by the kind of god you are.

Two directions of knowing, across one thread

It is worth holding these apart. Familiarity is you coming to know them — the character sheet opening under your attention. Awareness is them coming to know you — intuition ripening into faith. The two move independently: you can know a mortal intimately while they still have no idea you exist, or a whole village can hold you in faith while you have never once looked closely at any single face among them.

The trade — why depth is never freeEvery thread you hold, and every level you raise it to, costs you something. That cost is the point.

Threads are power, and power in Threadbearer is never given away. Holding a mortal has a running price, and holding them deeply costs more than holding them lightly — the upkeep rises with every level of investment. A large court is a heavy court. Most runs you cannot afford to deepen every thread you would like to, and choosing which few to raise is the texture of the game.

Depth buys real things in return. Each thread you hold widens your reservoir of divine fuel and the ceiling it can hold, and the intended reward of a deep, aligned bond is that shaping that mortal along your nature comes easier. But the same depth that makes your touch surer also makes you louder: the more of yourself you pour into a thread, the brighter your signature burns to the rival gods who scan their domains for exactly that light. Reach deep enough and you no longer whisper — you announce.

The god's dilemma in miniature

This is the whole game compressed into one relationship. To matter to a mortal you must invest, and to invest is to be seen — by them, and by your rivals. There is no way to hold a thread deeply and quietly. Every bond you treasure is a light in the dark that something else can follow home.

The thread edge & investment depth IMPL

  • A thread is a thread edge (ascendant → mortal) carrying ThreadEdgeProperties (src/types/influence.ts:52). The direction is god→mortal (flipped from the deprecated mortal→god worships edge). Investment depth lives in tier: InfluenceTier where InfluenceTier = 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 (src/types/influence.ts:35).
  • Upkeep runs in processInfluenceMaintenance() (src/engine/influence.ts): each tick, every thread deducts TIER_MAINTENANCE[tier] from the ascendant's primary sphere pool and increments ticksAtCurrentTier; a thread that cannot pay sets maintenanceCurrent = false and stops accruing.
  • Promotion runs in checkTierPromotion(): a thread rises to the next tier once ticksAtCurrentTier ≥ TIER_PROMOTION_THRESHOLDS[nextTier] and maintenance is current. Tiers never auto-demote; the surface phase is phaseInfluenceTierPromotion (emits a backstory_unlock alert per promoted mortal).
  • Dropping a thread (dropAgent()) removes the edge and scales the consequence by tier: tier 0–1 minimal, 2 crisis_of_faith, 3 wild_card, 4 catastrophic_severance; tier ≥ 2 also assigns the trait.abandoned_by_divine scar (cool-failure — a drop is plot, not just bookkeeping).
  • Each thread also feeds the economy: ESSENCE_PER_THREAD = 0.1/tick income and MAX_ESSENCE_PER_THREAD = 5 added to the pool ceiling (computeEssenceGeneration / computeMaxEssence, src/engine/influence.ts). Full essence economy is the Essence, Control & Sustained Power page (W6).

The tier-name ladder — rulebook-resolved, code map lags IMPL OPEN

  • The engine keeps five integer tiers (0–4). The rulebook resolved the naming (Open Q #1, 2026-06-23, THR-414): the five narrative names Unaware → Curious → Recognized → Devoted → Enthralled map one-to-one onto tiers 0–4, and 'Aspect' is a separate apex milestone, not a sixth rung. The player layer above uses these resolved names — do not re-open this question here (referenced, not minted).
  • The current code map has not yet been reconciled to that verdict: TIER_NAMES (src/data/influence-content.ts:44) still reads {0:'Unaware', 1:'Touched', 2:'Devoted', 3:'Champion', 4:'Aspect'} — working names that predate the resolution and put "Aspect" at tier 4 rather than modelling it as the separate apex. The rulebook flags "a small code ticket for the name-map + Aspect flag" as a follow-up; this page treats the rulebook names as authoritative and the code map as the drift to close.

Awareness — the mortal's half of the thread IMPL DESIGN

  • ThreadEdgeProperties.awareness (src/types/influence.ts:70) is the mortal's experience of the connection: 'unaware' | 'intuition' | 'faith' | 'communion'. It is stored and moves independently of tier — the field is IMPL.
  • The mortal's response through that awareness — the two-way texture the rulebook insists on — is the Agent Feedback System (Docs/plans/2026-05-11-agent-feedback-system.md, THR-402), still DESIGN. The awareness ladder exists as data ahead of the full loop that lets mortals answer back.

Court positions IMPL

  • CourtPosition = 'the_first' | 'retinue' | 'watched' | 'dormant' (src/types/influence.ts:40), stored as ThreadEdgeProperties.courtPosition (nullable — a thread may carry no named position). It is portfolio organisation layered over threads, read by systems that prioritise which mortals get attention/vignettes (e.g. journey beats gate on courtPosition === 'the_first').
  • Naming-collision note (UL drift candidate, not an open question): the influence CourtPosition here is a different concept from the "Court Position" in Docs/ubiquitous-language/Encounters.md (the numeric encounter-role property set via the CLI --courtPosition flag). Same words, two systems. Flagged for a future UL disambiguation pass; the page uses the influence meaning throughout.

The First's Campbellian journey IMPL DESIGN

  • CampbellianPhase = 'call' | 'road_of_trials' | 'crisis' | 'ordeal' | 'return' (src/types/influence.ts:43), tracked as ThreadEdgeProperties.storyPhase on First-position threads. The arc is doom-clock scheduled: getJourneyPhase(doomProgress) maps the doom clock's progress fraction to a phase via PHASE_BOUNDARIES (src/types/journeyEngine.ts), and phaseJourneyBeat() (src/engine/journeyEngine.ts) fires a beat when the clock crosses a beat threshold not yet in beatHistory.
  • Beats select a template+variant from JOURNEY_BEAT_TEMPLATES (src/data/journey-content.ts) by scoring variants against a four-axis StateSnapshot (power / influence / relationship / ambition), then seeded-weighted-random over the top candidates (deterministic per hashSeed(seed, agentId, phase, beatIndex)). The chosen beat becomes a PendingVignette at max priority for the UI to render.
  • The Return phase is algorithmic, not a player choice: executeReturn() (src/engine/returnEngine.ts) runs a convergence that resolves the arc and produces ripple results, surfaced as a choice-less return vignette. Fail-soft (NFP #4): a beat with no valid template is skipped rather than throwing; an Ordeal that never fired is force-fired once doomProgress ≥ ORDEAL_FORCE_FIRE_THRESHOLD so no First reaches Return without their defining trial.
  • Two faction reads on this arc were dead until 2026-08-17 (THR-1151). buildStateSnapshot's influence axis counts factionsLed, and gatherRippleTargets collects the factions a Return ripples through — both matched member_of.rank against an integer tier (>= 3 and >= 2) on a scale declared 0 (recruit) → 1 (leader), so both clauses were permanently false. Neither broke visibly, because each ORs with role === 'leader' and quietly collapsed to that half: a First who had risen high in a faction without taking its single top slot counted as leading nothing, and their Return rippled through no faction at all. They now read FACTION_RANK_SENIOR and FACTION_RANK_NOTABLE. The ripple significance formula (rank + leader bonus) needed no change — on the 0–1 scale it lands in the same 0–3 band the other target kinds already use.
  • Overall wiring is IMPL for scheduling, snapshotting, template selection, and Return; the rulebook flags the broader journey wiring as "partially implemented," so richer beat content and downstream consequence remain DESIGN in places.

Familiarity — player-side knowledge fog-of-war IMPL

  • Familiarity is a separate system from the thread's awareness, and points the other way: it is the player's progressive disclosure of a mortal, not the mortal's perception of the god. FamiliarityMap is Map<actorId, number> ∈ [0,1] (src/engine/familiarity.ts), with knowledge levels stranger → recognised → known → intimate → transparent at thresholds 0 / 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8 (src/types/familiarity.ts).
  • Gains (FAMILIARITY_GAINS): proximity 0.01/tick (phaseFamiliarityGain, actors near the avatar hex), scry 0.15, narrative_contact 0.05, dilemma 0.10, and thread-bind grants worship_tier_1/2/3 = 0.3/0.5/0.7 (the "Phase 2a.1" grant when a bind_thread_* action resolves). Sphere bonuses: eye ×1.5 multiplier, shadow reveals traits one level early, heart reveals bonds one level early (SPHERE_FAMILIARITY_BONUSES).
  • The plan's one-liner conflates the two directions. W5's brief says "familiarity — how mortals come to know you," but the familiarity engine is you coming to know them (character-sheet disclosure). The mortal-knows-god direction is the awareness field above. The player layer keeps them distinct; this note records the source discrepancy per the plan's own thesis that writing the player layer surfaces exactly this kind of ambiguity.

The Aspect apex IMPL

  • Beyond the five tiers, an Aspect is modelled as its own aspect_of edge with AspectEdgeProperties (src/types/influence.ts:105), not a sixth tier (THR-479). It is permanent (survivesDeath: true) — on the mortal's death the edge persists as a mythicEcho rather than being garbage-collected. A living Aspect channels ASPECT_ESSENCE_PER_TICK of extra income (a mythic echo channels none).
