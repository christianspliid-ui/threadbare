Settlements, Prosperity & Trade

Villages rise into cities and slump back into hamlets, all on their own. Prosperity and unrest are a settlement's pulse; trade routes are its arteries; the land beneath it sets the ceiling on how large it can ever grow.

Settlements grow and shrink on their ownA place that thrives for long enough grows into something bigger; a place that suffers for long enough falls. You do not build settlements tier by tier — you shape the conditions, and the world does the rest.

Every built place in the world sits at one of four sizes. A hamlet is a scatter of homes; a town is a proper market; a city is a seat of trade and power; and a capital is the fixed heart of a realm. The first three are alive to their fortunes: hold a hamlet in good times long enough and it grows into a town, then a city — and let a city rot in hard times long enough and it shrinks the same way, city to town to hamlet. Growth is never a single lucky season; a place has to sustain its fortune for a good stretch before it changes.

A capital is the exception — it never grows and never falls. It is the anchor its realm is measured against, not a fortune that swings. And a settlement that is sick — its wells poisoned, its people ailing — cannot grow no matter how rich it is; a place must be healthy as well as prosperous to rise.

HamletA handful of homes. The smallest settled place. Grows into a town on sustained good fortune.
TownA market and a wall. The middle rung — can rise to a city or slip back to a hamlet.
CityA seat of trade and power. The largest that fortune alone can build — and the first to feel the cost of hard times, since it must be fed.
CapitalThe fixed heart of a realm. Never promotes, never demotes. An anchor, not a fortune.
When a settlement changes, it is a moment

A rise or a fall is not a silent number. When a place grows or shrinks a tier, the world marks it — a chronicle entry is written ("A Settlement Rises", "A Settlement Falls"), and the place is quietly re-appraised: a grown city gains the kinds of places a city should have, a fallen hamlet loses what it can no longer support.

Prosperity and unrest — a settlement's pulseProsperity is how well a place is doing; unrest is how close it is to boiling over. Together they are the vital signs that decide whether a settlement rises, holds, or falls.

Prosperity is a settlement's wealth and health of trade, measured on a scale from destitute to flourishing. It does not drift at random — it always moves toward a target set by the place's real conditions: live trade routes and a strong faction presence lift the target; corruption in the ground, high unrest, and a sickly economy drag it down; a matching sphere in the world's makeup nudges it either way. If nothing about a place changes, its prosperity settles and stays put. When it moves, it moves for a reason you can name — a road opened, a raid struck, a blessing landed. Growth and decline always trace to a visible cause.

Unrest is the other half of the pulse — political instability, the pressure of a people ready to riot, defect, or be sacked. Unrest is pressure, not a spike: every day it creeps toward what the town lives through — hunger above all, then want, an army at the walls, sickness and its recent dead, eased by contentment — by a few points at most. Sudden blows (a betrayal in an encounter, the Doom Clock's reach, a god stirring the pot on purpose) land on top and fade back over days. A hungry town turns Restless (people pack up and leave for a fed, calm town nearby) and then Lawless (nobody enforces anything, the granaries are rioted over, and the town pays its holder only half its tax). The place page says it in one sentence, the Fortunes row: "Millbrook is struggling, and its people are restless with hunger." Unrest colours everything that happens there — the odds of what mortals attempt, the omens the world throws up, the tone of the reports you read.

Two dials worth watching

Prosperity and unrest pull against each other. A rich, calm settlement climbs toward a promotion; a poor, seething one slides toward a fall. If you want a place to grow, you feed its prosperity and drain its unrest — open trade, plant your influence, settle its people. If you want a rival's stronghold to crumble, you do the reverse.

Trade routes — the arteries between placesSettlements that trade with one another are richer for it. A route is a living thing: use it and it thrives, neglect it and it withers to nothing.

Where two settlements exchange goods, a trade route runs between them — a road carrying a named cargo manifest of wares, with a volume that measures how much flows along it. The manifest is worked out when the route forms, from what each end has spare and what each end wants — so a road between a grain-glutted valley and a hungry mining town carries grain, and you can tell at a glance what severing it would cost. Merchants prefer partners whose shortages answer their surpluses, which means the web of roads that grows over a run maps the world's real scarcities rather than mere proximity. A busy route is worth real prosperity to both ends. A route between two standing towns stays alive on its own: ordinary traffic keeps it open without anyone having to walk it, and once a day its volume settles a step toward how busy it naturally is — busier when the two ends' surpluses answer each other's shortages, quieter while bandits threaten it. What a merchant adds on top of that fades back a step a day unless someone keeps working the road. A road dies for a reason you can see: one of its towns is razed or dwindles out of being a settlement, or its roads are cursed — then its volume bleeds away, turn by turn, until the road falls silent and the route dissolves entirely. A blockade does not kill a road; it holds it, and the road carries again when the blockade lifts. A road that someone founded gets a grace on that: opening a route is a season's work, so for a while after it opens the road stands whether or not anyone travels it, and only once that warranty runs out does the ordinary bleeding begin. Long enough for the world to notice the road is there — for a caravan to use it, a faction to tax it, or a warlord to decide it should stop carrying anything at all. A route can also be threatened by bandits or war, or controlled by a faction that taxes what passes, and either shows in what the route is worth.

This is why a web of trade is a fragile, tended thing rather than a permanent fixture. Roads that carry commerce hold the realm together; roads that carry nothing quietly disappear, and the places they once fed feel the loss.

A road falling silent is news

When a trade route finally dies, the world records it — "A Road Falls Silent" — and the settlements at either end take a one-time knock to their prosperity, feeling the artery close. New routes, likewise, arrive as good news and a small lift. The economy speaks in these small, legible events.

The trade bonus was dead until 2026-08-19

Routes have always lifted a settlement's prosperity target on paper, and until THR-830 they never lifted it in practice. A route hangs off the two places it joins, but phaseProsperity went looking for it on the people standing in the settlement — walking to each actor present and reading their routes, of which they have none and never had any. So the count came back zero for every settlement in every run, and the arteries described above fed nothing. The declaration was the reason: EDGE_SCHEMA called a route a link between actors, warned twice on every real route for as long as the system has existed, and was read as documentation by everything built near it. Both halves are corrected now, and the same wrong turn had been taken by the trade-route prose, the caravan-rest sublocation gate, and the trade-baron trait count.

The land sets the ceilingResources are what the ground beneath a settlement can give. They do not make a place grow — they decide how large it is allowed to grow, and where the rare and remarkable can be found.

Every place is seeded, at the world's making, with the resources its terrain affords: timber from forests, stone and ore from mountains, water from rivers and coasts. These common goods set a settlement's carrying capacity — the ceiling on how prosperous it can become. A rich vein of ore or a broad stand of timber lets a place grow large; a barren hex holds it small, no matter how much trade or blessing it receives. Resources are the ground's promise, not a river of passive income; they cap what is possible rather than pouring wealth in.

Scattered across the map, too, are rarer places — anomalies. A gem deposit, a crystal cavern, a golden grove, a sunken treasury: each hides an uncommon resource found nowhere in ordinary terrain, and each quietly draws the curious and the perceptive toward it. The sharp-eyed and the veil-touched among mortals feel the pull of an undiscovered anomaly like a scent on the wind, and the discovery of one is a small event in itself.

Honest scope — this is texture, not a trading sim

Threadbearer's economy exists to make the world feel alive and consequential, not to be played as a market. You will never balance a ledger or set a tax rate by hand. Settlements rise and fall, roads open and close, fortunes turn — and all of it feeds the story of a place: who prospers, who suffers, what a god's meddling did to a corner of the world. The economy is a story engine wearing a merchant's coat.

Settlement tier promotion / demotion IMPL

  • phaseSettlementPromotion(state, runtime?) runs once per tick, after phaseProsperity. It walks all location nodes whose locationSubtype is a settlement subtype (hamlet, town, city, capital), skipping capital entirely — capitals never change tier.
  • Each settlement carries two sustain counters on its node properties: prosperitySustainAboveTicks and prosperitySustainBelowTicks. When prosperity is ≥ SETTLEMENT_PROMOTION_PROSPERITY 70 the above-counter increments (and the below-counter resets); when ≤ SETTLEMENT_DEMOTION_PROSPERITY 20 the below-counter increments (above resets); the mid-band leaves both alone. A tier change fires when a counter reaches SETTLEMENT_PROMOTION_SUSTAIN_TICKS 20 — promotion hamlet→town→city, demotion city→town→hamlet. Counters reset to 0 after any change.
  • Health gate (THR-401): promotion is suppressed while a settlement is sickened (wellsSickenedUntilTick in the future) or its populationHealth is below LOC_HEALTH_DAMPENER_THRESHOLD — a sick place cannot grow, and its above-counter is reset to be safe. Demotion has no such gate.
  • A tier change mutates locationSubtype in place, calls applyEncounterCacheUpdate(runtime, …onLocationTypeChanged) so encounter scoring sees the new type, emits a settlement_tier_change trace, resolves an economic-chronicle entry (significance 0.9, fail-soft to a plain TickEvent if template resolution returns null), and sets pendingReassessment for the next phase.

Prosperity — equilibrium model IMPL

  • phaseProsperity(state) models prosperity as a 0–100 value that drifts toward a target at PROSPERITY_DRIFT_RATE 0.05 (minimum step PROSPERITY_DRIFT_MIN 0.1, per-tick change clamped to PROSPERITY_DELTA_CLAMP 2). Pure arithmetic — no PRNG. If conditions are static, prosperity settles; every movement traces to a named input.
  • Target inputs: PROSPERITY_TRADE_BONUS_PER_ROUTE 8 per live route; FACTION_PRESENCE_BONUS 5 per distinct faction with a living member located at the settlement or a place inside it (cap 15; live since THR-1862 — the old read looked for a faction node type that does not exist, so it was always 0; on seed 42 medium at t300 turning it on moved the settlement median from 38.9 to 47.8); corruption penalty above CORRUPTION_TARGET_THRESHOLD 0.2 (up to −25); divine-influence bonus above DIVINE_TARGET_THRESHOLD 0.3 (up to +15); unrest penalty above UNREST_TARGET_THRESHOLD 30 (up to −20); a small sphere-affinity term (Life/Energy bonus, Entropy penalty, capped at PROSPERITY_SPHERE_CAP 0.15).
  • Carrying capacity is a ceiling, not income: BASE_CARRYING_CAPACITY 30 + resources × RESOURCE_CAPACITY_RATE 0.5, capped at MAX_CARRYING_CAPACITY 100. Cities and capitals pay upkeep (UPKEEP_CITY 5, UPKEEP_CAPITAL 8) — they decay toward decline without active support.
  • Shocks: one-time deltas pushed by upstream phases via state.prosperityShocks — e.g. SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_ESTABLISHED +3, SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_LOST −3, SHOCK_FACTION_ARRIVAL +2. Tier bands (PROSPERITY_TIER_FLOURISHING 80 / PROSPEROUS 60 / MODEST 40 / STRUGGLING 20) are the named prose thresholds.

Unrest — pressure toward a target (THR-1934 S1) IMPL DESIGN

  • Pressure model (THR-1934 S1, src/engine/unrestPressure.ts + unrestPressure/{inputs,hunger,bands,leave,describe}.ts, constants src/data/unrest-pressure-constants.ts, behind UNREST_PRESSURE_ENABLED). Once a day (tick % 12 === UNREST_TARGET_TICK_OFFSET) every place-tier settlement's unrestTarget is the clamped sum of six inputs written as unrestInputs: hunger (HUNGER_BASE 10 + HUNGER_PER_DAY 3 × (hungerDays − 1), cap 55; hungerDays counts days in a row with a scarce staple, or an empty food store once the settlement economy lands), poverty ((35 − prosperity) × 0.6, cap 20), siege (30 while a siege actor names the town and for 5 days after lastSiegeSeenTick), sickness (10 below health 40), deathSite (THR-1925's deathSiteUnrestTarget) and contentment (−10 above prosperity 60). Every tick unrest moves toward the target by at most UNREST_DRIFT_PER_DAY 4 a day, clamped 0–100. Pushes stay pushes and drift back (an Incite Unrest now lasts about five days). Under the flag the flat decay, the prosperity damper, the per-tick health bleed, THR-1925's lift in phaseProsperity and the scarcity arc's +12 / +25 jumps all retire; the arc's unrest-phase seed is now encounter.town.granary_riot. Restless (trait.condition.location.restless, enter 30 / release 15): every RESTLESS_LEAVE_DAYS 3 days one person leaves for the nearest fed, calm settlement within MIGRATION_RADIUS_HEXES 6 (same tax holder first) — population −1 / +1 once the settlement economy counts people, traced as skipped before — and the town's most recent arrival gets a relocationIntent there, plus Flee the Ravaged Land when hunger is the largest input. Lawless: townTaxOf(settlement, graph) pays LAWLESS_TOWN_TAX_FACTOR 0.5, and the day a hungry town turns Lawless one granary riot is seeded (10-day cooldown). Traces unrest.target, unrest.leave / .none / .skipped, unrest.riot_seed; window.__DEBUG.getUnrest(id); Economy debug tab. Measured (600 ticks, medium): towns ever Restless 8 / 9 / 4 on seeds 42 / 99 / 7, Lawless peak 14% / 9.6% / 3.6% of settlements, 0 death-site-only towns at 50+, 0 leave-rule violations. On the edge (70) and Rising (90) are S2 (THR-1957).
  • Flag off (rollback): phaseUnrest(state) is decay-only. Per settlement it applies UNREST_DECAY_RATE 1/tick, plus an extra UNREST_PROSPERITY_DAMPER 0.5 when prosperity > 60, minus a health-bleed when population health is low (THR-401). It clamps to [0,100], writes back location.properties.unrest, and emits an unrest_tick trace. It is not where unrest rises.
  • Unrest is driven up by other systems: encounter complications (complicationEffects.ts), Doom Clock effects (phaseDoom.ts), and player actions (e.g. Incite Unrest). Death-site haunting (THR-1925, phaseProsperity step 9.5) is bounded pressure, not a per-tick add: once a day a place where someone died recently is lifted toward a target of the doom identity's deathSiteUnrestBonus × DEATH_SITE_UNREST_TARGET_SCALE 4, capped at DEATH_SITE_UNREST_TARGET_MAX 12, fading to 0 over DEATH_SITE_HAUNTING_WINDOW_TICKS 120 (ten days) after the last death (lastDeathTick, written by markMortalDead, or the latest fight/battle stamp). It never lowers unrest other causes raised, and the current target sits on the place as deathSiteUnrestTarget. Before, the bonus was added every tick for as long as deathCount > 0, which pinned a town at 99 for good under Reckoning and Breach (seed 7 medium: 397 settlement-ticks at 70+ from one death; now 0). Constants: src/data/death-site-unrest-constants.ts. It is read as a real pressure by action resolution (unifiedActionResolution.ts — unrest at the location shifts outcomes), omen aggregation (phaseOmenAgenda.ts), and survey prose (surveyProseComposer.ts). That loop is IMPL.
  • IMPL The town turns (THR-1957, hunger and unrest S2). runTownTurns (src/engine/unrestPressure/turns.ts) runs once a day after every settlement's daily pass. On the edge is a location trait on unrest (ON_THE_EDGE_ENTER 70 / ON_THE_EDGE_RELEASE 55). A held town bearing it, out of its TOWN_TURN_COOLDOWN_DAYS 20 cooldown, weighs every faction holding a settlement within DEFECTION_RADIUS_HEXES 8: view = 1.0·protection + 0.3·standing + 0.8·fed + 0.5·solvent (unrestPressure/defection.ts; protection is the crown's offer term over this one town, standing −1 for a siege by that lord within 30 days else +1 for a hall or resident member, fed the share of its towns not hungry, solvent 0 when Destitute). It turns when the best beats the holder by DEFECTION_MARGIN 0.4 (the plan said 0.25; raised by its kill criterion after a seed-99 town turned three times): controls is retargeted (via: 'defection', takenFrom), the old holder's relates_to drops to −0.35 and it adopts a revenge ambition naming the lord and the town, and the town takes DEFECTION_RELIEF −15. At RISING_THRESHOLD 90 a held town rises (rising.ts): the holder's resident members split off through splinterFaction (else the highest-ranked resident founds a free town through foundFaction), the new faction takes controls (via: 'rising') and hostile_to is written both ways; RISING_RELIEF −30. An unheld town at 90 empties (people leave twice as fast). The sack and emptying need the settlement economy's buildings and population (THR-1923): traced and skipped until then. Four chronicle triggers (town_on_the_edge, town_defects, town_rises, town_empties, full-detail towns only) and an event popup for On the edge and a rising when a threaded mortal lives there or the god holds a source there.
  • Legacy flag-off reading — threshold names: UNREST_DEFECTION_THRESHOLD 70 and UNREST_SACK_THRESHOLD 90 are named risk markers. The phase only emits defection_cleared / sack_risk_cleared traces when unrest decays back below them; an automatic defection roll or settlement-sack event at those thresholds is not auto-fired here. Treat the thresholds as documented risk lines feeding scoring and prose, not as a wired settlement-loss trigger.

Trade route decay IMPL

  • Trade routes are enriched trades_with graph edges (tradeRoute.ts, additive over the pre-existing volume field): volume 1–TRADE_ROUTE_MAX_VOLUME 10, plus goodsType, established, lastTraded, controlledBy (a taxing faction/agent, or null), taxRate, and threatened. All fields fail-soft to canonical defaults via readTradeRouteProps. Honest reader status (THR-1188): controlledBy is consumed — phaseEconomicTraits counts routes an actor controls, proseResolvers narrates the controlled status and names the controller. taxRate is written and read by nothing: no subsystem deducts the controller's take, though action-technical-effects.ts describes the toll to the player. Typed so the gap is legible; the missing collector is THR-1189.
  • Cargo manifest (THR-616, additive over goodsType): TradeRouteProperties.manifest is a CargoManifest naming the goods actually moving, derived at route formation from both endpoints' P1 stock tiers by buildRouteManifest() and assigned in strategicGraphOps.ts::createTradeRoute (which also emits the route_cargo_assigned trace). Read it only through readCargoManifest() — it is fail-soft in both directions (legacy goodsType → single-good manifest; neither present → EMPTY_CARGO_MANIFEST), which is what keeps pre-P2 routes working. goodsType remains populated as the manifest's primary good. Tunables: ROUTE_MANIFEST_MAX_GOODS 4, ROUTE_EXPORT_QUANTITY_FLOOR 60, ROUTE_WANT_QUANTITY_CEIL 30, ROUTE_BALANCE_SCORE_DIVISOR 3.
  • Complementarity bias in route formation IMPL: scoreRoutePairBalance() is a 0..1 signal of how well two endpoints' surpluses answer each other's scarcities; computeRouteFormationBias() (strategicActionCandidates.ts) folds ROUTE_FORMATION_BALANCE_BIAS (0.25) × that score into the target-specific worldImpact scoring component, gated on createsRoute(template) — the legacy create_trade_route hint or a create × route cell (THR-1862; the cells carry no_mutation, so the hint-only gate had left the bias dead under the cells model) — and fail-soft to 0 on a missing source node. Source endpoint resolves via getAgentLocationId() — the same located_at node createTradeRoute uses.
  • The four catalog trade verbs IMPL (THR-1188): action.gold.establish-trade, .trade, .disrupt-trade and .tax-trade-route are the player-facing way to touch the trade web, and they are typed graph ops (establish_trade_route, conduct_trade, disrupt_trade_route, tax_trade_route in tradeRouteOps.ts), not generic edge ops. The reason is structural, not stylistic: unifiedCandidates.ts sets a cast's targetId to the caster's own location, so $location and $target are the same node and no sentinel can name a trade partner. A generic add_edge bound $actor → $target therefore resolved to actor → location — refused by the EDGE_SCHEMA row, unreadable by every consumer, and for the update_edge/remove_edge three, unable to match any edge createTradeRoute ever wrote. Each op now resolves its own pair: the anchor is the caster's durable settlement (resolveDurableActorLocation, shared with the merchant strategic pack), and establish_trade_route picks the partner by scoreRoutePairBalance within TRADE_PARTNER_MAX_HEX_RANGE 6, scanning at most TRADE_PARTNER_MAX_CANDIDATES 24. The write delegates to createTradeRoute, so a player-formed route is indistinguishable from a merchant-pack one — same manifest, same establishedBy, same birth-tick lastTraded. conduct_trade refreshes the stalest route at the anchor (the one decay is about to take); disrupt_trade_route and tax_trade_route act on the busiest, tax preferring an untolled one. All deterministic (no RNG, id tie-breaks) and fail-soft — no anchor, no partner, no route each return an error result the action layer narrates as a failure.
  • Not yet wired DESIGN: route state does not materialize into encounters (banditry on a rich manifest, embargo or toll on a staple one) — THR-669; and route lines on HexMapV2 do not yet surface their cargo — THR-670. Caravans stay route state, never simulated agents, per the spotlight budget.
  • phaseTradeRouteDecay(state): a route is stale when currentTick − lastTraded > TRADE_ROUTE_FRESHNESS_WINDOW 5. Stale routes lose TRADE_ROUTE_DECAY_RATE 1 volume/tick; at volume 0 the edge is removed, a trade_route_dissolved summary trace fires, a SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_LOST −3 prosperity shock is pushed to both endpoint settlements (the lane's own ends; a Place end resolves up to its Location — until THR-1615 the shock walked located_at out of the settlements and never fired), the lane's Route identity node is removed with any holding on it (THR-1615 — it used to outlive the lane and stay claimable; the trace's identityNodeIds names it), and an economic-chronicle "A Road Falls Silent" entry is resolved.
  • Lane traffic IMPL (THR-1636): before the grace and staleness tests, each lane is classed once per tick by laneTraffic(graph, edge, tick) in tradeRoute.ts. carrying — both ends resolve (via resolveToParentLocation) to Locations in LOCATION_CLASSES.settlement and neither end's roads are cursed (isLocationRoutesCursed, exported from phaseProsperity.ts, not copied): lastTraded = tick, so the lane is never stale. suspended — blockadedBy set and threatened still true: lastTraded = tick too, never dissolved. idle — anything else: today's decay. On a settle tick (tick % LANE_TRAFFIC_SETTLE_INTERVAL_TICKS 12 === 0) volume steps one toward clamp(LANE_TRAFFIC_BASE_VOLUME 2 + round(balance × LANE_TRAFFIC_CARGO_VOLUME 2) − (threatened ? LANE_TRAFFIC_THREATENED_PENALTY 1 : 0), 1, LANE_TRAFFIC_MAX_VOLUME 4) (carrying) or toward 1 (suspended), traced as trade_route_volume_change cause traffic, with one trade_route_upkeep aggregate per settle tick. Kill switch LANE_TRAFFIC_ENABLED. The hex tooltip appends ·blockaded / ·fading; window.__DEBUG.getTradeLanes() reads the class. Measured (seeds 42 · 99, medium, 300 ticks, switch off → on, one process per arm): worldgen lanes standing at t300 went 0 → 4 of 6 on seed 42 and 0 → 6 of 6 on seed 99. The two seed-42 deaths came at t100, when their capital was razed to ruins; both were idle and traced. Route-event scans that planted a seed after t36 went 0 → 9 on seed 99.
  • Founder's grace IMPL (THR-1320): before the staleness test, a route carrying an establishedBy stamp is skipped entirely while currentTick − established < TRADE_ROUTE_FOUNDING_GRACE_WINDOW 36. A route with no founder gets no grace, so weather still decays on inactivity alone. Why it exists: createTradeRoute is the sole minter of trades_with and mints at volume 1, and nothing in the strategic path ever refreshes lastTraded — only the divine trade verbs do. So a route founded by a six-tick undertaking went stale at +6, lost its only point of volume and was removed, measured as 0 routes standing at tick 150 on seeds 42 and 99. The trade_route kind's counter-play (strategic_blockade_route) was offered and pursued 9 and 16 times and returned no_route every time — every board-level instrument read healthy while the counter-play did nothing. After the fix, seed 42: the Thornhaven–Hawkgate Road was founded, christened and blockaded at tick 96, and Hawkgate's prosperity fell 35.35 → 6.73, recovering to 29.58 by tick 130 as routeEvents lifted the horizon.
  • Reading the birth tick: every writer stamps it as establishedTick, so readTradeRouteProps reads that spelling and keeps established as a legacy fallback. Until THR-1320 it read only established, which no writer sets — so it resolved to 0 on every route ever minted, and the trade_route_dissolved trace reported the absolute tick as a lifetime.

Settlement genome reassessment IMPL

  • phaseSettlementReassessment(state) consumes the pendingReassessment flag set by a tier change (or any other trigger). After PROMOTION_REASSESSMENT_DELAY (so it never runs on the same tick as the change), it re-runs runSettlementGenome to update the settlement's archetype, sublocation menu, and NPC budget for its new size. Actual sublocation creation/destruction from the genome result is handled by phaseSublocations (materialization).
  • phaseSublocations(state, cache, runtime) spawns and dissolves sublocations per predicate — e.g. a mine spawns only where the location has ore or stone resource — so a settlement's interior places track both its tier and its ground.
  • Since THR-1296 both dissolution call sites (phaseSublocations and the orchestrator's own pass) consult a makeDissolutionHold predicate built from the binder's ledger: a sublocation currently bound as a long work's stage is skipped rather than dissolved, and the deferral traces itself as binding_severed with cause dissolution_deferred. The node is made busy, never immortal — dissolution resumes the turn the binding releases. This is the housekeeping half of a deliberate split: chores wait for a bound stage, narrative reapers (a siege razing it) proceed and break the binding loudly instead. Dormant until a long work declares a cast, which no shipped template does yet.

Economic traits & the economic chronicle IMPL

  • phaseEconomicTraits(state) (after phaseProsperity) assigns/removes eight economic has_trait traits that emerge from activity: trade-baron, guild-sworn, monopolist, smuggler, patron (reputations/masteries) and bankrupt, debt-laden, coin-cursed (scars/conditions) — gated by wealth tiers (WEALTH_TIER_WEALTHY etc.) and thresholds such as ECON_TRAIT_TRADE_BARON_MIN_ROUTES. debt-laden counts debts through readAgreement (agreementShape.ts, THR-1920), which reads both agreement edge shapes — the reward writer's flat agreement: true + agreementType and the fixture's nested agreement.type; before it, no encounter-granted debt ever counted. A flat-shape debt counts only for its source (the debtor), never its creditor, and stops counting once its term (acquiredTick + ticksRemaining) has run out — agreementLapsed, since nothing expires agreement edges.
  • phaseEconomicChronicle(state) and economicChronicle.ts resolve economic events into chronicle chapters. Fifteen triggers are titled: settlement promotion/demotion, trade route established/died, guild founded, monopoly established/broken, mercenary hire, assassination commissioned, agreement broken, wealth tier up/down, resource scarcity/glut, and — since THR-1428 — holding_income ("A Holding Pays"). The wealth-tier pair reads individual actor nodes (THR-1862: it read a non-existent agent node type and never fired before) and writes only for spotlight-tier mortals (WEALTH_TIER_CHRONICLE_SPOTLIGHT_TIERS); every individual's tier is still tracked. Unfiltered it wrote 143 entries by t300 on seed 42, mostly notables at graduation; filtered, 6. Deterministic (seeded per node), fail-soft to a plain event if a template is missing.
  • "A Holding Pays" is the quiet one (significance 0.3, five templates). It narrates what somebody collects from holding a thing rather than what a place produces: a seized road's tolls, a freehold's keep, a controlled town's tithe. Income is paid every twelve ticks by the holding_income phase, but the line is rate-limited per holder to once per HOLDING_INCOME_CHRONICLE_INTERVAL_TICKS 72 and names only the largest source — a chronicle that retold a daily toll every day would be an accounts ledger. No amount ever appears; what moves on the player's side is the holder's wealth tier word on their sheet.
  • The trigger table is a Record over the union, and that is load-bearing: adding a trigger without a title in formatChronicleTitle is a type error rather than a chapter titled undefined. Worth naming because the first draft of the THR-1428 trigger did exactly that and the typecheck ratchet is what caught it.
  • IMPL — regional monopolies, scarcity rent and embargo (THR-1955, war aims S2): phaseEconomicPower §1 now runs the regional rule (phases/regionalMonopolies.ts over regionalMonopoly.ts): for each good, a faction holds a monopoly when, around an anchor place it holds, at least MONOPOLY_REGION_MIN_PLACES place-tier bearing places lie within MONOPOLY_REGION_RADIUS_HEXES and it holds MONOPOLY_CONTROL_FRACTION (0.6) of them. Stored as faction.regionalMonopolies (resourceId, anchorLocationId, share, placeIds, sinceTick); faction.monopolies stays as the deduplicated good ids. The chronicle lines now say "holds most of the {good} around {town}" and are de-duplicated by MONOPOLY_MIN_HOLD_DAYS. The purse pays MONOPOLY_SCARCITY_RENT on the held good (placeYieldOf(place, scarcityRentFor(…))). The embargo faction action stores one activeEmbargo on the holder; embargoed towns and lanes are derived, and the stock pass cuts each embargoed town's balance on the good by EMBARGO_BALANCE_CUT. The Purse panel shows "Holds the region's {good}" chips; REGIONAL_MONOPOLY_ENABLED = false restores the world-wide rule.

Resources & anomalies IMPL

  • seedLocationResources(graph, locationIds, cosmology, rng) (resourceSeeding.ts, called during seedWorld()) places terrain-based resources — the four base goods timber, stone, ore, water from TERRAIN_RESOURCE_TABLE, with cosmology sphere weights biasing quantities (COSMOLOGY_RESOURCE_BONUS 15). Resources feed prosperity's carrying-capacity ceiling, not passive income.
  • Anomalies: ten special anomaly locations map to uncommon resources via ANOMALY_RESOURCE_MAP (gem_deposit→gemstones, crystal_cavern→arcane_crystal, golden_grove→golden_sap, herb_garden→medicinal_herb, ancient_vault→ancient_relic, sunken_treasury→sunken_gold, fossil_bed→fossil_amber, iron_seep→star_metal, pearl_shoal→pearls, glowcap_hollow→glowcap). An undiscovered anomaly (isAnomalyLocation) grants an encounter-scoring discovery bonus to Eye/Veil-skilled agents (computeAnomalyDiscoveryBonus, encounterScoring.ts) — the perceptive are drawn to find them.

Tick placement IMPL

  • Order within the tick (orchestrator.ts): phaseTradeRouteDecay → … → phaseProsperity → phaseEconomicTraits → phaseSettlementPromotion → phaseSettlementReassessment → … → phaseUnrest → … → phaseSublocations → phaseEconomicChronicle. Prosperity is computed before promotion reads it; reassessment follows the tier change it reacts to; the chronicle closes the economic beat.
