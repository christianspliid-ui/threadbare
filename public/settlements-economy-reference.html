Settlements, Prosperity & Trade

Villages rise into cities and slump back into hamlets, all on their own. Prosperity and unrest are a settlement's pulse; trade routes are its arteries; the land beneath it sets the ceiling on how large it can ever grow.

Settlements grow and shrink on their ownA place that thrives for long enough grows into something bigger; a place that suffers for long enough falls. You do not build settlements tier by tier — you shape the conditions, and the world does the rest.

Every built place in the world sits at one of four sizes. A hamlet is a scatter of homes; a town is a proper market; a city is a seat of trade and power; and a capital is the fixed heart of a realm. The first three are alive to their fortunes: hold a hamlet in good times long enough and it grows into a town, then a city — and let a city rot in hard times long enough and it shrinks the same way, city to town to hamlet. Growth is never a single lucky season; a place has to sustain its fortune for a good stretch before it changes.

A capital is the exception — it never grows and never falls. It is the anchor its realm is measured against, not a fortune that swings. And a settlement that is sick — its wells poisoned, its people ailing — cannot grow no matter how rich it is; a place must be healthy as well as prosperous to rise.

HamletA handful of homes. The smallest settled place. Grows into a town on sustained good fortune.
TownA market and a wall. The middle rung — can rise to a city or slip back to a hamlet.
CityA seat of trade and power. The largest that fortune alone can build — and the first to feel the cost of hard times, since it must be fed.
CapitalThe fixed heart of a realm. Never promotes, never demotes. An anchor, not a fortune.
When a settlement changes, it is a moment

A rise or a fall is not a silent number. When a place grows or shrinks a tier, the world marks it — a chronicle entry is written ("A Settlement Rises", "A Settlement Falls"), and the place is quietly re-appraised: a grown city gains the kinds of places a city should have, a fallen hamlet loses what it can no longer support.

Prosperity and unrest — a settlement's pulseProsperity is how well a place is doing; unrest is how close it is to boiling over. Together they are the vital signs that decide whether a settlement rises, holds, or falls.

Prosperity is a settlement's wealth and health of trade, measured on a scale from destitute to flourishing. It does not drift at random — it always moves toward a target set by the place's real conditions: live trade routes and a strong faction presence lift the target; corruption in the ground, high unrest, and a sickly economy drag it down; a matching sphere in the world's makeup nudges it either way. If nothing about a place changes, its prosperity settles and stays put. When it moves, it moves for a reason you can name — a road opened, a raid struck, a blessing landed. Growth and decline always trace to a visible cause.

Unrest is the other half of the pulse — political instability, the pressure of a people ready to riot, defect, or be sacked. Unrest builds when things go wrong: a betrayal in an encounter, the poisonous reach of the Doom Clock, a god stirring the pot on purpose. Left alone, it cools a little every turn, and prosperity cools it faster — a content, well-fed town settles quickly. But push it high enough and it becomes a real danger: a deeply unsettled place is a place where loyalties fray and worse things wait. Unrest colours everything that happens there — the odds of what mortals attempt, the omens the world throws up, the tone of the reports you read.

Two dials worth watching

Prosperity and unrest pull against each other. A rich, calm settlement climbs toward a promotion; a poor, seething one slides toward a fall. If you want a place to grow, you feed its prosperity and drain its unrest — open trade, plant your influence, settle its people. If you want a rival's stronghold to crumble, you do the reverse.

Trade routes — the arteries between placesSettlements that trade with one another are richer for it. A route is a living thing: use it and it thrives, neglect it and it withers to nothing.

Where two settlements exchange goods, a trade route runs between them — a road carrying a named cargo manifest of wares, with a volume that measures how much flows along it. The manifest is worked out when the route forms, from what each end has spare and what each end wants — so a road between a grain-glutted valley and a hungry mining town carries grain, and you can tell at a glance what severing it would cost. Merchants prefer partners whose shortages answer their surpluses, which means the web of roads that grows over a run maps the world's real scarcities rather than mere proximity. A busy route is worth real prosperity to both ends. But a route is only as alive as its last caravan: leave it un-travelled for a while and its volume begins to bleed away, turn by turn, until — if no one trades on it again — the road falls silent and the route dissolves entirely. A route can also be threatened by bandits or war, or controlled by a faction that taxes what passes, and either shows in what the route is worth.

This is why a web of trade is a fragile, tended thing rather than a permanent fixture. Roads that carry commerce hold the realm together; roads that carry nothing quietly disappear, and the places they once fed feel the loss.

A road falling silent is news

When a trade route finally dies, the world records it — "A Road Falls Silent" — and the settlements at either end take a one-time knock to their prosperity, feeling the artery close. New routes, likewise, arrive as good news and a small lift. The economy speaks in these small, legible events.

The trade bonus was dead until 2026-08-19

Routes have always lifted a settlement's prosperity target on paper, and until THR-830 they never lifted it in practice. A route hangs off the two places it joins, but phaseProsperity went looking for it on the people standing in the settlement — walking to each actor present and reading their routes, of which they have none and never had any. So the count came back zero for every settlement in every run, and the arteries described above fed nothing. The declaration was the reason: EDGE_SCHEMA called a route a link between actors, warned twice on every real route for as long as the system has existed, and was read as documentation by everything built near it. Both halves are corrected now, and the same wrong turn had been taken by the trade-route prose, the caravan-rest sublocation gate, and the trade-baron trait count.

The land sets the ceilingResources are what the ground beneath a settlement can give. They do not make a place grow — they decide how large it is allowed to grow, and where the rare and remarkable can be found.

Every place is seeded, at the world's making, with the resources its terrain affords: timber from forests, stone and ore from mountains, water from rivers and coasts. These common goods set a settlement's carrying capacity — the ceiling on how prosperous it can become. A rich vein of ore or a broad stand of timber lets a place grow large; a barren hex holds it small, no matter how much trade or blessing it receives. Resources are the ground's promise, not a river of passive income; they cap what is possible rather than pouring wealth in.

Scattered across the map, too, are rarer places — anomalies. A gem deposit, a crystal cavern, a golden grove, a sunken treasury: each hides an uncommon resource found nowhere in ordinary terrain, and each quietly draws the curious and the perceptive toward it. The sharp-eyed and the veil-touched among mortals feel the pull of an undiscovered anomaly like a scent on the wind, and the discovery of one is a small event in itself.

Honest scope — this is texture, not a trading sim

Threadbearer's economy exists to make the world feel alive and consequential, not to be played as a market. You will never balance a ledger or set a tax rate by hand. Settlements rise and fall, roads open and close, fortunes turn — and all of it feeds the story of a place: who prospers, who suffers, what a god's meddling did to a corner of the world. The economy is a story engine wearing a merchant's coat.

Designer Notes

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). The systems-spec authority for this page is Docs/plans/2026-03-17-gold-reach-economic-systems-design.md (the Gold Reach economic systems design). Honest UL note: the Ubiquitous Language shards do not yet carry dedicated entries for Prosperity, Unrest, Trade Route, or the settlement-tier subtypes — this is a known documentation gap, noted not re-opened; the code (phaseProsperity.ts et al.) is the working authority until a UL shard is minted.

Settlement tier promotion / demotion IMPL

  • phaseSettlementPromotion(state, runtime?) runs once per tick, after phaseProsperity. It walks all location nodes whose locationSubtype is a settlement subtype (hamlet, town, city, capital), skipping capital entirely — capitals never change tier.
  • Each settlement carries two sustain counters on its node properties: prosperitySustainAboveTicks and prosperitySustainBelowTicks. When prosperity is ≥ SETTLEMENT_PROMOTION_PROSPERITY 70 the above-counter increments (and the below-counter resets); when ≤ SETTLEMENT_DEMOTION_PROSPERITY 20 the below-counter increments (above resets); the mid-band leaves both alone. A tier change fires when a counter reaches SETTLEMENT_PROMOTION_SUSTAIN_TICKS 20 — promotion hamlet→town→city, demotion city→town→hamlet. Counters reset to 0 after any change.
  • Health gate (THR-401): promotion is suppressed while a settlement is sickened (wellsSickenedUntilTick in the future) or its populationHealth is below LOC_HEALTH_DAMPENER_THRESHOLD — a sick place cannot grow, and its above-counter is reset to be safe. Demotion has no such gate.
  • A tier change mutates locationSubtype in place, calls applyEncounterCacheUpdate(runtime, …onLocationTypeChanged) so encounter scoring sees the new type, emits a settlement_tier_change trace, resolves an economic-chronicle entry (significance 0.9, fail-soft to a plain TickEvent if template resolution returns null), and sets pendingReassessment for the next phase.

Prosperity — equilibrium model IMPL

  • phaseProsperity(state) models prosperity as a 0–100 value that drifts toward a target at PROSPERITY_DRIFT_RATE 0.05 (minimum step PROSPERITY_DRIFT_MIN 0.1, per-tick change clamped to PROSPERITY_DELTA_CLAMP 2). Pure arithmetic — no PRNG. If conditions are static, prosperity settles; every movement traces to a named input.
  • Target inputs: PROSPERITY_TRADE_BONUS_PER_ROUTE 8 per live route; FACTION_PRESENCE_BONUS 5 (cap 15); corruption penalty above CORRUPTION_TARGET_THRESHOLD 0.2 (up to −25); divine-influence bonus above DIVINE_TARGET_THRESHOLD 0.3 (up to +15); unrest penalty above UNREST_TARGET_THRESHOLD 30 (up to −20); a small sphere-affinity term (Life/Energy bonus, Entropy penalty, capped at PROSPERITY_SPHERE_CAP 0.15).
  • Carrying capacity is a ceiling, not income: BASE_CARRYING_CAPACITY 30 + resources × RESOURCE_CAPACITY_RATE 0.5, capped at MAX_CARRYING_CAPACITY 100. Cities and capitals pay upkeep (UPKEEP_CITY 5, UPKEEP_CAPITAL 8) — they decay toward decline without active support.
  • Shocks: one-time deltas pushed by upstream phases via state.prosperityShocks — e.g. SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_ESTABLISHED +3, SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_LOST −3, SHOCK_FACTION_ARRIVAL +2. Tier bands (PROSPERITY_TIER_FLOURISHING 80 / PROSPEROUS 60 / MODEST 40 / STRUGGLING 20) are the named prose thresholds.

Unrest — decay-only phase, driven up elsewhere IMPL DESIGN

  • phaseUnrest(state) is decay-only. Per settlement it applies UNREST_DECAY_RATE 1/tick, plus an extra UNREST_PROSPERITY_DAMPER 0.5 when prosperity > 60, minus a health-bleed when population health is low (THR-401). It clamps to [0,100], writes back location.properties.unrest, and emits an unrest_tick trace. It is not where unrest rises.
  • Unrest is driven up by other systems: encounter complications (complicationEffects.ts), Doom Clock effects (phaseDoom.ts), and player actions (e.g. Incite Unrest). It is read as a real pressure by action resolution (unifiedActionResolution.ts — unrest at the location shifts outcomes), omen aggregation (phaseOmenAgenda.ts), and survey prose (surveyProseComposer.ts). That loop is IMPL.
  • DESIGN — threshold consequences: UNREST_DEFECTION_THRESHOLD 70 and UNREST_SACK_THRESHOLD 90 are named risk markers. The phase only emits defection_cleared / sack_risk_cleared traces when unrest decays back below them; an automatic defection roll or settlement-sack event at those thresholds is not auto-fired here. Treat the thresholds as documented risk lines feeding scoring and prose, not as a wired settlement-loss trigger.

Trade route decay IMPL

  • Trade routes are enriched trades_with graph edges (tradeRoute.ts, additive over the pre-existing volume field): volume 1–TRADE_ROUTE_MAX_VOLUME 10, plus goodsType, established, lastTraded, controlledBy (a taxing faction/agent, or null), taxRate, and threatened. All fields fail-soft to canonical defaults via readTradeRouteProps. Honest reader status (THR-1188): controlledBy is consumed — phaseEconomicTraits counts routes an actor controls, proseResolvers narrates the controlled status and names the controller. taxRate is written and read by nothing: no subsystem deducts the controller's take, though action-technical-effects.ts describes the toll to the player. Typed so the gap is legible; the missing collector is THR-1189.
  • Cargo manifest (THR-616, additive over goodsType): TradeRouteProperties.manifest is a CargoManifest naming the goods actually moving, derived at route formation from both endpoints' P1 stock tiers by buildRouteManifest() and assigned in strategicGraphOps.ts::createTradeRoute (which also emits the route_cargo_assigned trace). Read it only through readCargoManifest() — it is fail-soft in both directions (legacy goodsType → single-good manifest; neither present → EMPTY_CARGO_MANIFEST), which is what keeps pre-P2 routes working. goodsType remains populated as the manifest's primary good. Tunables: ROUTE_MANIFEST_MAX_GOODS 4, ROUTE_EXPORT_QUANTITY_FLOOR 60, ROUTE_WANT_QUANTITY_CEIL 30, ROUTE_BALANCE_SCORE_DIVISOR 3.
  • Complementarity bias in route formation IMPL: scoreRoutePairBalance() is a 0..1 signal of how well two endpoints' surpluses answer each other's scarcities; computeRouteFormationBias() (strategicActionCandidates.ts) folds ROUTE_FORMATION_BALANCE_BIAS (0.25) × that score into the target-specific worldImpact scoring component, gated on mutationHint.type === 'create_trade_route' and fail-soft to 0 on a missing source node. Source endpoint resolves via getAgentLocationId() — the same located_at node createTradeRoute uses.
  • The four catalog trade verbs IMPL (THR-1188): action.gold.establish-trade, .trade, .disrupt-trade and .tax-trade-route are the player-facing way to touch the trade web, and they are typed graph ops (establish_trade_route, conduct_trade, disrupt_trade_route, tax_trade_route in tradeRouteOps.ts), not generic edge ops. The reason is structural, not stylistic: unifiedCandidates.ts sets a cast's targetId to the caster's own location, so $location and $target are the same node and no sentinel can name a trade partner. A generic add_edge bound $actor → $target therefore resolved to actor → location — refused by the EDGE_SCHEMA row, unreadable by every consumer, and for the update_edge/remove_edge three, unable to match any edge createTradeRoute ever wrote. Each op now resolves its own pair: the anchor is the caster's durable settlement (resolveDurableActorLocation, shared with the merchant strategic pack), and establish_trade_route picks the partner by scoreRoutePairBalance within TRADE_PARTNER_MAX_HEX_RANGE 6, scanning at most TRADE_PARTNER_MAX_CANDIDATES 24. The write delegates to createTradeRoute, so a player-formed route is indistinguishable from a merchant-pack one — same manifest, same establishedBy, same birth-tick lastTraded. conduct_trade refreshes the stalest route at the anchor (the one decay is about to take); disrupt_trade_route and tax_trade_route act on the busiest, tax preferring an untolled one. All deterministic (no RNG, id tie-breaks) and fail-soft — no anchor, no partner, no route each return an error result the action layer narrates as a failure.
  • Not yet wired DESIGN: route state does not materialize into encounters (banditry on a rich manifest, embargo or toll on a staple one) — THR-669; and route lines on HexMapV2 do not yet surface their cargo — THR-670. Caravans stay route state, never simulated agents, per the spotlight budget.
  • phaseTradeRouteDecay(state): a route is stale when currentTick − lastTraded > TRADE_ROUTE_FRESHNESS_WINDOW 5. Stale routes lose TRADE_ROUTE_DECAY_RATE 1 volume/tick; at volume 0 the edge is removed, a trade_route_dissolved summary trace fires, a SHOCK_TRADE_ROUTE_LOST −3 prosperity shock is pushed to both endpoints, and an economic-chronicle "A Road Falls Silent" entry is resolved.

Settlement genome reassessment IMPL

  • phaseSettlementReassessment(state) consumes the pendingReassessment flag set by a tier change (or any other trigger). After PROMOTION_REASSESSMENT_DELAY (so it never runs on the same tick as the change), it re-runs runSettlementGenome to update the settlement's archetype, sublocation menu, and NPC budget for its new size. Actual sublocation creation/destruction from the genome result is handled by phaseSublocations (materialization).
  • phaseSublocations(state, cache, runtime) spawns and dissolves sublocations per predicate — e.g. a mine spawns only where the location has ore or stone resource — so a settlement's interior places track both its tier and its ground.

Economic traits & the economic chronicle IMPL

  • phaseEconomicTraits(state) (after phaseProsperity) assigns/removes eight economic has_trait traits that emerge from activity: trade-baron, guild-sworn, monopolist, smuggler, patron (reputations/masteries) and bankrupt, debt-laden, coin-cursed (scars/conditions) — gated by wealth tiers (WEALTH_TIER_WEALTHY etc.) and thresholds such as ECON_TRAIT_TRADE_BARON_MIN_ROUTES.
  • phaseEconomicChronicle(state) and economicChronicle.ts resolve economic events into chronicle chapters. Twelve triggers are titled: settlement promotion/demotion, trade route established/died, guild founded, monopoly established/broken, mercenary hire, assassination commissioned, agreement broken, and wealth tier up/down. Deterministic (seeded per node), fail-soft to a plain event if a template is missing.

Resources & anomalies IMPL

  • seedLocationResources(graph, locationIds, cosmology, rng) (resourceSeeding.ts, called during seedWorld()) places terrain-based resources — the four base goods timber, stone, ore, water from TERRAIN_RESOURCE_TABLE, with cosmology sphere weights biasing quantities (COSMOLOGY_RESOURCE_BONUS 15). Resources feed prosperity's carrying-capacity ceiling, not passive income.
  • Anomalies: ten special anomaly locations map to uncommon resources via ANOMALY_RESOURCE_MAP (gem_deposit→gemstones, crystal_cavern→arcane_crystal, golden_grove→golden_sap, herb_garden→medicinal_herb, ancient_vault→ancient_relic, sunken_treasury→sunken_gold, fossil_bed→fossil_amber, iron_seep→star_metal, pearl_shoal→pearls, glowcap_hollow→glowcap). An undiscovered anomaly (isAnomalyLocation) grants an encounter-scoring discovery bonus to Eye/Veil-skilled agents (computeAnomalyDiscoveryBonus, encounterScoring.ts) — the perceptive are drawn to find them.

Tick placement IMPL

  • Order within the tick (orchestrator.ts): phaseTradeRouteDecay → … → phaseProsperity → phaseEconomicTraits → phaseSettlementPromotion → phaseSettlementReassessment → … → phaseUnrest → … → phaseSublocations → phaseEconomicChronicle. Prosperity is computed before promotion reads it; reassessment follows the tier change it reacts to; the chronicle closes the economic beat.
