Reading the Interface

A guided tour of the screen — the map at the centre, the frame of always-on gauges around it, the panels you drill into, and the drawer where you act. Every system in this manual shows its face somewhere here; this page is the map of the map.

One screen, no scrollingThe world fills exactly one viewport. The map is the centre; everything else is a frame that reads the same world back to you in a different register.

Threadbearer is played on a single screen. Nothing scrolls off the bottom, nothing hides below a fold — the whole interface is one glance, so your attention is spent on the world, not on hunting for controls. The hex map sits at the centre and is the world; wrapped around it is a frame of always-visible gauges, and tucked into the edges are the panels and drawers you open when you want to look closer or act.

The regions above are not exact pixels — layouts shift as the game grows — but the logic is stable: who you are and how the run is going live at the top, the world lives in the middle, what you can do and what just happened live at the bottom, and the deeper story is one click away in the rails.

The always-on frameA ring of gauges that are always on screen. You never open them to check the run's pulse — they are simply there, all the time.

These surfaces are mounted always: no click summons them, they are part of the frame. Read left-to-right and top-to-bottom, they answer "how is the run going?" without you asking.

Essence Panel

Your divine fuel, held per Sphere — the tight economy every action spends from. Always visible so a cost is never a surprise.

Simulation Controls

Pause, advance, and the tick counter. The world waits here until you tell it to move — this is where "do nothing" is expressed.

Doom Bar

The countdown made into a strip. Click it to expand the full archetype detail. The shadow you are always living under.

Omen Indicator

The world's atmospheric pressure — up to two active omens. Click for the omen detail. The mood of the hour.

Mandate Tracker

Your declared win-state, tilting toward or away. Click for the full stakes. The count-up against the count-down.

Ascendant Bar & Identity Chip

Who you remembered being — your two domains and hunger, in a compact strip. Click opens your full sheet.

Attention Indicator

You cannot watch everything. This shows how much attention is spoken for and when a thread is tugging for it.

World Pulse & Live-Location Bar

The world's breathing state (phase, tick rate) and a context strip naming whatever hex or place you have focused.

Toast Stack

Transient notices that rise and fade — a mortal became, a thread deepened. Filtered to what a player should feel.

Alert Bar

A persistent row for the loud events — the ones you should not miss even while looking elsewhere.

Rivals Button

A compact read on the other gods watching your domain. Click opens the full rival panel.

Avatar HUD

Appears only when you have taken a body in the world — the extra readout an embodied god needs.

The gauges are the manual made live

Almost every system this manual describes has a face in the always-on frame. The essence economy is the Essence Panel; the two clocks are the Doom Bar and Mandate Tracker; the omen agenda is the Omen Indicator; your remembered identity is the Ascendant Bar; rival gods are the Rivals Button; attention is its own indicator. Learning the frame is learning where each system lives.

The map, and drilling inThe map is the world. Click into it and you descend one tier at a time — hex, then location, then sublocation — never skipping a rung.

The hex map is not a picture of the world; it is the world. What you can see is governed by fog of war and your sight, and what sits on a hex — settlements, mortals, armies, works — is drawn there directly. Click a hex and the interface drills in: a breadcrumb appears, a sidebar lists what the hex holds, and a per-hex chronicle shows what has happened there.

Click a hex → Hex detail (breadcrumb · sidebar · chronicle) → Location view → Sublocation / agent card

This mirrors the world's own structure exactly: everything sits at hex → location → sublocation, and the interface walks you down that ladder one rung at a time rather than teleporting you into a wall of detail. A location view shows a settlement's sublocations and who lives there; an agent info card is the light, in-rail read on a single mortal before you commit to the full dossier. The world map's own systems — terrain, sight, three-tier position — get their own page in The World: Hexes, Terrain & Sight.

The panels — scanning your portfolioThe rails hold the panels where you read your relationships and the run's unfolding story: threads, retinue, chronicle, and the narrative feed.

Where the always-on frame answers "how is the run going?", the panels answer "who are my people and what is happening to them?" They open in the rails without leaving the map:

Threads Panel

Your woven bonds — every thread you hold, with its stakes. Click one for the full thread detail and its story-so-far.

Retinue Panel

The mortals bonded closest to you — your core portfolio-scan surface, where you take stock of who is in your orbit.

Chronicle Panel

The rolling feed of the run's most significant events — the book of the world being written as you play.

Narrative Feed

The prose stream — what is happening, told in the game's voice rather than as numbers. The run's ongoing narration.

Thread Detail & Strand View

A single bond up close — participants, stakes, history — with a story-so-far digest of what you may have missed.

Hex Sidebar & Hex Chronicle

The contents of the hex you selected and the events particular to it — the map's own rail companions.

The thread and court systems these panels surface are the subject of Threads, the Court & The First; the chronicle and narrative feed belong to Attention, the Digest & the Chronicle. The panels are the window; those pages are the machinery behind the glass.

The Action Drawer — where you actEvery intervention you make begins in one place: the drawer of action cards, filtered to whatever you have in focus.

Acting on the world happens through the Action Drawer. It is not a fixed menu of buttons — it is a pool of cards filtered by context: pick a target, and the drawer shows only the actions that target can actually receive, given your two domains, your Sphere alignment, your essence, and your reach. A card that never appears is telling you something true — that this act is outside what you are, here, now.

Why a card is missing is part of the interface

The drawer's silences are legible, not broken. An action outside your two permanent domains simply never shows; a Sphere-misaligned or too-costly card is filtered out; a target you have not yet found cannot be shaped. The full logic of the five verbs and their prerequisites is in Divine Actions & Prerequisites, and every template is browsable in the Action Catalog. Committing an intervention passes through a short confirmation step before it fires.

The curated moment — encounters on screenWhen a chapter frames itself around a mortal, the interface steps forward: the veil, the choice set, the vignette. These are the moments the whole turn is built to deliver.

Most of the turn is you scanning and nudging. But when an encounter reaches the surface, the interface changes register to give it room:

Encounter Veil

The overlay for an encounter you are watching in progress — the framed chapter given the screen's full attention.

Choice-Set Modal

When a branching encounter asks the player to decide, the choices surface here. Your call, not the mortal's.

Journey-Vignette Modal

A beat in The First's hero's journey, popped forward as a short authored moment when the clock schedules it.

Intervention Confirm

The nudge / whisper / vision confirmation — the last beat before your divine will touches the world.

These are the pay-off of the three-beat turn described in The Three-Beat Turn: scan, then the curated moment the interface makes room for, then the aftermath that reshapes a mortal's course.

The deeper sheetsOne more layer down are the full dossiers — modals you open when a compact card is not enough. They cover the whole cast of the world.

When you want everything about a thing, you open its sheet — a modal that takes over the screen with the full record. Each entity in the world has one:

Ascendant Sheet

Your own full profile — identity, echoes, and your bonds to the World-Soul across runs.

Agent Profile & Intelligence

A mortal's full dossier — backstory strata, traits, and what you have learned about them.

Faction Sheet

A faction's rank structure, agenda, membership, and trust — the shape of an organised power.

Army & Artifact Sheets

A host's composition, morale, and objective; an artifact's origin, properties, and the hands it has passed through.

Location Profile

A place in full — prosperity, factions present, inhabitants, and structures.

Rival / Doom / Omen / Mandate Detail

The expanded reads behind the frame's compact gauges — the full story of a clock, an omen, or a rival god.

Scry Overlay & Agenda Picker

The divine-vision overlay that reveals a hex, and the picker for setting a long strategic agenda.

Harvest Screen

The end-of-run ritual — where echoes are gathered and the World-Soul remembers. Only when the run is ending.

The Harvest Screen belongs to the run's close, covered in A Run, Start to Finish; the factions, agents, and armies these sheets open onto are the subject of the living-world pages once they land.

The other roomsBeyond the live game there are a handful of reference and setup views — the same interface language, different purposes.

The game is one view among several. Before a run you pass through the start page and ascendant selection (choosing who you were, with a cosmology drill-in). Alongside the live game sit reference rooms: the Codex (a browsable catalogue of divine actions, conditions, and agreements), the Content Browser (CMS), the Ubiquitous Language dashboard (the game's glossary), and the Style Guide (the component reference). These are not part of the moment-to-moment loop — they are where you go to look something up — but they share the same frame, the same nav, and the same one-screen discipline.

Designer Notes

The IA manifest as the authority, the 62-surface commitment with its view / mount / reads[] discipline, the viewport contract, WebGL assertion caveat, and the freshness contract that keeps this page honest.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). The synthesis authority for this page is not the rulebook but the IA manifest — src/data/ia-manifest.ts (IA_SURFACES) — browsable in-game at ?view=cms#ia-surfaces. The IA manifest is the commitment document of what the player should see; this page is its player-voice narration. Terminology authority remains the UL; where a surface name and the UL disagree, the UL wins and the surface is a drift bug.

The manifest is the authority IMPL

  • 62 surfaces — IA_SURFACES is an array of IASurface records (src/data/ia-manifest.ts). Each carries a surface id (e.g. game.action-drawer), a view, a mount, and a reads[] list. The in-game viewer at ?view=cms#ia-surfaces renders all of them with view/mount badges, the reads[] tables, and an "Open this surface" link where an openUrl is declared.
  • Seven views — SurfaceView = start | ascendant | game | codex | styleguide | cms | ul. Of the 62, 47 are the game view — the surfaces this page tours; the remaining 15 are the start/selection flow and the reference rooms (codex, CMS, UL, styleguide).
  • Three mounts — SurfaceMount = always | modal | drillin, the load-bearing distinction the player layer is organised around:
always the frame — mounted for the whole session, never summoned (17 game surfaces). drillin the panels — opened in the rails without leaving the map (11 game surfaces). modal the sheets — full takeover, opened and dismissed (19 game surfaces).

Surface → reads[] discipline IMPL

  • Every surface declares which GameState paths it reads and the reader component that consumes them, each with a visible_when predicate (e.g. game.mandate-tracker reads mandateState visible-when mandateState !== null). This is the contract the interface-audit and playtest-interface tooling asserts against — a surface that stops reading its declared state, or renders when its predicate is false, is a regression the manifest catches.
  • The player-facing filter matters: game.toast-stack reads tickEvents only with a notification directive, and game.alert-bar only those marked alert-level. Not every tick event reaches the player — the routing is deliberate (see Attention, the Digest & the Chronicle).
  • The game.action-drawer open/close state is exposed for tests via __DEBUG.getActiveUIState().actionDrawerOpen; the drawer's cards are context-filtered per target, not a fixed set (see Divine Actions).

The viewport contract IMPL

  • 1920×1080, one viewport, nothing scrolls. html, body, #root are pinned to height: 100dvh; overflow: hidden (src/index.css:201–202); full-screen layouts use h-screen flex flex-col overflow-hidden and child panels scroll internally via flex-1 overflow-y-auto. A component that renders off-screen at 1920×1080 is a bug of the same severity as a broken interaction. Modals cap at max-height: 85vh; absolute overlays use inset: 0 within their parent. Full rule: CLAUDE.md § Viewport Contract.
  • WebGL caveat. game.hex-map is a Three.js scene; DOM tools (Playwright snapshot/inspect) see only a blank <canvas>. Its assertions run through __DEBUG.snapshotScene(), and visual verification uses Claude-in-Chrome, not Playwright. The manifest note on the surface records this explicitly.

Freshness contract & wiring IMPL

  • This page's sources glob in public/wiki-manifest.json is src/data/ia-manifest.ts. When the manifest changes and this page does not, npm run check:wiki-freshness (advisory, chained in check:process) prints a warning — the mechanical form of the working agreement that a changed core surface updates its wiki page in the same PR.
  • Determinism & fail-soft (NFPs #3–#4): the manifest is a static data file and this page is static HTML — no runtime state, no PRNG, nothing to crash. Inspectability (NFP #2) is served by the in-game ?view=cms#ia-surfaces viewer and the per-surface reads[] tables rather than by traces.
