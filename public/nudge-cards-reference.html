The Nudge Card Library

Every choice the god can play into a mortal's moment — the types, the odds language, and the room left to grow

Doctrine (THR-883, 2026-07-30): prose does the scene, cards do the rules. The scene opening, stake block, and post-roll outcome prose carry the storytelling; each choice is a card — generic, reusable, mechanically plain. This page is the library's home surface: iterate the repertoire here before any content is authored against it. Spec authority: Docs/plans/2026-07-30-encounter-authoring-frameworks.md; engine substrate: THR-885.

  • Picture — one small painted image per library card, generic like the title. Manifest + fallback chain (type icon on a tinted band until painted); rides the encounter image library pattern.
  • Keyword + icon — the card's type, player-facing vocabulary learned once.
  • Title — two to four generic words, reusable everywhere. Interactive-plain: a label's job is to be unmistakable, so no metaphor here. Authored for every member since THR-1178.
  • Cost — essence pips, Free, or alternate-cost icons (detection, doom, obligation). A sphere-discounted card prints the effective price: the pips are what the commit path charges, never the authored number.
  • Effect — one plain mechanical sentence; odds shown in the pip vocabulary below.
  • Flavor quote — one short generic line in the serif voice. The card's only prose.

The odds pip vocabulary IMPL

Approved 2026-07-30, shipped in THR-890. Magnitude to the nearest ~5%: five pips per color tier, tier colors matching the attachment-rarity ladder, pip shape changes with tier so magnitude survives colorblindness — and shape escalation doubles as rarity feel. Stored values are raw numbers; the tiering is purely display (recorded on THR-885).

The ladder is twenty ~5% steps grouped into four tiers of five, so the tier index is floor((step − 1) / 5) and the filled count is ((step − 1) mod 5) + 1. Tier bounds and glyphs are named constants in src/data/nudge-pip-vocabulary.ts; the rows are drawn by the shared OddsPips / CostPips primitives, both registered in ?view=styleguide. Shape is the accessibility channel and color is secondary; each row carries its reading as an aria-label ("Strong, 3 of 5") because a screen reader spelling out geometric characters is worse than silence.

●●●○○  Faint — circles, +5 to +25 ■■□□□  Strong — squares, +30 to +50 ◆◇◇◇◇  Potent — diamonds, +55 to +75 ★★☆☆☆  Fated — stars, +80 to +100 ▼▼  Penalty — red down-triangles (costs, gambit downsides)

The step's own base forecast renders in the same vocabulary — one odds language across the whole encounter surface.

Test-panel factor lines carry a magnitude too (THR-892). Each line the panel draws now ships with a signed raw delta on EncounterStageFactorLineModel.delta, so a factor can be read in the same pip language as a card: the actor's Reach capability, each named world modifier (equipment, terrain, faction, sphere, conditions, divine attention), and any carryover from the previous step. The field is absent — never zero — on a line with no measurable contribution, because an empty pip row would promise a magnitude the line does not have. A contribution below FACTOR_LINE_EPSILON (0.005, one pip) draws no line at all. Carryover deltas ride the named-modifier channel as carryover:<outcome> alongside nudge:<id> and trait:<id>, and take no rng of their own.

How generic cards become specific — the binding model

  • Text slots — effect and flavor strings carry typed placeholders ({condition}, {host}, {target}) filled at deal time from the live encounter, through the same resolution as {cast:*} placeholders.
  • Target selectors — a card declares how to find its target, never the target itself: the agent's worst active condition, the owner of the place they're staying, the opposition slot in the scene's cast. Binding failure = the card is not dealt, so dealing is self-grounding: no Mend card where nothing needs mending.
  • Ascendant scaling — sphere discounts and (later) progression bonuses bend the effective numbers; the pips render the result. Same card, three pips for one god and four for another.

Hand-building doctrine

  • Hands are fully authored at encounter design time — 4–6 cards per step, picked from this library. No runtime generic deck; runtime only filters (trait, group, favor-availability, target binding).
  • No two cards in a hand answer the same question — at most one Boost per cost tier.
  • No two encounters in a family repeat a type composition — variety is audited by the coverage matrix (THR-884), not hoped for.
  • Supporting content ships with the card — a card granting an item, trait, ambition, or omen is only legal when that content exists; enforced by the grant-liveness build gate.

Every god holds the universal core (Boost, Insurance, Mercy, trait cards). Each of the 12 spheres owns signature types — primary sphere at full strength, secondary discounted (SECONDARY_SPHERE_DISCOUNT), off-sphere locked. First-cut mapping, iterated here:

The "⁺" is not decoration. order signs Insurance and energy signs Boost — types that are also universal core. The type is common; the signature member of it is not. Every god plays the core Insurance; only an order god plays order's. Access is read per member, never per type.

Hunger uniques: each hunger contributes exactly one unique starting card no other god holds — a Witness gets Seen (the mortal briefly knows a god watched); a Sever god holds the game's only mid-encounter bond-cutting card. A hunger unique ignores sphere entirely: it is identity, not alignment, so a chaos god still holds a Whisper-shaped Witness card that chaos does not sign.

Their two card types are read from the existing table, not minted: haunt's blessed mandate is to influence "through dreams, omens, and unseen presence", which is Compulsion almost verbatim; illuminate's is to "make the invisible visible" from a court that has "no shadows", so it takes Heavy hand — the one card whose defining trade is acting conspicuously, the exact inverse of Veil.

The run — broad start, progression as variation

  • Broad toolbox from turn one — every sphere-unlocked type is playable immediately, so hands are interesting from the first encounter.
  • Each type is a family that deepens — milestones (riding the existing progression card-grant machinery), god-earned traits (THR-791), and alignment add new member cards: same verb, different twist or cost channel. Almost nothing is strictly stronger — late-run hands contain cards an early-run god has never seen, but encounters stay decisions. Variation is the progression fantasy and the anti-boredom engine.
  • Pips as seasoning, not the arc — sphere alignment still discounts and occasionally sharpens a card, but power growth is deliberately minor.

Between runs — the echo card IMPL

At twilight the harvest preserves a fourth thing beside Legacy, Monument, and Relic: the card that defined the run — most played, ties broken by the most climactic moment it was played at — enters the World-Soul as a fourth EchoType and joins the next run's starting repertoire regardless of sphere. A triumphant age returns it whole; a somber one returns it scarred — cheaper by ECHO_CARD_SCAR_DISCOUNT, carrying an ECHO_CARD_SCAR_PENALTY red pip. A bittersweet age returns it whole: the scar is the price of a failed age, not a mixed one. Your dead gods leave you their favorite trick.

Selection is chosen, never rolled — no PRNG draw — and its final tie-break is card id, so a saved run replays to the same echo card. A card id that no longer exists in the library drops at world-seed with one warning and the rest of the harvest stands.

What "most storied" currently measures

The plan says the run's card is the most-played one, or the one played at the chronicle's biggest moment. The chronicle does not record which cards a beat was won with, so there is no direct signal to read. The tie-break uses doom-clock progress at the moment of play — the run's own tension curve, monotone and deterministic — as a first-cut proxy. Refine when the chronicle carries card provenance.

Card content status

The corpus is complete as of THR-1178 (2026-08-18): all 34 library members carry an authored title and quote. unauthoredCardCount() reads 0 and is pinned there by test, so adding a member without a face is a named CI failure rather than a card that silently deals as its own keyword.

Structure and content are separate tables in the same file, joined at assembly (CARD_CONTENT keyed by member id → withContent()). The universal-core and sphere-signature sections are generated from UNIVERSAL_CORE_TYPES and SPHERE_SIGNATURES, so hanging prose directly on the member literals would have meant hand-typing those members back out — the exact drift surface the generation exists to remove.

title/quote stay optional on the schema and cardDisplayTitle's keyword fallback stays live. That is deliberate: the fallback now serves the member added tomorrow before its face is written, and collapsing it would make adding a card a two-file edit whose half-done state renders blank instead of legible.

imageTag is absent on every member — the image library has no card rows to bind to, so each face falls back to its type's generic art. That is the documented chain, not a gap; minting card art slots is owned by THR-832/THR-1170.

Three rules govern the authored faces, and the test file enforces the mechanical half of each: genericity (a face must read wherever its type deals — scene grounding is the prose's job), sphere voice (a signature member reads as its sphere's mode of power; an omen card sounds like time, not like a recolored boost), and interactive-plain titles (2–4 words, unmistakable; the quote is the one element allowed a dry line).
