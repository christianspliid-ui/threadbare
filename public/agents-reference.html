Game Manual · The Living World

The Mortals

The world is full of people who are not yours. They wake with their own needs, chase their own ambitions, and become who their choices make them. You do not move them like pieces — you tend the ones you can reach, and watch the rest live.

People, not piecesEvery mortal is an individual with a life of their own. You are a god who nudges; you are never a puppeteer.

Threadbearer is not a world of tokens waiting for your command. It is populated by mortals — living individuals, each with a place on the map, a set of values, things they want, and the capacity to grow. They act whether or not you are watching. You touch their lives through threads and the occasional divine nudge, but the choices remain theirs. A mortal you shaped toward mercy can still choose cruelty; that friction — a god's will meeting a person's own nature — is where the story lives.

Because you cannot puppet them, understanding how a mortal chooses is how you learn to steer one. The rest of this page is that: how they decide, who they are, what they want, how they change, and how they live and die.

How a mortal choosesEach mortal weighs what they could do against who they are and what they need — survival first, then their values. The choosing is quiet arithmetic, never a visible die.

When a mortal is free to act, they look at what is within reach — the places, people, and unfolding moments around them — and weigh each one. The weighing runs in a fixed order of priority, a needs-first shape: a mortal in danger attends to survival before anything else, and only a mortal who is safe and settled spends their attention on ambition, mastery, or meaning. Above that floor, what tips the balance is who they are — their values pull them toward some acts and away from others.

Survival→ Safety→ Belonging→ Esteem→ Self-actualization→ Meaning

The choice is probabilistic, not mechanical. A mortal does not always take the single best option — the strongest pulls are the most likely, but there is room for the surprising choice, so two mortals with the same values in the same place will not march in lockstep. And crucially, doing nothing is always a valid choice for them too: when nothing in reach calls loudly enough, a mortal simply carries on — drifting toward a distant goal if they are restless, or staying close to home if they are content. Idleness is a person at peace, not a broken agent.

The same seed, the same world

None of this is random in the loose sense. Every weighing is deterministic — the same world, the same mortal, the same moment produces the same choice, every time. The "surprise" is a seeded roll, not true chance, so a run can be replayed exactly. What feels like free will is really a very consistent set of preferences meeting a very consistent world.

Who they are — values and their moral axisEvery mortal carries a signed reading on nine virtue-and-flaw scales. Eight are bound to the Eight Reaches; each Reach has a saint at one end and a monster at the other.

A mortal's character is not a bag of stats but a set of continuums — nine paired values, each running from a virtue at one pole to its shadow at the other. Eight of the nine belong to the Eight Reaches: the same axis that describes what a mortal does also describes the moral shape of how they do it. A ninth, courage against caution, sits across them all. A mortal sits somewhere along each scale, and where they sit is who they are.

Neither pole is "bad." A Conqueror is not a villain and a Protector is not a hero — they are two honest ways of holding the Iron Reach, and the world needs both. When you read a mortal's dossier, these scales are the truth of them, and they are what a wise god learns to work with rather than against.

How they change — the slow tiltA mortal is not fixed. Every meaningful choice tilts them a little along their Reach's axis, and enough tilts in one direction crystallize into a named trait.

Character is set at birth — seeded from the world's own logic and the mortal's origin story — but it is not frozen. Each meaningful choice a mortal makes nudges them along the matching axis: choose ruthlessness often enough and a mortal drifts toward the Conqueror pole; choose mercy and they drift back. Most of these nudges are small and fade — a single cruel act does not remake a soul, and drift decays back toward who a mortal fundamentally is. But a rare, defining moment can leave a formative mark: a permanent shift in the baseline, the kind of experience a person never quite returns from.

When a mortal drifts far enough along an axis and stays there, the tendency crystallizes into a visible emergent trait — the world stops describing them as "leaning ruthless" and starts calling them, plainly, ruthless. The trait sticks until they drift well back the other way, so a mortal's reputation lags their behavior slightly, the way a real reputation does. This is the engine of moral drift: not a slider you push, but a life accumulating into a character.

Your hand on the tiller

This is where a god's influence actually bites. You rarely command a mortal outright — but every encounter you steer, every choice you tilt, adds one more small pull in a direction. Tend a thread patiently and you can, over many turns, bend a mortal toward the person you need them to be — or watch, helpless and fascinated, as they become someone else entirely.

What they want — ambitionsBeyond the moment, most mortals carry a long arc they are quietly working toward. It colours everything they choose.

A mortal is not only reacting to the day in front of them; many carry an ambition — a long-term goal that gives their small choices a direction. A mortal might hold one or two of these at once, or none at all, and each falls into one of a handful of shapes:

Dominion

To rule — to hold power over people and place.

Mastery

To become the finest at their craft or Reach.

Vengeance

To answer a wrong done to them or theirs.

Legacy

To leave something that outlasts them.

Survival

Simply to endure — to see another season.

Discovery

To find what is hidden — knowledge, ruin, truth.

Devotion

To serve a person, a faith, or a cause utterly.

An ambition is not a quest marker for you to complete — it is theirs. It advances through milestones as the mortal lives toward it, and it quietly weights their choices: a mortal chasing Mastery leans harder into the work of their Reach; one bent on Vengeance is drawn toward the object of it. Ambitions can be fulfilled, abandoned when circumstances turn, or dashed. Knowing what a mortal is reaching for is the single best clue to what they will do next — and the easiest lever a god has, because helping a mortal toward their own ambition costs you far less resistance than pushing them off it.

IMPL Some ambitions ask for a qualification before a mortal may hold them at all — Forge a Legendary Weapon is only open to a master smith. That qualification can be earned or carried: since 2026-07-26 (THR-737) an item that grants a capability satisfies the requirement exactly as an owned trait does, so a smith who takes up The Worldforge Anvil becomes eligible for the ambition the anvil implies, and loses that eligibility with the anvil. The same rule governs the other two places the world asks whether a mortal has a capability — which encounters they may be offered, and which spells they may cast.

IMPL Ambitions are not only born with a mortal — the world mints them. When a mortal suffers or witnesses a world event (a raid, a blight, a collapse of fortunes, a numinous wonder) their recent history can write a new want into a free ambition slot: the victim of bloodshed turns toward vengeance or flight, a witness toward protecting or rebuilding their home. The event supplies candidates; the mortal's own personality still decides which takes — a craven soul flees where a proud one avenges the same ruin. A minted want carries the memory of its origin, so when it later steers a choice the motive receipt can say so aloud: "she seeks vengeance for the bloodshed at Thornhaven."

IMPL Not every ambition ends loudly. Some are simply outlived — since 2026-07-28 (THR-822) the world remembers where each mortal came from and how long they have stayed where they are, and two ambitions now end on that alone. A refugee fleeing a ruined country who stops somewhere and stays for six days is no longer fleeing; an exile sworn to retake a homeland who puts down roots elsewhere for ten days has, without ever deciding to, accepted the exile. Neither counts as failure. They are what happens when a life quietly answers a question the mortal had stopped asking — and if you want a mortal to keep their oath, the lever is to keep them moving.

How they grow — skill and mentorshipMortals get better at what they do by doing it, and the best of them pass their craft to the next generation.

A mortal's Domain Capability — their competence across the Reaches — is not fixed at birth either. It grows through doing: every encounter a mortal survives leaves them a little more capable, with harder trials teaching more than easy ones, and each Reach getting harder to improve the higher they climb. Failure still teaches — a botched attempt yields a fraction of the growth of a success — so a mortal's story of getting better is written in their scars as much as their triumphs.

Growth also passes between people. A mortal of real mastery can take an apprentice, forming a bond that plays out over many turns and ends in one of several ways — and how it ends is a story in itself:

Graduation

The bond held; the apprentice steps out competent and grateful.

The Surpassing

The student outgrows the master — pride and loss in one breath.

Falling Out

The bond soured; they part as rivals, sometimes as enemies.

Quiet Parting

Neither proud nor bitter — a professional bond that simply ran its course.

Dissolution

Circumstance — death, exile, distance — ended it before it could resolve.

A god can intervene here too: bless a fraying bond, or sever one with divine will. Mentorship is one of the clearest places to watch a lineage form across a run — a master's values and craft flowing down into an apprentice who will one day teach their own.

Birth, death, and becoming namedMortals are born, age, and die. And some — through their own deeds or your attention — rise from the background to become people the world tells stories about.

The population lives and turns over. New mortals are born where people gather thickly enough to sustain them, and mortals die — most quietly, when their fortunes have fallen far enough, some in the course of the world's violence. Death is not always an ending: a mortal bound closely enough to a god can be preserved as an echo rather than simply lost.

Not every mortal is simulated in full. The world holds far more people than any run could animate, so they exist at three tiers of attention:

Ambient→ Notable→ Spotlight
  • Ambient — the background population. Real enough to fill a settlement, but not individually simulated: they have no personal goals of their own yet.
  • Notable — a named person the world is tracking, worth interacting with, carrying a genuine identity and capabilities.
  • Spotlight — a fully living individual: their own values, ambitions, and autonomous choices, run every turn. These are the mortals this whole page describes.

A background face graduates upward as they accumulate importance — and importance is earned by mattering. Every time you act on a mortal directly, every time they are named in a moment, gain a defining trait, or become the centre of a contested place, they matter a little more — until a nobody becomes a somebody the game fully brings to life. You can also spend divine essence to elevate a mortal deliberately, pulling a face out of the crowd because you have decided they are important. This is exactly how The First begins — an ordinary person your attention raised into the anchor of your whole presence in the world.

The lesson of the tiers

You will never watch everyone, and you are not meant to. The tier system is the world's honest answer to a god's limited attention: it keeps thousands of lives plausible in the background and spends its real simulation on the handful you have chosen to care about. Choosing who to raise into the light is one of the quiet, load-bearing decisions of a run.

How a mortal decides IMPL

Two pipelines drive mortal behaviour, and it is worth keeping them distinct:

  • Per-tick encounter selection — phaseAgentDecision.ts uses encounterScoring.ts (scoreAndSelect) to pick which framed moment a free mortal engages. Candidates are spatially pre-filtered by hex awareness (MAX_AWARENESS_HOPS = 5 + EDGE_HEX_AWARENESS_BONUS = 1), capped at MAX_SCORED_CANDIDATES = 40 (of which PERSONAL_OFFER_CAP_RESERVE = 6 are held back for entries a generator built for this mortal — their guild membership, their rank — which are appended after the shared location cache and would otherwise never survive the cap; THR-814), scored (valuePerTick × desireMultiplier + resonance, with axiological amplification PERSONALITY_SELECTION_WEIGHT = 2.0 / PERSONALITY_SCORE_EXPONENT = 1.5), and anything below IDLE_SCORE_THRESHOLD = 0.0001 falls through to idleBehavior.ts.
  • Action-completion selection — when an actor's action frees up their AP, agentSelection.ts (header: "Maslow-inspired pipeline") scores candidates by axiological alignment (scoreByGoalAlignment), applies ambition/personality weights, takes top-N, and does a seeded weighted random pick (probabilisticSelect). Its SELECTION_CONFIG carries survivalThreshold: 0.8 (unifiedActionPhases.ts:38) — the survival override that makes a threatened mortal drop everything.
Accuracy note — "six-layer Maslow" is framing, not code

The rulebook narrates decision-making as a "six-layer Maslow pipeline (survival → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualization → transcendence)." The player layer above uses that framing because it is a faithful description of the felt behaviour — but the code implements a Maslow-inspired shape (a survival override at 0.8 plus axiological-alignment scoring), not six discretely-named tiers. The named layers safety/belonging/esteem/self-actualization appear only in prose content, never in the decision loop. Utility-function AI and behaviour trees are the rejected approaches and are correctly absent. Determinism (NFP #3) holds: scoring is pure, and the "surprise" is a seeded roll (a deprecated Math.random() fallback exists in agentSelection.ts but all production callers pass a seeded roll).

Personality & moral drift IMPL

  • The nine value pairs — AxiologicalProfile is Record<ValuePair, number> on −1.0 (flaw pole) … +1.0 (virtue pole), src/types/agent.ts. Eight Reach-bound pairs (mercy_ruthlessness Iron, asceticism_extravagance Gold, honesty_cunning Shadow, tradition_novelty Veil, loyalty_ambition Heart, revelation_discretion Eye, preservation_transformation Stone, sacrifice_survival Star) plus the meta pair courage_prudence. Archetype pole names live in ARCHETYPE_NAMES.
  • The shipped personality wave — origin baseline seeded once at birth (THR-561, phases/personalityOriginSeed.ts: deterministic hash + additive origin-vignette deltas recorded on originVignettes); emergent traits granted/released at hysteresis thresholds off live position clamp(baseline + drift) (THR-527, phases/personalityTraitEmerge.ts); and the separate five-continuum Core layer (THR-542, phases/corePersonality.ts).
  • Drift — choices apply signed drift to the dominant axis (PERSONALITY_REACTION_DRIFT_DELTA = 0.05); rare defining moments write a permanent baseline shift up to FORMATIVE_MARK_MAX_MAGNITUDE = 0.15; ordinary drift decays back toward baseline (encounters/driftAccumulator.ts). The lagging emergent trait is why reputation trails behaviour.

Ambitions IMPL

  • Seven categories (src/types/ambition.ts): dominion | mastery | vengeance | legacy | survival | discovery | devotion. Assigned 0–2 per mortal at birth (1 primary + 0–1 secondary) via a seeded selection funnel (ambitionAssignment.ts, ambitionSelection.ts), stored as pursues edges with priority/status/completedMilestones.
  • DESIGN A god can now kindle an ambition directly (THR-885) — assignAmbitionToActor (ambitionAssignment.ts) is the graph-writing helper extracted from the three in-phase copies of the find-or-create-node-then-write-pursues-edge sequence that lived in ambitionTick and agentLifecycle. That duplication was the bug: because the write existed only inside those phases, nothing outside the tick loop could assign an ambition at all, which is why reactive ambition templates had no dispatcher and sat unreachable (THR-812 / THR-726). The new assign_ambition aftermath effect — The Kindled Ambition nudge card — routes through the same helper, so a card-planted ambition is byte-identical on the graph to a world-minted one. It respects the same two-active-slot cap, refuses a duplicate, and fails soft on a retired template id rather than throwing. Reads DESIGN because no shipped card authors the grant yet; content lands under THR-883.
  • Progress checked each tick (phases/ambitionProgress.ts, ambitionTick.ts) against milestone graph conditions; active ambitions bias encounter scoring by AMBITION_REACH_BOOST = 0.5 when the encounter's reach matches. Ambitions complete, abandon (on failed triggers), or persist.
  • Milestone & abandonment vocabulary — both are GraphConditions (src/types/ambition.ts), evaluated by one pure function (graphConditions.ts) that fails soft to false on missing data: reach above/below a threshold, bond count by basis, controlling a location type, in/not-in the mortal's origin region, holding or lacking a trait, agent_deceased, target_agent_eliminated, and the two durational residence conditions below. (The region-literal pair still exists for callers holding a runtime-captured region id, but is not authorable — see the region bullet below.) Abandonment is checked before milestones and takes priority, so a trigger that fires ends the ambition even on a tick that would otherwise have completed it.
  • IMPL The founder's death ends the dynasty (THR-808) — Found a Dynasty abandons when its pursuer dies. It previously gated on a trait named living that no producer has ever minted, which left the ambition with no reachable abandonment path at all; aliveness is engine state (properties.deceased), not a trait, and is now read directly.
  • IMPL A mortal who stops moving has answered the question (THR-822) — two more ambitions gained reachable endings, and both end quietly rather than in failure. Flee the Ravaged Land abandons when its bearer stays in one place for six days after taking it up: the road ended, and they stayed. Reclaim the Homeland abandons when its bearer has been rooted somewhere that is not where they began for ten days: home became a memory, and the memory became enough. Both had gated on trait keys nothing mints; the concept was never a trait but a fact about where a mortal lives.
    The engine now records, for every mortal, where they originated and how long they have held their current position — agentResidence.ts, exposed as window.__DEBUG.getAgentResidence(name). It is observed rather than written by the two dozen movement writers: phaseAmbitionProgress already walks every mortal each interval, so it compares each one's position against the last recorded and restamps on a change. Any mover is covered, including ones not written yet.
    Crucially, dwell is measured from the later of arrival and the ambition's own assignment. That is what makes a "has stayed put" trigger safe: it cannot fire before assignedTick + minTicks however long the mortal was already sitting still, so an ambition can never abandon on the tick it is taken up. One ambition still has no reachable ending — protect kin, whose trigger names a loss of bonds rather than a place, and so waits on loss history the world does not record (THR-813).
  • IMPL "Home" is a fact about the mortal, not a place name (THR-841) — two milestones that could never fire now can, and one ambition became completable at all. The region conditions compared an authored literal against location.properties.regionId, a property nothing in the engine has ever written — measured at zero distinct values across all 727 locations of a live run — so both were false for every mortal in every world. Region membership is a contains edge from a region node, which is what they read now; a sublocation climbs to the location that carries it.
    Fixing the read changed nothing by itself, and that is the more useful half. Regions are minted per world (region_0…region_N, named from whichever historical culture held them), so a literal an author types can never match one: Reclaim the Homeland waited on a region called homeland and Escape a Cursed Land on one called cursed, and neither will ever exist. Both now read the mortal's own origin — the same residence the bullet above observes, one tier coarser: back in the country you came from rather than standing on the exact spot you left. Nothing for an author to know in advance, and it works in any world.
    Escape a Cursed Land is the one that mattered: it requires both its milestones, so a permanently-false one made the whole ambition unachievable for every mortal in every world — a mortal could take it up and pursue it forever. Reclaim the Homeland needs two of three and stayed completable, but the beat it is named for — the old soil underfoot again — could never fire. A test now fails any template that gates on a region literal, naming the site: unlike trait refs these had no reconciliation sweep, so a new dead one could have been authored and nothing would have said so.

Growth & mentorship IMPL

  • Capability growth (capabilityGrowth.ts) accrues from encounter steps: BASE_ENCOUNTER_GROWTH = 0.5, doubled on promotion-eligible steps (PROMOTION_ELIGIBLE_MULTIPLIER = 2.0), scaled by step difficulty, with diminishing returns 1 − cap × DIMINISHING_RETURNS_FACTOR (0.7) (floored at 0.1); failure still yields FAILURE_GROWTH_FRACTION = 0.2. Tiers 0–8 map from the score in domainCapability.ts.
  • Mentorship (THR-75, phaseMentorship.ts / mentorshipOutcomes.ts, constants in src/data/mentorship-constants.ts): mentor tier ≥ MENTOR_MIN_TIER (6), apprentice 2–4; a mentors edge carries bondQuality ∈ [−1,+1] drifting +0.15/−0.20 per checkpoint over MENTORSHIP_BASE_DURATION 8 ± 3 ticks. Terminal arcs: Graduation/The Surpassing (bond ≥ GRADUATION_BOND_THRESHOLD 0.2), Falling Out (< FALLING_OUT_BOND_THRESHOLD −0.3), Quiet Parting, Dissolution. The Sever-the-Bond divine action floors bondQuality.

Lifecycle & spotlight graduation IMPL

  • Birth/death (agentLifecycle.ts): BIRTH_CHANCE = 0.01 per tick at locations with ≥ BIRTH_DENSITY_THRESHOLD (3) agents; low-reputation death DEATH_CHANCE_LOW_REP = 0.02 below LOW_REP_THRESHOLD (0.1). Aspect echoes preserve a god's Aspect on death (THR-479).
  • Three tiers (SpotlightTier in src/types/npc.ts): ambient (no individual AI) → notable (named, tracked) → spotlight (full autonomous agent). Promotion by importance: NOTABLE_THRESHOLD = 10, SPOTLIGHT_THRESHOLD = 25 AND SPOTLIGHT_MIN_EDGES = 3. Importance increments: player action +3, trait gained +4, edge created +2, encounter reference +1, location contested +1 (npcGraduation.ts). Player-driven promotion costs max(1, PROMOTE_ESSENCE_BASE 8 − importance × PROMOTE_IMPORTANCE_DISCOUNT 0.5) essence.

Broken mortals — scoring and candidacy DESIGN

  • A mortal worn past BROKEN_ENTER_STATE ('critical') enters the Broken state (THR-773, isBrokenMortal in src/engine/brokenState.ts) — out of the story rather than dead. Derived from the live quintessence ratio on every read; the single stored property is brokenSince, which carries the hysteresis (release at BROKEN_EXIT_STATE = 'strained') so nobody flickers across the boundary tick by tick.
  • Two effects on agent behaviour, both currently inert. (1) filterByPrerequisites (encounterFilterPipeline.ts) excludes a broken agent from every candidate except templates flagged drawableWhileBroken — resolved once per agent, not per entry, since this pipeline runs for every agent every tick. (2) scoreAndSelect gains an additive broken_drift pull (computeBrokenDriftBonus) that decays with hex distance and favours settlement subtypes, so a broken mortal stops ranging and drifts toward tended ground. It reuses the already-resolved locationNode — no extra graph walk per candidate.
  • BROKEN_GATE_ENABLED ships false, so both are wired and tested but contribute nothing today: computeBrokenDriftBonus returns 0 and the candidacy filter passes everything, recovering the pre-THR-773 score term for term. Flipping it is a WS5 Done-when gated on the rebuild encounters existing — excluding an agent from all candidacy with nothing to draw is stun-lock, not story. The brokenSince bookkeeping and the transition traces run regardless, so telemetry accrues first.
  • Reconciled inside phaseQuintessence's existing actor walk (no second pass). It takes a node id, not a node reference — WorldGraph.updateNode replaces the map entry rather than mutating it, so a handle taken before the phase's own regen write is stale and a write through it is silently dropped. Traces agent_broken / agent_mended fire on transition only.
  • Measure live: await window.__DEBUG.getBrokenAgents() — returns brokenShare and per-agent ticksBroken alongside gateEnabled. The design's pacing falsifier is >5% of living mortals simultaneously broken, or median ticks-broken outside 84–168 (1–2 game weeks).

Why an agent is here — motive classification IMPL

  • classifyMotive (src/engine/encounters/motiveClassifier.ts, THR-773) is a pure read over the THR-631 motive receipt the scorer already writes — it adds no scoring term, mutates nothing, and takes no rng draw. It answers "why is this mortal here?" with one of divine | mission | choice | chance.
  • Order is deliberate, not a tie-break convenience: player/seed provenance wins outright (a god's hand is the loudest fact about a scene even when the mortal also wanted it); then whichever of assigned-work (ambition/chain/reputation/bond) or self-scored appetite (personality/resonance/doom_identity/exploration/rarity) clears MOTIVE_DOMINANT_SHARE. Fail-soft to 'chance': a missing receipt, an empty contribution list, or nothing dominant all classify as chance rather than guessing.
  • Consumed by the WS2 encounter header (THR-775) and exposed via __DEBUG.

Decision-phase performance IMPL instrumentation · OPEN fix

  • Standing optimizations in phaseAgentDecision.ts are shipped: a pre-computed busy-agent Set for O(1) lookup, moving-agent re-evaluation gating (only re-decide every DECISION_REEVALUATION_TICKS), hex-spatial pre-filtering before scoring, and per-location incremental encounter-cache invalidation.
  • THR-580 shipped the tick-loop observability layer (per-phase timing ring + rollup, orchestrator.ts, DebugPanel Phases tab) and the diagnosis that phaseAgentDecision cost goes super-linear on large+ maps. THR-581 (the fix) is OPEN — no commit lands it on any branch yet; the large-map decision stall is a known, tracked performance issue, not a resolved one. (This is why CLAUDE.md steers dev testing to ?view=game&seeded&size=medium.)
