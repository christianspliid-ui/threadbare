Livelihoods & the Resource Web

Every place lives on what its ground can give. A granary full or a granary empty, a road of cheap iron or a famine of grain — the world's livelihoods are visible, and when they turn, your bonded mortals feel it.

The land has a livelihood, and it showsLook at a place and you are told, in plain words, how its people are eating and working — not in numbers, but in the state of its granaries, its yards, its stalls.

Every settled place holds the resources its terrain affords — grain and grazing in the lowlands, timber in the forests, stone and ore in the mountains, fish and water along the rivers and coasts. Open a location and you will find its Livelihood written out: a place with more than it needs reads as full and easy, a place scraping by reads as thin and watchful. You never see a stock level or a price. You see a granary described as overflowing or a fishing town whose nets come up light.

What decides the reading is simple: how much the ground gives, against how much the people need. A thin harvest in a crowded city is a crisis; the same harvest in an empty valley is a surplus. Big, prosperous places eat far more than sparse ones, so a resource that is plentiful in the wild can still run short where the crowds are.

ScarceToo little to go around. Stores run low, prices climb, and the poor go without first. For a staple, this is a Famine.
AdequateEnough, most seasons. The unremarkable middle — the tables are set and the yards keep working.
SurplusMore than can be used. Plenty spills into the markets and worth collapses. For a staple, this is a Glut.

Famine and Glut — when a staple turnsFood and water are what a people live on. When a staple crosses into scarcity or drowns in surplus, it carries a name and a weight the rest of the economy does not.

Two words in the world's reports carry real force. Famine is a staple — grain, grazing, fish, water — fallen into scarcity: hungry people, and hunger breeds desperation and trouble. Glut is the opposite ruin: a staple so abundant its worth collapses, and those who traded in it are undone. Both are turning points, not background texture — a place in Famine is a place where things are about to go wrong, and a place in Glut is a place where a fortune just evaporated.

When a staple crosses one of these lines, the world writes it into the Great Chronicle — "A Scarcity Bites", "A Glut Descends" — so the turn of a livelihood becomes part of the story of a place, the same way a settlement's rise or a road's death does.

Scarcity and surplus feed prosperity

A place that meets its needs prospers; a place in shortage or drowning in unsellable surplus does not. The balance of a settlement's livelihoods is one of the forces that lifts or drags its prosperity over time — so a famine is not just grim flavour, it is a weight on the whole place's fortunes.

Your people feel it — the livelihood tugThe economy is not a spreadsheet you audit. It reaches into your turn through the mortals you are bound to.

When one of your threaded mortals — a soul you have reached down and bonded — lives in a settlement that falls into Famine or rises into Glut, the thread tugs. It joins the same pull the attention system gives you for a mortal facing a dangerous encounter: a signal that here, now, one of your own is caught in something worth your notice. A famine tugs hard; a glut, more softly. You are never forced to act — but the world has told you that your bonded farmer's village is starving, and left the choice in your hands.

The economy in your hands

This is the point of an activated economy: it is not a menu of taxes and tariffs, it is a set of pressures your people live inside. The livelihood tug puts the world's livelihoods into your turn loop from the first — so a shortage is something you feel through the thread, not a number you go looking for.

Resources have a cosmic grainWhat a land produces is not just wealth — it is a leaning toward one of the world's Spheres, so an economy is legible as cosmology.

Every kind of resource belongs to a class and leans toward a Sphere. Grain and grazing and fish and water are staples, aligned with Life — the things a people must have. Timber, stone, ore, and peat are strategic goods, the materials of building and war. Gems, pearls, dyes, and rare herbs are luxuries — high in worth, low in need, quick to glut. And the rarest finds — arcane crystal, star-metal, ancient relics, sunken gold — are arcane goods, worth the most and consumed the least. A valley of grain leans Life; a corridor of war-iron leans Matter and Force. The map's livelihoods and the map's cosmology are the same picture read two ways.

Staple · Life

Grain, grazing, fish, water. Eaten by everyone; the Famine and Glut drivers.

Strategic · Matter / Force

Timber, stone, ore, peat. Building and war materials, moderately consumed.

Luxury · Mind / Spirit

Gems, pearls, herbs, glowcap. High worth, low need, glut-prone.

Arcane · Time / Energy

Crystal, star-metal, relics, sunken gold. Rarest and most valuable; barely consumed.

Honest scope

The resource web is activated and felt: livelihoods read as prose, staples swing into Famine and Glut, your threaded mortals tug when their home turns, the balance feeds prosperity, and the goods of a Sphere now sustain the holy ground that shares it. The five divine economic verbs are in your hand. Trade caravans carrying named cargo, banditry on the iron road, and factions seizing a resource by the throat are coming — but they are not here yet. This page describes what lives today.

The land feeds the holy groundA shrine is not fed by faith alone. What a place grows of its Sphere sustains the source of power standing in it — and when the land turns thin, the holy ground thins with it.

Once you have found and claimed a source of essence and given it a Sphere, it stops being independent of the land around it. Each turn, the source reads the goods of its own Sphere at the place it stands: a shrine to Spirit looks to the pearls of its valley, a nexus of Matter to the ore and stone beneath it, a Life site to the grain and grazing around it. Goods running to surplus nurture the source. Goods gone scarce wither it. Where a place grows nothing of that Sphere, the bargain simply does not exist and nothing moves.

You see this in the same Livelihood line you already read — one more sentence, saying what the land is doing to the holy thing standing in it. No numbers, and nothing at all until you have found the source; an undiscovered source keeps its silence like everything else under the fog.

The land nurtures

Goods of the source's Sphere run to surplus. Sanctity climbs, slowly.

The land is even

The goods hold steady, or the place grows nothing of that Sphere. Nothing moves.

The land withers

Goods of the Sphere have gone scarce. Sanctity falls — as far as zero, if you let it.

The land readies; only you can make it flower

A generous valley will carry a source part of the way and then stop, short of Flowering. Getting it the rest of the way is your work — the Sanctify verb, cast by your own hand. Ruin runs the other way with no such courtesy: a starving land will empty a source completely, and a rival bleeding it at the same time takes both cuts in the same turn. The world gives less than it can take away, which is exactly why tending and defending a source is worth your attention.

Designer Notes

Resources as location properties, the stock-tier derivation phase and its supply-vs-demand model, the resource-class table with sphere affinities, the additive prosperity balance term, the Famine/Glut chronicle triggers, and the livelihood thread tug — with honest IMPL/DESIGN status.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). The systems-spec authority for this page is Docs/plans/2026-07-04-mortal-economy-resource-web.md (Mortal Economy — The Resource Web, Phase 1). The companion page Settlements, Prosperity & Trade owns settlements, the prosperity equilibrium model, trade routes, and carrying capacity. Honest UL note: there is still no dedicated economic UL shard — Livelihood, Stock Tier, Famine, Glut are code-authoritative until one is minted (the same known gap the settlements page records).

Resources are location properties, not nodes IMPL

  • Despite a resource node type existing in the schema, resources live as a Record<string, ResourceInstance> on location-node properties (src/types/resource.ts). Each instance is { quantity 0–100, renewable, renewalRate, stockTier? }. seedLocationResources (resourceSeeding.ts) populates them from TERRAIN_RESOURCE_TABLE at world-gen. THR-615 added the optional stockTier field (additive; absent → treated as adequate).

Stock-tier derivation IMPL

  • phaseResourceStockTiers(state, ctx) (src/engine/phases/resourceStockTiers.ts) runs in the declarative registry's pre-economy slot, ordered after reputation_decay and before the inline economy block — so phaseProsperity reads a fresh balance the same tick.
  • The pure derivation lives in resourceEconomy.ts. Local demand is a population proxy: DEMAND_BASE 0.15 + (prosperity/100)·DEMAND_PROSPERITY_WEIGHT 0.35 + min(sublocations/DEMAND_SUBLOCATION_CAP 6, 1)·DEMAND_SUBLOCATION_WEIGHT 0.30, clamped to [0,1]. Per resource, balance = clamp(quantity/100 − demand·scarcitySensitivity, −1, 1); the tier is scarce at ≤ STOCK_SCARCE_THRESHOLD −0.3, surplus at ≥ STOCK_SURPLUS_THRESHOLD +0.4, else adequate. Deterministic; no PRNG.
  • The location's aggregate resourceBalance (base-value-weighted mean of per-resource balances, ∈[-1,1]) is stored on the node for prosperity and the DebugPanel. Tier changes call touchWorld(runtime) (they shift encounter-scoring inputs, per the locationSubtype precedent).
  • Spotlight cap: locations are sorted prosperity-desc then id, and only the first ECON_PHASE_SPOTLIGHT_CAP 40 get full-detail work (crossing traces, chronicle, tugs). Beyond the cap, tiers and balance still update (coarse) — never skipped silently. First-derivation is silent: a resource crossing from no-tier to any tier emits no trace or chronicle, avoiding a tick-1 burst that would flood the 2000-entry trace ring; only genuine tier changes narrate.

Resource classes IMPL

  • src/data/resource-classes.ts keys 18 resource types to a ResourceClass { category, primarySphere, baseValue, scarcitySensitivity }. Categories: staple (Life food/water, high sensitivity, Famine/Glut drivers), strategic (Matter/Force building & war materials), luxury (glut-prone trade goods), arcane (rare, high value, barely consumed). primarySphere mirrors RESOURCE_DEFINITIONS[id].sphereAffinities[0] — a consistency test asserts they never drift.

Prosperity coupling IMPL

  • computeEquilibriumTarget in phaseProsperity.ts adds resourceBalance · RESOURCE_BALANCE_PROSPERITY_WEIGHT 0.15 · 100 (±15 max) to the settlement's equilibrium target, read via readLocationResourceBalance. It is an additive term on top of carrying capacity — capacity is the ceiling the land affords, the balance term is the surplus/scarcity nudge; they are distinct signals. Both resourceBalance and resourceBalanceBonus are attributable in the prosperity_tick trace breakdown.

Chronicle — Famine & Glut IMPL

  • Two triggers were added to economicChronicle.ts / economic-chronicle-content.ts: resource_scarcity ("A Scarcity Bites", significance 0.7) and resource_glut ("A Glut Descends", 0.5), each with four baseline-register templates using the existing {settlement}/{resource} placeholders. The phase resolves an entry only on a genuine crossing of a staple or strategic resource with baseValue ≥ CHRONICLE_MIN_BASE_VALUE 0.6 — keeping luxury noise out of the Great Chronicle.

Livelihood thread tug IMPL

  • When a bonded, non-dormant mortal's home settlement holds a staple in scarce or surplus, the phase pushes a ThreadTug (reach gold; threat hard for Famine, moderate for Glut) onto state.activeThreadTugs, riding the existing attention machinery (phaseAttention preserves it; useAgentInteraction attends it — it resolves by agentId, so no backing encounter is needed). Residents are grouped by their home resourced location, walking sublocation → parent so a mortal in a settlement's inn still counts. State-based and deduped by a ${agentId}::${resourceId} key carried on the tug, so a persistent famine tugs once, not every tick.

The essence bridge — economy → divine economy IMPL

  • What it is. The P4 coupling promised by the mortal-economy plan ("temple economies; bridges to essence") and left open by the divine-economy plan. essenceEconomyBridge.ts derives a signed sanctity drift per controlled source per tick: resolve the host to its economic location (a sublocation walks one parentLocationId hop), take the goods whose primarySphere matches the source's sphereAffinity, score their stock tiers (surplus +1 / adequate 0 / scarce −1) weighted by class baseValue, and multiply by ECON_SANCTITY_DRIFT_PER_TICK. Applied inside the existing recomputeControlledSourceTiers walk — one pass, no new phase, no second income channel.
  • The two asymmetries are the design, not an implementation detail. Upward drift stops at ECON_SANCTITY_NURTURE_CEILING (0.5), deliberately below SANCTITY_FLOWERING_THRESHOLD (0.6): the land readies a source, only a Build/Sanctify cast flowers it. Downward drift has no floor above zero, and stacks with the contested drain in the same tick. Both are asserted as contract tests in essenceEconomyBridge.test.ts.
  • Untyped sources are excluded by construction — a migrated, unbuilt place of power has no sphereAffinity, so it draws no drift and stays on the legacy alignment-distributed income term. A save that has done nothing keeps exactly its old income (NFP #6); the contract test pins it over 100 ticks on a doubly-surplus location.
  • Inspection. The aggregate essence_source_phase trace gains econNurtured/econWithered counts (still one trace per tick, never per source). __DEBUG.getEssenceSources() returns a per-source sustenance block — matched goods, affinity score, next-tick drift, and a reason naming why an inert source is inert (untyped / no-host / no-matching-goods / ceiling), so drawing-nothing is distinguishable from drawing-zero.

UI & inspection IMPL

  • Livelihood line — LocationView.tsx renders a prose-only Livelihood section from the location's resource tiers (scarce/surplus first, else the dominant resource's steady prose), with Famine/Glut as interactive in-prose keywords (renderProseWithIPK extended with an economic-keyword branch; tooltips in ECONOMY_KEYWORD_TOOLTIPS). When a discovered, sphere-typed, non-desecrated source draws on this location's larder, one sustenance sentence is appended from SUSTENANCE_PROSE (per-Sphere × nurturing/steady/withering).

    The source need not be the location's own (THR-840). A shrine or rite borne by a sublocation eats from this same larder, because resolveEconomicHost walks it up one parentLocationId hop — and until THR-840 the engine drifted those every tick while this line said nothing, leaving two of the five source kinds coupled invisibly. A sublocation has no Livelihood line of its own to fall back to: it carries no resources bag, which is exactly why the engine walks up. selectLocationSustenanceVoice (in essenceSources.ts) now decides which source speaks. Every source here eats the same larder, so they are one bargain read through different Spheres rather than independent ones — which is why the line stays one sentence and never becomes a list:

    1. The place's own holy ground speaks first. A location carrying a source is describing its own sanctity, and that is its sentence by right — so this never changes a line that already rendered.
    2. Otherwise the loudest borrowed source speaks for it, by largest |affinityScore| whichever direction it pulls, because the clause exists to make the drift visible and the loudest drift is the one worth seeing.
    3. Ties break on host id, ascending, so the same world renders the same sentence every tick.

    The clause no longer mirrors the engine's guards — it calls computeSanctitySustenance and renders only when the engine returns no reason, so the sentence cannot drift away from the drift it describes. That also closed a gap the mirrored version had: at ECON_SANCTITY_NURTURE_CEILING the engine returns reason: 'ceiling' with zero drift — the land has stopped giving — where the old guards still described generosity. Three guards stay on the UI side, because they are about what the player may be told rather than what the land is doing: an undiscovered source stays silent (fog-consistent), a desecrated one is the drain line's sentence, and an untyped one has no Sphere to speak in.

  • Source drain line (THR-621) — a sibling panel rendered directly beneath the Livelihood line whenever the location's source is discovered and carries contestedBy. It names the rival and what the drain costs: "<Rival> has opened a drain on this source…", or, once desecrated, "…has profaned this source. Nothing here reaches you now." Prose-first per THR-609 — the tier word appears only as the panel's label, never as a number or a key:value pair in the sentence. Fog-consistent for the same reason the sustenance clause is: an undiscovered source stays silent. This is the surface that lets a player identify which rival is bleeding which holding without reading code; the hex map's rival-influence marker says a hex is under attack, this says who and how far along. rivalDefinitions reaches LocationView as an optional prop, so an absent roster degrades to "Something you cannot yet name" rather than dropping the panel.
  • DebugPanel Essence Sources tab — EssenceSourcesDebugTab.tsx gains a per-source land: line: polarity, the matched goods, and the drift (or the naming of why it is inert), colour-coded by polarity.
  • DebugPanel Economy tab — EconomyDebugTab.tsx lists the tier distribution and every resourced location's per-resource tiers, aggregate balance, and monopoly state, sorted most-scarce first (numbers shown here, debug-only).
  • Traces — resource_stock_tier_change per crossing (with the driving balance and whether a tug fired); prosperity_tick now carries resourceBalance/resourceBalanceBonus. Inspect headlessly via the CLI eval command.
