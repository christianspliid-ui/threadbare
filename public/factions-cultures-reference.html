Factions, Cultures & Secrets

The world is not a crowd of strangers. Mortals band into guilds and orders with designs of their own, inherit the gravity of the cultures they were born into, and carry private debts and buried truths. This is the social fabric a god learns to read — and to pull.

The world organizes itselfOver the individual mortals lie three social layers — factions, cultures, and loose groups — and each one bends the choices its members make.

Every mortal is a person in their own right, but almost none of them stand alone. They are organized: sworn into a guild, born into a culture, drawn into a passing band. These layers are not decoration — each one changes what its members want, who they trust, and how they act. A god who reads only individuals reads half the world. The other half is written in the shape of the groups they belong to.

You do not command these organizations any more than you command a single mortal. A faction is not a unit you move; it is a living body with its own ambitions, and the most you can do is earn standing within it, anoint it as favored, or set a crisis loose inside it and watch how it breaks. This page is how those layers work — and how a patient god turns them into levers.

Factions — organizations with their own designsA faction is a structured body with goals of its own. It recruits, ranks its members by their standing, pursues long ambitions, and acts on the world without waiting for you.

A faction is a guild, order, company, court, or criminal network — a social entity that exists independently of any one member. The world is seeded with a dozen of them, spanning five kinds: the martial (mercenary companies, a civic guard), the mercantile (a merchant consortium, a builders' fellowship), the devout (a temple, a holy order), the arcane and lettered (an arcane circle, a lorekeepers' covenant), the shadowed (a thieves' guild, an underking's court), and the broad adventuring and ranging guilds between.

Your relationship with a faction is reputation — a standing you build by serving its ends. Complete the quests it commissions and your standing rises; drift away and it decays over time. Standing is not a single number in the eyes of the faction: each one has its own rank ladder, and crossing a threshold promotes a member up it. A thieves' guild recruit climbs from pickpocket to footpad to shadowblade to guildmaster; each rung unlocks richer rewards and, at the top, a seat that only one can hold.

Standing runs both ways

Whether a mortal rises fast or slowly depends on whether they fit. A faction quietly favors members whose nature matches its character and disfavors those who cut against it — a thieves' guild rewards the shadowed and the greedy, and gives the honest a colder welcome. Reputation is a mirror of alignment as much as effort.

Factions act on their own

The load-bearing truth about factions is that they are not hand-scripted set dressing. Each one carries a long ambition — to expand its territory, to hoard resources, to fortify, to spread its culture, to avenge an old grievance, or to carry out a god's declared will — and that ambition colors everything it does. Driven by it, a faction takes real action in the world: commissioning quests, declaring rivalries, proposing alliances, excommunicating a member, calling a conclave, or posting a bounty. Which action it reaches for is shaded by the temperament of whoever leads it — a rash leader declares rivalries, a merciful one seeks alliances.

And leadership itself is mortal. When a faction's head dies or is cast down, succession resolves: a named heir inherits, or the highest-standing member rises to fill the seat. A faction can even splinter — a schism, once seeded, resolves into either a clean break (a breakaway faction carrying off its share of members) or a bitter reunification that expels the dissenters. All of this happens whether or not you are watching.

Anointing a chosen faction

You have one deeper move here. Through a domain-locked divine act you can anoint a faction as your chosen — and from then on, every one of its members gains standing a little faster each turn, the exact flavor of the blessing set by the Sphere it flows through. A god who lifts a faction into favor gives it a slow, structural advantage no amount of ordinary questing can match. Sowing a schism and anointing a champion are the two ends of the same power: you can raise a faction up, or set it against itself.

Cultures — the gravity of belongingA culture is a whole civilization's worth of shared values, names, and customs. It is less a thing a mortal joins than a pull they are born inside of.

Where a faction is a body one enlists in, a culture is a gravity well one is born into. Each culture has its own venerated Spheres, its leanings across the Eight Reaches, and a deep foundational cast — and from those it grows a full identity: generated names that sound like they belong together, its own customs and mores, a banner, and a texture the world describes it in. A mortal carries their culture the way they carry their homeland — it shapes what they revere and how they are seen far more than it dictates any single act.

Cultures relate to one another by gravity — a measure of how alike or opposed two of them are, drawn from their Spheres, their Reaches, and their foundations. Kindred cultures share a pull; opposed ones repel. And where cultures meet unevenly, tension surfaces — not as a war mechanic, but as narrative texture that colors the encounters between people who do not share a world:

Mismatch

A stranger among a settlement's own culture — the friction of the outsider.

Conquest

A place whose living culture was imposed over an older one — grief and quiet resistance.

Dual identity

A mortal pulled evenly between two cultures — the ache of divided belonging.

Fanaticism

A mortal steeped so deeply in their culture that meeting another turns to zeal.

None of these are penalties you manage. They are seams the story finds — the reasons a moment between two mortals carries weight a bare description would miss. Culture is the game's answer to why these people, in this place, feel the way they do about each other.

Secrets & favors — the currency you can't seeBeneath the public order runs a private economy: what one mortal knows about another, and what one mortal owes another. Both are leverage.

Not all power is rank and reputation. Every mortal walks around with things they would rather were not known and debts they have not repaid, and these are as real a currency as coin. A secret is a true, damaging fact one mortal has learned about another — a hidden allegiance, a past crime, a forbidden bond, a private weakness, a concealed ambition, a buried debt, knowledge of your own divine hand, or word of a coming betrayal. A favor is the opposite ledger: a debt of gratitude, from a small kindness to a life owed.

Secrets are not sprinkled at random — a mortal can only hold a secret that is plausibly true given who the subject actually is, and how damaging it is depends on how it was learned. A wrung confession cuts deeper than a scrap of tavern gossip. The same goes for favors: they are earned in the doing, and weigh as much as the deed behind them.

Why they matter — leverage

When two mortals enter a social encounter, what they hold over each other tilts the ground before a word is spoken. A mortal who knows a damaging secret about the other, or is owed a favor by them, walks in with an advantage — and a god who has been quietly seeding secrets and banking favors is arranging the board turns before the play. There are divine acts for each: to reveal a secret and detonate it, to plant one where none existed, or to call in a favor and spend the debt.

This economy also decays, the way real leverage does. A minor secret that is never used quietly fades once it is old enough — small truths lose their teeth. And a favor left unpaid too long first sours the relationship between debtor and creditor, then, if it lingers past all patience, is simply forgiven and forgotten. Leverage is perishable: what you do not spend, you may lose.

Designer Notes

The faction reputation ladder and the twelve seeded definitions, autonomous ambitions/actions/succession/schism, chosen-faction powers, cultural gravity & tension, and the secrets-and-favors social-leverage economy — with the honest IMPL/DESIGN status of each.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). Terminology authority is the UL — Docs/ubiquitous-language/Agents.md (Faction, Actor, ActorType) and Docs/ubiquitous-language/Graph.md (edge types); where a term here and the UL disagree, the UL wins. Synthesis spine: Docs/canon/rulebook.md §2 "What the World Is." Factions and cultures are graph nodes (actor nodes with actorType: 'faction' | 'culture'); relationships are typed edges, never property bags.

Faction reputation & the twelve definitions IMPL

  • Reputation lives on the member_of edge (agent → faction) as a 0.0–1.0 score. Quest steps grant FACTION_REPUTATION_GAIN_PER_STEP = 0.04; full completion adds FACTION_REPUTATION_COMPLETION_BONUS = 0.08 (src/data/faction-constants.ts). An alignment multiplier (FACTION_ALIGNMENT_BONUS / FACTION_ALIGNMENT_PENALTY, agent-behavior-constants.ts) scales gains by how well the agent's traits match the faction's reputationAlignment. Decay is FACTION_REPUTATION_DECAY_PER_TICK = 0.001 after FACTION_REPUTATION_INACTIVITY_GRACE_TICKS = 36 ticks of inactivity, clamped at 0 (factionReputation.ts, phase faction_reputation_decay, orchestrator Phase 6.55, pre-economy).
  • Rank tiers are per-faction, not global — each FactionDefinition declares an ordered rankTiers[] with a minReputation threshold, optional maxSlots (e.g. one guildmaster), and per-tier bonuses. Crossing a threshold sets promotionPending and injects a promotion encounter; a drop emits faction_rank_changed.
  • Twelve seeded factions (FACTION_DEFINITIONS, src/data/faction-definitions.ts:165), five factionTypes (guild / military / religious / political / criminal): Adventuring Guild, Mercenary Company, Thieves Guild, Merchant Consortium, Temple of Spheres, Arcane Circle, Rangers Brotherhood, Civic Guard, Underking Court, Holy Order of the Dawn, Builders Fellowship, Lorekeepers Covenant. The Adventuring Guild is defined inline in faction-definitions.ts; the other eleven are one-per-file src/data/*-definition.ts. A definition carries id / name / motto / glyph / theme, reachWeights, rankTiers, join & promotion encounter ids, quest/social template ids, dispositions, and ambitionWeights.
  • Members join via member_of (properties: reputation, role, rank, factionDefId, joinedTick, lastFactionActivityTick, promotionPending). Factions are dynamic — created and dissolved during simulation (dissolved flag), exactly as the UL Faction entry asserts.

Faction autonomy — ambitions, actions, succession, schism IMPL

  • Ambitions (factionAmbitions.ts, phase faction_ambitions, post-economy): six categories in src/types/faction.ts:141 — territorial_expansion, resource_acquisition, defensive_consolidation, cultural_dominance, revenge, divine_mandate. Re-evaluated every FACTION_AMBITION_EVALUATION_INTERVAL = 5 ticks; territorial expansion gates on prosperity ≥ EXPANSION_PROSPERITY_THRESHOLD = 0.6; revenge grievances decay at REVENGE_GRIEVANCE_DECAY = 0.02/tick; divine mandate needs DIVINE_MANDATE_THREAD_THRESHOLD = 3 threads. Stored as ambition nodes on pursues edges.
  • Actions (phaseFactionActions.ts, phase faction_actions, post-economy, after faction_ambitions): six v1 action types (src/types/factionAction.ts) — commission_quest, declare_rivalry, propose_alliance, excommunicate, hold_conclave, issue_bounty — each with a treasury cost, cooldown, and minimum membership. The leader's axiological profile biases the action weights (e.g. declare_rivalry scales with courage_prudence, propose_alliance with mercy_ruthlessness).
  • Succession (THR-432, phaseFactionSuccession.ts, phase faction_succession, post-narrative): a leaderSnapshotId detects when the current leader (anointed via a leads edge, else highest-reputation member) exits. The most-recently-anointed will_succeed candidate inherits a fresh leads edge; absent one, score-derivation fills the seat silently.
  • Schism (THR-430): planted by the divine act action.faction.schism, resolved by phaseSchismResolution.ts (phase schism_resolution, post-economy, after faction_actions). decideSchismOutcome() in factionTopology.ts computes splitPressure from cohesion-drop, dissent, and spread, weighted SCHISM_WEIGHT_COHESION = 0.4 / SCHISM_WEIGHT_SPREAD = 0.4 / SCHISM_WEIGHT_DISSENT = 0.2 (src/data/game-config.ts:294) — these weights are defined and consumed; schism is fully wired, not a stub. Outcome is either performFactionSplit (a breakaway faction) or performFactionReform (expel the dissenters).

Chosen faction powers IMPL

  • THR-513, chosenFactionPowers.ts, phase chosen_faction_powers (orchestrator Phase 6.56, right after reputation decay). A domain-locked anoint act flags the faction chosen: { power, domain, byAscendantId }; each tick every member gains a power-keyed reputation bonus via CHOSEN_POWER_EFFECT_TABLE — chosen.gold.reputation 0.005, iron.leadership_aura / heart.faith_spread / star.fated_banner 0.004, eye.illumined_charter / stone.enduring_foundation 0.003, shadow.veiled_reach / veil.whispered_sanction 0.002 — applied with cause: 'chosen_power' (bypasses the alignment multiplier).

Cultures — gravity, tension, generation IMPL (narrative-context)

  • Culture nodes are actor nodes with actorType: 'culture'. Agents and locations link by belongs_to edges (a location carries a cultureLayer: 'current' | 'historical' to model conquest). A CultureIdentity holds venerated Spheres, reach preferences, an optional foundation bias, and traits.
  • Cultural gravity (culturalGravity.ts): a symmetric similarity in [-1, 1] = GRAVITY_SPHERE_WEIGHT 0.4 × sphere + GRAVITY_REACH_WEIGHT 0.4 × reach + GRAVITY_FOUNDATION_WEIGHT 0.2 × foundation, with a sphere-opposition map (life↔entropy, force↔mind, matter↔spirit, energy↔time).
  • Cultural tension (culturalTension.ts): four types surfaced at encounter time for prose — mismatch (severity 3), conquest (4), dual (2, when two cultures sit within DUAL_CULTURE_THRESHOLD = 0.15), fanaticism (3, at strength ≥ FANATICISM_THRESHOLD = 0.8), scored by CULTURAL_TENSION_SCORES.
  • Generation is static at worldgen: culturePhonetics.ts (names), cultureMores.ts (customs), cultureFlag.ts (banner), cultureDetail.ts / culturalProse.ts (flavor), orchestrated by cultureGenerator.ts. Honest scope: there is no dedicated culture tick phase — gravity and tension are computed inline during encounter scoring and prose enrichment. Cultures shape narrative texture and settlement identity today; a mechanical culture-drift lever on agent decision-making is the DESIGN edge, not yet wired.

Secrets & favors — the social-leverage economy IMPL

  • Model: a secret is a knows_secret_of edge (holder → subject); a favor is an owes_favor edge (debtor → creditor). Both carry magnitude ∈ [0,1], source/context, and revealed/redeemed/broken flags (src/types/secretsFavors.ts). Caps: MAX_SECRETS_PER_AGENT = 10, MAX_FAVORS_PER_AGENT = 8.
  • Eight secret types: hidden_allegiance, past_crime, forbidden_relationship, hidden_weakness, secret_ambition, financial_secret, divine_mark, betrayal_planned. Generated at discovery from real graph state (secretGeneration.ts) so only plausible secrets exist; magnitude is scaled by a SOURCE_QUALITY table (confession 0.80 … tavern_gossip 0.35).
  • Leverage (THR-30, socialLeverage.ts): at social-encounter start a mortal's advantage sums a power bonus, a faction-rank-gap bonus, secretMagnitude × SECRET_LEVERAGE_MULTIPLIER 0.30 (an unrevealed secret about the target), and favorMagnitude × FAVOR_LEVERAGE_MULTIPLIER 0.25 (a favor the target owes), clamped to LEVERAGE_INITIAL_CAP. Divine consumers are wired: action.secrets.reveal_secret, action.secrets.plant_secret, action.secrets.call_in_favor (graph ops in graphOpExecutor.ts).
  • Decay (phaseSecretsFavors.ts, phase secrets_favors, every SECRET_FAVOR_CHECK_INTERVAL = 10 ticks): a minor secret (magnitude < SECRET_DECAY_THRESHOLD 0.20) is removed once older than SECRET_MAX_AGE_TICKS = 100; an unpaid favor drifts sentiment by FAVOR_TENSION_SENTIMENT_DELTA −0.02 after FAVOR_TENSION_THRESHOLD_TICKS = 30, and is forgiven (removed) after FAVOR_MAX_AGE_TICKS = 80.
Accuracy note — "clue decay" is a different subsystem

The W11 plan titles this domain "secret generation + clue decay," but secret decay in code is a binary fade of minor stale secrets — there is no per-tick magnitude erosion; magnitude is fixed once at discovery by source quality. The orchestrator's separate clue_decay phase (ruins/clueLifecycle.ts) is the ruin/mystery clue lifecycle — an unrelated subsystem, not the secrets economy, and not documented on this page.

Tick placement IMPL

  • Pre-economy: faction_reputation_decay (6.55) → chosen_faction_powers (6.56). Post-economy cluster (registry-ordered): … → faction_ambitions → faction_actions → secrets_favors → schism_resolution → …. Post-narrative: faction_succession. Cultural gravity/tension are helper calls at encounter scoring, not a scheduled phase.
