Factions, Cultures & Secrets

The world is not a crowd of strangers. Mortals band into guilds and orders with designs of their own, inherit the gravity of the cultures they were born into, and carry private debts and buried truths. This is the social fabric a god learns to read — and to pull.

The world organizes itselfOver the individual mortals lie three social layers — factions, cultures, and loose groups — and each one bends the choices its members make.

Every mortal is a person in their own right, but almost none of them stand alone. They are organized: sworn into a guild, born into a culture, drawn into a passing band. These layers are not decoration — each one changes what its members want, who they trust, and how they act. A god who reads only individuals reads half the world. The other half is written in the shape of the groups they belong to.

You do not command these organizations any more than you command a single mortal. A faction is not a unit you move; it is a living body with its own ambitions, and the most you can do is earn standing within it, anoint it as favored, or set a crisis loose inside it and watch how it breaks. This page is how those layers work — and how a patient god turns them into levers.

Factions — organizations with their own designsA faction is a structured body with goals of its own. It recruits, ranks its members by their standing, pursues long ambitions, and acts on the world without waiting for you.

A faction is a guild, order, company, court, or criminal network — a social entity that exists independently of any one member. The world is seeded with a dozen of them, spanning five kinds: the martial (mercenary companies, a civic guard), the mercantile (a merchant consortium, a builders' fellowship), the devout (a temple, a holy order), the arcane and lettered (an arcane circle, a lorekeepers' covenant), the shadowed (a thieves' guild, an underking's court), and the broad adventuring and ranging guilds between.

Your relationship with a faction is reputation — a standing you build by serving its ends. Complete the quests it commissions and your standing rises; drift away and it decays over time. Standing is not a single number in the eyes of the faction: each one has its own rank ladder, and crossing a threshold promotes a member up it. A thieves' guild recruit climbs from pickpocket to footpad to shadowblade to guildmaster; each rung unlocks richer rewards and, at the top, a seat that only one can hold.

Standing runs both ways

Whether a mortal rises fast or slowly depends on whether they fit. A faction quietly favors members whose nature matches its character and disfavors those who cut against it — a thieves' guild rewards the shadowed and the greedy, and gives the honest a colder welcome. Reputation is a mirror of alignment as much as effort.

Factions act on their own

The load-bearing truth about factions is that they are not hand-scripted set dressing. Each one carries a long ambition — to expand its territory, to hoard resources, to fortify, to spread its culture, to avenge an old grievance, or to carry out a god's declared will — and that ambition colors everything it does. Driven by it, a faction takes real action in the world: commissioning quests, declaring rivalries, proposing alliances, excommunicating a member, calling a conclave, or posting a bounty. Which action it reaches for is shaded by the temperament of whoever leads it — a rash leader declares rivalries, a merciful one seeks alliances.

A faction can also put people in the field. Every couple of days it may gather a handful of its own members who happen to be standing together and send them out as a band — the same kind of travelling company your mortals form, but wearing a guild's colors and steering by the guild's ambition. A band is not a monster spawn and not an army: it is three to six named people who can be met, bargained with, outmatched, or killed. A faction never empties its hall to do this, and it keeps at most two bands abroad at once, so the world thickens with rivals without drowning in them. When your company finally has enemies its own size, these are they.

Most of a guild's people are not folk you will ever watch closely, and they work anyway. Between the quests you witness, a guild keeps putting its ordinary members on ordinary jobs — a contract filled, a survey walked, a debt collected — and those jobs succeed or fail without you. You do not see them happen; what reaches you is the consequence. Someone's standing rises, and one day word arrives late that they answer to a new title. This is the shape of a god's attention as a resource: the world does not pause where you are not looking, and the parts you never attend still climb their own ladders. A member who keeps working also stops fading — standing decays only in the idle.

And leadership itself is mortal. When a faction's head dies or is cast down, succession resolves: a named heir inherits, or the highest-standing member rises to fill the seat. A faction can even splinter — a schism, once seeded, resolves into either a clean break (a breakaway faction carrying off its share of members) or a bitter reunification that expels the dissenters. All of this happens whether or not you are watching.

Anointing a chosen faction

You have one deeper move here. Through a domain-locked divine act you can anoint a faction as your chosen — and from then on, every one of its members gains standing a little faster each turn, the exact flavor of the blessing set by the Sphere it flows through. A god who lifts a faction into favor gives it a slow, structural advantage no amount of ordinary questing can match. Sowing a schism and anointing a champion are the two ends of the same power: you can raise a faction up, or set it against itself.

Cultures — the gravity of belongingA culture is a whole civilization's worth of shared values, names, and customs. It is less a thing a mortal joins than a pull they are born inside of.

Where a faction is a body one enlists in, a culture is a gravity well one is born into. Each culture has its own venerated Spheres, its leanings across the Eight Reaches, and a deep foundational cast — and from those it grows a full identity: generated names that sound like they belong together, its own customs and mores, a banner, and a texture the world describes it in. A mortal carries their culture the way they carry their homeland — it shapes what they revere and how they are seen far more than it dictates any single act.

Cultures relate to one another by gravity — a measure of how alike or opposed two of them are, drawn from their Spheres, their Reaches, and their foundations. Kindred cultures share a pull; opposed ones repel. And where cultures meet unevenly, tension surfaces — not as a war mechanic, but as narrative texture that colors the encounters between people who do not share a world:

Mismatch

A stranger among a settlement's own culture — the friction of the outsider.

Conquest

A place whose living culture was imposed over an older one — grief and quiet resistance.

Dual identity

A mortal pulled evenly between two cultures — the ache of divided belonging.

Fanaticism

A mortal steeped so deeply in their culture that meeting another turns to zeal.

None of these are penalties you manage. They are seams the story finds — the reasons a moment between two mortals carries weight a bare description would miss. Culture is the game's answer to why these people, in this place, feel the way they do about each other.

Secrets & favors — the currency you can't seeBeneath the public order runs a private economy: what one mortal knows about another, and what one mortal owes another. Both are leverage.

Not all power is rank and reputation. Every mortal walks around with things they would rather were not known and debts they have not repaid, and these are as real a currency as coin. A secret is a true, damaging fact one mortal has learned about another — a hidden allegiance, a past crime, a forbidden bond, a private weakness, a concealed ambition, a buried debt, knowledge of your own divine hand, or word of a coming betrayal. A favor is the opposite ledger: a debt of gratitude, from a small kindness to a life owed.

Secrets are not sprinkled at random — a mortal can only hold a secret that is plausibly true given who the subject actually is, and how damaging it is depends on how it was learned. A wrung confession cuts deeper than a scrap of tavern gossip. The same goes for favors: they are earned in the doing, and weigh as much as the deed behind them.

Why they matter — leverage

When two mortals enter a social encounter, what they hold over each other tilts the ground before a word is spoken. A mortal who knows a damaging secret about the other, or is owed a favor by them, walks in with an advantage — and a god who has been quietly seeding secrets and banking favors is arranging the board turns before the play. There are divine acts for each: to reveal a secret and detonate it, to plant one where none existed, or to call in a favor and spend the debt.

This economy also decays, the way real leverage does. A minor secret that is never used quietly fades once it is old enough — small truths lose their teeth. And a favor left unpaid too long first sours the relationship between debtor and creditor, then, if it lingers past all patience, is simply forgiven and forgotten. Leverage is perishable: what you do not spend, you may lose.

Only a person can owe you (THR-1175, 2026-08-18). Every way a favour is ever collected runs through one mortal's regard for another — leverage in a social scene, sentiment souring between debtor and creditor, the call-in itself. So a favour owed by a town, or by a guild, is not a stronger version of the same thing; it is a debt with nobody on the other end of it, and nothing in the game can ever cash it. The graph now refuses to write one: an owes_favor edge requires an individual debtor, a knows_secret_of edge requires actors at both ends, and a refusal is traced loudly rather than dropped. This closed a real one — a gratitude scene had been recording that a settlement owed the traveler a favour, truthfully reported on screen and quietly deleted, unread, a week later.

What a place can hold instead is reputation with it — the same social score you build inside a guild, pointed at a town, a shrine, or a person you have no formal tie to. A family whose kindness you repaid keeps a door open for you in their town; that is your reputation with the town, and it reads as one of the same five words a guild standing does.

Reputation is the one social score (THR-1206, 2026-08-23). Wherever the game asks "how do these two regard each other" — a member's standing in a guild, a traveler's welcome in a town, one mortal's regard for another, or what the world at large has heard — the answer is reputation, spoken in one vocabulary from Distrusted through Accepted to Revered. It is directional: what a town thinks of you is not what you think of the town. It modifies real things — which encounters open to you, and how far ahead you start a social scene. And it fades: standing nobody keeps alive drifts back to indifference, slowly enough that a season's work still means something. Before this, four different mechanisms wore the word and disagreed, and a fifth did reputation's job under a bespoke noun the player had to learn separately. That noun (a standing welcome) is retired; the door it named is now simply your reputation with the place.

Faction reputation & the twelve definitions IMPL

  • Rule overrides now bind here (THR-1241): faction_influence_multiplier scales the reputation delta in applyFactionReputationGain — never the stored score, which would rewrite history each time a signet ring was put on. It scales losses as well as gains on purpose: a charm that makes you twice as persuasive makes your betrayals land twice as hard, and a one-directional version would be a strictly-good item.
  • Reputation lives on the member_of edge (agent → faction) as a 0.0–1.0 score. Quest steps grant FACTION_REPUTATION_GAIN_PER_STEP = 0.04; full completion adds FACTION_REPUTATION_COMPLETION_BONUS = 0.08 (src/data/faction-constants.ts). An alignment multiplier (FACTION_ALIGNMENT_BONUS / FACTION_ALIGNMENT_PENALTY, agent-behavior-constants.ts) scales gains by how well the agent's traits match the faction's reputationAlignment. Decay is FACTION_REPUTATION_DECAY_PER_TICK = 0.001 after FACTION_REPUTATION_INACTIVITY_GRACE_TICKS = 36 ticks of inactivity, clamped at 0 (factionReputation.ts, phase faction_reputation_decay, orchestrator Phase 6.55, pre-economy). Decay is per membership, not per agent (THR-781, 2026-08-06): the phase walks member_of edges, so a member who holds standing in two factions fades in both, and a tick that drops them below a rank threshold in both announces both demotions. Faction nodes are minted per chapter (faction_def_{defId}{suffix}), so two memberships can share one factionDefId — hence one decay rate and a threshold crossed in lockstep. The demotion event id therefore names the faction as well as the agent (faction_decay_rank_{tick}_{agent}_{faction}); until THR-781 it named only the agent, the two notices collided on a duplicate React key, and one of them could be dropped without ever reaching the player.
  • Rank tiers are per-faction, not global — each FactionDefinition declares an ordered rankTiers[] with a minReputation threshold, optional maxSlots (e.g. one guildmaster), and per-tier bonuses. Crossing a threshold sets promotionPending and injects a promotion encounter; a drop emits faction_rank_changed.
  • Rank now gates encounter access (THR-805, 2026-07-26). A rung on the ladder buys more than richer rewards: meetsFactionRankRequirement (factionReputation.ts) is read by the encounter prerequisites stage, so a template whose FACTION_ENCOUNTER_META.questType is senior / elite / leadership reaches only members standing at or above the tier its minRank names. Rank is recomputed from reputation on every check via computeRankFromReputation, never read from the edge's cached rank / role — those refresh only when a tier boundary is crossed, so a decay in progress would otherwise read stale. Two rejected alternatives are worth recording: EncounterChain (a stage holds exactly one template id, while a guild tier holds several) and the faction_rank: predicate in effects.ts, which reads agentNode.properties.factionRank — a node property nothing writes, so that predicate is permanently false for any threshold above 0 and its integer-tier semantics conflict with this edge's normalized 0–1 rank. The apex tiers (minReputation 0.85) are currently unreachable, and THR-810 established why: not because decay outpaces quest income, but because there is no income — the economy records zero reputation gain in a live run.
  • Every reader now derives rank the way the gate does (THR-1211, 2026-08-24). The bullet above records that meetsFactionRankRequirement recomputes from reputation rather than trusting the cached member_of.rank. Three other readers did not — getHighestFactionRank (socialLeverage.ts), the faction-intel gate in factionAwareness.ts, and the walk's shared-faction bonus in reputationWalk.ts — so the same member could stand at one rank for the gate and another for leverage. The disagreement was minted in rather than drifting: both write paths rewrite rank on every tier change, so a decaying member stayed consistent, but npcSeeding writes rank: 0.1 beside reputation: 0.12 (and 0.16 beside 0.22 for leadership roles) — both inside the entry tier — for every seeded faction NPC from tick 0, and nothing rewrites it until a boundary is crossed. All four now read getDerivedMembershipRank (factionReputation.ts), which computes the tier index from reputation and falls back to the cached value only where no FactionDefinition resolves — armies, bands, and the splinter/drift memberships, which the gate does not see either. That fallback also closed a live NaN: armySpawning writes rank: 'army', a string in a numeric field, which ?? 0 does not catch, so the walk's rank × FACTION_RANK_TRUST_BONUS poisoned clamp(raw + distortion + NaN) for any path routed through an army.
  • The third rank-bonus type finally has a consumer (THR-1211, 2026-08-24). FactionRankBonusType declares three kinds, and factionRankBonus.ts shipped application functions for two of them (getEncounterRewardMultiplier, getScoringBoost) while describing reputation_walk_bonus as "(future)". Nine faction definitions author values for it on their upper tiers — it is the most-authored of the three — and none of those numbers had ever moved a result. It is now applied in reputationWalk step 8b, once per faction the source shares with a path intermediary rather than once per hop: the bonus means "your guild network vouches for you", which is a property of the shared membership, not a toll paid per intermediary, and accumulating it per hop would let a long path multiply a flat authored value straight into the clamp.
  • Guild work does not yet reach the members who could do it (THR-814, 2026-07-27). Two mechanical blockers on that zero are now fixed. First, the per-agent quest and lifecycle offers a member generates were appended after the ~4,650-entry location cache and cut by the candidate cap before scoring ever saw them — 0 of 1,263 survived; they now claim reserved slots (PERSONAL_OFFER_CAP_RESERVE), and survival rose to 60.6%. Second, Merchant Consortium addressed a namespace that does not exist: all four of its rank tiers declared encounterAccess: ['mc_trade.…'] while every one of its 15 templates is mct.*, so the guild matched zero templates at every tier and its 49 memberships drew no work at all. Repaired, and pinned across all twelve factions by a test comparing namespace roots. The dominant blocker remains and is not a tuning question: faction membership is seeded overwhelmingly onto ambient NPCs (225 of 227), while the phase that draws faction content iterates spotlight actors only — so 2 of 227 members can reach the draw path at all, and no change to scoring, rewards, or gates can reach the rest. Resolving guild work faction-side, at the tier that is simulated, was tracked as THR-815 and has now shipped — see the next bullet; npm run sweep:rank-reach reports this eligibility count on every run.
  • Ambient members now do guild work at the faction tier (THR-815, 2026-07-27). Because ambient actors are by definition not simulated individually, content that reaches them resolves at the tier that is — the faction node. factionMemberWork.ts, swept from phaseFactionActions on its own FACTION_MEMBER_WORK_INTERVAL = 6 gate, walks each faction's off-loop members and settles one authored job apiece. It re-uses the attended path rather than paralleling it: rank access comes from the same getAccessibleTemplates, and payout from the same processFactionEncounterReputation, scaled by FACTION_MEMBER_WORK_REWARD_SCALE = 0.5 so unattended work cannot out-earn a watched encounter. Two selection rules are load-bearing. The window rotates with the pass counter, because filling it from the head every pass is exactly the positional starvation THR-814 found at the cap stage. And it is proportional to membership (FACTION_MEMBER_WORK_MEMBER_FRACTION = 0.15, clamped to 2–8): under a flat cap, per-member cadence scaled inversely with guild size, and the 45-member Merchant Consortium topped out at 0.765 against an apex of 0.85 while a 3-member court saturated — the largest guilds, carrying the most authored content, were the ones structurally unable to reach it. Measured on seed 42 / medium / 150 ticks, the guild economy goes from 0 reputation gains to 449 (+32.6 total across 185 memberships), 944 jobs resolved with 462 successes and 135 promotions, apex holders 0 → 33, and rank-blocked templates 21 → 0; sweep:rank-reach now exits 0 and is a live gate. Cost is 0.109 ms/tick amortized — the axis on which promoting members to spotlight (~14× the decision cost) was rejected. Only promotions surface to the player, as faction_rank_changed; individual jobs stay in the aggregate faction_member_work trace, one per faction per pass. The five remaining unreachable bf.* templates are a separate defect — Builders' Fellowship is seeded zero members because pickFactionForNpc takes the first faction matching a type and six definitions share factionType: 'guild' — tracked as THR-816 and reported by the sweep as unowned, distinct from blocked.
  • The third income channel was dead until 2026-08-17 (THR-1150). Quest steps and off-loop member work both pay standing through processFactionEncounterReputation, which passes a faction node id. The third channel — an authored faction_reputation_gain on an encounter's ending — passed the definition id ('mercenary_company'), because that is the id every faction content file carries. factionSeeding keys the node faction_def_<definitionId><chapterSuffix>, so applyFactionReputationGain's e.target === factionId match never matched, and every authored faction-standing consequence in the shipped game was a no-op. Measured on seed 42: the ascendant's membership after mc.join read reputation: 0.05 — the join baseline alone — where the encounter's two reactions each author +0.1. It now reads 0.15. The resolution is a bind pass (bindFactionDefinitionIds, encounterAftermath.ts) running before dispatch over all seven faction-carrying effect kinds, widening-only because resolveFactionNodeId tries the exact node id first. The reason it survived is the other half: the dispatcher's "not a member" sentinel used to break silently, so a corpus-wide dead effect emitted nothing to grep and was found only when an unrelated ticket (THR-1144) walked the same field. A no-op now carries failReason: 'not_a_member' | 'faction_not_found'. Note this does not retire THR-810's finding above — quest income was separately zero, and THR-815 fixed that; this was a third channel dark alongside it.
  • Twelve seeded factions (FACTION_DEFINITIONS, src/data/faction-definitions.ts:165), five factionTypes (guild / military / religious / political / criminal): Adventuring Guild, Mercenary Company, Thieves Guild, Merchant Consortium, Temple of Spheres, Arcane Circle, Rangers Brotherhood, Civic Guard, Underking Court, Holy Order of the Dawn, Builders Fellowship, Lorekeepers Covenant. The Adventuring Guild is defined inline in faction-definitions.ts; the other eleven are one-per-file src/data/*-definition.ts. A definition carries id / name / motto / glyph / theme, reachWeights, rankTiers, join & promotion encounter ids, quest/social template ids, dispositions, and ambitionWeights. Every reachWeights key must be one of the eight live ReachDomains (THR-1345). Two names are specifically not: flesh is the retired 9th Reach — migrate it per the table in Docs/canon/cosmology.md, never reintroduce it — and force is a Sphere name, not a Reach, the two being orthogonal axes. Stale keys are not inert: factionSeeding stores this bag on the faction node, and computeSettlementReaches normalizes across every key present, so a key no consumer can read still scales down every legitimate reach at that settlement. Enforced twice over — the typecheck ratchet catches an annotated literal, and assertValidReachWeights (src/testing/contentInvariants.ts) sweeps the real definition corpus, which is the layer that survives a widened Record<string, …> annotation.
  • Members join via member_of (properties: reputation, role, rank, factionDefId, joinedTick, lastFactionActivityTick, promotionPending). Factions are dynamic — created and dissolved during simulation (dissolved flag), exactly as the UL Faction entry asserts.

Faction autonomy — ambitions, actions, succession, schism IMPL

  • Ambitions (factionAmbitions.ts, phase faction_ambitions, post-economy): six categories in src/types/faction.ts:141 — territorial_expansion, resource_acquisition, defensive_consolidation, cultural_dominance, revenge, divine_mandate. Re-evaluated every FACTION_AMBITION_EVALUATION_INTERVAL = 5 ticks; territorial expansion gates on prosperity ≥ EXPANSION_PROSPERITY_THRESHOLD = 0.6; revenge grievances decay at REVENGE_GRIEVANCE_DECAY = 0.02/tick; divine mandate needs DIVINE_MANDATE_THREAD_THRESHOLD = 3 threads. Stored as ambition nodes on pursues edges.
  • Actions (phaseFactionActions.ts, phase faction_actions, post-economy, after faction_ambitions): six v1 action types (src/types/factionAction.ts) — commission_quest, declare_rivalry, propose_alliance, excommunicate, hold_conclave, issue_bounty — each with a treasury cost, cooldown, and minimum membership. The leader's axiological profile biases the action weights (e.g. declare_rivalry scales with courage_prudence, propose_alliance with mercy_ruthlessness).
  • NPC bands (THR-731, src/engine/groups/bandSpawner.ts, swept from phaseFactionActions after its own action loop, on its own BAND_SPAWN_INTERVAL = 24 gate — a multiple of FACTION_ACTION_INTERVAL = 8, since the sweep sits behind that phase's interval return): a faction fields its own members as an ordinary faction_band company (THR-74), marked bandRole: 'defender' and bandFactionId. Structural eligibility gates the roll rather than the reverse — measured on --seed 42 --map medium, only ~10 of 49 factions hold a colocated cluster of three, so rolling first spent the BAND_SPAWN_CHANCE = 0.15 budget on barren guilds and produced zero bands. Constants in src/data/group-constants.ts: BAND_SIZE_MIN/MAX = 3/6, MAX_ACTIVE_BANDS_PER_FACTION = 2, BAND_FACTION_MEMBER_RESERVE = 2, BAND_COHESION_START = 0.7. Band members are drawn from the faction's full roster, deliberately bypassing isGroupEligibleAgent's spotlightTier check — that gate is an attention budget for player-facing company formation, and applying it left exactly one faction in the world able to muster. Bands are companies in every other respect: isCompanyNode accepts them, so phaseGroups moves, frays, and dissolves them unchanged. Emits band_spawned; surfaced by __DEBUG.getGroups() (bandRole/bandFactionId) and CLI groups. Monster factions are skipped — they are abstract lair-owners with no roster (a legendary lair mints exactly one named elite, ever), so the outbound raider path awaits a monster-population design — THR-767.
  • Succession (THR-432, phaseFactionSuccession.ts, phase faction_succession, post-narrative): a leaderSnapshotId detects when the current leader (anointed via a leads edge, else highest-reputation member) exits. The most-recently-anointed will_succeed candidate inherits a fresh leads edge; absent one, score-derivation fills the seat silently.
  • Schism (THR-430): planted by the divine act action.faction.schism, resolved by phaseSchismResolution.ts (phase schism_resolution, post-economy, after faction_actions). decideSchismOutcome() in factionTopology.ts computes splitPressure from cohesion-drop, dissent, and spread, weighted SCHISM_WEIGHT_COHESION = 0.4 / SCHISM_WEIGHT_SPREAD = 0.4 / SCHISM_WEIGHT_DISSENT = 0.2 (src/data/game-config.ts:294) — these weights are defined and consumed; schism is fully wired, not a stub. Outcome is either performFactionSplit (a breakaway faction) or performFactionReform (expel the dissenters).

Cultures — gravity, tension, generation IMPL (narrative-context)

  • Culture nodes are actor nodes with actorType: 'culture'. Agents and locations link by belongs_to edges (a location carries a cultureLayer: 'current' | 'historical' to model conquest). A CultureIdentity holds venerated Spheres, reach preferences, an optional foundation bias, and traits.
  • Cultural gravity (culturalGravity.ts): a symmetric similarity in [-1, 1] = GRAVITY_SPHERE_WEIGHT 0.4 × sphere + GRAVITY_REACH_WEIGHT 0.4 × reach + GRAVITY_FOUNDATION_WEIGHT 0.2 × foundation, with a sphere-opposition map (life↔entropy, force↔mind, matter↔spirit, energy↔time).
  • Cultural tension (culturalTension.ts): four types surfaced at encounter time for prose — mismatch (severity 3), conquest (4), dual (2, when two cultures sit within DUAL_CULTURE_THRESHOLD = 0.15), fanaticism (3, at strength ≥ FANATICISM_THRESHOLD = 0.8), scored by CULTURAL_TENSION_SCORES.
  • Generation is static at worldgen: culturePhonetics.ts (names), cultureMores.ts (customs), cultureFlag.ts (banner), cultureDetail.ts / culturalProse.ts (flavor), orchestrated by cultureGenerator.ts. Honest scope: there is no dedicated culture tick phase — gravity and tension are computed inline during encounter scoring and prose enrichment. Cultures shape narrative texture and settlement identity today; a mechanical culture-drift lever on agent decision-making is the DESIGN edge, not yet wired.

Secrets & favors — the social-leverage economy IMPL

  • Model: a secret is a knows_secret_of edge (holder → subject); a favor is an owes_favor edge (debtor → creditor). Both carry magnitude ∈ [0,1], source/context, and revealed/redeemed/broken flags (src/types/secretsFavors.ts). Caps: MAX_SECRETS_PER_AGENT = 10, MAX_FAVORS_PER_AGENT = 8.
  • Eight secret types: hidden_allegiance, past_crime, forbidden_relationship, hidden_weakness, secret_ambition, financial_secret, divine_mark, betrayal_planned. Generated at discovery from real graph state (secretGeneration.ts) so only plausible secrets exist; magnitude is scaled by a SOURCE_QUALITY table (confession 0.80 … tavern_gossip 0.35). Seven of the eight could actually be generated: past_crime was unreachable by construction until 2026-08-24 (THR-1211). Its branch read relates_to.properties.reputation, and nothing writes that property — the edge carries sentiment, strength, basis, and trust — so the read was always ?? 0, the test was 0 < 0, and no mortal in any world had ever borne that secret. It now reads getReputationWith, the one social score between two parties (THR-1206), and fires when the subject stands below neutral with a party outside their own factions. Because that function returns the neutral default when nothing covers a pair, an agent with no standing anywhere still does not qualify — the branch stayed selective rather than becoming always-true.
  • Leverage (THR-30, socialLeverage.ts): at social-encounter start a mortal's advantage sums a power bonus, a faction-rank-gap bonus, secretMagnitude × SECRET_LEVERAGE_MULTIPLIER 0.30 (an unrevealed secret about the target), and favorMagnitude × FAVOR_LEVERAGE_MULTIPLIER 0.25 (a favor the target owes), clamped to LEVERAGE_INITIAL_CAP. The faction-rank-gap bonus was unreachable until 2026-08-17 (THR-1151): getHighestFactionRank divided the edge's rank by a RANK_NORMALIZATION_DIVISOR = 10 justified by a comment describing "raw rank numbers (typically 1–10)" — a scale nothing has ever written, since member_of.rank is declared 0 (recruit) → 1 (leader). That capped any agent's value at 0.1 against a LEVERAGE_RANK_GAP of 0.30, so LEVERAGE_RANK_BONUS never applied to any agent in any world. It now reads the scale directly, clamped to FACTION_RANK_MAX. It also now filters by what the membership points at (THR-1177, 2026-08-18): EDGE_SCHEMA declares member_of as actor→actor — the narrowest NodeType can express — so an individual→individual member_of is schema-legal, and the loop used to read rank off it and let it win, handing its bearer a permanent leverage advantage sourced from a membership in nothing. The filter accepts all three shapes the repo actually mints (actor + actorType faction, actor + actorType group for companies and armies, and a bare faction node) — matching only the first would have silently zeroed every company member's rank. It is a reader-side guard on purpose: twelve writers mint member_of and only one goes through joinFaction, and worlds already saved cannot be re-minted. Unlike the three sibling readers THR-1151 fixed, this one had no role === 'leader' fallback to collapse to, so the axis contributed nothing at all rather than contributing less. Divine consumers are wired: action.secrets.reveal_secret, action.secrets.plant_secret, action.secrets.call_in_favor (graph ops in graphOpExecutor.ts).
  • Decay (phaseSecretsFavors.ts, phase secrets_favors, every SECRET_FAVOR_CHECK_INTERVAL = 10 ticks): a minor secret (magnitude < SECRET_DECAY_THRESHOLD 0.20) is removed once older than SECRET_MAX_AGE_TICKS = 100; an unpaid favor drifts sentiment by FAVOR_TENSION_SENTIMENT_DELTA −0.02 after FAVOR_TENSION_THRESHOLD_TICKS = 30, and is forgiven (removed) after FAVOR_MAX_AGE_TICKS = 80.
Accuracy note — "clue decay" is a different subsystem

The W11 plan titles this domain "secret generation + clue decay," but secret decay in code is a binary fade of minor stale secrets — there is no per-tick magnitude erosion; magnitude is fixed once at discovery by source quality. The orchestrator's separate clue_decay phase (ruins/clueLifecycle.ts) is the ruin/mystery clue lifecycle — an unrelated subsystem, not the secrets economy, and not documented on this page.
