Systems & Interfaces

The world is a web of systems feeding systems. The richness of a run lives in these arrows — and when one is quietly severed, a feature leaks out of the game without any test noticing.

The world at a glance Every box is a living subsystem — one box for every row of the generated systems inventory, plus the run-start generators. Arrows are what one system does to another, at design level.

verified live leaked — broken, repair ticketed partial — works, but a stated gap orientation only — not yet audited

Thin gray arrows are orientation, not verified contracts — each gets promoted (or corrected) as its subsystem is audited. The two audited systems are drawn in color. Far fewer arrows exist than in the real game: only the strongest couplings are drawn, and unlisted ones are absence of audit, not absence of connection.

Why this map exists Tests assert that a system writes its data; almost nothing asserts that another system still reads it. That gap is where five attachment contracts quietly died while every test stayed green. The audited truth lives in Docs/canon/interface-map.md; this page is its picture.

  • Effect executors ⟶ The world keeping what they did — blighted ground that stays blightedLIVE Closed 2026-08-25 (THR-1240), stage 2 of the effect-vocabulary activation. executeAlterTerrain and executeModifyRules were never broken: both built a fully-formed overlay, hung it on ExecutionResult, and returned success: true.   • A nearby agent’s aura ⟶ Someone else’s roll — a captain whose company fights better around himLIVE Closed 2026-08-25 (THR-1243), stage 5 of the effect-vocabulary activation. effectAura.ts shipped complete and tested with zero production importers — resolveAuraModifiers appeared in exactly two places in the repo, its own definition and its own test. Why it was never wired is the interesting half: an aura is the only resolution modifier sourced from an agent other than the one being resolved, so unlike equipment, traits, spheres or rule overrides it has no walk-outward-from-the-actor to hang on, and the tempting home — the tick loop — is an O(agents²) proximity scan every tick for a number almost nobody reads. It resolves lazily instead, for one agent, at the moment a step resolves, with collectAuraEffectsNear putting the distance test before the attachment walk so only genuinely nearby agents are ever walked. Two bounds do different jobs: AURA_STACKING_CAP (3) bounds how many emitters may speak, EFFECT_MODIFIER_CAP clamps how loud the answer may be — without the first, the identical step reads differently in a crowded capital than in a hamlet through sheer attendance, for reasons no player could see or influence. The content half is load-bearing rather than decorative: deriveContributionLines drops any contribution whose kind has no authored sentence, so a new aura kind without prose would have tilted the roll while rendering nothing — the exact failure the factor panel exists to prevent. The sentence pair names the emitting agent, never their item, since the acting mortal neither owns nor sees that banner. Falsified both ways: forcing the distance test true fails exactly one test, deleting the prose pair fails exactly one other. Reach note: no shipped catalog entry authors an aura yet — this makes the primitive real for content rather than changing an encounter already in the game.
  • An item’s promise to bend a rule ⟶ The rule actually bending — a ward that stops a death, a charm that shortens a roadLIVE Closed 2026-08-26 (THR-1241), stage 3 of the effect-vocabulary activation, and the exact mirror of the event-raise row above. That contract had a healthy consumer and almost no producer; this one had a healthy producer and, for eleven of thirteen RuleOverrideKeys, no consumer at all. Stage 2 stored every override correctly and getActiveRuleOverride folded them correctly — nothing asked. So an artifact could promise death_prevented and its bearer died on schedule, because the one function that decides a death never read the key. Five keys had shipped catalog content making a promise the engine could not keep (death_prevented, awareness_range_bonus, healing_multiplier, spawn_rate_multiplier, tier_advancement_cost_multiplier) — every symbol an audit greps for existed on both sides, which is what makes this pair worth reading together rather than filing one as a repeat of the other. The repair is one shared reader (ruleOverrideConsumers.ts) called from the single site owning each rule, because eleven inline reads are eleven chances to disagree about the neutral, the fold or the trace, and an inline ?? 1.0 at a *_bonus site is a permanent bug no test catches — the number it produces is plausible. Note which key needed migrating: doom_rate_multiplier, the only one that already had a consumer, was also the only one that disagreed — blind to persisted overrides, unclamped, and ignoring duration and cooldown state. Reach note: backlash_severity_multiplier reads inside evaluateBacklash, whose caller activateSpell still has no production caller, so that one key is live in code and goes live in play when spell activation does; the rulebook and wiring guide both say so in those words rather than listing it flatly beside the ten that bite today.
  • The words content may author ⟶ The mechanisms that run them — one capability, one spellingLIVE Closed 2026-08-26 (THR-1242), stage 4 of the effect-vocabulary activation, and the one stage that removes vocabulary rather than adding consumers. Nine members left the AttachmentEffect union. Three (graph_mutation, outcome_shift, auto_succeed) had no content and no executor. The other six are the interesting half: each was a second spelling of a mechanism that was already live under a different name, and in every case the spelling shipped content used was the dead one — 13 artifacts authored haste/slow/freeze_duration while modify_rules sat beside them with a working executor, 5 authored create_barrier beside alter_terrain, 3 authored reroll beside test_shaper (one item carrying both). So the union was not merely oversized; it was actively misdirecting authors toward the arms that did nothing. Retiring a spelling is not the same claim as keeping the capability, which is why the tests come in two shapes: retirement sweeps run against the real catalogs and match only a type: 'x' position, because a bare substring sweep for "slow" hits an adjective table in archetype-content and would report a false positive forever. Three primitives were wired rather than migrated, each a different shape of dead. suppress was the inverse of the usual case — a consumer with no producer: four readers have honoured EffectRuntimeState.suppressed since the primitive architecture landed and nothing ever set it, so four artifacts promised to silence magic and silenced nothing. reveal had 17 content refs and no consumer of any kind. tag_immunity had a complete query with zero callers and a namespace mismatch that would have made wiring it alone insufficient — condition trait nodes carry #-prefixed tags while most immunity content wrote them bare, so fear could never have matched #fear, and the ward would have run its check and blocked nothing, which is worse than dead because the trace shows the comparison happening. The create_barrier migration additionally forced the stage-2 overlay store's first production reader (movementCost for warded, encounterAwareness for shrouded) — without it, five artifacts would have moved from a dead spelling onto a dead mechanism: persisted, traced, and still changing nothing a player could feel.
  • A mortal being hurt ⟶ The gear that was meant to notice — wards that answer a wound, charms that burn out on the first blowLIVE Closed 2026-08-26 (THR-1244), stage 6 of the effect-vocabulary activation and the last of its trigger families with no source event. damaged and healed have been in the EffectEvent union since the primitive architecture landed, read by three separate families — the damaged/healed reactive triggers, the on_damaged/on_heal stack triggers, and the take_damage expiry event — and nothing ever raised either. This is a third shape of one-sidedness, and the one this map is least equipped to name: not a producer someone forgot to write, but a producer that could not exist, because the thing it would produce is absent from the domain. There is no per-agent damage model, so there was no hit-point subtraction to raise from; an audit grepping for a producer would have found none and been correct and useless. The fix was a design question wearing a wiring question's clothes — what, in this game's own vocabulary, is being hurt? — and the game already had the word: a wound is a condition with a countdown. So inflicting a harmful condition raises damaged and lifting one early raises healed, from all three aftermath condition writers through conditionProxyEvents. Natural expiry deliberately raises nothing, enforced by where the raise lives rather than by a check: conditionDecay is the one tick-driven expiry path and simply does not call the module, because a proxy keyed on "a condition went away" would fire every ward in the world on every sweep. Falsified rather than assumed: each of the four guards was disabled in turn and each reddened exactly its own test and no others, and adding a raise into conditionDecay reddens exactly the natural-expiry silence test. The generalisable warning for this map: when a contract's producer half is missing, check whether the thing it would produce exists in the domain at all before filing it as unwired work. A fourth writer of the same edge (actionTriggerPayloads) is live and still silent, ticketed with its tag-vocabulary mismatch as THR-1257 — the two must be fixed together, or wiring the site alone makes the raise live and silently wrong rather than merely absent.

Spotlight — Companies & bands Audited 2026-07-24/25 (audit-on-touch, THR-74 / THR-731). Companies were built to act together; the later contracts make them act against something — and make that legible.

A weak leader resolves above his own solo capability; an emptied company falls back to the individual path without throwing.
  • Company ⟶ Band opposition — a company that walks into a band fights it, as one contested pairLIVE THR-731 PR 2. The band's counter-action is synthesized and transient; both sides then price their step through the same company math above, which is what stops a six-strong band from fighting exactly as hard as its leader alone. Proven organically on a 150-tick run (seed 42): a Temple defender band met two separate companies, lost a named member, and disbanded.

That value is the derived per-resource stockTier, deliberately not resourceBalance and not prosperity, so what the Livelihood line shows the player and what the divine economy acts on cannot drift apart. Two asymmetries carry the design, and both are contract-tested rather than left to the constants: upward drift stops at ECON_SANCTITY_NURTURE_CEILING (0.5), deliberately below SANCTITY_FLOWERING_THRESHOLD (0.6) — the land readies a source and only a Sanctify cast flowers it, without which a well-sited shrine would flower unattended and the Build verb would become decoration; withering has no floor above zero and stacks with the contested drain in the same tick, so the world takes away more than it gives, which is what makes tending and defending a source worth the player's attention. Untyped sources are excluded by construction rather than by a default: a migrated, unbuilt place of power carries no sphereAffinity and stays on the legacy alignment-distributed term, preserving THR-611's load-bearing NFP #6 contract that a save which has done nothing keeps exactly its old income — pinned by a 100-tick test on a doubly-surplus location. Non-vacuous by measured values, not symbols: two controlled Spirit shrines over 60 ticks driven through the served browser bundle — the surplus valley climbed 0.30 → 0.50 and stopped at the ceiling, tier still dormant; the scarce valley fell 0.30 → 0.00. Known gap, filed rather than absorbed: the Livelihood sentence reads only a location's own source bag while the engine resolves a sublocation host up one parentLocationId hop, so a shrine or rite on a sublocation drifts invisibly (THR-840) — whose sentence it is when several sublocation sources pull different ways is a voice question, not a wiring one.

Spotlight — What the trial cost in spirit Audited 2026-08-12 (audit-on-touch, THR-1082). A quantity the engine had moved for four months and no encounter could ever spend.

  • Authored consequence ⟶ Quintessence — an encounter can finally charge a price in spirit rather than in coinLIVE THR-1082. Quintessence has moved since TB-075, but only ever under the engine's own hand — overchannel, encounter failure, doom, passive regen. Spotlight — What a place is going through Audited 2026-08-16 (audit-on-touch, THR-1143). A capability the substrate had permitted since it was written, and nothing had ever used.

  • Authored consequence ⟶ Movement cost & template gating — an ending can put a place into a state other systems act onLIVE THR-1143. The interesting half of this row is how little of it was new. has_trait.sourceNodeType has listed location since the edge schema was written; decayConditions walks has_trait by edge type, never by source node kind, so a condition on a place always expired correctly; and buildLocationTargetContext has always read a location's trait edges into traitIds, so requiredTargetTraits gating against places was live and had simply never had content to match. What was missing was the authoring surface — no effect field named a place — plus one reader and a player surface. So the arrow is a dormant capability activated rather than a system built (the THR-614 shape), and the tests are written to prove the untouched parts really carry it rather than assuming so. The new reader is movement cost: LOCATION_CONDITION_MOVEMENT_TAX multiplies the price of entering a place carrying a condition, pass_closed at ×8 — a soft block, because a hard one strands an agent whose only road home runs through the pass. Both readers key on the edge itself rather than caching a derived flag, which is what makes expiry free: when the decay loop removes the edge, the tax lifts and the gate closes in the same tick, with no second lifecycle to keep in step. Deliberately not a reader: encounter-type bias, which stays the omen system's job — two bias channels would drift.
  • Authored consequence ⟶ The location's own page — the third reader, and the only one a player meetsLIVE THR-1175, 2026-08-18. This row has always had three readers and listed two, because those were the two THR-1143 built: LocationProfileModal reads a place's condition edges generically — any has_trait whose definition carries subcategory: 'condition' — so it picked up every location condition for free and went unrecorded for exactly that reason. It stops being incidental with standing_welcome, the first location condition a person earns rather than suffers: a gratitude scene had been recording "there is a roof in this town that opens for them now" as an owes_favor edge with the town as debtor, which is schema-illegal and, worse, uncollectable — every favour consumer is individual-shaped, so the edge's whole remaining lifecycle was to be deleted unread at expiry. Re-expressed as a condition on the place, it carries no movement tax by design and gates nothing until the return encounter lands (THR-1182), so the modal is the only thing standing between it and the hollowness this row exists to prevent. A map that omitted the reader would read as "this condition has no consumer" to the next auditor — the opposite of true, and an invitation to delete a live payoff. The lesson generalises past this row: a reader that works generically is the one most likely to go unnamed, because nobody had to write anything to make it work.

Spotlight — What the ending hands you Audited 2026-08-17 (audit-on-touch, THR-1146). Two routes, and the decision not to let them become two implementations.

  • Authored consequence ⟷ Attachment library — a specific ending hands out a random matching prize, drawn exactly the way the step route draws itLIVE THR-1146. The recipe machinery was never the missing part: RewardPoolRecipe and assembleRewardPool have always resolved a tag-filtered, tier-weighted draw. It was reachable only from step successMetadata/failureMetadata, so a band or a reaction could spawn one fixed authored artifact and never a random matching one — "the keeper pays you off with a blade from the strongbox" had to name the blade. reward_draw is that recipe reaching the rest of the effect vocabulary, and it adds no tuning surface of its own: the tier curve comes from the action's already-resolved outcome band, so a better ending draws a better prize for free. The row exists for what was deliberately not done. Building the dispatcher case by copying the twenty lines out of resolveUnifiedReward yields two draws that agree on the day they are written and diverge the first time either curve is tuned, with nothing to notice — so the draw moved into drawSeededReward and the existing step route moved onto it in the same commit. There is now exactly one seeded draw and two callers of it, and identity is a property of the code rather than a claim a test keeps re-checking. The test asserts it anyway, comparing the dispatcher's prize against a direct call and showing a different seed key draws differently — otherwise "identical" would be satisfied by a draw that ignored its seed; falsified by perturbing the dispatcher's key, which reddens that arm alone. The authoring-time gate is the second arrow: validateRewardDrawPools answers "would this recipe draw anything?" by calling the engine's own category mapping and tag predicate over the seed catalogs, because a gate that reimplemented the mapping would drift from the runtime it guards — which is the exact failure it exists to prevent (THR-844: 66 dead references nobody saw for months, since a dead reference costs nothing at runtime). Tags carry their #; 'weapon' is a well-typed string that matches zero templates. Known limit, recorded rather than papered over: the gate is band-agnostic, so a recipe matching only tier-4 items really is empty at critical_failure — the band is not knowable at authoring time, and the runtime aftermath_reward_draw_empty trace covers the rest.

Spotlight — Where a mortal belongs Audited 2026-08-17 (audit-on-touch, THR-1144). A write whose reader was not missing but present and dead.

  • Authored consequence ⟶ Prerequisite gating — an ending can put someone in a faction, or move them up it, and a later scene can require the rank it gave themLIVE THR-1144. The write half was the easy half. The bulk faction verbs could already scatter or absorb a membership wholesale, and faction_reputation_gain could move an existing member's standing — but nothing could admit or expel one person, so a recruitment ending was a chip over an unwritten edge. membership_change writes member_of through one shared module in the same shape the quest-driven join path writes, so a card-made member is byte-identical to a quest-made one and no existing reader can tell them apart. Falsified in both directions, which is the point: a gate that never fires passes every negative-only test, and that is how this one stayed dead through a prior audit — reverting the context builder leaves "below-rank agent blocked" green and turns "at-rank agent admitted" red. How this map stays true A hand-drawn diagram is a lie waiting to happen. This one is designed to become a projection.

Source-of-truth chain. The contract registry (scripts/interface-contracts.ts, arriving with the interface-map framework ticket) is the single source of truth. The canon page Docs/canon/interface-map.md holds the audited tables and the binding design protocol. This page is the human-readable projection — when the registry lands, its generator takes over the badges here, and a severed contract turns the build red before it can quietly rot this picture.

The rule now: one box per row of the generated systems inventory (Docs/canon/systems-inventory.md, regenerated from the engine), plus the run-start generators (worldgen, encounter seeding) and the cross-cutting capability math. If an inventory row has no box here, that is a bug in this page. Membership was swept against the inventory, the pre-Linear backlog history, and the full Linear archive (ARC team, ~240 done issues) on 2026-07-23.

Attachments, Ambitions, Player casts, Companies & bands, Personality & emergent traits, (from THR-885) the nudge-card dispatch boundary, and (from THR-1292) three seams of Strategic Projects & Control have been contract-audited so far. Three rows, and the rest of the subsystem still gray. Everything drawn in thin gray is orientation — a reasonable sketch of the coupling, not a verified claim. Each future design session that touches a gray system writes its real contract table (audit-on-touch), and a weekly sweep works through the cold ones.

Wired is not live. The four nudge-card rows — the three added by THR-885 (grants dispatching to host systems, cost channels reaching detection and doom, and the hand's runtime filters) plus The Compulsion's decision bias from THR-886 — are each fully wired and test-covered, and each is deliberately not badged 🟢 LIVE. No shipped card authors a grant or a cost channel yet; card content lands under THR-883. Badging a path that nothing travels is precisely the error class this map exists to catch (THR-614), so the rows carry a deferral ticket until the first authored hand ships. A green badge has to mean traffic, not intent — otherwise the map becomes another surface that reads healthy while the thing underneath it is dead.
