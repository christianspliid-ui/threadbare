Systems & Interfaces

The world is a web of systems feeding systems. The richness of a run lives in these arrows — and when one is quietly severed, a feature leaks out of the game without any test noticing.

The world at a glance Every box is a living subsystem — one box for every row of the generated systems inventory, plus the run-start generators. Arrows are what one system does to another, at design level.

verified live leaked — broken, repair ticketed partial — works, but a stated gap orientation only — not yet audited

Thin gray arrows are orientation, not verified contracts — each gets promoted (or corrected) as its subsystem is audited. The two audited systems are drawn in color. Far fewer arrows exist than in the real game: only the strongest couplings are drawn, and unlisted ones are absence of audit, not absence of connection.

Why this map exists Tests assert that a system writes its data; almost nothing asserts that another system still reads it. That gap is where five attachment contracts quietly died while every test stayed green. The audited truth lives in Docs/canon/interface-map.md; this page is its picture.

Spotlight — Attachments Audited 2026-07-23. The engine is alive (items are granted, held, displayed) — but half its outbound promises were severed.

  • Encounters ⟶ Attachments — victories and disasters grant items, blessings, wounds, debtsLIVE
  • Attachment ⟶ Action resolution — what you carry shapes the dice when you actLIVE
  • Attachment ⟶ Traits while held — a treasure map makes you a ruin-seekerLIVE
  • Attachment ⟶ Character sheet — the Attachments tab shows what an agent carriesLIVE
  • Attachment ⟶ Capability tiers — a legendary blade should make its bearer visibly mightierLIVE Restored 2026-07-24 (THR-718). Items carry a stat_contribution effect on the effects[] substrate; collectStatContributions feeds an additive per-Reach term into computeRawScore's possesses/bonded_to walk, through the same sigmoid as traits. A dot-scale magnitude indicator sits beside the capability tier on the character sheet.
  • Attachment ⟶ On-use consequences — swords that shatter, curses that bite on a critical failureLIVE Closed 2026-07-25 (THR-719). The orphaned attachmentTriggers.ts resolver and the onUseTriggers field it read are deleted, not adopted — wiring them would have built a second resolution path beside effects[]. On-use behavior is now the production-wired action_trigger primitive, extended with outcome-band events (a blade snaps on a critical failure, not any loss), a seeded probability guard, item-behavior payloads (condition_grant, condition_remove, self_remove), and authored prose that reaches the aftermath feed. All nine authored triggers ported; checkAndFireActionTriggers fires them from resolution, the orchestrator, and movement, and applyActionTriggerPayloads applies them to the graph.
  • Attachment ⟶ Granted abilities — "this item grants cavalry_charge"LIVE Closed 2026-07-26 (THR-737). THR-722 had retired the possession-edge grants[] property and moved its abilities onto effects[] as trait_grant — which relocated the leak rather than closing it, because hasGrantedTrait still had no production caller. A collectGrantedTraits aggregate now feeds the three gates that ask whether an agent has a capability: encounter eligibility (requiredTraits and, symmetrically, blockedByTraits), spell prerequisites, and ambition eligibility. The obvious wiring would have been vacuous — the precedent read site gates on ruin_seeker, which no trait_grant payload grants; the live intersection is master_smith (The Worldforge Anvil grants it, Forge a Legendary Weapon requires it), now asserted directly so re-authoring either side fails loudly. Seed 42 at tick 30 carries 2 agents holding 3 granted traits that no gate had previously consulted.
  • Attachment ⟶ Activated powers — deliberately invoking an artifact's abilityPARKED · THR-720 A real future feature, explicitly parked rather than quietly lost.
  • Tier advancement ⟶ item strength — items grow with their bearers, but currently pump a stat nothing readsPARTIAL · THR-723

  • Ambitions ⟶ Encounter choice — a mortal chasing mastery seeks different scenes than one chasing revengeLIVE
  • Ambitions ⟶ Milestones & completion — drives progress, complete, or get abandoned, with story beatsLIVE

  • World events ⟶ Ambitions — a sacked town mints avengers and refugees; the deed becomes their desire (THR-726)LIVE
  • Minted ambition ⟶ Foreshadowing — the receipt names the origin event: "vengeance for the blighted fields" (THR-726)LIVE
  • Faction ambitions ⟶ Faction action — guilds and orders pursue agendas of their ownLIVE
  • Ambitions ⟶ The player's eye — the character sheet reveals a mortal's primary drive at first meaningful knowledge (THR-721)LIVE THR-721 (2026-07-24) lowered the primary-ambition gate — one meaningful exposure, or recognition-level knowledge, now surfaces the drive (was: two interactions or "known"). Spotlight — Player casts Audited 2026-07-25 (audit-on-touch, THR-728). The god's own actions finally run on the world's resolution machinery instead of beside it.

  • Authored step difficulty ⟶ Player resolution — the price a content author wrote into a card is finally chargedLIVE Restored 2026-07-25 (THR-728). 84 of 138 ascendant-castable templates carry authored difficulties (0.1–0.65); the player branch of resolveUncontestedStep discarded every one of them behind an unconditional auto-success early-return. That return is now gated behind PLAYER_CAST_VARIANCE_ENABLED and casts fall through to the shared capability-vs-difficulty path. targetActions.ts reads the same field to render the card's risk line, so the number the author wrote is what the player is warned about.
  • Player resolution ⟶ Divine Receipt — the receipt reports how a cast landed, not merely that it didLIVE THR-727 built the six-band receipt; until THR-728 only two bands were reachable for a player, because no roll happened. All six frame lines were pre-authored and needed no change — the bands simply began to vary. A player cast is floored at success-at-cost, so setback and catastrophe stay unreachable by construction.
  • Ascendant affinities ⟶ Cast capability — the god's innate aptitude reaches the rollLIVE Found and closed in the same pass (THR-728). The ascendant node carries domainAffinities (2–5, a reach ranking weight); mortals carry domainCapabilities (10–40) on the raw scale the capability sigmoid consumes. Read literally, every god rolled at capability 0.02 and 94% of casts landed on one band — measured over 400 seeds. The player path now maps affinity onto the raw scale via ascendantCastRawBonus + computeCapabilityWithRawBonus. Deliberately not folded into computeRawScore, so THR-613's Deepening tier-crossings keep reading the score they were tuned against; a test pins that separation.

Spotlight — Companies & bands Audited 2026-07-24/25 (audit-on-touch, THR-74 / THR-731). Companies were built to act together; the later contracts make them act against something — and make that legible.

  • Company ⟶ Action resolution — the best-suited companion acts and the others assist, cappedLIVE THR-74. resolveGroupStep substitutes the member strongest in the step's Reach and adds a capped assist, so a company of ten is not ten times a lone agent. A weak leader resolves above his own solo capability; an emptied company falls back to the individual path without throwing.
  • Company ⟶ Band opposition — a company that walks into a band fights it, as one contested pairLIVE THR-731 PR 2. Pairing is hex-granular and rides opposingGroupId — a field THR-74's plan named and never shipped (zero hits across src/ at implementation time), so this row lands with symbols on both sides rather than as a deferred intention. The band's counter-action is synthesized and transient; both sides then price their step through the same company math above, which is what stops a six-strong band from fighting exactly as hard as its leader alone. Proven organically on a 150-tick run (seed 42): a Temple defender band met two separate companies, lost a named member, and disbanded.
  • Contested resolution ⟶ Chronicle & receipt — losing a fight reads differently from merely failingLIVE Found and closed in the same pass. contested_won/contested_lost shipped with the contested-pair system (TB-044) and carried display strings in ChapterView, a receipt severity mapping, and an isActionSuccess branch — with zero producers. The contested branch resolved through plain success/failure, so the vocabulary the UI was already built to speak was never spoken. Group contests are its first producer.
  • Group grudge ⟶ The mortal sheet — blood between companies is legible without a trace viewerLIVE THR-731 PR 4. The hostile_to edge already had faction-scale readers; this is its first group-scale player-facing consumer, so the row extends a contract rather than adding one. agentDetail reads both edge directions off the company node and dedupes the mutual pair the contest writes, and the Company section renders it as one sentence — "There is blood between them and The Errant Keys of The Arcane Circle" — never a count and never the since tick. Also fixed in this pass: The Guild Falls and encounter.band_defend both gated on sublocation-type.guildhall, an id worldgen never mints (it mints guild-hall), so the capstone confrontation and the band's own counter-encounter were unreachable by construction on every seed while the suite stayed green. A derived content-integrity test now fails on any template gating on an unmintable sublocation.
  • Reunite ⟶ Draw Together's convergence — a god calling a dead company back does not invent a new kind of pullLIVE THR-732. The contract is the property triple, not a function: reunite_company writes the same convergePullHexCol/_HexRow/_UntilTick keys that computeConvergenceBonus already reads, so the scattered bend their own encounter choices homeward through machinery that shipped with THR-74. Writing them under any other name would leave Reunite completely inert while every test that only checked "a window opened" still passed — which is why the property names are the mechanism named here. Falsified during authoring: renaming the written key fails the guard, so it is not vacuous.
  • Dissolution ⟶ Who once rode — the record of a company outlives the companyLIVE THR-732, and the trap that would have shipped a dead verb. Reunite's plan named the disbanded company's roster as its input, and that property genuinely exists, is documented, and is persisted — but dissolveGroup sets roster: [] on the way out, so a faithful implementation finds nobody, silently, forever, on exactly the input the verb takes. What survives is the member_of edges dissolution stamps with leftAtTick rather than removing. The general rule this earns: for anything read after an entity's death, grepping the property tells you it is real — only reading the teardown path tells you whether it is still populated.
  • Sunder window ⟶ Company decay — quarrels bite harder, people leave sooner, the drama starts earlyLIVE THR-732. Bless's exact mirror, read at three sites through isGroupSundered: dissent hits doubled (groupCohesion), leave probability doubled for a fraying company (groupDissolution), and the fray drama pool forced open whatever the true cohesion band says (phaseGroups) — so the player sees scenes rather than a falling gauge. Bless and Sunder do not cancel: both windows may be open at once and each is read independently, with blessing suppressing dissent first for as long as it lasts. The leave multiplier scales the rate inside the existing per-tick ceiling, so a sundering reaches that ceiling sooner but never raises it — a company leaves in ones and twos, which is the shape the drama needs.

Spotlight — Factions & standing Audited 2026-07-26 (audit-on-touch, THR-805). A guild ladder that ranked its members and gated nothing.

  • Guild rank ⟶ Encounter eligibility — a guild's senior work asks what you are to the guildLIVE THR-805, and the row is interesting for what it replaced. FACTION_ENCOUNTER_META.minRank names the rank tier a template demands, authored on roughly 150 metas and typed at types/faction.ts:72 — with no production reader at all, so every tier-restricted guild template was drawable by any agent standing in the right hall. filterByPrerequisites now consults it through meetsFactionRankRequirement for the senior/elite/leadership tiers (RANK_GATED_QUEST_TYPES); the 123 standard metas stay open, because minRank is a required field and gating on its presence would have shut the entry tier behind mere membership. Rank is recomputed from member_of.reputation on every check rather than read from the edge's cached rank/role, which refresh only when a tier boundary is crossed. Two adjacent substrates were rejected and are recorded here so a later reader hunting for a rank gate does not revive them: the faction_rank: predicate in effectPredicates.ts reads agentNode.properties.factionRank, a node property nothing writes — grep the assignment side and every other hit is a local display string — so it is permanently 0 and false for any threshold; and FactionRankTier.encounterAccess, a per-tier prefix allowlist, is equally unread and already drifted, declaring mc_trade.* where the Merchant Consortium's templates are mct.*. Non-vacuous by measured reach: 60 rank-gated metas exist, exactly 12 sit in a live tick-150 seed-42 cache — the guild tail THR-779/THR-803 rescued — and a live sweep shows the gate closed for a real low-rank member and open for that same member once promoted. Six of those 12 require a reputation the economy does not currently reach (THR-810), and the gate deliberately does not depend on getAnyEncounterById, which cannot resolve these ids at all and therefore leaves the sibling trait, broken and group gates inert for them (THR-811).

Spotlight — The nudge & the broken mortal Audited 2026-07-26 (audit-on-touch, THR-773). The first contract slice for two ⚪ UNAUDITED subsystems — Encounters & Dilemmas and Spheres & Quintessence. Their full-subsystem audits remain open; these two rows are what this ticket touched, not a clean bill of health.

  • Authored nudge hand ⟶ Step resolution — the god bends named odds with essence-priced cardsLEAKED THR-773 (WS0). The read side is live and tested: collectNudgeModifiers contributes named nudge:<id> modifiers into resolveUncontestedStep's existing additive channel, and selectActiveRider/applyRider remap the band afterwards taking zero rng draws. It is badged LEAKED anyway, deliberately: no shipped template authors a nudges[] hand, so nothing travels the path yet. Badging it LIVE today would claim behaviour no run can produce — the THR-614 error class this map exists to catch. Authoring is WS1; the row promotes when the first hand ships. Remediation: THR-774.
  • Quintessence threshold ⟶ Candidacy & movement — a mortal worn to nothing goes out of the storyPARTIAL THR-773 (WS0), and the contract's real subject is an absence: the weakened/critical threshold states had shipped since Phase 2 with no behavioural consumer at all, so grinding a mortal down changed a number and nothing else. This adds the first one — isBrokenMortal gates encounter candidacy (all templates except drawableWhileBroken) and adds a broken_drift pull to scoreAndSelect. PARTIAL, not LIVE, and the reason is on purpose: BROKEN_GATE_ENABLED ships false, so both consumers are wired, tested, and inert until WS5 authors the rebuild encounters that let a mortal climb back out. A gate that locks an agent out of every draw with nothing to draw is stun-lock, not story — failure is plot, not punishment. What is live is the erosion scaling and the brokenSince bookkeeping, so telemetry accrues before the gate ever opens. Remediation: THR-778.

Spotlight — Trait gates Audited 2026-07-27 (audit-on-touch, THR-802). One consumer the shared-predicate migration could not see — because the compiler had already given up on it.

  • Trait predicate ⟶ Branching-distance KPI — the tuning readout counts the same gate the filter pipeline enforcesLIVE THR-802, and the row's subject is how a migration misses a site. THR-786 routed six trait vocabularies through the shared collectBearerTraitRefs + bearerMatchesPredicate pair; src/engine/kpi/branchingDistance.ts was a seventh production reader it never saw. Both of its requiredTraits reads sat inside the THR-489 red typecheck baseline as Property 'requiredTraits' does not exist, so the sweep's grep for typed readers passed straight over them and the KPI kept comparing e.target to req.traitId — matching the raw trait node id alone, blind to tag refs, display-name refs and item grants (THR-737). It now resolves through the shared pair like every other site, and gap reads the level backing the matched ref instead of an edge lookup keyed on the raw id. Deliberately no per-agent ref cache, unlike filterByPrerequisites: this path computes one report for one cache entry under kpi:branching-audit and never enters the tick loop, so a cache would have no second reader to serve. That same diagnostic-only reach is why the damage was to the readout rather than to any gate outcome — which is the worse failure of the two, because a tuning number is trusted precisely when nobody re-derives it. Non-vacuous by falsification: the six new tests were run against the pre-fix build and 5 went red, the one green being the negative control, which must pass both ways by construction. The reusable lesson: a red-baseline type error can hide a production read site from a migration sweep — when the baseline is red, grep the errors too, not only the code that compiles.

Spotlight — The land and the altar Audited 2026-07-28 (audit-on-touch, THR-618). Two economies drawn on the same map that had never once touched.

  • Stock tier ⟶ Source sanctity — a god's power is rooted in the land their people workLIVE THR-618, and the row exists because a deferral was read as a finding. The 2026-07-22 Flow Web extraction checkpoint measured zero shared code between the mortal and divine economies — correct grounds to defer the abstraction, and simultaneously the diagnosis that a god's essence and a god's people had nothing to do with each other. A typed source now drifts its sanctity each tick from the stock tiers of the goods at its host location sharing its sphereAffinity, weighted by class baseValue (surplus +1 / adequate 0 / scarce −1), applied inside the controls-edge walk that was already running — no new tick phase, no new node or edge type, no second income channel. The mechanism is the node-prop, not the bridge's own function names. The first draft of this row declared computeSanctitySustenance at the producing phase and pinned itself LEAKED, because a producer has no reason to name its consumer's functions: the boundary is crossed by the value, and the reader sits on the far side of it — the same trap economy-verbs-answered records one row above. That value is the derived per-resource stockTier, deliberately not resourceBalance and not prosperity, so what the Livelihood line shows the player and what the divine economy acts on cannot drift apart. Two asymmetries carry the design, and both are contract-tested rather than left to the constants: upward drift stops at ECON_SANCTITY_NURTURE_CEILING (0.5), deliberately below SANCTITY_FLOWERING_THRESHOLD (0.6) — the land readies a source and only a Sanctify cast flowers it, without which a well-sited shrine would flower unattended and the Build verb would become decoration; withering has no floor above zero and stacks with the contested drain in the same tick, so the world takes away more than it gives, which is what makes tending and defending a source worth the player's attention. Untyped sources are excluded by construction rather than by a default: a migrated, unbuilt place of power carries no sphereAffinity and stays on the legacy alignment-distributed term, preserving THR-611's load-bearing NFP #6 contract that a save which has done nothing keeps exactly its old income — pinned by a 100-tick test on a doubly-surplus location. Non-vacuous by measured values, not symbols: two controlled Spirit shrines over 60 ticks driven through the served browser bundle — the surplus valley climbed 0.30 → 0.50 and stopped at the ceiling, tier still dormant; the scarce valley fell 0.30 → 0.00. Known gap, filed rather than absorbed: the Livelihood sentence reads only a location's own source bag while the engine resolves a sublocation host up one parentLocationId hop, so a shrine or rite on a sublocation drifts invisibly (THR-840) — whose sentence it is when several sublocation sources pull different ways is a voice question, not a wiring one.

How this map stays true A hand-drawn diagram is a lie waiting to happen. This one is designed to become a projection.

Source-of-truth chain. The contract registry (scripts/interface-contracts.ts, arriving with the interface-map framework ticket) is the single source of truth. The canon page Docs/canon/interface-map.md holds the audited tables and the binding design protocol. This page is the human-readable projection — when the registry lands, its generator takes over the badges here, and a severed contract turns the build red before it can quietly rot this picture.

Box membership is a rule, not a judgment call. The first version of this diagram silently dropped nine systems — Culture, Ruins, Stealth, Reputation, Personality, Strategic Projects, Secrets & Favors, Essence, Omens — proving the drift pattern on the drift-prevention page itself. The rule now: one box per row of the generated systems inventory (Docs/canon/systems-inventory.md, regenerated from the engine), plus the run-start generators (worldgen, encounter seeding) and the cross-cutting capability math. If an inventory row has no box here, that is a bug in this page. Membership was swept against the inventory, the pre-Linear backlog history, and the full Linear archive (ARC team, ~240 done issues) on 2026-07-23.

Audited vs. orientation. Attachments, Ambitions, Player casts, Companies & bands, Personality & emergent traits, and (from THR-885) the nudge-card dispatch boundary have been contract-audited so far. Everything drawn in thin gray is orientation — a reasonable sketch of the coupling, not a verified claim. Each future design session that touches a gray system writes its real contract table (audit-on-touch), and a weekly sweep works through the cold ones.

Wired is not live. The three nudge-card rows added by THR-885 — grants dispatching to host systems, cost channels reaching detection and doom, and the hand's runtime filters — are each fully wired and test-covered, and each is deliberately not badged 🟢 LIVE. No shipped card authors a grant or a cost channel yet; card content lands under THR-883. Badging a path that nothing travels is precisely the error class this map exists to catch (THR-614), so the rows carry a deferral ticket until the first authored hand ships. A green badge has to mean traffic, not intent — otherwise the map becomes another surface that reads healthy while the thing underneath it is dead.

What your feedback looks like. This map is a design instrument, not just a debugging aid. "That arrow should exist and doesn't" is a feature directive. "That system is an island" is a richness gap — the game's depth lives in coupling, so a box with few arrows is usually the next place to invest. And "I remember this working" pointed at a green arrow is exactly the drift report that catches what automation can't.
