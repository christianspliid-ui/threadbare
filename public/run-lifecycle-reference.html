Game Manual · The Run

A Run, Start to Finish

One world, one lifetime of a god. This is the shape of a whole run — from remembering who you were, through the middle game, to the ending that feeds the next world.

What a run isA single playthrough is the life of one god over one world, beginning to end.

You are an Ascendant — a mortal who climbed the long ladder, transcended, and now looks down at a world still full of mortals on it. You are not the protagonist and you never will be. The protagonists are the mortals below; the whole game lives in the gap between what they choose and what you can lean them toward.

A run carries you from the moment you remember who you were, through your first threads and the middle game, to a Twilight that ends the world one way or another. Every run asks a single question and answers it in the story you leave behind: what kind of god are you?

Remembrance — choosing who you wereBefore the world opens, you assemble the god you became out of the mortal you were.

A run opens in remembrance. You piece together two halves of yourself:

  • The mortal echo — where you came from and what drove you, and the name you carried when you were still one of them.
  • The divine transformation — the hunger that now defines you (Witness, Maker, Sovereign, and others), the sphere that fuels you, and the court you keep.

This identity is a starting taste, not a class. It tilts which mortals catch your eye and which spheres come cheaply — but the world, not the character sheet, reveals whether you keep it. Nothing here locks you into a playstyle; it colours where you begin.

Your two domainsEvery god favours exactly two Reaches, fixed for the entire run.

Of the Eight Reaches — the activities a god acts through — your remembered identity settles on one primary and one secondary domain. These two are permanent for the run. They decide which divine actions ever appear to you: cards that belong to Reaches outside your two domains simply never show, no matter how much power you gather. This is a boundary, not a wall to climb — it is the shape of this god, and a different remembrance next run draws a different pair.

What grows within your domains is your reach — your capability climbs as you act, opening deeper and rarer expressions of the two Reaches you already own.

The opening — The First, and a path that opensA short scripted arc teaches the god's basic gestures and ends with a choice.

The world does not drop you into open play cold. A brief authored spine walks you through a god's first five gestures — each one a small, voiced moment, each one granting the tool it describes:

The First
Of all the souls below, one burns at a pitch only you can hear. You lean close and a single thread spins out — your first bond to a mortal. Whatever kind of god you become is answered first in The First.
The Seat
A god adrift is only weather. You bind a place to yourself and call it your seat — the faith that gathers there rises back to you as power, tide after tide.
A Thing Left Behind
You press a sliver of your nature into a humble object and it wakes — your first artifact, carrying your will into hands you will never touch.
The First Word
A god is known by what it asks of the world. You speak your first word into a mortal mind — not a command, but a pressure, a leaning, a dream they mistake for their own.
A Path Opens
Three shapes of devotion lie before you. The dreamer reads the deep water of sleeping minds; the prophet writes warnings across the waking sky; the patron lifts the worthy higher than they could climb alone. You choose one — and with it receive your primary reach signature, your domain's headline power.
Your second signature comes later

The primary reach signature is granted here, in the opening. The secondary signature — the headline power of your other domain — arrives later in the run as its own story moment, not at the start. Acquiring divine power is itself an event, not a menu.

The middle gameThe long stretch where you play the god you chose to be.

Play settles into the three-beat turn: you scan the world, step into a curated moment, and sit with the aftermath before scanning again. The world is turn-based — the clock only advances when you say so, and one tick is two in-world hours, twelve to a day. Doing nothing is always a valid move.

Across the middle game your threads deepen from distant intuition toward devotion; your essence pools fill from worshippers and places of power and drain into the effects you sustain; your court grows around The First. And two clocks tighten in the background — the middle game is the space you have before one of them runs out.

How a run endsEither clock ends the run. Neither is, structurally, a win or a loss.

Two clocks pressure every run from opposite directions, and whichever lands first ends it:

The Mandate completes

Your declared win-state is satisfied before the Unmaking arrives. The world survives — and you shaped what survives.

The Doom Clock culminates

The Unmaking comes before your Mandate is met. The world is remade or unmade, in the shape of its doom.

Either way, the run passes into the Twilight Phase — its closing chapter. In Twilight you do not act; you witness. The world collapses or transforms, the threads you held resolve, and echoes are harvested from what mattered most: Legacy from mortals whose arcs completed, Monument from places where the world changed, Relic from artifacts you bound deeply.

Both endings are kinds of endings, not kinds of wins

The game is not, structurally, about winning. It is about what kind of being you chose to be toward a world you could not save unconditionally. A run that produced a story you can tell in prose was a good run — whichever clock ran out first.

What carries overThe kind of god you were becomes an ingredient in the next world.

You are not playing for a high score. The echoes harvested in Twilight update the World-Soul that underlies every cycle, and the next run draws on it — thematic weight, biased generation, a world subtly shaped by every god that came before. A pacifist Witness whose run ended in Doom seeds the next world with grief; a triumphal Sovereign who completed a Mandate seeds it with order. The next world is not a sequel — it is a response.

Planned: alongside the World-Soul, a run also feeds a per-account layer of permanent unlocks that widen the run-start picker — the remembrance options you can choose from — so the account, not just the world, accumulates across runs. This metaprogression layer is still being built.

Designer Notes

Implementation status, key surfaces, and canon pointers for the run lifecycle.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced from the rulebook — this page mints none).

Identity & remembrance IMPL

  • AscendantIdentity (src/types/remembrance.ts) carries the mortal echo (mortalName, originFragmentId, driveFragmentId, timeSinceAscension) and the divine transformation (hungerId, sphereAlignment, courtType, mandateDirection, domainAffinities, personalitySeed).
  • The "two domains" are the top-ranked entries in domainAffinities: Partial<Record<ReachDomain, number>> — the primary and secondary Reaches for the run.
  • Dev seeding: DEV_ASCENDANT_IDENTITY (hunger.witness) backs the ?view=game&seeded quick-start; see CLAUDE.md dev URLs.
  • Remembrance flow, ascendant selection, and worldgen are the run's entry points; identity is a starting taste, not a class (rulebook §1).

The opening spine & beat director IMPL

  • ASCENDANT_SPINE (src/data/ascendant-beat-content.ts) — five scripted BeatDefinitions in order: beat.spine.opening (The First), the_seat, thing_left_behind, the_first_word, a_path_opens. Authored copy in SPINE_BEAT_PRESENTATION; surfaced by AscendantBeatModal.tsx.
  • Beat 4 (a_path_opens) is kind: 'selection' — the 1-of-3 god-path choice (dreamer / prophet / patron) — and also carries grantsReachSignature: 'primary'. The two grants are orthogonal: the selection grants the chosen path, the signature slot grants the run's primary reach signature on top (THR-523).
  • The secondary reach signature is a dynamic grantsReachSignature: 'secondary' pool beat, not part of the scripted spine — acquired later in the run (THR-523 / THR-553 / THR-555). Both signatures are resolved from the ascendant's ranked domainAffinities.
  • Beat scheduling & resolution: src/engine/ascendantBeat.ts, src/engine/ascendantBeatSeeding.ts. Types: src/types/ascendantBeat.ts.

Two-domain permanence IMPL

  • The domain boundary is a permanent set-membership filter, not an aspiration: getTargetActionSlots() applies the requiresReach gate (THR-503) so action cards for out-of-domain Reaches never surface. Capability grows within a domain; the pair itself does not change mid-run.
  • Deep drill-down: Action Catalog (real availability buckets) and the divine-actions manual page (W4).

Run end, Twilight & metaprogression DESIGN

  • Both clocks and the Twilight trigger are specified in rulebook §8–§9. The Doom Clock is IMPL (DOOM_CLOCK_ARCHETYPES / DOOM_STAGE_NAMES, src/types/doomClock.ts); the Victory Mandate's three-stage structure is named but not all stages are wired.
  • Twilight, echo harvest (Legacy / Monument / Relic), and World-Soul next-cycle seeding are partial: src/types/worldSoul.ts plus adjacent plans. Sphere aggregation into the soul is the most-wired piece; harvest and next-cycle injection are partial.
  • Per-account permanent unlocks + run-start picker are the metaprogression carryover layer; unlock-model verdicts live in the THR-390 design audit. Not yet built as an account-persistent layer.
