Build the world first.
Find the game inside it.

Product Strategy — Updated 12 April 2026
The Fantasy World Simulator is a systemic god-game where emergent narrative comes from the collision of deep, interconnected systems — not from scripted events. Like CK3 and Stellaris, the simulation comes first. The game emerges from watching systems interact in surprising ways.
Core Philosophy

Four truths about what we're building

01

Simulation before game design

Build a world that behaves coherently when nobody's watching. The "game" is a lens the player points at the simulation — mandates and doom shape each run, but the world underneath is the real product.

02

Emergent stories, not scripted ones

The best moments will be ones we never designed. A guild war escalating into a siege because one faction hired mercenaries. A trade empire collapsing because the World-Soul shifted. Systems surprising each other.

03

Prose over numbers

The player reads stories, not spreadsheets. Every mechanical interaction surfaces through narrative prose. Numbers exist under the hood for tunability and determinism, but the player sees a world of language.

04

Three imperfect systems beat one perfect one

Depth comes from interaction, not polish. A cosmic balance system, a conflict system, and an economy don't need to be individually perfect — they need to surprise each other when they collide.

The world awaits
Development Sequence

Three milestones to a living world

Each milestone makes the world feel more alive and gives the player more meaningful choices. Doom and mandates wait — they shape runs, but the world must exist first.

M1 First

World-Soul Connection

Core complete
The world breathes

Every entity in the world tracks its connection to all eight creation spheres. Player actions, agent behavior, doom progression, and sustained control effects create sphere pressure that builds up or erodes entity scores. Magic is sphere fluency — no separate system. The world resists change through homeostasis, and the player must push hard to overcome its inertia.

The sphere pressure engine is operational: six upstream sources push pressure, opposition cancellation and allied defense create natural tension, and the triangle number scale provides built-in homeostasis. Downstream, sphere scores modify prosperity, shift encounter scoring, and influence agent decisions. The World-Soul emerges from the aggregate of all entity scores.

Delivered: Per-entity sphere affinity (triangle number scale) · Sphere pressure resolution engine (6 upstream sources) · Downstream modifiers (prosperity, encounters, agent decisions) · Magic as sphere fluency with overchannel · IPK prose keywords · WorldSoulIndicator · Debug panel Sphere State tab · 109 sphere tests
M2 Second

Conflict & Destruction

Engine complete
The world breaks

The full conflict engine is operational. Mercenary companies form within factions. Faction ambitions drive war goals. Armies spawn, march across the hex map with quintessence attrition, and engage in multi-step battle resolution. Sieges escalate over ticks with sortie, negotiation, and assault options. Victorious armies sack settlements, destroying sublocations, tanking prosperity, and generating refugees. The aftermath reshapes the world.

This is where emergent storytelling gets visceral. A guild war that escalates into a siege because one faction hired mercenaries and the other called in allies — that's the kind of story players screenshot and share. Monster lairs now escalate through sphere feedback, and agents can be divinely targeted to confront them.

Delivered: Mercenary company + faction ambition system · Army entities with spawning encounters · Army movement + quintessence attrition · Battle resolution with spotlight encounters · Siege resolution (multi-tick, escalating) · Location destruction + aftermath + refugees · Monster encounters (lairs, escalation, sphere feedback, divine targeting) · Armies debug tab · 102+ conflict tests
M3 Third

Dynamic Economy

Design phase
The world trades

Connect encounters, factions, and player actions to the prosperity/wealth/trade systems. Now that sphere balance affects prosperity and armies can destroy infrastructure, the economy inherits real drama from the other two pillars. It won't need artificial tension — it reacts to real events.

Eight phases from low-effort high-impact (encounter → economy feedback) through to deep systemic connections (resource scarcity, guild activation, wealth-spending crossovers between Reaches). Design docs are in progress.

Planned: Encounter ↔ economy feedback loops · Wealth spending across Reaches · Trade route lifecycle · Unrest from inequality · Active guilds · Gold+Stone player CRUD actions · Resource scarcity

Strategic DNA

Principles that don't change

When you feel lost, come back here. These are the load-bearing decisions that define what this project is and isn't.

CK3/Stellaris model
The simulation comes first. The "game" emerges from deep systems interacting. We build the world, then discover the fun inside it. We don't design fun and bolt systems onto it.
Graph-native
Everything is a node or an edge. No relational tables, no special-cased entity types. Armies, trade routes, curses, alliances — all graph structures. This is settled and load-bearing.
World-Soul as backbone
The World-Soul is the connective tissue. Every action shifts sphere balance, which ripples into everything else. Without it, systems are isolated islands. With it, they form a living ecosystem.
Destruction matters
Building is only compelling when loss is possible. Armies sack cities. Doom erodes infrastructure. Trade routes get severed. The player builds things they might lose, and that's what makes building meaningful.
Prose, not numbers
The player reads stories, never spreadsheets. Domain word scales replace stats. Encounters are narratives, not dice rolls. Numbers exist under the hood for tunability and determinism, never for display.
Doom & mandates wait
They're the game-shaping layer — they make each run different. But they need a living world to shape. Build the world first, then tune the lenses that make each playthrough unique.
Collision is the goal
Three imperfect systems surprising each other create more emergent narrative than one perfect system. We optimize for interaction surface between systems, not depth within any single one.
Economy as third pillar
Cosmos, conflict, commerce. Three forces that generate story when they collide. Economy inherits drama from the other two — it doesn't need to manufacture its own tension.
