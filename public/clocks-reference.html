Doom, the Mandate & Omens

Two clocks pressure every run from opposite directions — one counting down to an Unmaking that will arrive, one counting up toward the world you declared you would make. The run is the space between them, and the omens are that pressure made visible.

Two clocks, opposite directionsDoom counts down to a catastrophe that always lands. The Mandate counts up toward your declared win. You never have essence enough for both.

A run in Threadbearer is not open-ended. From the first tick, two clocks are already running, and they pull against each other:

Counts down · always lands

The Doom Clock

An Unmaking is coming. It has a shape — one of seven catastrophes — and it escalates through five thematic stages until it arrives. Your interventions can slow it. The rival gods' interventions speed it. Either way, it lands.

Counts up · your win path

The Victory Mandate

Your declared win-state — a shape you want the world to take before the end. It advances through three stages as you push the world toward it. Reach it before Doom culminates, and you shaped what survived.

The tension is the whole game. Pushing the Mandate harder spends essence that could have decelerated Doom. Bracing against a Doom escalation eats turns you would have spent advancing the Mandate. There is one pool of divine fuel and two demands on it, and you will never satisfy both cleanly. That is the point.

The Doom Clock — seven shapes of an endingEvery run's doom is one of seven archetypes, each named for the shape of its catastrophe. The archetype colours everything the clock does.

At worldgen your run is given a doom archetype — the particular way this world is going to end if nothing stops it. There are seven, and the archetype is not cosmetic: it decides the flavour of every escalation, the sphere the ending is aligned to, and the language the world uses as the shadow lengthens.

Breach

An outside force breaking through reality.

Strange Whispers → The Breach

Convergence

All forces drawn toward a single point.

Distant Pull → The Singularity

Changing

A new cosmic order replacing the old.

Old Winds Die → The New Order

Sundering

The world itself breaking apart.

Hairline Fractures → The Sundering

Failing

A core force of creation weakening.

Waning Light → The Failing

Ascension

Something climbing toward godhood.

Mortal Spark → The Ascension

Reckoning

Past debts coming due.

Old Debts Surface → The Reckoning

Whatever its shape, a doom escalates through the same five-stage arc — each archetype simply names the stages in its own voice:

Whispers → Signs → Tremors → Crisis → Culmination

Each stage transition is not just a change of mood — it fires a world event. Reality frays, a settlement's fortune shocks, corruption creeps across hexes, threaded mortals feel the pressure. These are real changes to the world, flavoured by the archetype's sphere: a Breach thins the barriers between realms; a Reckoning sharpens memory and gathers witnesses. The clock does not merely warn you it is coming — it changes the world on the way.

You cannot stop it — only shape how you meet it

The Doom Clock is not a boss you defeat. It is the horizon of the run. You can slow its approach, blunt a given escalation, and choose what you protect while it closes — but a run that lets the clock run out is not a run you failed. It is a run that ended the other way. The question the clock keeps asking is never "can you win?" It is "who were you, in the time you had?"

The Victory Mandate — the world you meant to makeYour declared win-state, advancing through three stages. Fulfil it before Doom culminates and the world survives in a shape you chose.

Against the countdown runs your Victory Mandate — the win path. You declare (or have declared for you, out of who you remembered being) a goal for the world: a sphere brought to dominance, a court raised, a configuration of the world made real. It is a graph-state goal with a clock of its own, running in parallel to Doom.

Every mandate moves through the same three stages, from declaration to fulfilment:

Setup

The intent is declared and the first ground is laid. The world begins to lean your way.

Escalation

The push in earnest — the world visibly bends toward the shape you named, checkpoint by checkpoint.

Culmination

The declared state is reached. If Doom has not yet arrived, the world survives — and it survives as yours.

You are never told a raw percentage. The Mandate surfaces as a sense of the world tilting: a sphere rising through the reaches, a court thickening around you, an old order giving way. The mandate's shape is drawn from the god you remembered being at the start of the run — a builder's mandate is not a sovereign's — so pursuing it is also an expression of who you are.

One pool, two clocksThe clocks are not independent. How you fare against the Mandate feeds directly back into how hard Doom bites.

The two clocks talk to each other. The Mandate lays down checkpoints pinned to the Doom Clock's own progress — moments where the world takes stock of whether you are keeping pace. Beat a checkpoint handily and you bank against the dark: the surplus softens the next doom escalation when it lands. Fall short of one, and the arrears are added to the next escalation's bite — a doom you neglected arrives harder.

Outperform a checkpoint → Bank a counter-omen → Next doom escalation softened
Fall behind a checkpoint → Severity owed → Next doom escalation bites harder

This is the exchange rate of the whole game made concrete. There is no separate "doom meter" you top up in isolation — the way you spend your attention on the Mandate is your relationship with Doom. Race the win too hard and you arrive at each escalation unbraced; hunker down against Doom and the Mandate slips out of reach. The good runs live in the narrow band where both are barely survivable at once.

Omens — the pressure made visibleBetween the big escalations, the world speaks in omens: short atmospheric tracks that colour the mood, tilt what happens, and tell you which way the wind is turning.

You do not read the clocks off a dial. You read them off the world — and the world's chief instrument for that is the omen. An omen is a short-lived thematic pressure line, a run of atmosphere that lasts a handful of days and then passes. At any moment the world can carry up to two: a primary omen — usually an echo of the doom you are living under, or a seasonal hush when doom is quiet — and an optional secondary omen when some sphere surges or a culture strains.

Omens are not only decoration. While one runs, it tilts the world: it makes some kinds of moment more likely to surface and others less, and a surging sphere presses gently on the balance of the cosmos. And when a doom stage turns over, its omens turn with it — the old atmosphere is swept aside for the new, so the mood of the world always matches the hour of its ending.

Three registers of the same pressure

The clocks make themselves felt in three ways, softest to loudest: omens (ambient mood tracks that bias and colour), escalation events (the world-changing cards a doom stage fires), and — for the mortal closest to you — journey beats, the chapters of The First's hero's journey, which are themselves scheduled against the Doom Clock. As the clock deepens, your First's story deepens with it. The horizon and the hero move together.

Both endings make the next worldWhichever clock runs out, the run does not simply stop. It resolves into an ending — and the ending seeds what comes after.

A run ends one of two ways, and neither is a game-over screen. If the Mandate completes, the world survives in the shape you chose. If the Doom Clock culminates first, the Unmaking arrives and the world is remade or unmade after its archetype. Both are kinds of endings, not kinds of wins.

Either way, the ending flows into the Twilight — the sequence where you no longer act, only witness. What mattered most is harvested into echoes, the World-Soul remembers, and the next world is shaped as a response to this one. A run that ended in grief seeds the next world with grief; a triumphant mandate seeds it with order. You are never playing for a score. You are playing for the kind of god you were this run to become an ingredient in the next.

Neither clock is a fail state

It is worth saying plainly: losing the race to Doom is not losing the game. The game is not, structurally, about winning — it is about what kind of being you chose to be toward a world you could not save unconditionally. The run that produced a story you can tell was a good run, whichever clock ran out first.

The Doom Clock IMPL

  • Seven archetypes — DOOM_CLOCK_ARCHETYPES = breach, convergence, changing, sundering, failing, ascension, reckoning (src/types/doomClock.ts:13). Each carries a one-line description in the type and a full card blueprint (5 stage cards + climax variants, sphere-aspected) in src/engine/doomClock.ts:63–589: breach→entropy, convergence→force, changing→chaos, sundering→force, failing→time, ascension→spirit, reckoning→mind.
  • Five generic stages — DOOM_STAGE_NAMES = ['Whispers','Signs','Tremors','Crisis','Culmination'] (src/types/doomClock.ts:19). Each archetype renames them thematically in src/data/doom/<archetype>.json (e.g. breach: Strange Whispers → Reality Cracks → The Thinning → Barriers Fail → The Breach). Default stage thresholds are a uniform [0.20, 0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.0] fraction of totalTicks (DEFAULT_THRESHOLDS, loaded from JSON via src/data/doom-content.ts).
  • Escalation events carry a systemic payload: DoomCardEffectType ∈ {hex_corruption, hex_divine_drain, location_unrest, prosperity_shock, location_pressure, agent_pressure} against a DoomCardTargetKind ∈ {random_hexes, settlements, prosperous_settlements, all_locations, threaded_agents} (doomClock.ts:23–36). Magnitudes are tuned in src/data/game-config.ts: DOOM_CARD_HEX_TARGET_COUNT 6, DOOM_CARD_HEX_DELTA 0.18, DOOM_CARD_UNREST_DELTA 12, DOOM_CARD_PROSPERITY_DELTA −8, DOOM_CARD_PRESSURE_MAGNITUDE 3.
  • Progression & severity (src/engine/phaseDoom.ts): currentTick advances by tickModifier (default 1.0, floor 0.1) — player interventions decelerate, rival interventions accelerate; progress = currentTick / totalTicks. Each escalation resolves at severity = max(DOOM_MIN_SEVERITY 0.5, 1 + penalty − counterOmensSpent × DOOM_COUNTER_OMEN_SOFTENING 0.5). Determinism (NFP #3): seeded card draws, no Math.random(); traces on stage transition + card resolution.
  • Doom identity milestones — src/engine/doomIdentityMilestones.ts flips triggered=true and emits a doom_milestone trace when progress crosses a milestone threshold. The trigger hook is IMPL; the milestone matrix population (which milestones exist, per identity) is DESIGN.

The Victory Mandate IMPL (sphere-growth) · DESIGN (narrative type)

  • Three stages — MandateStage = 'setup' | 'escalation' | 'culmination' (src/types/mandate.ts:15); four MandateTypes (graph_state, narrative, sphere_dominance, simulation_achievable). Twelve authored templates in src/data/mandate-content.ts (MANDATE_TEMPLATES + MANDATE_MILESTONE_PROSE).
  • Two runtime evaluators (src/engine/phaseMandate.ts): the live path is sphere_growth (remembrance-driven) — it compares worldSoul.aggregate.totalBySphere for the primary/secondary spheres against run-start baselines and advances setup→escalation→culmination by checkpoints passed. The legacy graph-condition evaluator (src/engine/mandate.ts) is IMPL but rarely selected. The narrative type has no evaluator (type:'custom' always returns false) — DESIGN.
  • Targets & court shapes (src/data/game-config.ts): MANDATE_PRIMARY_TARGET_DELTA 0.18, MANDATE_SECONDARY_TARGET_DELTA 0.10 — the fractional sphere growth required for culmination. A court-shaped secondary objective (web_relationships / circle_retinue / high_house_settlements / abyss_anomalies, one of four MandateCourtTypes) tracks alongside.

Clock interplay — wired, tuning open IMPL mechanism · OPEN tuning

  • The doom↔mandate feedback is live code, not a design sketch: phaseMandate.ts:334–339 writes counterOmens and nextEscalationSeverityModifier straight into state.doomClock. Checkpoints fire at MANDATE_CHECKPOINT_THRESHOLDS = [0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 1.00] of doom progress. A checkpoint passed and exceeded (by ≥ MANDATE_COUNTER_OMEN_MARGIN 0.02) banks counterOmens += 1; a missed checkpoint adds severityPenalty += 1. Banked counter-omens soften the next escalation via DOOM_COUNTER_OMEN_SOFTENING 0.5 in the severity formula above.
  • What is un-tuned (rulebook Open Q #5): the exchange-rate constants themselves — the softening factor, the penalty/reward magnitudes, the checkpoint deltas — are held as design discipline pending real playtest/KPI data. The mechanism is wired; the balance dial is not turned. This page references that open question; it mints none.

The Omen Agenda IMPL

  • Two slots, four categories — OmenState holds a primary + optional secondary ActiveOmen plus capped history (OMEN_MAX_HISTORY 20). Categories (OmenCategory): doom_echo (primary — matched to the run's archetype + doom stage), seasonal (primary fallback when doom is quiet), sphere_surge and cultural (secondary, trigger-gated). Templates in src/data/omenTemplates.ts.
  • Effects & selection (src/engine/phaseOmenAgenda.ts): omens bias encounter-type scoring (capped at OMEN_ENCOUNTER_BIAS_CAP 0.4) and a sphere_surge presses per-tick sphere pressure (capped OMEN_SPHERE_PRESSURE_CAP 0.05, gated by OMEN_SPHERE_DOMINANCE_THRESHOLD 0.25). First activation at OMEN_FIRST_ACTIVATION_TICK 3; secondary needs score ≥ OMEN_SECONDARY_THRESHOLD 0.4; a doom stage transition force-expires the primary omen (OMEN_DOOM_STAGE_FORCE_EXPIRE = true). Beats emit on interval (OMEN_BEAT_INTERVAL_DEFAULT 3).
  • Emitted omens (THR-115) are a distinct, aftermath-spawned kind: EmittedOmen with global/regional/local scope, decayed by the registered emittedOmenDecayPhase; they bias encounters via EMITTED_OMEN_ENCOUNTER_AFFINITY. Content gap flagged honestly: doom_echo omen tracks are authored for six archetypes but Ascension is deferred (THR-79), and 6 of 12 sphere_surge tracks are stubs.

Tick-loop wiring IMPL

  • doomPhase runs in the pre-doom slot and mandatePhase in post-narrative (both registered in src/engine/phases/index.ts). emittedOmenDecayPhase is registered in post-doom. The main phaseOmenAgenda still runs inline in src/engine/orchestrator.ts:2006 (not yet migrated to the phase registry — a known pre-registry holdover, not a bug). For the full tick order see Tick Cycle Reference.
  • Determinism & fail-soft (NFPs #3–#4): all clock/omen draws are seeded; a missing doom definition or malformed omen template falls through to the seasonal/no-op path rather than throwing — the tick loop never crashes on clock state.
