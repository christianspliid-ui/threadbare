Game Manual · Who They Are

Traits & Marks

A trait is a name the world can recognise. It does nothing on its own — its whole power is that other things read it, and react. This is how the world comes to treat a mortal as somebody in particular rather than as anybody at all.

A trait is a name, not a bonusNothing happens because a mortal has a trait. Things happen because something else reads it.

When a smith survives three battles and comes out the other side Battle-Hardened, nothing changes in that instant. No number moves. What has changed is that the world now has a word for them — and every system that cares about that word can now find them by it.

A recruiter looking for veterans reads it. A tavern brawl offers a line only a hardened fighter would take. A rival god's scheme picks its mark by it. The trait is inert; the reaction is everything. That separation is deliberate: it means any part of the game can start caring about a trait without the trait itself having to know about that part of the game.

The rule that makes traits legible

Whenever a trait changes what happens, the game says which trait did it. A hidden modifier that quietly tilts an outcome is not a trait reaction — it is noise. If a mortal's Silver Tongue opened a door, the door says so.

Ten kinds of markThe kind of a trait is a contract about its life: how it is earned, how it is lost, and when it speaks.

Traits are not a flat list. Each belongs to one of ten kinds, and the kind decides the trait's whole lifecycle — every condition is temporary in the same way, every scar is permanent in the same way. An individual trait only supplies the flavour.

Most kinds record something that has already happened. Destiny is the one that points forward — a promise, waiting to be kept. It is declared and reserved, but nothing in the game mints or reads a destiny yet; it is named here so the word is settled before the content that will fill it is written.

How a trait is earnedA trait is a discrete identity minted when something continuous crosses a line — and held there, so it does not flicker.

Beneath the traits are the sliding scales the simulation actually tracks: a mortal's moral axes, their standing with a faction, their skill in a Reach, their wealth. Those move constantly. A trait is what happens when one of them travels far enough to deserve a name.

a scale drifts→ crosses a threshold→ a trait is minted→ systems can now read it→ it deepens, fades, or is lost

The scale stays underneath and keeps moving; the trait is the part the world can talk about. This is why a trait is worth more than the number that produced it — Feared is a thing a village can act on, where a hostility score is not.

A trait can also deepen. Reinforce a mastery often enough and it grows; leave it alone and it slips back down and eventually falls away. The game never shows you the level as a number — it shows you a stronger word.

Four ways the world reactsEvery place a trait matters is one of four shapes — and each one names the trait out loud.

It opens or closes a door

An option, an encounter, or an action that only exists for someone carrying the trait — or that is barred to someone carrying the wrong one.

It shifts the odds

The trait tilts a step's forecast or difficulty, and says so on the forecast line: the player sees which mark is doing the work.

It deals a card only they could play

A nudge that enters the hand for this mortal and no other, because of what they are.

It colours the telling

Prose that only appears because the trait is present — the world describing them as what they have become.

A trait reaction always belongs to the moment it is part of. It shades the scene in front of you; it never interrupts with a notification of its own. One story at a time.

What you are allowed to knowSome marks are plain on sight. Some must be discovered. None that you know are ever hidden from you.

A trait can be public (obvious to anyone), discoverable (learnable through play), or divine only (visible to you, and not to the mortals). The same trait can be plain on one bearer and secret on another.

Visible once known

Visibility decides whether you have found out yet — never whether a mark you have already found gets shown to you. Once a trait is known, it is listed in that mortal's own panel, always. There is no state where the game knows you know and still hides it.

What a trait never doesBeing interesting and being capable are different things, and traits are not allowed to blur them.

A trait may steer what a mortal reaches for — which encounters attract them, which ambitions they take up. That is selection, and personality traits do exactly that and nothing more: a cruel mortal seeks out cruel opportunities, but is not thereby better at cruelty.

Competence is separate and deliberately small. Where a trait does contribute to how well someone performs — a mastery, or a hard-won reputation — it does so through a narrow, capped channel, never as an open-ended bonus. The ceiling is the point: a mortal with a shelf of impressive marks is a mortal with a rich story, not a mortal who cannot fail.

Words, never numbers

Trait strength is never shown as a numeral. The game says Tempered, not level 3. If you can read a number off a trait anywhere in the interface, that is a bug on this page's contract.

Designer Notes

The graph substrate and bearer legality, the ten categories as lifecycle contracts with shipped definition counts, the unified ref/predicate resolution across six read sites, which producers mint what, and the honest state of authored hook content — including the dead-ref defect that has kept most trait gates permanently false.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question. Architecture authority for this page is Docs/plans/2026-07-26-traits-trigger-architecture.md; terminology authority is the UL Traits shard. Where any surface disagrees, the UL wins on terms.

Substrate IMPL

  • Definitions are nodes, assignments are edges. A trait definition is a type: 'trait' graph node whose properties are typed TraitDefinitionProperties (src/types/traits.ts); a bearer holds one through a has_trait edge. Relationships are edges, never property fields — the property-vs-edge rule applies here as everywhere.
  • The assignment carries the per-bearer state (TraitAssignmentProperties): level, acquiredTick, lastReinforcedTick, source, visibility, optional ticksRemaining, optional modifiers. The definition is shared across every bearer; only the edge is per-bearer. ticksRemaining is the live countdown the condition decay path decrements — distinct from the authored durationTicks provenance value, which is never decremented (THR-761).
  • Bearer legality is fixed by the edge schema — has_trait.sourceNodeType = ['actor', 'location', 'sublocation'] (src/types/edgeSchema.ts:67). That covers every ActorType (individual, group, faction, culture, god, ascendant) plus places. Artifacts and reified relationships are named as bearers by the architecture but are not schema-legal yet — each is a one-line additive sourceNodeType extension landing with its own wave. DESIGN
  • The field is subcategory, not category. Code reading properties.category reads undefined; that exact dead read kept reputation titles from ever rendering until THR-787 repaired it. Worth stating because the name reads wrong.
  • A definition must be in the graph before anything can point at it. has_trait is an edge, and addEdge rejects a missing target — so an effect naming a definition that was never inserted takes a fail-soft branch and silently no-ops. That is not hypothetical: until THR-809, the mastery and condition definitions reached the graph only through phaseEncounterTraits.ensureTraitNodes, called from inside the orchestrator's loop over the legacy state.encounterProgress collection — which the unified-action pipeline no longer populates. The loop body never ran, trait.condition.wounded did not exist, and every condition_attachment / apply_condition effect naming a definition-file condition hit template_missing. A lost tavern brawl wounded nobody. Both families are now seeded at world construction by seedEncounterTraitDefinitions (src/engine/traitDefinitionSeeding.ts), called from seedWorld, so definition presence is an invariant of every graph rather than a race against whichever system fires first.
  • The same silence returns whenever an effect names an id no catalog defines (THR-1171). Seeding fixed the race; it cannot fix a typo or a wish. The apotheosis capstone attached trait.condition.grieving from a critical_failure band while no definition of that id existed, so condition_attachment took the identical template_missing branch and wrote nothing — and the chip above it went on reporting the grief to the player (UI Law 56). What let it survive was a comment: the file stated the conditions were "chosen from the live catalog, not minted", naming wounded and grieving together, and the claim was half true. A sentence that reads as checked is what stops the next reader from checking it. grieving is now a real definition — the vocabulary genuinely lacked a word for loss with no body to bury, and two independent encounters had reached for it. The durable half is the gate: validateNudgeGrantRefs had been checking condition_attachment against CONDITION_TRAIT_DEFINITIONS the whole time — it walked step.nudges[].grants and nothing else, so the entire aftermath surface went unswept and the corpus test reported green about three of the four places an effect can be authored. It now delegates to allTemplateEffects, the same walk the reward_draw gate uses. A gate whose traversal is narrower than the surface it claims to cover reports green about what it cannot see. IMPL
  • Seed per node, never on a first-node probe. Four ensure*TraitNodes helpers used to short-circuit on DEFINITIONS[0] — "if the first exists, they all do". Any foreign path minting that one id (an attachment, a direct assignTrait) then skipped the whole remainder permanently. THR-809 replaced all four with per-node checks, which complete a partially-seeded graph instead of skipping it. phaseEconomicTraits always had the correct shape and was the model.
  • Trade-baron counted routes it could never see. countControlledRoutes reached a route by scanning every actor node's outgoing trades_with edges, on a premise its own comment stated — "trades_with edges are between actors". They are not, and never were: routes join two locations. THR-830 corrected both the traversal and the EDGE_SCHEMA row that had asserted the premise. controlledBy is a property of the route rather than of an endpoint, so reading the family directly is also the simpler expression — the same shape economicPower.ts has always used. The trait is still unreachable in a live world, now for an unrelated reason: its only writer of controlledBy is action.gold.tax-trade-route, one of four trade ops refused at the edge chokepoint (THR-1188).

  • destiny is declared and otherwise inert. It appears in the TraitCategory union and in the agent sheet's display ordering (src/engine/agentDetail.ts:781) and nowhere else. The UL Destiny entry is its first definition anywhere — deliberately written ahead of the content so the wave that fills the category inherits settled wording instead of minting a rival term. DESIGN
  • Decay is mastery-only. processTraitDecay (src/engine/traits.ts:161) skips every non-mastery subcategory and every definition without a decayPeriod; it drops one level per elapsed period and removes the edge at zero. Condition expiry is a separate path keyed on ticksRemaining.
  • A condition can sit on a place (THR-1143). trait.condition.location.* — pass_closed, festival, plague_scare, under_watch, harvest_blight, standing_welcome — are ordinary condition definitions whose bearer is a location node. Nothing about the substrate changes: same has_trait edge, same ticksRemaining counter, same single expiry path. It is worth saying plainly because the substrate always permitted it (has_trait.sourceNodeType has listed location all along) and nothing used it, which is the dormant-capability shape. An aftermath reaches a place through an additive targetLocationId on apply_condition / remove_condition / condition_attachment; $target binds it when the action targets a location. Sublocations keep their own field — targetLocationId deliberately does not bind a node carrying a parentLocationId, so one node never resolves to two fields with different tax and gating meanings.
  • Every location condition ships with its readers (UI Law 56 at world scope — a write no system consumes is hollowness one level down). Two read it: template gating, where requiredTargetTraits already matched against a location target's has_trait edges and now has content to match (a festival-gated template becomes eligible while the festival runs); and movement cost, where LOCATION_CONDITION_MOVEMENT_TAX maps a condition id to a multiplier on the cost of entering that place. pass_closed maps to LOCATION_IMPASSABLE_MULTIPLIER (8) — a soft block by design: a hard block can strand an agent whose only route home runs through the pass, so a closed pass is a price, not a wall (NFP #4). The tax lifts by itself when the edge expires, because the tax reads the edge; there is no second lifecycle to keep in step. under_watch carries no tax on purpose — being observed changes what you can do in a place, not how long it takes to walk in. IMPL
  • A place can be owed gratitude, and that is not the same as owing a favour (THR-1175, 2026-08-18). standing_welcome is the first location condition a person earns and the first that reads positive; the other five are things that happen to a place. It exists because a gratitude scene had been expressing "there is a roof in this town that opens for them now" as an owes_favor edge with the town as debtor — schema-illegal (the edge is declared actor→actor and the schema layer only warned), and inert even had it been legal, because every favour consumer is individual-shaped: social leverage, sentiment drift, call-in and break all read one person's regard for another. The write was real, well-formed, correctly anchored by its chip, and its entire remaining lifecycle was to be silently deleted at expiry. The condition is the honest shape of that fiction, because a welcome is a property of the ground. It carries no movement tax, for under_watch's reason from the other side — being welcome somewhere does not shorten the road to it — and its readers are template gating and the location detail panel, where a player can open the place and read it. The edge family is now enforced at the graph layer rather than declared: an owes_favor debtor must be an individual actor, and a refusal emits one loud trace naming which endpoint was wrong and what it actually was. IMPL
  • standing_welcome is now a retired definition with zero writers (THR-1206, 2026-08-23). It was the right shape and the wrong noun. The director's ruling: "custom concepts are difficult for players to learn and understand. if we do have reputation as our concept for 'the social score that modifies interactions between a and b', then lets use that everywhere." A standing welcome at a town is the traveler's reputation with that town, so the Grateful Kin bands now write a reputation_with edge (src/engine/reputation.ts) and the return-visit gate asks requiredReputationWith rather than naming a condition trait. The definition stays registered on purpose, the THR-1177/1183 read-tolerance pattern: worlds saved before this carry live has_trait edges pointing at it, and deleting it would render them as a raw id (UI Law 14) or drop them in silence. One unwritten row is what lets those worlds finish their season honestly. Do not author new writers against it. The other five location conditions are unaffected — they are things that happen to a place, which is not a score between two parties. IMPL
  • domainContributions on a location condition is empty, not absent. Those values are read off an agent's traits to move that agent's capability, and a place has no capability to move. The five are declared with satisfies TraitDefinitionProperties rather than the as cast the personal set uses, so the required-field contract is checked at authoring time instead of asserted past.

Refs, the index, and the one predicate IMPL (THR-786)

  • Four ref forms, all legal: full node id (trait.mastery.smithing), short id (mastery.smithing), display name (Master Smith), or tag (#craft). TraitRefIndex (src/engine/traitRefIndex.ts) maps one ref to the set of definitions carrying it and predicates pass on ANY-match. A ref resolving to several definitions is legal, not a conflict — that is exactly what reproduces the pre-unification union semantics by construction.
  • One resolver, six read sites. resolveTraitPredicate(graph, bearerId, pred, opts?) (src/engine/traits.ts:43) is the single gate for: the encounter filter pipeline, the has_trait:/lacks_trait: effect predicates, ambition graphConditions, ambition eligibility snapshots, spell prerequisites, and item-granted traits. For several predicates against one bearer, collectBearerTraitRefs + bearerMatchesPredicate walks the graph once.
  • The index is owned by SimulationRuntime, not module scope — the engine-caches-per-session rule. A module-level singleton would survive across playthroughs on a soft reload.
  • Name collision, settled in the UL. Ambition templates carry requiredTraits: string[] (bare refs, src/types/ambition.ts); UnifiedActionTemplate.requiredTraits is readonly TraitPredicate[]. Same name, two shapes, one resolver — so behaviour agrees and only the declaration differs. Say ambition trait keys and template trait predicates when the difference matters. blockedByTraits is deliberately asymmetric (readonly string[], no minLevel): a requirement can demand a depth, a prohibition cannot.

The dead-ref defect — most trait gates have always been false DESIGN

Read this before authoring a trait hook

Authored refs and shipped trait definitions have never intersected. Authored refs are bare snake_case keys (master_smith, negotiator, pacifist); every definition uses trait.<category>.<kebab-case> ids, Title Case display names, and #-prefixed tags. No form matches, so the predicate is permanently false.

  • Measured by __DEBUG.validateTraitRefs() against the 64 shipped definitions: 62 dead refs — 43 in ambition boostingTraits/blockingTraits/requiredTraits, 15 in ambition graphConditions, 4 in has_trait: choice-set predicates (negotiator, dauntless, leader, interrogator — all four in src/data/choice-set-catalog.ts) — plus 21 phantom grant keys naming traits with no definition behind them.
  • Why it survived: the one path that works is self-closing. A trait_grant effect returns its bare key, consumers union it into the bearer's ref set, and a gate naming the same bare key passes — grant key to gate key, never touching a trait definition. Every gate depending on an actually minted trait has always evaluated false, so the boosting/blocking contribution in ambition scoring is 0 in every real run.
  • Consequence for content authors: a hook must name a trait some producer actually mints. A ref that names nothing is not a missing engine feature — it is an authoring defect, and it fails silently. Run the sweep before shipping hooks. Reconciliation is tracked as THR-800; this page describes the substrate, not a fixed state.

Selection vs competence — stated accurately IMPL

  • Selection flows through scoringModifiers (per-Reach additive bias in encounter scoring). Verified: every personality definition carries domainContributions: {} and a scoringModifiers entry (e.g. trait.personality.iron.virtue → { iron: 0.15 }). Personality steers what an agent reaches for and never what they are good at.
  • Competence flows only through capped channels: domainContributions multiplied by assignment level in getEffectiveDomainContributions (traits.ts:205), plus the resolution-bonus cap. The shorthand "never crossed" is wrong and worth correcting wherever it appears: reputation definitions do carry domainContributions (e.g. trait.reputation.iron.positive → { iron: 0.10, heart: 0.05 }). The law is not that reputation never touches competence — it is that competence only ever moves through the capped channels, never as a bespoke uncapped bonus.
  • A third field is neither. axisContributions is a signed per-axis delta on the 0–1 personality scale, summed once by the origin-baseline computation at birth (personality/originBaseline.ts, THR-561). It steers moral flavour, and nothing reads it for prerequisites or resolution.
  • A separate registry worth knowing about: LOS_TRAIT_DEFINITIONS (src/data/trait-modifiers.ts) maps 5 trait ids to line-of-sight los_range modifiers. It is keyed by trait id but is not a trait-definition source — it does not participate in the 64, and an id present there is not thereby a defined trait.

Inspection IMPL

  • window.__DEBUG.validateTraitRefs() (src/engine/traitRefValidation.ts) sweeps all six authored surfaces and returns { resolved, dead[], phantomGrants[], … }, each dead entry carrying its authoring path. The sweep is shape-driven, not an aggregate list — it recognises refs by their authored shape while walking the content namespaces, so a new content file is swept the moment it is imported, with no registration step to forget.
  • Runtime tracing is deliberately absent for the read path (pure predicates, ring-buffer discipline). Unresolvable refs warn once and evaluate false rather than throwing — the tick loop never crashes on a bad ref.

Open questions OPEN

  • What fills destiny. The category has a contract and a definition but no content, no producer, and no reader. Which system mints a destiny, and what "the promise is kept" means mechanically, is unanswered.
  • Whether non-mortal bearers earn their reactions. Location traits are schema-legal today and no production path assigns one — every assignTrait caller targets an actor. The architecture's own kill criterion applies: if place traits ship and headless runs show no measurable shift in encounter-pool composition at a marked location versus an unmarked one, the react-verb integrations need rework, not the substrate.
