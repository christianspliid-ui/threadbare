Essence, Control & Sustained Power

Divine fuel is the one thing you never have enough of. Every act spends it, every held thread costs it, and every sustained working keeps costing it — turn after turn — until you let go or a rival tears it away.

Essence is your divine fuelNot one pool but twelve — one for each Sphere. Power is coloured, and colour is a constraint.

Influence Essence is what a divine act is made of. You do not have a single bank of power; you have a separate pool for every Sphere — Force and Mind, Matter and Time, Light and Darkness, all twelve. An action drawn from Force spends your Force pool; a working of Matter draws down Matter. The kind of god you are decides which pools fill fastest, and which run dry the moment you overreach.

This is why two gods with the same total essence can afford completely different turns. A Force-aligned Ascendant swims in the pool that fuels raw imposition and starves in the pool that fuels subtle mind-work. Your Sphere alignment is not a flavour label — it is the shape of your wallet.

The economy is tight on purpose

Most turns you cannot afford every intervention you can see. That is not a balance oversight — it is the texture of play. The pressure to choose which one act matters this turn is the whole game leaning on you. A god who could do everything would feel nothing.

Where essence comes fromWorshippers, holy ground, and the depth of your portfolio. Fuel is drawn from the world you are woven into.

Essence regenerates every turn from the reach you have into the world. The more of the world holds you — in belief, in place, in bonded lives — the more fuel flows back:

A baseline flow

You are a god; a thin current of essence returns to you each turn simply for existing in the world.

Worshippers (your threads)

Every mortal you hold a thread to feeds you a little each turn. A wide portfolio is a wide intake.

Places of power

Holy ground and wells of the numinous you control return more than ordinary land — and your home seat, the throne of your presence, most of all.

An Aspect's conduit

A mortal raised into a living Aspect of you becomes a channel, pouring back extra fuel while they live.

But income does not arrive in a lump — it is poured into your pools along your alignment. The largest share flows to your primary Sphere, a smaller share to your secondary, and the remainder is split thin across the other ten. Your nature decides not just how much you gain but where it lands:

Holding threads costs as well as feeds: the upkeep of your deepest bonds is drawn from your primary pool each turn, so a large, deep court quietly taxes the very Sphere you rely on most. (The thread ladder and its upkeep are the Threads, the Court & The First page; here we care only that they are a running cost against your fuel.)

Control — power you keep spendingMost acts happen and are done. A Control working does not end — it holds, and holding is a commitment renewed every turn.

Four of the five divine verbs resolve in a moment: you Create, Find, Change, or Destroy, and the world shifts. Control is different — it is the signature verb, and it does not finish. When you seize a hex, bind a well, or hold a mind under your will, you open a sustained effect that keeps working turn after turn: draining fuel, pressing its Sphere onto the land, doing its quiet job for as long as you can pay.

Every held working is a standing cost. Each turn, your Control effects are charged in order of age — oldest first — from the pools they draw on. Two things follow from that, and they are the heart of Control as a strategic choice:

  • Sustained power is rationed by upkeep, not just by cast cost. You can afford to start more workings than you can afford to keep. The question is never "can I do this once" but "can I hold this while holding everything else."
  • Control slots cap how many workings you can hold at once. Beyond the essence drain, there is a hard ceiling on concurrent sustained effects — a limit that rises with your Domain Capability. Holding is a scarce commitment, and choosing what to let lapse is part of the game. (The exact slot ceiling is still being tuned — see Designer Notes.)
A held working is a visible thing in the world

When you sustain a Control effect it is not a hidden buff on your sheet — it becomes a standing presence on the hex, a node the world can see and act against. That visibility is the price of holding ground: what you keep, others can find, and what others can find, they can take.

How a working endsA Control effect lapses one of three quiet ways — or is torn from you by a rival who wanted it more.

No sustained effect lasts forever. It ends when you can no longer meet its terms. There are three ways a working lapses on its own:

The fuel runs out

The most common end. If your pools cannot cover the upkeep this turn, the effect lapses — and when several fail at once, the newest are dropped first, so your oldest, deepest holds survive longest.

A condition breaks

Some workings hold only while the world stays a certain way — a hex kept corrupted, a place kept prosperous. Let that condition slip and the effect falls, no fuel spent, its reason gone.

Its anchor is lost

A working bound to a relic pays no upkeep while the relic endures — but if that anchor is shattered, the effect it was holding up collapses with it.

And then there is the end you do not choose. Because a held working is a visible node, a rival god with the right power can contest it — reach onto your hex and try to take what you hold. Contestation runs two ways:

Usurp

The rival seizes the working — ownership transfers, and everything you sank into it now serves them. Your investment does not vanish; it changes hands. And the theft is loud: you will almost always feel it happen.

Destroy

The rival shatters the working outright. It is simply gone, and the ground it held goes with it. Nothing transfers — this is denial, not conquest.

The longer you hold, the harder you are to unseat

An old working is a rooted one. The longer a Control effect has stood, the harder it is for a rival to usurp or destroy — age is its own defence. A working you have held for a season is a fortress; one you seized last turn is a tent. This rewards patience and punishes the god who grabs everything and holds nothing long enough to matter.

The long worksSome undertakings are too large for a single act. They run as projects — advancing a step at a time, and eroding if you look away.

Not everything resolves in one moment of divine will. The largest ambitions — a settlement raised, a stronghold consolidated, a region turned — run as strategic projects: multi-turn works that advance a step each turn rather than snapping into being. And the holds you establish through them are not self-tending. A control stance left unattended begins to degrade after a short grace period — the world reasserts itself against neglect, and a grip you stop renewing slowly opens.

The lesson is the same one the whole system keeps teaching: in Threadbearer, power is not a thing you own. It is a thing you keep spending to keep.

Fuel is where the two clocks meet

Every drop of essence you pour into holding ground is a drop you did not spend slowing the Doom Clock or pushing your Mandate. The tight economy is not just about this turn's action — it is the exchange rate between everything you want. What that trade costs, exactly, is the subject of the Doom, the Mandate & Omens page.

Designer Notes

Per-sphere pools, the alignment split, the essence phase, the sustained-effect tick engine and its three lapse models, the contestation resolver, and the strategic-project lifecycle.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partially or not yet wired, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced from the rulebook — this page mints none). Synthesis authority for this page is Docs/canon/rulebook.md §6 ("Your Resources"). The plan doc cites "§5"; that predates the 2026-06-23 rulebook renumber (THR-414) that inserted §5 The Cosmology and pushed Resources to §6. Where any surface disagrees, the rulebook and the UL win on their respective boundaries.

Per-Sphere essence pools IMPL

  • Essence is EssencePool = Record<SphereName, number> (src/types/influence.ts) — one numeric pool per Sphere, twelve in all. There is no single aggregate balance; every action, upkeep charge, and detection loss is debited from a specific Sphere's pool.
  • The pool ceiling scales with portfolio size: BASE_MAX_ESSENCE = 50 plus MAX_ESSENCE_PER_THREAD = 5 per held thread (computeMaxEssence, src/engine/influence.ts). Wider court, deeper reservoir.

Essence earned — the lifetime counter IMPL

  • Beside the pool sits a second, monotonic reading: GameState.essenceEarnedBySphere (EssenceEarnedBySphere = Partial<Record<SphereName, number>>, src/types/influence.ts). The pool says what you have left; this says how much has ever come through each Sphere. Spending never decrements it. Absent ⇒ all-zero, so every legacy save reads as un-attuned rather than needing a migration (THR-1180).
  • It is accrued at the phase-merge seam (applyEssenceEarned, src/engine/essenceEarned.ts, hooked into both runInlinePhase and runRegisteredPhases), not at instrumented grant sites. Every grant path in the economy — capped cosmic generation, control-effect perTickIncome, effect deltas in action resolution, place-of-power streams, the elder-site reward — lands in some phase's returned pool, so diffing there counts the site nobody has written yet. Trade-off: accrual is net movement within one phase, so a phase that both grants and spends (only phaseControlEffects does) banks the difference. Across phases nothing nets.
  • Its consumer is the Repertoire's sphere_attunement unlock — see the nudge cards page. Crossing a mark in SPHERE_ATTUNEMENT_THRESHOLDS emits one nudge_attunement_unlock trace, once ever, on the phase that reaches it.
  • Reachability, measured. INITIAL_ESSENCE_PER_SPHERE = 50 equals BASE_MAX_ESSENCE = 50, so a god with no threads open starts at the ceiling and generation clamps to nothing. A headless 100-tick run at seed 42 with no spending earned zero in every Sphere. Nothing is earned until something is spent — which makes the counter a reading of activity, not of tenure.

Generation & the alignment split IMPL

  • Gross per-tick generation sums a fixed set of source terms (computeEssenceIncome, src/engine/essenceIncome.ts; mirrored by computeEssenceGeneration in influence.ts): BASE_ESSENCE_PER_TICK = 1.0, plus ESSENCE_PER_THREAD = 0.1 × threads, plus ESSENCE_PER_PLACE_OF_POWER = 0.5 per controlled place of power, plus ESSENCE_PER_SEAT = 1.0 for the home seat (a named higher-yield place of power identified by homeSeatLocationId, THR-502 — replaces rather than stacks with the ordinary place-of-power term for that location), plus ASPECT_ESSENCE_PER_TICK = 0.3 per living (non-mythic-echo) aspect_of conduit (THR-479).
  • The total is then distributed by Sphere alignment, not credited flat (distributeByAlignment): primary × 0.35, secondary × 0.25, and the remaining 0.40 split evenly across the other ten Spheres (perOther = total × 0.40 / 10). These three fractions are the EssenceDistribution shape; identity defaults set primary/secondary from the Ascendant's SphereAlignment.
  • Thread upkeep is netted out of the primary pool: computeEssenceIncome subtracts Σ TIER_MAINTENANCE[tier] across all threads from net[primary]. So a deep court is a standing tax on your primary Sphere specifically.
  • Active Control effects with perTickIncome add per-sphere income on top (not distributed by alignment) — an effect can be a net fuel source (e.g. a "Tap the Source" working, TAP_SOURCE_BASE_INCOME = 0.8 vs TAP_SOURCE_SUSTAIN_COST = 0.2).

The essence phase & the control-effect tick engine IMPL

  • Order matters: phaseControlEffects (src/engine/phaseControlEffects.ts) runs after the essence phase, so income is credited before upkeep is charged. It iterates active effects oldest-first by establishedTick for payment, and lapses failures LIFO (newest-first) — the design intent that your oldest, deepest holds are the last to fall when fuel runs short.
  • Per effect per tick: (a) threshold check → lapse threshold_failed if unmet; (b) relic-upkeep substitute check → lapse upkeep_relic_destroyed if the sustaining relic is gone, else waive cost while it exists (THR-509); (c) debit perTickCost per Sphere, refunding partial debits and marking essence_depleted if any Sphere can't pay; (d) apply perTickMutations / perTickGraphOps / thread auras / sphere-pressure; (e) credit perTickIncome; (f) increment ticksActive. Every step emits a ControlEffectTickTrace or ControlEffectLapseTrace (NFP #2).
  • The three sustain models in the player layer map to real branches: drain = perTickCost (the default), threshold = sustainThreshold (a hex/location field comparison), ritual/relic = getUpkeepStatus waiving cost against a sustaining relic. All three are IMPL in the tick engine. The rulebook's §6 "[DESIGN — contestation reactions specified, not all wired]" flag applies to the full Control mechanic (slot cap + rival-driven contestation), not to the per-tick sustain engine, which ships.
  • Fail-soft (NFP #4): a missing owner node lapses the effect as voluntarily_released; unknown threshold targets are treated as satisfied; arithmetic-only, never throws. THR-551 adds a seeded per-tick rift-leak roll (deterministic per seed + tick × RIFT_LEAK_SEED_OFFSET + hash(effectId)).

Control slots — the cap is not finalised DESIGN

  • The rulebook §6 states the concurrent-slot cap "scales with your Domain Capability tier" but the slot-cap formula is not yet in code — no CONTROL_SLOT/maxControlSlots constant exists (confirmed by grep). The player layer describes the intended ceiling and flags it as tuning-pending; the tick engine currently sustains as many effects as essence allows.
  • Design pointer: the Control mechanic and slot model live in Docs/plans/2026-03-17-world-state-and-hex-actions-design.md.

Contestation — usurp / destroy resolver IMPL DESIGN

  • Resolution is IMPL in src/engine/controlContestationResolver.ts (TB-045) and invoked from unifiedActionResolution.ts: findContestableEffects gates on active + not-owner + prerequisite match (meetsPrerequisites — a reach-tier and/or aligned/opposing-sphere check); resolveContestation rolls probability = clamp(capability − difficulty, 0.05, 0.95); applyContestationOutcome either transfers ownerId (usurp — inherits all sunk investment) or sets active = false, lapseReason = 'destroyed'.
  • Age is the defence: computeContestationDifficulty = min(CONTESTATION_MAX_DIFFICULTY 0.85, CONTESTATION_BASE_DIFFICULTY 0.5 + ticksActive × CONTESTATION_TICKS_ACTIVE_SCALING 0.01). Usurp is loud: USURP_DETECTION_RISK = 0.8.
  • The DESIGN half is the full rival-driven loop — the resolver exists and is callable, but the autonomous rival contestation cadence is the "not all wired" part the rulebook flags. A parallel src/engine/contestation.ts covers the encounter-side contest path.

Strategic projects & control degradation IMPL

  • phaseStrategicProjects (src/engine/phaseStrategicProjects.ts, orchestrator phase 2a.55) is a progression phase — it advances existing state.strategicState.projects and degrades neglected controls, it does not plan. Gated by ENABLE_STRATEGIC_ACTIONS = true (currently on); no-op when there is no strategic state. Since THR-1184 it has one further duty: when a completing project mints an edge that changes what its destination location can host — the families in ENCOUNTER_POOL_INVALIDATING_EDGE_TYPES, currently just sacred_route — the phase refreshes that location's encounter-cache entries via applyEncounterCacheUpdate(… onLocationTypeChanged). Without it the new pool waits for an unrelated structural invalidation: measured on seed 42 / medium, only 1 of 4 consecrated destinations had its pilgrimage pooled by tick 120. strategicActionLifecycle stays runtime-free by design and reports the touched location instead of refreshing it itself.
  • Cadence constants (src/data/strategic-action-constants.ts): STRATEGIC_PROJECT_PROGRESS_PER_TICK = 1, STRATEGIC_DEFAULT_PROJECT_TIMEOUT_TICKS = 18 (fail-soft timeout for stalled projects), STRATEGIC_CONTROL_NEGLECT_GRACE_TICKS = 10 (ticks before an unattended control stance begins degrading), STRATEGIC_CATALYST_SEED_CHANCE = 0.65 (completions may seed a follow-up encounter), STRATEGIC_CATALYST_SEED_DELAY_TICKS = 3 (narrative pacing before that follow-up may spawn), STRATEGIC_CATALYST_SEED_PRIORITY = 0.6 (a follow-up, not an interrupt). Progression logic in advanceStrategicProjects (src/engine/strategicActionLifecycle.ts).
  • The catalyst follow-up is planted as an ordinary PendingEncounterSeed on state.pendingEncounterSeeds — the same shared pool route events, army supply and encounter aftermaths write to — and is drawn by evaluateEncounterSeeds once eligibleAfterTick passes. Its label reads "the wake of <action>". IMPL THR-992 corrected the shape written here: maybeSeedCatalyst had been writing id/sourceActionId instead of seedId/sourceEncounterId and omitting priority and seedLabel, so catalyst entries reached the shared pool with no id — invisible in traces, and a live crash hazard for any consumer that read one.
  • Scope note: this is the ambition-driven strategic-action lifecycle, shared by actors pursuing ambitions and the Ascendant's held stances. The player layer frames it as "the long works"; the mechanism is the same system.
