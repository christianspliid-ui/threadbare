Armies, Battles & Lairs

War in Threadbearer is a story generator, not a wargame. Armies raise themselves, march, tire, and clash without a single order from you; battles resolve out in the world, never on a tactics screen; and the monsters in the dark grow stronger the longer they are left alone.

Armies raise themselves, march, and wear downYou do not build or command armies. Factions raise them when their ambitions turn to conquest — and from the moment a host takes the field, it is losing strength to the road, the weather, and its own hunger.

An army is a body of soldiers a faction musters when it decides to take by force what it cannot get by other means. A faction reaches for war only when its heart is set on it — when it hungers to expand its territory or to take revenge — and only when it has both a capable warlord to lead the host and the coin to raise it. When those conditions align, a host takes the field on its own. You never press the button; you shape the pressures that make a faction want war, and the world does the mustering.

Hosts come in three sizes. A warband is a fast, small raiding force; a regiment is a proper standing army; and a host is a great slow horde. The bigger the army, the harder it hits and the slower it moves — a warband darts across the map while a host grinds forward one weary league at a time. All of them move far slower than a lone traveller, and the land punishes them: forests, mountains, and swamps drag at a marching column far more than they slow a single mortal, while a good road is worth more to an army than to anyone.

WarbandA fast raiding force. The smallest and quickest host — strikes and slips away.
RegimentA standing army. The middle strength — steady on the march, dangerous in the field.
HostA great horde. The largest force — hits hardest, crawls slowest, costs the most to keep fed.

Every host is wearing down from the day it is raised. Marching costs it; hard terrain costs it more; camping off the roads and going unfed cost it most of all. You never see the number that measures a host's condition — you see the consequences when it slips too far, delivered as encounters: a supply crisis, then desertion, then open mutiny, and finally the host dissolving into a scatter of broken men. A war that drags on does not need to be lost in the field; a host left too long in the wild will simply come apart.

And an army eats. Not from its own baggage train for long, but along the roads and trade routes that run back to the towns its faction holds — and only from towns that have food to spare. That line is the thing a war is actually fought over. Stretch it too far and it thins with every league; let bandits settle on it and it is strangled rather than cut; take the towns at the far end into famine, or break the road entirely, and the host is simply cut off. What follows is not a number going down. It is foraging parties going out into somebody's farmland and the question of how they behave there; then, if nothing changes, the men deciding for themselves. A besieging army can starve before the city it surrounds does, and when it does, the siege lifts — which is how most sieges in most histories actually ended.

The consequence worth holding onto: you can break an army without ever touching it. Sour the harvest at the town that feeds it, or set bandits on the road that carries the grain, and the host in the field comes apart on its own. War is not only answered with war. A god of gold or shadow has a way into a conflict that a god of iron would never think to use.

The land is the third army

An army that marches through mountains in the rain, far from any road and with an empty treasury behind it, is bleeding strength every single turn — and if its territory is haunted by monsters, it bleeds faster still. You do not have to meet a host in battle to defeat it. Cut its roads, ruin the faction that pays it, or simply let the wilderness do the work.

Battles resolve in the world, not on a boardWhen two hostile hosts meet on the same ground, a battle begins — and it plays out as a story unfolding over several turns, tilted this way and that by moments you can reach into, never a grid of units you push around.

When two armies belonging to rival factions stand on the same hex, they fight. A battle is not a puzzle you solve — it is a momentum struggle that swings back and forth over a handful of turns. One side gains the upper hand, then the other; both bleed strength as they clash; and when the momentum tips far enough one way, or the fighting simply drags on too long, the battle breaks in a victory, a stalemate, or the ruin of both.

What decides which way it tips are its turning points — the charge that breaks a line, the captain who falls, the flank that folds. When you have a thread to someone caught up in the fighting, these turning points come to you as moments you can shape: intervene, and you can hand a wavering line its courage or its rout. When no thread ties you to the battle, it still resolves — you simply read about it afterward in the chronicle, decided without you.

A battle is a chapter, not a chess match

There is no tactics screen, no unit selection, no orders to give. A battle is a sequence of framed moments — the same kind of encounter you meet everywhere else in the game — strung along a shifting tide. Your leverage is the same leverage you have everywhere: the mortals you are bound to, and what you are willing to spend to move them.

The aftermath is where war leaves its mark

When a battle ends, its consequences ripple outward. A crushing defeat can annihilate the losing host outright; a lesser one sends it reeling in retreat. The defeated commander may escape, be dragged off in chains, or die on the field. Settlements caught in the path of war lose prosperity, refugees stream out from the ruin, and the very ground takes on the character of the slaughter — a battlefield is a wound in the world, not just a line in a ledger.

Sieges — when the walls become the battleMarch a hostile army up to a settlement and the fight changes shape. Walls favour the defender, hunger favours the attacker, and the longer it grinds on the tighter the noose draws.

When an army arrives at a settlement held by an enemy faction, it does not simply fight — it lays siege. A siege is a slower, crueller cousin of a field battle. The defenders begin with the advantage, because walls are worth a great deal: a fortress is far harder to take than an open field, and a garrison that has had time to prepare its ground is harder still. But a siege is a waiting game the attacker can win by patience alone — as the days drag on, the pressure mounts, the moments come faster and sharper, and eventually starvation begins to gnaw at whoever is trapped inside.

A siege does not stay contained to its own walls. It pulls in the country around it — nearby villages, wandering mortals, and rival powers within a few hexes are all drawn into its orbit, caught up in the encounters a great siege throws off. A city under siege is a regional event, and the whole land feels it.

Every siege is a place with a name

Because a siege is fought over a settlement you can see on the map, its fall is never abstract. A taken city loses its prosperity and its people; a siege lifted is a deliverance the chronicle will remember. War, in Threadbearer, always has a where and a who.

Lairs escalate if you leave them aloneThe wild places of the world are not empty. Monster lairs sit in the borderlands and the deep wilderness, and every one you ignore grows — stronger, then named, then a power of its own — while poisoning the land around it.

Scattered through the untamed hexes at the world's making are lairs — the dens of the things that live beyond the reach of any faction. A lair is not a static hazard. Left undisturbed, it escalates: a minor den festers into a major one, crowned by a named elite — a monster with a name and a story of its own — and a major lair left long enough becomes legendary, seeding an entire monster faction that can contest the map like any other power. Worse, a thriving lair breeds: it can throw off new dens into the neighbouring wilds, so an ignored corner of the map does not stay one lair — it becomes a spreading blight.

A lair also poisons the ground it sits on. It presses its own sphere into the surrounding hexes, warping the land's character over time, and armies that march through monster-haunted territory bleed strength faster for it. And clearing a lair is not always the end of it: if the tainted ground is left untended and no faction takes the site, the darkness can seep back in and the lair reinfests.

Minor lairA fresh den. The starting state. Festers into a major lair if left alone long enough.
Major lairCrowned by a named elite. A monster with a name — a real encounter, not a nameless threat.
Legendary lairA monster faction is born. The den has become a power on the map with designs of its own.
Honest scope — war is a story engine, not a wargame

None of this is a strategy game hiding inside Threadbearer. You will never draw up a battle line, set a supply chain, or issue a march order. Armies, battles, sieges, and lairs exist to make the world feel like it has weight and consequence — to give conquest, ruin, and the creeping dark a place in the story you are shaping. War is texture with teeth: it happens to the world whether you watch it or not, and your part is to decide when, and for whom, to reach in.

Designer Notes

Armies as faction-raised actor nodes with cohesion attrition and threshold encounters; momentum-driven field battles with thread-gated spotlights and IMPL aftermath; sieges as an accelerating battle subtype with wall fortification and starvation; and lair escalation minor→major→legendary with reinfestation — with honest IMPL/DESIGN status and the two deliberate simplifications flagged.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partial or a deliberate simplification, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). Systems-spec authority: Docs/plans/2026-03-29-conflict-and-destruction-design.md (TB-073 conflict & destruction, Phases 1–4) for armies/battles/sieges, and the M2.5 monster-encounters plan for lairs. The whole chain is IMPL and wired — orchestrator.ts runs phaseArmyMovement → phaseArmyAttrition → phaseBattleDetection → phaseBattleTick → phaseLairEscalation → phaseArmyNotifications in Act 2.35x every tick. Terminology collision (honest note): an army's quintessence field is its cohesion / health (0–quintessenceMax), a different meaning from cosmological Quintessence (narrative centrality, see Reaches, Spheres & Quintessence). The code reuses the word; the player layer above deliberately calls it "strength / condition" to avoid the collision.

Armies — spawning & size IMPL

  • Armies are actor nodes carrying an armyState bag (src/types/army.ts); commander, faction, location, and objective are all graph edges (commanded_by, member_of, located_at). Three sizes — warband/regiment/host (headcount 100/1000/10000) — determined from the faction's Gold capability tier (determineSizeCategory).
  • phaseFactionAmbitions raises an army (armySpawning.spawnArmy) when a faction pursues a military ambition and isEligibleForArmySpawn passes: the ambition type is in MILITARY_AMBITION_TYPES (only territorial_expansion and revenge), a member sits at Iron capability tier ≥ ARMY_SPAWN_IRON_TIER_MIN 4 (the commander — highest-Iron member), the faction's Gold tier ≥ ARMY_SPAWN_GOLD_TIER_MIN 3, and the faction is under MAX_ARMIES_PER_FACTION 1. Armies are also seeded at worldgen (worldSeed.spawnArmy) and raised by the reach-signature warhost aftermath (encounterAftermath.raiseWarhostForce, see Divine Actions & Prerequisites).
  • DESIGN — deliberate low cap: MAX_ARMIES_PER_FACTION = 1 is annotated "tune upward later" in army.ts. One host per faction at a time is a chosen throttle, not a bug — flag it so it is not mistaken for a richer force economy.

Movement IMPL

  • phaseArmyMovement(state) pathfinds each army toward its objective.targetNodeId via findShortestPath + tickMovement. Deterministic, no PRNG; fail-soft (unknown terrain → plains cost, no path → stay put and keep taking attrition).
  • Army terrain costs (ARMY_MOVEMENT_COST_MULTIPLIERS) are steeper than an agent's — plains 1.5, forest 2.5, mountains/glacier 4.0, water/ocean/lake ∞ (impassable). Roads discount ARMY_ROAD_DISCOUNT 0.4 (40% off — stronger than for agents). Speed is inverse to size (ARMY_SPEED warband 2 / regiment 1.5 / host 1).

Attrition & threshold encounters IMPL DESIGN

  • phaseArmyAttrition(state) is deterministic (no PRNG). Per tick a host loses ARMY_BASE_ATTRITION 0.5 + terrain (terrainCost × TERRAIN_ATTRITION_FACTOR 0.3) + OFF_ROAD_ATTRITION_PENALTY 0.8 when not on a road + UNDERFUNDED_ATTRITION_PENALTY 1.5 when the faction can't pay + MONSTER_TERRITORY_ATTRITION 1.2 when an active lair sits on the hex + SUPPLY_ATTRITION_PENALTY[supplyTier] (THR-626: supplied 0, strained 0.6, starving 2.0). Clamped to 0. The supplied term is deliberately 0 so the shipped TB-073 arithmetic is unchanged for every army the supply web has not starved — an additive coupling, not a rebalance.
  • Four one-way COHESION_THRESHOLDS (fractions of max) fire encounters as strength drops: strained 0.70 → army.threshold.supply_crisis, weakened 0.50 → army.threshold.desertion, critical 0.30 → army.threshold.mutiny, collapse 0.10 → army.threshold.disbandment. One per tick, each fires once (tracked in thresholdsFired). Cohesion 0 → disbandArmy removes the node.
  • DESIGN — "underfunded" is a heuristic, not a treasury: checkUnderfunded returns true when the faction's prosperity < 0.2 (or no faction). There is no per-tick gold ledger deducting maintenanceCost yet — the maintenance constants exist on armyState but the funding check is a prosperity proxy. Flag it; do not describe a real war chest.
  • IMPL — supply is a second, independent axis (THR-626): phaseArmySupply (post-economy, afterPhase: ['route_events'], every ARMY_SUPPLY_SCAN_INTERVAL_TICKS 4) resolves each army's supply line with a bounded BFS over road + trades_with conduits, capped at ARMY_SUPPLY_MAX_HOPS 4, to the nearest location the army's own faction controls whose resourceBalance ≥ ARMY_SUPPLY_HOST_MIN_BALANCE −0.2. Throughput is ARMY_SUPPLY_BASE_THROUGHPUT 30 × ARMY_SUPPLY_HOP_DECAY 0.7^hops, multiplied by ARMY_SUPPLY_THREATENED_PENALTY 0.4 if any hop carries threatened (banditry strangles a line, it does not sever it). The larder moves toward that number against ARMY_SUPPLY_CONSUMPTION (warband 4 / regiment 9 / host 18 per scan) and is clamped to [0, ARMY_SUPPLY_MAX 100]. Nothing is simulated in transit — pull-derived, never token-pushed; there are no wagons.
  • Two axes, not one: underfunded is the faction failing to pay; unsupplied is the trade web failing to feed. A rich faction whose roads are cut still starves, which is the state this coupling exists to make reachable. Severing a trades_with route is therefore an economic act with a military consequence — a Shadow/Gold answer to an Iron problem.
  • ArmySupplyTier (supplied | strained | starving, thresholds 0.5 / 0.2 of max) is the only read surface; the larder scalar is private and never rendered. Fail-soft direction is load-bearing: an army raised before this system (no supply field) reads supplied, never starving — never provisioned is not the same as starving.
  • Anomalies materialize as PendingEncounterSeeds through the shipped seed system (the web proposes, the curator disposes — nothing interrupts the player): strained → army.supply.forage, starving → army.threshold.mutiny, starving + besieging → army.supply.siege_lifted. Bounded by ARMY_SUPPLY_MAX_SEEDS_PER_SCAN 2 and one live seed per army. Note army.threshold.mutiny is shipped THR-104 content that had no spawn path at all — THRESHOLD_ENCOUNTER_TEMPLATES in armyAttrition.ts has zero non-test consumers, so cohesion thresholds only ever produced a notification. THR-626 gives one of the four its first real trigger; the other three remain unspawned.

Field battles — detection, momentum, resolution IMPL DESIGN

  • phaseBattleDetection(state) groups armies by hex; two colocated armies create a field_battle node (attacker = later-raised); an army colocated with a hostile-controlled settlement creates a siege instead (createSiegeNode).
  • DESIGN — hostility is faction-difference only: areHostile returns true whenever two armies belong to different factions ("simplified — no alliance system yet"). There is no alliance, truce, or neutrality model — any two foreign hosts that meet will fight. Flag it.
  • Initial momentum is log2(sizeRatio) × SIZE_MOMENTUM_SCALE 1.5, clamped to ±(BATTLE_RESOLUTION_THRESHOLD 8 − 2) so spotlights always matter. tickBattle applies BATTLE_COMBAT_ATTRITION 2.0/tick to both hosts, shifts momentum, and resolves when |momentum| ≥ 8, at FIELD_BATTLE_MAX_DURATION 5 ticks (stalemate if |momentum| < 2), or on army collapse.
  • Spotlights (battleSpotlights.ts + battle-spotlight-content.ts, 10 templates): if the player has a thread to a battle participant (hasThreadToBattle), selectSpotlight picks a condition-filtered template and a seeded roll (55% success) applies its momentumOnSuccess/momentumOnFailure. No thread → chronicle-only: a simple seeded roll nudges momentum with no surfaced encounter.

Battle aftermath IMPL

  • battleAftermath.applyAftermath(state, battleState, resolutionType) scales consequences by destruction severity — minor/major/total from final momentum magnitude vs the loser's remaining strength (TOTAL_DESTRUCTION_THRESHOLD 10, MAJOR_DESTRUCTION_THRESHOLD 6).
  • Commander fate (CommanderFate): minor → always retreated; major → 50/50 captured/retreated; total → COMMANDER_DEATH_CHANCE_TOTAL 0.30 killed else captured (out of play for COMMANDER_CAPTURE_DURATION 10 ticks). Settlements take PROSPERITY_LOSS_MINOR 0.20/PROSPERITY_LOSS_MAJOR 0.50; refugees generate (REFUGEE_GENERATION_MAJOR 1/TOTAL 3); the hex gets aftermath sphere pressure (AFTERMATH_BASE_SPHERE_PRESSURE 3 × severity multiplier).

Sieges IMPL

  • siegeResolution.ts reuses the battle node with battleType: 'siege'. Pacing accelerates: spotlight interval = max(1, SIEGE_INITIAL_INTERVAL 5 − floor(elapsed / SIEGE_ACCELERATION_RATE 6)). Defenders open with SIEGE_DEFENDER_MOMENTUM_BONUS 2 and bleed less (SIEGE_COMBAT_ATTRITION_DEFENDER 0.5 vs attacker 1.0). Fortification multiplies effective defender size (FORTIFICATION_BASIC 10, FORTIFICATION_GRAND 30, PREPARED_DEFENSE_MULTIPLIER 3, capped TACTICAL_MAX_MULTIPLIER 20; a breach drops it to BREACH_FORTIFICATION_REDUCTION 0.3).
  • Resolution at |momentum| ≥ SIEGE_RESOLUTION_THRESHOLD 12 or SIEGE_MAX_DURATION 40 ticks. A starvation beat fires once at SIEGE_STARVATION_TICK 15 — or at 15 × SIEGE_SUPPLIED_DEFENDER_STARVATION_FACTOR 2 when hasReliefLine(settlementId) holds (THR-626, swapping the provisions source to the trade web as the notables plan deferred). hasReliefLine deliberately excludes the self-host case: a besieged city is nearly always a stocked, faction-controlled location, so asking whether it can feed itself answers "yes" until the moment it doesn't — precisely the fixed clock being replaced. It asks instead whether anything can still get in, and a threatened line does not count. The consequence for play: cut the roads before investing the walls. Regional encounters (SIEGE_REGIONAL_TEMPLATES, 14 siege-encounter entries total) pull in entities within SIEGE_REGIONAL_ENCOUNTER_RANGE 5 hexes; tiers resolve via attentionTier with a retinue court-position fallback so siege beats still surface.

Lairs — seeding, escalation, reinfestation IMPL

  • lairSeeding.seedMonsterLairs (called from gameInit) places lairs at worldgen by danger-zone density: wilderness 0.08, borderland 0.03, heartland 0.005, capital 0.0 (never), boosted by tile danger, min separation 3.
  • phaseLairEscalation(state) runs every LAIR_ESCALATION_INTERVAL 25 ticks. Each active lair emits LAIR_SPHERE_PRESSURE_EMISSION 8 of its dominant sphere onto its site (environmental feedback); upgrades minor→major after LAIR_UPGRADE_MIN_TICKS 30 (spawns a named elite actor via createNamedElite, placed to enter the encounter pipeline) and major→legendary after LAIR_LEGENDARY_MIN_TICKS 60 (seedMonsterFaction); and rolls adjacent spawning (LAIR_ADJACENT_SPAWN_CHANCE 0.15/neighbor) in wilderness/borderland only. Seeded PRNG (seed + tick × 71).
    Adjacent spawning is authored but unreachable, and has never fired (THR-817 finding, filed THR-995). The guard asks lairExistsNearby(..., ADJACENT_SPAWN_MIN_SEPARATION 3) against a lair list that includes the source lair, and every hex getHexNeighbors returns sits at |Δcol|+|Δrow| ∈ {1,2} — always under 3. So a lair is always too close to its own neighbours, all six continue, and spawnAdjacentLair has no reachable call site. Treat the 0.15 chance as a dormant tunable, not an observed spread rate; the upgrade, elite, monster-faction and reinfestation paths above are live and unaffected.
  • Cleared lairs (locationSubtype 'cleared_lair') reinfest when ticksSinceCleared ≥ LAIR_REINFESTATION_MIN_TICKS 20, the hex's dominant-sphere score ≥ LAIR_SPAWN_SPHERE_THRESHOLD 60, and no non-monster faction controls the site. Armies on an active-lair hex take MONSTER_TERRITORY_ATTRITION 1.2 extra attrition (cleared lairs don't count).

Player-facing surfacing IMPL

  • phaseArmyNotifications(state, nextEventId) converts army/battle/siege traces into player TickEvents: army_raised, army_disbanded, battle started/resolved, siege_established, and attrition threshold crossings. Battle/siege spotlights surface as encounters through the normal encounter + attention pipeline (see Encounters & Aftermath and Attention, the Digest & the Chronicle). All army/battle traces use category faction_ambition.

Tick placement IMPL

  • Order within the tick (orchestrator.ts, Act 2.35x): phaseArmyMovement (2.352) → phaseArmyAttrition (2.355) → phaseBattleDetection (2.356) → phaseBattleTick (2.357, routes field vs siege) → phaseLairEscalation (2.3575, gated to every 25th tick) → phaseArmyNotifications (2.358). Movement and attrition run before detection so colocation and strength are current; escalation runs after battles so a fight at a lair hex resolves first.
