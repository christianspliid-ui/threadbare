Attention, the Digest & the Chronicle

You are one god watching a whole living world. You cannot see all of it at once — and the game is built around that limit, not in spite of it. What you miss is kept for you; what matters is brought to you; and everything, watched or not, is written down.

You cannot watch everythingAttention is a pool that fills slowly and empties when you lean in. It is the game's honest answer to a world too large for one pair of eyes: you choose the few moments to truly attend, and trust the rest to unfold without you.

A world of hundreds of mortals is always moving, and you are one witness. Threadbearer does not ask you to keep up with all of it. Instead it gives you a small, renewing pool of attention — the measure of how much you can lean into the world right now. It refills a little every turn, up to a modest ceiling, and it drains when you choose to attend a moment closely rather than let it pass.

Because attention is scarce, every choice to look is a real choice. Leaning into a hard, dangerous encounter costs more than a small one. And it is cheaper to attend the mortals closest to you: turning your gaze on your First — the mortal at the heart of your story — costs half what it does to attend someone you merely watch from afar. The court you have built shapes what you can afford to notice.

Doing nothing is always allowed

The pool is not a bar you must keep full or empty. Letting a turn pass without spending attention is a legitimate move — the world runs on, the digest fills, and your pool climbs back toward its ceiling. Scarcity here is a lens that makes the moments you do choose feel chosen, not a punishment for looking away.

When a thread strains, it tugsYou do not have to hunt the world for the moments worth your attention. A curator watches the threads on your behalf and surfaces the few that matter as gentle tugs — and quietly files the rest into the digest so nothing is lost.

Left to raw chance, the moments worth attending would be buried under the noise of an entire simulated world. So a hidden curator does the sifting for you. Each turn it looks over the encounters forming on the mortals you are threaded to, weighs them — how close that mortal is to you, whether the moment advances a longer story, whether it touches your ambitions — and lifts the strongest few into view as thread tugs: a felt pull toward a life that has reached a moment.

A tug does not wait forever. It lingers a few turns and then lets go; you can hold only a handful at once. If you attend it, you step into that moment. If you let it expire, the encounter still resolves on its own at a quiet baseline — and it is written into the digest as a moment you did not reach, so you can find it later. Even the moments the curator decides not to raise are filed away rather than erased.

A rarer kind of moment overrides the ordinary rhythm entirely. When a story reaches a genuine turning point — a chain of encounters coming to its head, several mortals converging, the Doom clock crossing a stage — the curated moment is promoted to a story beat, a spotlighted scene the pacing holds space for so it does not collide with another. Beats are rationed on a cooldown so they land like chapter breaks, not a stream.

The digest — what happened while you looked awayEverything you did not attend is kept. The digest is the world's memory of the turns you spent elsewhere, and you can spend a little essence to sit down and read it — for the whole world, or for a single thread.

Every unattended moment — the tugs that expired, the encounters the curator set aside, the quiet resolutions across the map — is recorded in the digest: a running account of what the world did while your gaze was elsewhere. You are never told you missed something and left to wonder what. It is all there, waiting.

You reach it through Read the Threads, a divine act that costs a little essence and shows you the recent past. You choose how far back to look — a short window is cheap, a long one costs more — and the further you reach, the more of the world's quiet life you take in at once. It is a deliberate pause: the god sitting down to catch up on the turns spent looking away.

For a single mortal, the same record composes into a story so far — a short, ordered account of that thread's recent life, drawn from its most significant beats: the wounds, the discoveries, the turns that changed them. A run against a hundred lives becomes, thread by thread, a set of small stories you can read whenever you return to a face you care about.

Honest scope — the digest keeps everything, on purpose

Today the digest holds the full record of the run — it is not yet trimmed to a rolling window as it grows, so the reading views deliberately limit themselves to a recent span (a few game days) rather than the buffer doing the trimming. In practice you read the recent past; the long-tail record simply sits behind it. The Designer Notes below say exactly which part of this is shipped and which is still on the drawing board.

The chronicle — the book of your runBeneath the tugs and the digest, the game is always writing. Ordinary turns become plain lines; notable turns get a richer telling; and the true turning points are set down in the chronicle — the run's book, remembered even after the world ends.

Threadbearer never shows you a number where it can show you a moment. Every event the world produces is turned into prose — and how richly depends on how much it matters. A routine happening becomes a plain line in the feed. Something notable — a mortal wounded, an attachment lost, a rank won, a hard loss of standing — earns a fuller, more coloured telling. And the genuine turning points of a run are set down in the chronicle: the lasting record of who your world became, kept even after the run is over.

The prose is not generic. It takes its colour from the sphere the moment moved through, so a deed of Iron reads differently from one of Veil; and where a mortal belongs to a culture, the telling picks up that culture's voice. This is the narrative feed — the surface where the run tells itself back to you, turn by turn, as a story rather than a ledger.

Woven through that prose are interactive keywords — the names of spheres, resources, and forces, rendered so you can hover or tap them to learn what they mean. They are how the game teaches you its own systems: not a manual you must go read, but the words in the story itself, ready to explain themselves. You learn Threadbearer by reading it.

Three ways the world tells itself

The same event can be told plainly, told richly, or set down for keeps — the game chooses the register to match the weight of the moment. Most turns are plain; the striking ones are coloured; the pivotal ones become chronicle. The point is that nothing is reduced to a stat readout: the story is the interface.

Designer Notes

A renewing attention pool and a curator that surfaces thread tugs (both IMPL & wired in phaseAttention); a digest that is appended every tick but not yet pruned at runtime (append IMPL, prune DESIGN/dormant); and a three-tier prose engine feeding the narrative feed and chronicle, with IPK as the learning surface (IMPL) — honest status flags throughout.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partial or dormant, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). The load-bearing honest call for this page

The digest buffer accumulates but is not pruned at runtime. appendDigestEntry is wired (orchestrator.ts:1190 and inside phaseAttention), but pruneDigestBuffer and DIGEST_BUFFER_RETENTION (48) have no production caller — grep finds them only in digestBuffer.test.ts. The buffer grows for the length of the run; the reading surfaces self-limit by lookback window (STORY_DIGEST_LOOKBACK_TICKS 36) rather than the buffer trimming itself. Treat the 48-tick retention as DESIGN / dormant — the constant and the pure prune function exist and are unit-tested, but nothing calls them each tick. The player layer above is written around what actually runs (windowed reads over an untrimmed record).

Attention pool — renewing, node-held IMPL

  • phaseAttention(state, templates, rng) runs once per tick (orchestrator.ts:2669). It reads/initialises the pool from the ascendant node properties (attentionPool / attentionCapacity / attentionRegen), regens via regenAttention (attentionPool.ts: min(pool + regen, capacity)), and writes back in place per the graph-mutation convention.
  • Ceiling ATTENTION_BASE_CAPACITY 6, regen ATTENTION_BASE_REGEN 0.4/tick. Visual bands (attentionPoolVisualState): focused / busy / strained thresholds at fill fractions 0.6 / 0.3 / 0.1.
  • Attend cost = threat base × court multiplier (attentionPool.ts). Base: ATTEND_COST_MODERATE 1.0, ATTEND_COST_HARD 1.5 (deadly shares the hard base), ATTEND_COST_STORY_BEAT 2.0. Court multiplier: the_first 0.5, retinue 1.0, watched 1.5 — attending your First is half price; dormant threads generate nothing.

Attention tiers & mid-encounter promotion IMPL

  • resolveEffectiveTier(intrinsicTier, courtPosition) (attentionTier.ts) maps an encounter's intrinsic tier through the agent's court position via TIER_MATRIX: watched demotes one tier (story_beat→shaping, shaping→background), retinue preserves, the_first promotes background→shaping. Dormant or unthreaded → 'invisible'.
  • checkMidEncounterPromotion can lift a tier during resolution: tier-1 triggers (wound, discovery, chainAdvance, tierPromotion) promote background→shaping; tier-2 triggers (chainCulmination, multiAgentConvergence, doomThreshold, battleEscalation) promote any tier → story_beat. story_beat is the ceiling.

Curator & thread tugs IMPL

  • Inside phaseAttention: shaping-tier unified actions (and legacy encounterProgress) on threaded, non-dormant agents become CurationCandidates scored by scoreCurationCandidates (curator.ts) — signals include court position, recency, chain-stage (classifyChainStage), faction-thread count, and ambition alignment (agentPursuesReach). The top set become ThreadTug records.
  • Tugs linger THREAD_TUG_LINGER 3 ticks; the player holds at most MAX_CONCURRENT_TUGS 3. An expired unattended tug writes a "missed" DigestEntry; a curated-out (demoted) candidate writes a DigestEntry with wasCuratedOut=true. Emits attention_pool and curator_decision traces.
  • Pacing governor (pacingGovernor.ts): story_beat-tier encounters fire immediately if the slot is open (canFireStoryBeat) or queue (enqueueStoryBeat/dequeueStoryBeat). STORY_BEAT_COOLDOWN 12, STORY_BEAT_QUEUE_MAX 3; overflow demotes the ejected beat back to shaping for curator pickup (STORY_BEAT_QUEUE_OVERFLOW_DEMOTE true). Note the 1-tick delay: phaseAttention runs before phaseAgentDecision, so encounters created this tick surface next tick — intentional per the design doc.

Digest & Read the Threads IMPL (append + read) · DESIGN (prune)

  • digestBuffer.ts exposes appendDigestEntry (wired), plus pure pruneDigestBuffer and queryDigest. Prune is not wired (see the red callout) — DIGEST_BUFFER_RETENTION 48 is dormant.
  • ReadTheThreadsPanel (GameView.tsx:3811, modal registered :3007) reads the digest over a chosen window at an essence cost: READ_THREADS_COST_6/12/24/36 = 2/4/8/12, gated by READ_THREADS_COOLDOWN 6.
  • Per-thread story-so-far (threadDigest.ts → StorySoFarPanel.tsx via useThreadStorySoFar.ts): composes up to STORY_DIGEST_MAX_BEATS 5 beats over the STORY_DIGEST_LOOKBACK_TICKS 36 window (min STORY_DIGEST_MIN_BEATS 2 to render), ranked by BEAT_SIG_* weights (notable 0.40 dominant; capability / attachment / quintessence / recency 0.15 each), with BEAT_SIG_PIVOT_MIN 0.55 distinguishing pivot from compound beats. LRU cache STORY_DIGEST_CACHE_SIZE 32; master flag STORY_SO_FAR_DIGEST_ENABLED true.
  • Notability (isNotableEntry, attentionTier.ts): quintessence loss ≥ NOTABLE_QUINTESSENCE_LOSS 0.3, |reputation delta| ≥ NOTABLE_REPUTATION_DELTA 0.3, any attachment lost, a tier promotion, a chain completion, or a wound.

Narrative feed, chronicle & the prose pipelines IMPL

  • narrative.ts generates three prose tiers: Routine (template-stitched fragments), Notable (enhanced templates with conditional clauses), Chronicle (structured, highest weight). SPHERE_VOCABULARY flavors verbs/adjectives by sphere; cultural palettes substitute at ~30% (culturalProse.ts).
  • Chronicle-tier events become ChronicleEntry records on GameState.chronicleEntries[] (tier-3, retained for the end-of-cycle chronicle). The narrative feed and recent-activity surfaces (NarrativeFeed.tsx, RecentActivityLog.tsx, ChroniclePanel.tsx, HexChronicle.tsx) render them.
  • Four composing pipelines (see Docs/canon/prose.md): graph-walking resolvers (proseGenerator.ts / proseResolvers.ts / proseComposer.ts), the narrative engine, vignette prose (Scene / Lens / Stakes / Forecast), and enrichProse() (proseEnrichment.ts) for placeholders ({name}, {ally}, {artifact}, {title}) and conditional blocks.
  • Reputation titles ({title}, {?has_title} / {?no_title}): gatherNarrativeContext collects up to three titles from the agent's traits whose definition carries subcategory: 'reputation' — the 17 reach-polarity definitions in reputation-trait-content.ts plus the economic reputations. An agent with no reputation trait resolves {title} to their plain name. Live from 2026-07-26 (THR-787): the filter previously read a category field no trait definition has ever written, so titles resolved empty for every agent in every run and {?has_title} was permanently false.
  • IPK = Interactive Prose Keyword (src/components/ProseKeyword.tsx) — sphere and economy names rendered as hover/tap-to-explain keywords. Per canon/prose.md this is "the learning engine": the player learns the systems through interactive keywords in the prose, never through raw numbers. IPK labels stay mechanical-plain (register hard rule). Not an inference / player-modelling system — the name means the interactive keyword, not "inferred knowledge".

Where it runs

  • Per-tick attention work is a single phase — phaseAttention (orchestrator.ts:2669), which runs before phaseAgentDecision so newly-created encounters surface one tick later. Digest entries are appended both there and during encounter resolution (orchestrator.ts:1190). Prose is generated on demand by the feed/chronicle surfaces, not on a dedicated tick phase.
