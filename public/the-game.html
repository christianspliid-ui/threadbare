FOLIO I · OF XII
◆I · THE PREMISE◆

— a gloss, in the scribe's hand —

"The god is young. The world is old. Both things matter."

You are a young god.
The world below is already alive.

Thousands of mortals live out their days. A handful carry a thread that, pulled at, will remake the age. You find them. You shape them. You watch what they do with the lives you have touched.

Fig. i — the Sphere of Spirit, by which whispers travel.

◆II · THE WORKING◆

Uncover. Shape. Witness.

I · UNCOVER

Your world is full of people. You listen.

A scholar finds a word that should not exist. A swordbearer wakes the morning of a duel she will not survive. A refugee crosses a border that no longer exists. You pick one. You follow her.

II · SHAPE

You do not command. You whisper a dream.

You send a sign at the crossroads. You place an old coin where she will find it. You let rain fall on the night she rides out. She decides what to do with it. She always does.

III · WITNESS

Seasons later, she comes home. Her left hand is hidden.

The scholar has started a heresy. The refugee has founded a new faith in a cold place. The thread you pulled is now a seam in the world. You choose the next one.

◆III · FOUR PROMISES◆

What this game will and will not do.

i

Your world is yours alone.

A new world every time. The same seed gives you the same world, so you can share one with a friend and walk into the same dawn. No two reigns play out the same myth twice.

ii

Failure is a turn, not a loss.

Your swordbearer loses the duel. She wakes in a cell with a pact she should not have made. That is not a game over. That is the next chapter.

iii

You whisper. They choose.

No units, no abilities. You send a dream. You plant a doubt. You withhold a sign. They decide what to do with what you have given them.

iv

Prose, not spreadsheets.

A merchant is not Gold: 7 — she is a shrewd trader whose ambition outruns her caution. The world is told to you, never tabulated.

The spheres are the grammar of creation. Mortals favour some and fear others. Your whispers travel through them. Your reign takes their colour.

PL. I — AN OFFERING AT THE SHRINE
◆V · DIVINE POWER◆
Shape the world through will alone.

You do not move units. You send a dream.

Dream prophecies into mortal minds. Inspire heroes. Curse the land. Forge artifacts of terrible weight. Shift the cosmic balance between Life and Entropy, Order and Chaos.

Your will reaches the world through the few mortals you watch most closely — the one whose story defines your reign, and the circle of those drawn into their orbit.

  • ◆Dozens of divine actions across four layers — Land, Soul, People, Ruins.
  • ◆Eight intervention types, from subtle dreams to devastating afflictions.
  • ◆Every whisper costs. Every whisper can be misread.
PL. II — THE GOLDEN CAPITAL
◆VI · A LIVING WORLD◆
Civilizations that grow, trade, and fall.

Settlements rise. Markets open. Cities burn.

Hamlets swell into cities. Trade routes thread between capitals. Guilds form, compete, and scheme. Prosperity flows where commerce thrives, and collapses where war arrives.

Every settlement has character. A mining town in the mountains feels different from a port city on the coast — not because someone wrote each one, but because the world remembers what kind of place it is.

  • ◆Dynamic prosperity and trade, with decay where war arrives.
  • ◆Economic locations — mines, markets, harbors, shrines.
  • ◆Settlements rise from hamlet to capital, or fall to ruin.
◆IX · THE WEAVE◆

A living world with real depth.

Threadbearer is not a game with a world attached. It is a world with a game discovered inside it. Nothing is scripted. Everything is connected.

Worlds You Can Share

Share a seed with a friend and walk into the same world. Your choices write a different myth; theirs write another. The same dawn. Two reigns. Two endings.

Everything Is Connected

Every entity, relationship, and effect is part of the same living web. Armies, alliances, trade routes, curses — all queryable, all traceable. Pull one thread and the whole tapestry shifts.

Procedural Terrain

Realistic climate zones, river systems, biome distribution, and organic coastlines. Mountains form ranges. Rivers flow downhill. Deserts gather behind rain shadows.

Encounters with Weight

Each meeting carries its own weather — a culture, a history, a way of going wrong. The same scene plays differently in a city, a shrine, a borderland, a ruin. Outcomes ripple outward.

Factions That Act Alone

Guilds, cults, and factions form, recruit, scheme, and turn on each other without your permission. Some will hate you and never know why. They are playing their own game.

The Age Ends

Seven paths an age can fail — a breach, a convergence, a slow failing, a sundering. Each one has stages that change how the world feels as the ending nears. You choose what to do with what is left.

PL. III — THE SHADOW COURT
◆VII · WAR & RUIN◆
Empires fall. Cities burn.

Armies march. Sieges grind. The map scars.

Armies cross the map, led by faction champions with war goals of their own. Sieges test the patience of city walls. When a settlement is sacked, its markets close, its people flee, and what was once a thriving city becomes ruins for future wanderers to uncover.

Rival gods field their own armies. Ancient monsters roam the borderlands. The world is beautiful and dangerous in equal measure.

  • ◆Faction-driven warfare with siege mechanics.
  • ◆Settlements can be sacked, demoted, or razed.
  • ◆Divine intervention at the moment of battle.
PL. IV — THE FOREST SHRINE
◆VIII · MORTAL LIVES◆
The one you watch most closely.

Every mortal carries their own will.

Every mortal has values, ambitions, and a way of refusing the things they were not raised to want. They do not follow orders. They listen, sometimes, to the small voice between their thoughts — and decide what to do with what they heard.

One of them is your particular concern. Their origin unfolds through choices you only half-make for them. You watch them become an oathkeeper, or a wanderer, or a schemer, or a cautionary tale.

  • ◆Nineteen narrative archetypes. Nineteen shapes a life can take.
  • ◆A hero's journey arc tied to the pressure of the age.
  • ◆Prose-driven character sheets that unfold as you learn them.
◆XII · THE TAPESTRY WAITS◆

The tapestry waits.

Threadbearer is for readers who love living casts who carry their own will into your plans, worlds deep enough to surprise their own makers, prose as the primary interface, and epics where the gods stand at the periphery and history is half-remembered.
