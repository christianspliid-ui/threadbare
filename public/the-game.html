I · The Premise

A world without gods is not without prayer.

Somewhere, a scholar is rereading a word that should not exist.
Somewhere, a swordbearer is waking to a morning she will not survive.
Somewhere, the wind is changing direction for the first time in an age.

You are new. The world is old. It will not wait for you to learn its name.

II · The Working

You whisper. They choose.

Awaken as a young god in a world that has forgotten what gods are. You will not move them; you will move what they see, what they dream, what falls in their path. Civilizations will rise and fall around the few mortals you watch most closely. Every thread you pull sends a tremor through the tapestry — and somewhere ahead, an age is ending.

"The world exists before you. It will exist after. What you do in between is the story."
III · Four Promises

What we promise the thread you pull.

Threadbearer is not a game with a world attached. It is a world with a game discovered inside it.

✦

Your world is yours alone

The same seed produces the same world; your choices produce a different age. No two reigns are the same myth twice. Share a world with a friend and watch your stories diverge from the same dawn.

✷

Failure is a turn, not a loss

A failed gambit deepens the story rather than ending it. A mortal who falls short of greatness becomes a cautionary saga. A war you lose redraws the map and the next age inherits the scars.

✵

Stories, not spreadsheets

You will never see a stat block. A merchant is not Gold: 7 — she is a shrewd trader whose ambition outpaces her caution. The world speaks in prose. You read stories, not data.

✹

Every age must end

The world frays as your reign deepens. Resources wither. Old debts come due. Armies converge on places they have always converged. You do not race a clock — you choose what becomes of the world before its ending becomes your own.

IV · Your Hand

You do not move units. You send a dream.

You are a god. You dream prophecies into the heads of sleeping kings. You inspire a coward to draw his sword. You curse a harvest. You set a single word into the mouth of a wandering scholar and watch what he builds with it. You shift the cosmic balance between Life and Entropy, Order and Chaos — not by command, but by where you place your weight.

Your will reaches the world through the few mortals you watch most closely — the one whose story you are most invested in, and the smaller circle of those drawn into their orbit. You whisper. They choose. Sometimes they hear you. Sometimes they hear something else.

Whispers, dreams, omens, blessings, curses, visions, calls and visitations — every divine touch arrives as something a mortal can refuse, misread, or carry too far.
V · The Weather

Civilizations that grow, trade, and fall.

Settlements rise from hamlets to cities. Markets open. Trade routes thread between capitals. Guilds form, compete, and scheme. Prosperity flows where commerce thrives — and collapses where war arrives.

Every settlement has character. A mining town in the mountains feels different from a port city on the coast — not because someone wrote each one, but because the world remembers what kind of place it is.

Markets and mines, harbors and shrines, ruined keeps and golden capitals. Settlements that climb, settlements that fall, and the trade roads that thread them together.

VI · War & Ruin

Empires fall. Cities burn.

Armies march across the hex map, led by faction champions with war goals of their own. Sieges grind against city walls. When a settlement is sacked, its markets close, its people flee, and what was once a thriving city becomes ruins for future adventurers to explore.

Rival gods field their own armies. Ancient monsters roam the borderlands. The world is beautiful and dangerous in equal measure.

Sieges that grind. Cities that fall and become ruins for the next age to plunder. Rival gods who field armies of their own. Old monsters who remember when the borderlands were theirs.
VII · Mortal Lives

The one you watch most closely.

Every mortal carries values, ambitions, and a way of refusing the things they were not raised to want. They do not follow orders. They listen, sometimes, to the small voice between their thoughts — and decide what to do with what they heard.

One of them is your particular concern. Their origin unfolds through choices you only half-make for them. You watch them become an oathkeeper, or a wanderer, or a schemer, or a folk hero. You watch them become a cautionary tale. You read the page that turns next.

Seekers and oathkeepers, schemers and wanderers, doomed innocents and reluctant kings. A handful of shapes a life can take, all of them yours to witness as the page turns.
"Twelve cosmic powers. Nine ways a mortal can reach. One world, fraying toward an ending only you can shape."
VIII · The Cycle

Every age turns.

A reign is not a campaign. It is a season in the world's long memory.

A world you can re-enter

Share a world with a friend and play the same dawn. Your choices write a different myth from the same starting page; their choices write theirs. The same world. Two reigns. Two endings.

Everything is connected

An assassination redraws a trade route. A new heresy reshapes an army. A drought wakes an old monster. Pull a thread and the whole tapestry shifts — you find out where the ripples end only by following them.

A world made by weather

Mountains form ranges. Rivers flow downhill. Deserts gather behind rain shadows. Coasts curl around drowned valleys. The land is not decoration; it shapes what people grow, what they fight over, and where the old powers sleep.

Encounters with their own weight

Each meeting carries its own weather — a culture, a history, a way of going wrong. The same scene plays differently in a city, a shrine, a borderland, a ruin. Outcomes ripple outward.

Factions that act on their own

Guilds, cults and orders form, recruit, scheme, and turn on each other without your permission. Some will hate you and never know why. Some will pray to you and never reach you. They are playing their own game.

The shape of an age

Each age fails its own way — a breach, a convergence, a slow failing, a sundering. The world feels different as the ending nears, and the ending it earns becomes the next age's foundation.

"What kind of god will you be?"
