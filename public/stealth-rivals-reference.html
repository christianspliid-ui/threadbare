Stealth, Detection & Rival Gods

You are not the only power in the world, and nothing you do is truly unseen. Every act you commit in a region leaves a wake — and somewhere out in the dark, a rival god is watching for exactly that wake to rise.

Two audiences are watching your handEverything you do in the world is noticed by someone. The mortals you touch slowly come to sense the divine; and the other gods scan the land for the signature of a rising power. Acting is never free of consequence — only of cost you can see.

When you reach into the world, two kinds of watcher take note. The first is the mortals themselves: a hand that keeps intervening in a life is a hand a mortal begins, dimly, to feel. That slow awakening — from a stranger to something felt in dreams to open faith — is the story of a thread, and it belongs to that page. The second watcher is the one this page is about: the rival gods, cosmic powers who share your world and scan the regions of it for signs of another god at work.

You cannot act loudly and stay hidden. But you can act quietly. Some deeds draw far more notice than others, and acting inside the reach of your own strongest sphere is quieter than acting far outside it. The whole art of stealth in Threadbearer is choosing where, how heavily, and how visibly to spend — knowing that the region remembers your presence for a while, and that a rival is counting.

Being seen is a resource, not a fail state

Detection is not a stealth meter you must keep at zero. It is a pressure you spend against, the way you spend essence. A bold god who draws rivals down on their works is playing a different game from a patient one who never shows their hand — neither is wrong. The question is only whether you are ready for who is coming.

Detection pressure — the price of being seenEach region of the world carries a quiet tally of how much divine meddling it has felt lately. Your choices push it up; time lets it fade. Push it high enough and a rival strikes at that place.

Every region — a stretch of the map with its own name and character — carries a hidden measure of detection pressure: how conspicuous the divine hand has been there of late. When you commit to a choice inside an encounter, the weight of that choice raises the pressure of the region it happened in. A small, careful nudge barely stirs it; a deep, forceful intervention pushes it hard. And the same act is louder or quieter depending on your alignment — working through the sphere you are strongest in blends into the world and draws less notice, while acting far outside your nature stands out and draws more.

Pressure does not only climb. Left alone, a region forgets — its pressure ebbs a little every turn, so a place you have not touched in a while goes quiet again. This is the rhythm of stealth: act, then let the wake settle before you act again in the same place, or spread your work across regions so no single one lights up.

As a region's pressure rises it crosses thresholds you feel as escalation. First it draws a faint notice; then it colours the turn around it; and if it climbs to the very top, it boils over into a rival strike — an encounter in which an opposing power reaches into that region precisely because you made too much noise there.

NoticeThe first stir. The region has felt enough meddling to register it. The wake is visible but harmless.
TurnThe mood shifts. Pressure is high enough to colour what happens here — attention is gathering on this place.
Rival strikeThe reckoning. Pressure peaks and an opposing god reaches into the region — a strike seeded because you were seen.

Rival gods — generated, opposed, and aliveYour rivals are not a fixed cast. Each world spins up a handful of gods built to stand against exactly the kind of power you are, each with a temperament of its own — and they act on the world whether or not you are watching.

Threadbearer has no fixed pantheon of villains. At the making of each world, a small number of rival gods are conjured to oppose you specifically — their natures drawn from the spheres you are weakest in, so a rival is always a counterweight to your own strengths. Each is given a generated name and a temperament that shapes how it behaves, and each carries a measure of hostility toward you that grows every time it turns its hand against your works.

Left to themselves, rivals do not sit idle. Turn by turn a rival chooses a move according to its nature — quietly recruiting mortals to its cause, meddling in the world as you do, expanding its grip over new regions, or striking directly at what you have built. The more hostile a rival becomes, the more its choices tilt toward open attack.

Aggressive

Reaches for the sword first. Strikes at your works far more readily than it schemes or waits.

Subtle

Prefers the quiet knife. Works through intervention and recruitment; rarely attacks in the open.

Territorial

Guards and grows its ground. Leans toward expanding its regions and holding what it has.

Expansionist

Hungers for reach. Recruits and spreads relentlessly, always pushing into new country.

A rival is a mirror, not a menu

Because a rival is built to oppose your spheres, it will always be strong where you are weak. You cannot pick your enemies from a list — the world hands you the gods you were made to struggle against, and part of learning a run is learning who woke up across the map from you.

Schemes — the slow move against your worksA rival's most dangerous acts are not single blows but schemes: patient, multi-stage plots that build in the dark, announce themselves, and strike — and which you can catch and unravel before they land.

Beyond its turn-by-turn meddling, a rival can commit to a scheme — a deliberate, staged plot aimed at your works or at a place you care about. A scheme unfolds in four movements. It begins as a rumor, a barely-there stirring; it gathers into a materialization as the rival commits real weight to it; the world offers a chance for response; and finally it reaches its crack — the payoff blow that lands hardest of all.

Schemes grow bolder as a run darkens. The nearer the world drifts toward its Doom, and the higher you have climbed in power, the more ambitious and the faster a rival's schemes become — and the more of them a single rival will run at once. A quiet early game invites at most a cautious plot; a late game against a mighty ascendant invites several converging at speed.

A scheme is not an unstoppable clock. When you catch one forming, you can counter it — each successful stall buys time and drags the plot back, and enough counters unravel it entirely before it ever cracks. Reading the world for the first rumor of a scheme, and choosing when to spend against it, is one of the quiet arts of a long run.

RumorThe first whisper. The rival begins to invest in a plot; almost nothing shows yet.
MaterializationIt takes shape. The scheme gathers real weight and becomes something you can see and name.
ResponseThe world reacts. The plot's pressure ripples outward; the window to counter is widest here.
CrackThe blow lands. If uncountered, the scheme reaches its payoff — the hardest-hitting beat of all.

What the world remembers, and forgetsTwo kinds of hidden knowledge move beneath the surface of a run — consequences you have planted that wait to surface, and things mortals have learned that slowly go stale. Both fade if never used.

Some of your deeds do not resolve at once. A hidden mark is a concealed consequence you leave on a mortal — a debt, a curse, a buried truth — that does not announce itself. It waits, invisible, until a fitting encounter comes along and reveals it, turning a moment of play into the delayed echo of something you did long ago. The deeper the mark, the more likely it is to surface when its moment arrives.

But a mark does not wait forever. After a grace period it begins to fade, losing weight each turn, and if nothing ever triggers it the mark eventually slips away — quietly noted in the chronicle so the loss is never invisible. A consequence you plant and never cash in is a consequence the world will, in time, forget.

The mortals of the world carry their own hidden knowledge, too — intelligence about shrines, agent networks, trade routes, troop movements, court secrets, and lore. Fresh knowledge is reliable; but knowledge ages, drifting from reliable to uncertain to dubious as the world moves on and no one refreshes it. What a mortal knew last season is not what they can trust today.

Honest scope — what ships, and what is still on the drawing board

The detection you feel today is region-level: each region carries one pressure value that your choices raise and time lowers, and a rival strikes when a region boils over. A finer, per-mortal model — where a single person's suspicion of you climbs from unease to open realization to revelation — is designed but DESIGN-only and not yet wired into play. Rivals, their schemes, hidden marks, and ageing intelligence are all live. Where a system is only partly built, the Designer Notes below say so plainly.

Designer Notes

Region-level detection pressure (IMPL) driven by encounter choice-cost and sphere visibility; a fully-authored but dormant per-actor two-audience model (DESIGN); generated rivals with four temperaments and a four-phase scheme system (IMPL); and the delayed-reveal hidden-mark and ageing-intelligence primitives (IMPL) — with honest status flags throughout.

Status flags follow the rulebook convention: IMPL shipped and wired, DESIGN specified but partial or dormant, OPEN an unresolved question (referenced, never minted here). The load-bearing honest call for this page

There are two detection systems in the tree, and only one is live. Region detection pressure (phaseDetectionPressure, wired at orchestrator.ts:2640) is the shipped surface. The per-actor two-audience escalation model in stealth.ts — mortal suspicion and rival scrutiny ladders — is fully authored and unit-tested (stealth.test.ts) but has no live caller: nothing outside tests invokes processMortalDetection / processRivalDetection. It is DESIGN / dormant. The player layer above is deliberately written around the region-pressure model that actually runs. (Unrelated: regionDetection.ts is geographic region clustering, not stealth.)

Detection pressure — region-level, choice-cost driven IMPL

  • phaseDetectionPressure(state) reads state.pendingChoiceCommits: every encounter choice a mortal commits this tick resolves to the mortal's region (resolveRegionIdForAgent walks located_at → sublocation → parent location → regionId) and pushes that region's pressure up via applyDetectionDelta. Then the whole set decays (decayDetectionPressure). Pressure is clamped [0, MAX_DETECTION_PRESSURE 1].
  • Delta per choice = base choice-cost × sphere-visibility multiplier. Base cost is the choice's essence weight: small_breath 1, fuller_breath 2, deep_breath 3 (baseDetectionDeltaForCost). The multiplier is set by how the encounter's sphereAffinity lines up with your identity: acting in your primary sphere = 0.8 (quieter), secondary = 1.0, off-alignment = 1.2 (louder) — resolveSphereVisibilityMultiplier in phaseDetectionPressure.ts.
  • Thresholds (getDetectionThresholdCrossings): DETECTION_THRESHOLD_NOTICE 0.5 → turn at DETECTION_THRESHOLD_TURN 0.8 → encounter at DETECTION_THRESHOLD_ENCOUNTER 1.0. Crossing encounter seeds a shadow.rival_strike encounter (buildDetectionSeed, priority 100, guarded against duplicates per region). Each crossing emits a detection_threshold_crossed trace.
  • Decay is DETECTION_DECAY_RATE_PER_TICK 0.005/tick (all constants in src/data/encounter-experience-constants.ts). State is held on GameState.regionalDetectionPressure (mirrored to legacy regionDetection).

Two-audience per-actor model — authored, dormant DESIGN

  • stealth.ts implements a richer model that is not wired: per-actor MortalDetectionState with a suspicion ladder unaware → suspicion (3) → realization (8) → revelation (15) (MORTAL_AWARENESS_THRESHOLDS), and per-rival-per-region RivalDetectionState with a scrutiny ladder unaware → noticed (5) → identified (12) → targeted (20) (RIVAL_AWARENESS_THRESHOLDS). Each intervention adds a per-type score (DETECTION_SCORE_BY_TYPE: coincidence 3.0, afflict_bless 2.5, deceive/intimidate 1.5, persuade/omen 1.0, dream/inspire 0.5) and both ladders decay.
  • The functions are pure, exported, and covered by stealth.test.ts / stealth-mandate-integration.test.ts, but grep confirms no tick phase calls them — a build-but-dormant model, not a shipped one. Activating it (or reconciling it with the region-pressure model) is design/engine work, not documented here as live.

Rival generation IMPL

  • generateRivals(playerAlignment, seed) (called from gameInit.ts:281) seeds 2 + floor(rng()*3) = 2–4 rivals per world. Each rival's primary sphere is drawn from the player's four lowest spheres (opposingSpheres), so rivals are counterweights by construction; buildOpposingProfile gives them a full CosmologyProfile (primary weight 0.30–0.45). Names come from RIVAL_NAME_PREFIXES × RIVAL_NAME_SUFFIXES (rival-content.ts), de-duplicated. oppositionStrength 0.5–1.0. Rejected: fixed pantheon — rivals are generated from the World-Soul, per the load-bearing decisions.
  • createRivalState(id) initialises hostilityToPlayer 0.5, counters, and region list. Rival RNG is a local mulberry32(seed) for deterministic generation (NFP #3).

Rival actions IMPL

  • phaseRivalActions(state) (orchestrator.ts:2859) drives each rival. selectRivalAction(rival, state, roll, identityBias) picks from recruit / intervene / expand / attack / wait using BEHAVIOR_WEIGHTS[behavior] (rival-content.ts): e.g. aggressive attacks 0.45 / waits 0.10; subtle intervenes 0.35 / attacks 0.10; territorial expands 0.30; expansionist expands 0.35 / recruits 0.30. When hostilityToPlayer > 0.7, attack weight gets +0.15. A doom-identity bias (IDENTITY_RIVAL_BIAS_WEIGHT) nudges the weights toward the world's doom archetype.
  • updateRivalState applies the outcome: recruit ++agentsControlled, intervene/attack ++interventionCount, expand adds a region, attack also bumps hostilityToPlayer +0.05 (capped 1.0). The seeded roll comes from the tick RNG — the Math.random() fallback in the signature is a dev-only safety net (breaks determinism).

Rival schemes (THR-66) IMPL

  • A scheme is a four-phase arc — rumor → materialization → response → crack — that rides the shipped THR-225 composition phase runner (rival-schemes/ holds the corruptive and territorial families). The rival invests each tick to arm the next phase via world-flags (schemeFlags); the runner activates armed phases; phaseRivalActions executes each phase's concrete move on activation. All selection/escalation/launch code is pure + deterministic (seeded rival RNG, no Math.random()).
  • Escalation tier (computeRivalEscalationTier, 0–RIVAL_MAX_ESCALATION_TIER 3) blends the doom-clock stage (RIVAL_ESCALATION_DOOM_WEIGHT 0.6) with a player-advancement proxy — the highest thread InfluenceTier across the ascendant's threads (RIVAL_ESCALATION_ADVANCEMENT_WEIGHT 0.4), fail-soft to doom-only. Monotonic in both inputs.
  • Gates (selectRivalScheme): concurrent-scheme cap by tier RIVAL_MAX_CONCURRENT_SCHEMES [1,1,2,3]; launch cooldown RIVAL_SCHEME_LAUNCH_COOLDOWN_TICKS 24; a RIVAL_SCHEME_PROBE_WEIGHT 0.35 chance to make a cheap probe instead; ambitious (territorial) families unlock at RIVAL_SCHEME_AMBITIOUS_FAMILY_MIN_TIER 1. Phase-arm investment ticks drop with tier ([14,11,8,6]) so higher tiers escalate faster.
  • Counter-play: a player stall adds RIVAL_SCHEME_STALL_TICKS 12; RIVAL_SCHEME_COUNTERS_TO_FAIL 2 successful counters unravel a scheme. Each phase move pushes RIVAL_SCHEME_SPHERE_PRESSURE_PER_PHASE 0.04 in the rival's primary sphere; the crack beat multiplies it RIVAL_SCHEME_CRACK_PRESSURE_MULTIPLIER 2.5×. Surfaced via RivalPanel + a hex overlay; inspect with __DEBUG.getRivalSchemes() / __DEBUG.forceRivalScheme().

Hidden marks — delayed reveal + decay IMPL

  • A HiddenMark is a concealed consequence on an agent, held on GameState.hiddenMarks (not a graph node), planted by encounter aftermath. At scoring time matching marks boost candidate encounters; at resolution consumeMatchingMarks rolls rng() < severity × REVEAL_PROBABILITY_MULT 0.9 per mark whose revealFamilies prefix-match the resolved templateId, and on a hit removes the mark, emits hidden_mark_revealed, and appends a high-significance (0.7) chronicle event. A mark is never consumed the same tick it is placed.
  • phaseHiddenMarkDecay(state) (orchestrator.ts:2916): after MARK_DECAY_GRACE_TICKS 20, severity decays × (1 − MARK_DECAY_PER_TICK 0.02) per tick; below MARK_DECAY_FLOOR 0.05 the mark is dropped with an observable hidden_mark_revealed trace (revealedBy: 'decay:severity_floor') and a low-significance (0.3) chronicle event so the fade is never silent (THR-132).

Intelligence — ageing knowledge IMPL

  • IntelligenceRecords are queryable knowledge held by agents on GameState.intelligenceRecords across six categories (shrine_location, agent_network, trade_route, military_position, political_secret, cultural_knowledge). They feed scoring boosts, prose enrichment, resolution-match observation, and difficulty modifiers; consumption never removes a record.
  • Reliability bands: reliable ≥ RELIABILITY_THRESHOLD_RELIABLE 0.7, uncertain ≥ RELIABILITY_THRESHOLD_UNCERTAIN 0.4, else dubious. phaseIntelligenceDecay(state) (orchestrator.ts:2921) drops reliability by INTEL_RELIABILITY_DECAY_PER_TICK 0.001/tick after INTEL_RELIABILITY_GRACE_TICKS 20 (floor 0.0); a band crossing emits a low-significance (0.25) staleness chronicle event. Pure arithmetic, no PRNG.

Tick placement IMPL

  • Detection pressure runs mid-tick (phaseDetectionPressure, orchestrator.ts:2640, after choice commits are gathered); colocation-based detection at phaseColocationDetection (:2759); rival actions at phaseRivalActions (:2859); then the two decay phases cluster late — phaseHiddenMarkDecay (:2916) immediately before phaseIntelligenceDecay (:2921), alongside the other decay phases.
