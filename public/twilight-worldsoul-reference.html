Twilight, Echoes & the World-Soul

Every world ends. When yours does, you do not fight it — you witness it. What the age was gets weighed, a few bright things are kept, and the next world begins as a response to the last. This is how a run closes, and how one run becomes an ingredient of the next.

How a run ends — you witness, you do not actWhen the Doom clock runs out, or you fulfil the Mandate, the world enters its last chapter. Your part changes. You stop shaping and start watching the ending you have been steering toward all along.

A run does not end with a defeat screen. It ends with the Unmaking — a twilight the world passes through before it is gone. When your Doom clock reaches its end — or when you fulfil the Mandate, and the age ends in triumph — time stops and the game shows you the ending in three short beats: how the age ended, what became of your First, and what the World-Soul will remember of it. Then you open the harvest. The mood is the game's — cosmic melancholy, not failure. You were never the protagonist who could be beaten; you were the god who watched a world live and now watches it end.

Your hand is gone here. The ending is a passage, not a battle: you read it, and you look on. It is the deliberate opposite of the frantic finale most games reach for — an ending you inhabit rather than one you scramble against.

Planned — the world at its weakest

The design intent is for these closing turns to be weakened ones, where the god's touch no longer lands as surely — a felt loss of grip as the world unravels. Today the ending is a ceremony you watch with time stopped, so there are no closing turns to weaken; the harder-to-act penalty is planned, not yet in play. The Designer Notes below say exactly what runs and what is still on the drawing board.

The harvest — what the age leaves behindWhen the twilight ends, the world's most significant things are gathered as echoes: the mortals who mattered, the places that mattered, the artifacts that mattered. A few survive the ending. The rest pass on with the world.

After the last turn comes the harvest: the world is weighed, and its brightest few threads are preserved as echoes. An echo is not the thing itself — the world is ending, and everything in it ends with it — but a remembered shape of it, strong enough to carry into what comes next. Echoes come in three kinds, one for each kind of thing a world holds:

Legacy

An echo of a mortal who mattered — a life whose deeds and bonds left the deepest mark on the age.

Monument

An echo of a place — a settlement, a ruin, a wonder that became a fixed point in the world's story.

Relic

An echo of an artifact — an object whose passage through the age gathered enough weight to outlast it.

How many echoes survive depends on how the age ended. A world carried to a fulfilled purpose ends in a triumphant age and keeps the most. A world that simply ran out of time ends somber and keeps the fewest. Anything between is bittersweet. The ending is not just a verdict on your play — it decides how much of this world reaches the next.

Which things become echoes

Echoes are chosen by significance, not by you directly — the world offers up the mortals, places, and artifacts that were most woven into its story, and the strongest are preserved. Alongside them, the age is meant to grant a few divine echoes — traces of the kind of god you were, chosen by your hand. The world picks them for you, by rule: the places you held most surely, the mortals you raised highest, the relics your chosen carried. The closing screen names them — "Your age leaves behind: …" — or says the age leaves little to remember.

The closing screen is a full-screen ceremony, like meeting your First: the age ends on a dark ground with the world hidden behind it, and every preserved echo shows at once in rows rather than in a strip you scroll. Only Wake in a new world leaves it: this world ends, and a new one wakes — a new map, not this one continued — shaped by what this one left behind. You choose your next god in Remembrance; it starts as strong as your first.

The World-Soul rememberingBeneath every world is the World-Soul — the deep substrate that outlives any single cycle. The harvested echoes fold into it, and the chronicle of the run is bound into a finished volume. Nothing that mattered is simply deleted.

The world you played was one expression of something older: the World-Soul, the substrate from which each cosmos is drawn and to which each returns. When a world ends, its harvest does not vanish — the surviving echoes settle into the World-Soul, and the run's story is bound shut as a completed chronicle volume: the book of that age, kept even though the age is over.

Echoes carried this way are not permanent. Each cycle they endure, they fade a little, until the faintest of them are finally let go. A Legacy that shaped one world may still colour the next, and perhaps the one after — but given enough cycles, even the brightest memory softens and is released. The World-Soul remembers, but it does not hoard.

Planned — the deeper ledger

The design reaches further than what runs today. The World-Soul is meant to keep a coefficient ledger — a running sense of which cosmic forces have grown strong across many worlds — and a set of curated memory fragments drawn from each run's chronicle, which would tilt the tone and balance of the next cosmos. Those layers are designed and tested but not yet feeding the game; what carries across cycles right now is the echoes and the chronicle. The Designer Notes name exactly which part is which.

The next world — a response, not a sequelA new cycle is not "level two." It is the next answer the World-Soul gives, shaped by what the last world became — and, one day, by the kind of god you chose to be. The end of one run is the seed of another.

When you choose to go on, the world turns over into a new cycle. This is not a fresh start that forgets everything, nor a sequel that continues the same plot. It is meant as a response: the next world reflecting the shape of the last, its echoes surfacing as familiar names, half-remembered places, and objects whose story reaches back beyond living memory. The chronicle grows another volume; the long book of your play keeps being written.

This is where the game's longest promise lives — the metaprogression idea that the kind of god you were becomes an ingredient of what comes next. A world you ruled with mercy and a world you unmade in shadow should not seed the same successor. That promise is the horizon the run-end systems are built toward.

Honest scope — how much of "the next world" runs today

Today a new cycle carries its echoes and its chronicle forward and counts up — but the successor world is the same one continued, not a freshly re-drawn cosmos, and the echoes do not yet reshape it. The mechanism for an echo to seed the next world's culture, places, or quests is built but not yet switched on. The "next world as a response" is real as a direction and partial in practice. The Designer Notes below draw the line precisely.

The load-bearing honest call for this page

Echoes carry across cycles, degrade, and enter the chronicle — but they do not yet mechanically seed the next world. Each EchoDefinition is built with an injection hook (echo.ts: legacy→cultural_template, monument→location_feature, relic→quest_seed), and collectInjections gathers the active ones — but collectInjections has no non-test caller. Nothing consumes the injections, so an echo's mechanical influence on the next cosmos is DESIGN. Since THR-1813 the next run does re-run worldgen: a new world on a derived seed, with the account's live echoes loaded into GameState.echoDefinitions and the cosmology tilted by the carried fundament. Placing those echoes on the map (a monument host, a Legacy heir, an unborne relic) is slice 2, THR-1843 (DESIGN).

Per-tick sphere aggregate IMPL

  • phaseSphereAggregation(state) runs once per tick (orchestrator.ts:3024) and writes state.worldSoul.aggregate — a SphereAggregate of totalBySphere (weighted sum of every entity's sphere scores), the dominantSphere, and entityCount. This is the only live field of GameState.worldSoul; the Fundament and Resonance layers on the same object are unwired (below).
  • Before the global sum, the same phase writes each faction’s sphereAggregate (rounded mean of its individual members’ scores, written only on change; THR-1768). The global sum still reads each faction’s own bag, so members are not counted twice.

Twilight — the Unmaking IMPL (entry + ceremony) · DESIGN (weakened play, other triggers)

  • Entry: the Mandate, then doom IMPL (THR-1381). phaseDoomExpiry first starts the Twilight when mandateState.completed (no floor; trace age.ended { by: 'mandate' }). Otherwise, when doomClock.expired && phase==='playing' and the floor has passed (tick ≥ wokeAtTick + DOOM_MIN_RUN_TICKS_AFTER_BOND, 720; the clock wakes at the bond with The First, THR-1646), it sets phase:'twilight' and raises the alert (Chronicle) line "The Breach: the Unmaking begins." (the final stage name), tracing age.ended { by: 'doom' }. player_concession is still not an entry point (DESIGN).
  • Twilight is a ceremony, not ticks IMPL (THR-1381). useSimulation builds the beats once when the phase becomes 'twilight' (openTwilight → buildTwilightBeats, twilight.ts) and opens AgeTwilightScreen (The age ends, homed surface age-twilight, time stopped): the ending's title and line (the doom kind's last stage name, or the Mandate's), the First's fate (Aspect / lived / died, from state), and up to three memory lines. Its one button, See what the age leaves, calls completeTwilight → phase:'harvest'. A throw building the beats, or a ceremony that cannot mount, goes straight to the harvest (twilight.fallback). The old seven-tick countdown is deleted. The designed weakened play (worldSoul.ts TRIGGER_PENALTY) has no turns to apply to and stays DESIGN.

Harvest — significance-scored echoes IMPL

  • computeHarvest(state) (cycleEnd.ts) runs when twilight completes. It scores every individual actor, location, and artifact node by computeSignificanceScore(edgeCount, eventCount, nodeType) — edges held plus chronicle entries that name the node — and keeps the top few as cosmic echo candidates.
  • Harvest type is set by how the age closed: triumphant if mandateState.completed, else somber if doomClock.progress > 0.9, else bittersweet. Cosmic echo counts scale with it — 5 / 4 / 3 (triumphant / bittersweet / somber) — and a one-line chronicleSummary is generated naming the brightest echo.
  • Echo kinds. echo.ts nodeTypeToEchoType maps actor→'legacy', location→'monument', artifact→'relic' — the player-facing Legacy / Monument / Relic. Each EchoDefinition takes the top-two cosmology spheres as sphereAffinities and a built (but unconsumed) injection hook.
  • Surface. HarvestScreen (GameView.tsx:3753, fed by harvestResult in useSimulation.ts) shows the age title, the summary, the preserved cosmic echoes as cards (name, summary, sphere tags, and a banded significance word — ECHO_SIGNIFICANCE_BANDS, Law 13: no percentages player-facing, THR-1357), echo and slot counts spelled as words via countWord, the divine echoes it picked ("Your age leaves behind: …", tooltip ui.harvest_leaves), and a Wake in a new world button (ui.harvest_wake). If the record could not be saved, a line under the button says so, and the next world wakes as if for the first time (THR-1813).

Divine echoes IMPL (THR-1813)

  • computeHarvest fills its divineEchoSlots (3 / 2 / 1) by rule in pickDivineEchoes (cycleEnd.ts): slots rotate Monument → Legacy → Relic. A Monument is a top-level place the god reads Sovereign, else Held. A Legacy is a threaded mortal, an Aspect first, then by thread tier. A Relic is an artifact a threaded mortal possesses. Ties break on significance, then id, and a kind with no candidate yields its slot. Each slot traces harvest.divine_echo_picked. A node picked as divine is not also carried as a cosmic echo, and every echo carries the god's bought primary and secondary, not the cosmology's top two.

Cycle transition — echo persistence & chronicle binding IMPL · DESIGN (re-seeding)

  • THR-1813 — a new world each run. The production path no longer calls transitionToNewCycle. When the harvest opens, concludeRun (accountRecord.ts) folds the run into the account record and saveAccount (accountStore.ts, key threadbare.account.v1) keeps it. In the record, carried echoes degrade, this run's echoes join under run<N>_ ids, the fundament blends toward the god (FUNDAMENT_BLEND_PER_RUN 0.1) and back toward even (FUNDAMENT_REVERT 0.05), two sealed hungers stir, and the next seed is derived. Wake in a new world runs resetDecisionCache(), and App enters Remembrance on nextWorldSeed. initializeGameStateFromIdentity(…, account) then tilts the cosmology profile (WORLD_TILT_GAIN) and loads the live echoes, so the card echo reaches the repertoire. The paragraphs below describe transitionToNewCycle, which is kept for its tests.
  • transitionToNewCycle (cycleEnd.ts): degrades every surviving echo by ECHO_DEGRADATION_RATE 0.15 (fade at ECHO_FADE_THRESHOLD 0.9, then pruned), keeps the survivors, appends the new cosmic echoes, and binds a chronicle Volume from this cycle's chronicleEntries — titled with the doom-identity chapter title when available (doomIdentityMatrix.chronicleChapterTitles, THR-21) and closed with the harvest summary. Echo-thread appearances are recorded. Then cycle+1, tick:0, phase:'transition', and the per-cycle logs reset. The new cycle also gets a fresh Doom clock — same archetype and stage definitions, DEFAULT_DOOM_TICKS long, starting at stage 1 with every doom-identity milestone un-fired, and already awake (wokeAtTick: 0, THR-1646: a later cycle has no opening to protect, and a surviving First is already bonded) (THR-1642; before this the expired clock carried over and the next cycle fell straight back into the Unmaking on its first tick).
  • No fresh cosmos. The function does not re-run worldgen — the same graph continues into the next cycle. A regenerated world seeded by the last one's echoes is DESIGN (and blocks on the unwired collectInjections, see the red callout).

The World-Soul ledger — Fundament & Resonance IMPL (carry) · DESIGN (the rest)

  • worldSoul.ts defines a full persistence model: Fundament (a 12-sphere sphereWeights coefficient ledger + cycleCount) and Resonance (up to MAX_RESONANCE_MEMORIES 10 curated ResonanceMemory fragments, each degrading per cycle). It ships helpers — applyFundamentShift, blendFundaments, captureMemory, degradeMemories, initiateTwilight, buildHarvestOutcome, executeCycleTransition — all pure and unit-tested.
  • Resonance is written IMPL (THR-1381). At the harvest concludeRun fades every carried memory by RESONANCE_DEGRADE_PER_RUN (0.15, gone after seven ages), prunes the gone, then captureMemorys up to three of this age's (captureAgeMemories): how it ended (doom_scar 0.9 / mandate_triumph 0.85), the sphere the world leaned to most (sphere_dominance 0.7), and the god's first divine echo (divine_intervention 0.8). Each stores its rendered line. Traces resonance.memory_captured / resonance.memory_evicted. The fundament blends at the same moment (THR-1813). Showing carried memories in the next world is THR-1971 (DESIGN). applyFundamentShift, executeCycleTransition, buildHarvestOutcome and initiateTwilight still have no non-test caller.

Where it runs

  • Per-tick: phaseSphereAggregation (orchestrator.ts:3024) and the doom-expiry twilight trigger phaseDoomExpiry (orchestrator.ts:2338). The twilight → harvest → transition sequence itself is driven from the React layer in useSimulation.ts (doTick's twilight branch, the phase-watch effect, and handleBeginNextCycle), not from a registry tick phase — because it is a between-cycles flow, not a per-tick one.
