Twilight, Echoes & the World-Soul

Every world ends. When yours does, you do not fight it — you witness it. What the age was gets weighed, a few bright things are kept, and the next world begins as a response to the last. This is how a run closes, and how one run becomes an ingredient of the next.

How a run ends — you witness, you do not actWhen the Doom clock runs out, the world enters its last chapter: the Unmaking. Your part changes. You stop shaping and start watching the ending you have been steering toward all along.

A run does not end with a defeat screen. It ends with the Unmaking — a twilight the world passes through before it is gone. When your Doom clock reaches its end, the world tips into this closing phase: a short stretch of quiet, waning turns that carry the story to its close. The mood is the game's — cosmic melancholy, not failure. You were never the protagonist who could be beaten; you were the god who watched a world live and now watches it end.

In these last turns your hand lightens. The Unmaking is a passage, not a battle: the world lives out its final turns and then falls still, and you look on. It is the deliberate opposite of the frantic finale most games reach for — an ending you inhabit rather than one you scramble against.

Planned — the world at its weakest

The design intent is for these closing turns to be weakened ones, where the god's touch no longer lands as surely — a felt loss of grip as the world unravels. Today the twilight is a plain countdown; the harder-to-act penalty is planned, not yet in play. The Designer Notes below say exactly what runs and what is still on the drawing board.

The harvest — what the age leaves behindWhen the twilight ends, the world's most significant things are gathered as echoes: the mortals who mattered, the places that mattered, the artifacts that mattered. A few survive the ending. The rest pass on with the world.

After the last turn comes the harvest: the world is weighed, and its brightest few threads are preserved as echoes. An echo is not the thing itself — the world is ending, and everything in it ends with it — but a remembered shape of it, strong enough to carry into what comes next. Echoes come in three kinds, one for each kind of thing a world holds:

Legacy

An echo of a mortal who mattered — a life whose deeds and bonds left the deepest mark on the age.

Monument

An echo of a place — a settlement, a ruin, a wonder that became a fixed point in the world's story.

Relic

An echo of an artifact — an object whose passage through the age gathered enough weight to outlast it.

How many echoes survive depends on how the age ended. A world carried to a fulfilled purpose ends in a triumphant age and keeps the most. A world that simply ran out of time ends somber and keeps the fewest. Anything between is bittersweet. The ending is not just a verdict on your play — it decides how much of this world reaches the next.

Which things become echoes

Echoes are chosen by significance, not by you directly — the world offers up the mortals, places, and artifacts that were most woven into its story, and the strongest are preserved. Alongside them, the age is meant to grant a few divine echoes — traces of the kind of god you were, chosen by your hand. That personal harvest is planned: the closing screen shows the slots, but the choosing arrives in a later update.

The World-Soul rememberingBeneath every world is the World-Soul — the deep substrate that outlives any single cycle. The harvested echoes fold into it, and the chronicle of the run is bound into a finished volume. Nothing that mattered is simply deleted.

The world you played was one expression of something older: the World-Soul, the substrate from which each cosmos is drawn and to which each returns. When a world ends, its harvest does not vanish — the surviving echoes settle into the World-Soul, and the run's story is bound shut as a completed chronicle volume: the book of that age, kept even though the age is over.

Echoes carried this way are not permanent. Each cycle they endure, they fade a little, until the faintest of them are finally let go. A Legacy that shaped one world may still colour the next, and perhaps the one after — but given enough cycles, even the brightest memory softens and is released. The World-Soul remembers, but it does not hoard.

Planned — the deeper ledger

The design reaches further than what runs today. The World-Soul is meant to keep a coefficient ledger — a running sense of which cosmic forces have grown strong across many worlds — and a set of curated memory fragments drawn from each run's chronicle, which would tilt the tone and balance of the next cosmos. Those layers are designed and tested but not yet feeding the game; what carries across cycles right now is the echoes and the chronicle. The Designer Notes name exactly which part is which.

The next world — a response, not a sequelA new cycle is not "level two." It is the next answer the World-Soul gives, shaped by what the last world became — and, one day, by the kind of god you chose to be. The end of one run is the seed of another.

When you choose to go on, the world turns over into a new cycle. This is not a fresh start that forgets everything, nor a sequel that continues the same plot. It is meant as a response: the next world reflecting the shape of the last, its echoes surfacing as familiar names, half-remembered places, and objects whose story reaches back beyond living memory. The chronicle grows another volume; the long book of your play keeps being written.

This is where the game's longest promise lives — the metaprogression idea that the kind of god you were becomes an ingredient of what comes next. A world you ruled with mercy and a world you unmade in shadow should not seed the same successor. That promise is the horizon the run-end systems are built toward.

Honest scope — how much of "the next world" runs today

Today a new cycle carries its echoes and its chronicle forward and counts up — but the successor world is the same one continued, not a freshly re-drawn cosmos, and the echoes do not yet reshape it. The mechanism for an echo to seed the next world's culture, places, or quests is built but not yet switched on. The "next world as a response" is real as a direction and partial in practice. The Designer Notes below draw the line precisely.

The load-bearing honest call for this page

Echoes carry across cycles, degrade, and enter the chronicle — but they do not yet mechanically seed the next world. Each EchoDefinition is built with an injection hook (echo.ts: legacy→cultural_template, monument→location_feature, relic→quest_seed), and collectInjections gathers the active ones — but collectInjections has no non-test caller. Nothing consumes the injections, so an echo's mechanical influence on the next cosmos is DESIGN. Likewise transitionToNewCycle (cycleEnd.ts) does not re-run worldgen — it returns {...state, cycle+1, tick:0, phase:'transition'}, degrading echoes and binding a chronicle volume but continuing the same graph. The player layer above is written around what actually runs (echoes preserved + chronicle bound + cycle counter advanced), with the "next world reshaped by the last" marked planned.

Per-tick sphere aggregate IMPL

  • phaseSphereAggregation(state) runs once per tick (orchestrator.ts:3024) and writes state.worldSoul.aggregate — a SphereAggregate of totalBySphere (weighted sum of every entity's sphere scores), the dominantSphere, and entityCount. This is the only live field of GameState.worldSoul; the Fundament and Resonance layers on the same object are unwired (below).

Twilight — the Unmaking IMPL (entry + countdown) · DESIGN (weakened play, other triggers)

  • Entry is doom-expiry-only. phaseDoomExpiry (orchestrator.ts:2338): when doomClock.expired && phase==='playing', it sets phase:'twilight' and fires the "The Unmaking begins. The world trembles." popup. The two other designed triggers in worldSoul.ts's UnmakingTrigger — mandate_complete and player_concession — are not wired as entry points (DESIGN).
  • Twilight is a plain countdown. runTwilightTick (cycleEnd.ts) increments a counter each tick until TWILIGHT_TICKS (game-config.ts = 7), then sets phase:'harvest'; useSimulation.ts drives this (doTick's twilight branch). The designed weakened play — a per-action success penalty during the Unmaking (worldSoul.ts TRIGGER_PENALTY 0.4/0.3/0.2, via initiateTwilight's successPenalty) — has no wired consumer; grep successPenalty finds no non-test caller. Today the twilight applies no penalty (DESIGN).

Harvest — significance-scored echoes IMPL

  • computeHarvest(state) (cycleEnd.ts) runs when twilight completes. It scores every individual actor, location, and artifact node by computeSignificanceScore(edgeCount, eventCount, nodeType) — edges held plus chronicle entries that name the node — and keeps the top few as cosmic echo candidates.
  • Harvest type is set by how the age closed: triumphant if mandateState.completed, else somber if doomClock.progress > 0.9, else bittersweet. Cosmic echo counts scale with it — 5 / 4 / 3 (triumphant / bittersweet / somber) — and a one-line chronicleSummary is generated naming the brightest echo.
  • Echo kinds. echo.ts nodeTypeToEchoType maps actor→'legacy', location→'monument', artifact→'relic' — the player-facing Legacy / Monument / Relic. Each EchoDefinition takes the top-two cosmology spheres as sphereAffinities and a built (but unconsumed) injection hook.
  • Surface. HarvestScreen (GameView.tsx:3753, fed by harvestResult in useSimulation.ts) shows the age title, the summary, the preserved cosmic echoes as cards (name, summary, sphere tags, significance %), and a Begin Next Cycle button.

Divine echoes DESIGN

  • computeHarvest returns divineEchoSlots (3 / 2 / 1 by harvest type) and HarvestScreen renders them, but the copy reads literally "(selection coming in a future update)". handleBeginNextCycle calls transitionToNewCycle(..., [] /* divine echoes */, ...) (useSimulation.ts:134) with an empty divine array — the player cannot yet choose the god's-own echoes. Cosmic echoes ship; divine echoes are the personal-harvest slot awaiting its picker.

Cycle transition — echo persistence & chronicle binding IMPL · DESIGN (re-seeding)

  • transitionToNewCycle (cycleEnd.ts): degrades every surviving echo by ECHO_DEGRADATION_RATE 0.15 (fade at ECHO_FADE_THRESHOLD 0.9, then pruned), keeps the survivors, appends the new cosmic echoes, and binds a chronicle Volume from this cycle's chronicleEntries — titled with the doom-identity chapter title when available (doomIdentityMatrix.chronicleChapterTitles, THR-21) and closed with the harvest summary. Echo-thread appearances are recorded. Then cycle+1, tick:0, phase:'transition', and the per-cycle logs reset.
  • No fresh cosmos. The function does not re-run worldgen — the same graph continues into the next cycle. A regenerated world seeded by the last one's echoes is DESIGN (and blocks on the unwired collectInjections, see the red callout).

The World-Soul ledger — Fundament & Resonance DESIGN

  • worldSoul.ts defines a full persistence model: Fundament (a 12-sphere sphereWeights coefficient ledger + cycleCount) and Resonance (up to MAX_RESONANCE_MEMORIES 10 curated ResonanceMemory fragments, each degrading per cycle). It ships helpers — applyFundamentShift, blendFundaments, captureMemory, degradeMemories, initiateTwilight, buildHarvestOutcome, executeCycleTransition — all pure and unit-tested.
  • None of it is wired. Every one of those functions has no non-test caller. GameState.worldSoul.fundament / resonance exist on the type but are never shifted by play nor blended at harvest in the running game — the wired cross-cycle memory is the echo/chronicle path above, not this ledger. Treat the entire Fundament/Resonance layer as designed-but-dormant.

Where it runs

  • Per-tick: phaseSphereAggregation (orchestrator.ts:3024) and the doom-expiry twilight trigger phaseDoomExpiry (orchestrator.ts:2338). The twilight → harvest → transition sequence itself is driven from the React layer in useSimulation.ts (doTick's twilight branch, the phase-watch effect, and handleBeginNextCycle), not from a registry tick phase — because it is a between-cycles flow, not a per-tick one.
